## Summary

The CCSDS procedure for **requesting, assigning and relinquishing Spacecraft Identifiers (SCIDs)**, the field that names a spacecraft in TM, TC, AOS, Proximity-1 and USLP transfer frames. It matters to any project that needs a registered SCID and to anyone reading the SCID field on the [space link identifiers](../ccsds/space-link-identifiers.md) page.

The key idea of issue 7 is the **Qualified SCID**: Q-SCID = frequency band . Transfer Frame Version Number (TFVN) . SCID. Each TFVN and each ITU frequency band forms a separate namespace, so one spacecraft may hold several Q-SCIDs. SANA tries to give it the same number in all of them. Table 1-1 gives the SCID size per frame version: 10 bits for TM/TC (version 1), 8 bits for AOS (version 2), 10 bits for Proximity-1 (version 3) and 16 bits for USLP (version 4, TFVN `1100`). Table 2-1 maps ITU near-Earth and deep-space downlink allocations to SANA frequency band designators such as S-NE, X-DS and Ka-DS.

Section 3 sets out the roles of the CCSDS Secretariat, the Agency Heads of Delegation, the Agency Representatives (ARs) and SANA, the assignment manager. Requests go through the online SANA form. SANA does not reserve blocks of numbers or assign SCIDs to simulators. Every assignment also returns a permanent ISO **Object Identifier (OID)**, because SCIDs are reused. Archived data should be labelled with the OID rather than the SCID. ARs must relinquish Q-SCIDs within two months of the end of operations, and freed numbers go to the bottom of the stack. Annex A describes the SANA Spacecraft Identifier registry columns and its registration rules.


## Key points

- Issue 7 changed the document from a Recommended Standard (Blue Book) to a Recommended Practice (Magenta Book) and introduced the frequency band as a qualifier of the SCID. *(PDF p. 7)*
- Technical Corrigendum 1 (July 2019) adds USLP (version-4) transfer frames and ITU-defined frequency ranges. *(PDF p. 8)*
- The procedures must be followed by every organisation that needs a SCID to use CCSDS space communication protocols, and by SANA, which registers the identifiers. *(PDF p. 10)*
- GSCID = TFVN . SCID and Q-SCID = FB . TFVN . SCID ('.' is concatenation); the OID is a permanent ISO identifier, whereas the SCID is valid only for the mission's operational lifetime. *(PDF p. 11)*
- SCID sizes by TFVN: version 1 (00, TM and TC) 10 bits, 0-1,023; version 2 (01, AOS) 8 bits, 0-255; version 3 (10, Proximity-1) 10 bits, 0-1,023; version 4 (1100, USLP) 16 bits, 0-65535. *(PDF p. 12)*
- Each TFVN needs its own SCID assignment table and each frequency band per TFVN its own Q-SCID table; SANA attempts, without guarantee, to assign the same SCID in every domain. *(PDF p. 14)*
- SANA no longer assigns SCIDs for simulation, testing or ground simulators, which do not radiate; missions may self-assign such SCIDs internally. *(PDF p. 15)*
- Table 2-1 maps ITU bands to SANA frequency band designators, for example S-NE (2.200-2.290 GHz) and S-DS (2.290-2.300 GHz), X-NE (8.025-8.400 GHz) and X-DS (8.400-8.450 GHz), and Ka-DS (31.8-32.3 GHz); only downlink allocations are shown. *(PDF p. 16)*
- The management system has four elements: the CCSDS Secretariat, the Agency Heads of Delegation, the Agency Representatives and SANA. *(PDF p. 18)*
- ARs must relinquish all Q-SCIDs no later than two months after the end of the spacecraft's operational phase. *(PDF p. 19)*
- After telemetry is received, the SCID should be replaced by the OID as the permanent label of the data, because SCIDs are reused and identifying archived data by SCID is problematic. *(PDF p. 20)*
- Requests are submitted by the designated AR through the online SANA form, stating the TFVN and the uplink and downlink frequency bands; one form per Q-SCID request. *(PDF p. 21)*
- SANA assigns codes on availability; specific numbers are honoured only exceptionally; each Q-SCID is unique within its TFVN and frequency band during its assignment period. *(PDF p. 21)*
- Every Q-SCID request also returns a unique persistent OID; reservation of blocks of IDs for unspecified spacecraft is not accepted, and protoflight models or simulators may get OIDs but not separate SCIDs. *(PDF p. 22)*
- Relinquished SCIDs are placed at the bottom of the available stack to maximise the time before reuse. *(PDF p. 23)*
- The SANA Spacecraft Identifier registry records spacecraft name, channel, version number, SCID, GSCID, OID (1.3.112.4.7...), requestor, status, transmit and receive frequency bands, and expected launch and end dates. *(PDF p. 24)*
