## Summary

The CCSDS Recommended Standard for the **Synchronization and Channel Coding Sublayer** on return (and optionally forward) links that carry fixed-length [TM](../ccsds/tm-sdlp.md), [AOS](../ccsds/aos.md) or [USLP](../ccsds/uslp.md) Transfer Frames. It sits between the data link protocol and the RF and modulation layer, and it defines three functions: error-control coding with frame validation, synchronization, and pseudo-randomization. This is the document a ground-station or decoder engineer uses to turn a stream of coded symbols back into Transfer Frames. The rationale is in the companion Green Book [130.1-G-3](ccsds-130-1-130x1g3e1.md).

The coding sections specify:

* the rate-1/2, K=7 convolutional code (G1 = 171 octal, G2 = 133 octal, G2 inverted) and its punctured rates 2/3 to 7/8;
* the (255,223) E=16 and (255,239) E=8 Reed-Solomon codes in Berlekamp dual-basis representation, with interleaving depths 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8 and virtual fill;
* concatenated RS outer and convolutional inner coding;
* Turbo codes (rates 1/2 to 1/6, information blocks of 1784 to 8920 bits);
* LDPC coding of a Transfer Frame: the (8160,7136) rate-223/255 code and the AR4JA family with rates 1/2, 2/3 and 4/5 and k = 1024, 4096 or 16384;
* LDPC coding of a stream of Sync-Marked Transfer Frames, with Code Sync Markers.

Section 9 defines the Attached Sync Marker (0x1ACFFC1D, plus longer markers for turbo and LDPC) and the CADU. Section 10 makes the new 131071-bit randomizer (h(x) = x^17+x^14+1) the default and keeps the legacy 255-bit sequence for backward compatibility. Section 11 fixes the allowed frame lengths for each coding option, section 12 tabulates the managed parameters, and section 13 covers use on ground-to-space and space-to-space links. Annexes give the ChannelAccess service primitives, patent notices, the LDPC generator circulants and the Berlekamp-to-conventional RS transformation.


## Key points

- Issue 5 (September 2023) replaces issue 4 (April 2022); its substantive change is a revised pseudo-randomizer to avoid spectral spikes on high-data-rate links. *(PDF p. 7)*
- Scope: synchronization and channel coding for TM, AOS and USLP Transfer Frames over the CCSDS RF physical layer (401.0-B), on space-to-ground, space-to-space or ground-to-space links. *(PDF p. 13)*
- Bit numbering: Bit 0 is the first transmitted bit and the MSB of any binary field. *(PDF p. 19)*
- Frame validation uses the RS or LDPC decoder's own decision; if neither code is used, the TM/AOS/USLP Frame Error Control Field must be present. *(PDF p. 23)*
- Basic convolutional code: non-systematic, rate 1/2, K = 7, connection vectors G1 = 1111001 (171 octal) and G2 = 1011011 (133 octal), with inversion on the G2 output; punctured rates 2/3, 3/4, 5/6 and 7/8 use no inversion. *(PDF p. 27)*
- Reed-Solomon E=16 gives the (255,223) code and E=8 the (255,239) code; allowable interleaving depths are I = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8, normally fixed on a Physical Channel for a Mission Phase. *(PDF p. 32)*
- The 32-bit ASM 0x1ACFFC1D is used for uncoded, convolutional, RS, concatenated, rate-7/8 LDPC and SMTF-stream LDPC data; 64-bit 0x034776C7272895B0 for rate-1/2 turbo and rate-1/2, 2/3, 4/5 LDPC; longer markers for rate-1/3, 1/4 and 1/6 turbo. *(PDF p. 66)*
- The embedded-data-stream ASM (for example frames played back inside another stream) is 0x352EF853. *(PDF p. 68)*
- The ASM is never presented to the RS, turbo or LDPC encoder or decoder, and it is not randomized. *(PDF p. 68)*
- Randomization is required unless the designer verifies proper operation without it; its presence is fixed per Physical Channel and known a priori by the receiver. *(PDF p. 69)*
- The default pseudo-random sequence is 131071 bits long, generated by h(x) = x^17 + x^14 + 1 and seeded with '11000111000111000'; the legacy 255-bit sequence h(x) = x^8+x^7+x^5+x^3+1 (all-ones seed) may still be used for backward compatibility. *(PDF p. 70)*
- Frame lengths: uncoded or convolutional-only frames may be any number of octets up to 2048 (TM/AOS) or 65536 (USLP); RS frames are L = (255-2E-q)*I octets; turbo frames are 223, 446, 892 or 1115 octets; rate-7/8 LDPC frames are 892 octets and rate-1/2, 2/3, 4/5 LDPC frames 128, 512 or 2048 octets. *(PDF p. 73)*
- All managed parameters are fixed for all Transfer Frames on a Physical Channel during a Mission Phase. *(PDF p. 76)*
- The service (Annex A) is ChannelAccess.request/indication; the receiving end gets a Quality Indicator (uncorrectable error) and a Sequence Indicator (frames lost through loss of frame sync). *(PDF p. 83)*
- The turbo-code patent (US 5,446,747, France Telecom/TDF) expired in 2012; the rate-1/2, 2/3 and 4/5 LDPC codes are covered by US Patent 7,343,539 (Caltech). *(PDF p. 86)*
