## Summary

The overview paper of the Astronomy & Astrophysics Solar Orbiter special issue, written after launch on 10 February 2020. It summarises the four top-level science questions, the spacecraft, the ten instruments, the mission design and the science operations approach.

Table 1 gives the mission summary: closest perihelion 0.28 au and solar-inclination milestones of 18, 24 and 33 degrees. The nominal mission starts with the Earth gravity assist in November 2021 and lasts 7 years including cruise, with a 3-year extension. The data downlink is 150 kbit/s at 1 au, stated as the requirement, with higher performance in orbit. Section 3 describes the heat shield, whose front reaches about 500 °C at perihelion, and the solar arrays, which rotate about their long axis for temperature control. Section 5 describes the sequence of Venus resonances and the resulting perihelion distances and inclinations.


## Key points

- Launched on 10 February 2020 at 04:03 UTC on an Atlas V 411 from Cape Canaveral (Table 1). *(PDF p. 4)*
- Solar inclination milestones: 18 degrees (first reached 22 March 2025), 24 degrees (28 January 2027), 33 degrees (24 July 2029); closest perihelion 0.28 au (Table 1). *(PDF p. 4)*
- Data downlink: 150 kbit/s at 1 au spacecraft-Earth distance, a requirement with higher in-orbit performance (Table 1). *(PDF p. 4)*
- Nominal mission duration 7 years including cruise, the nominal mission starting with the Earth gravity assist; extended mission 3 years (Table 1). *(PDF p. 4)*
- The heat shield's front reaches around 500 °C at perihelion; the solar arrays can be rotated about their longitudinal axis for temperature control (§3). *(PDF p. 18)*
- Venus resonances 5:4, 4:3, 3:2, 3:2 and 3:2 progressively raise the solar inclination; the last Venus gravity assist yields 33.4 degrees (§5). *(PDF p. 25)*
