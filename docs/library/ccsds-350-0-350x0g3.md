## Summary

A CCSDS Green Book that guides mission planners on **where and how to add security** to a mission built on CCSDS protocols, and on what that costs in interoperability. It is the overview behind the [security in CCSDS space links](../ccsds/security.md) page and puts [SDLS](../ccsds/sdls.md) in context.

Section 2 introduces the vocabulary: confidentiality, integrity, authentication, access control and availability, the System Security Policy, threat assessment, and accidental versus intentional and passive versus active threats. Section 3 maps five implementation points onto the CCSDS stack (Physical, Data Link, Network, Transport and Application). It sketches the likely requirements for high-, moderate- and minimal-security missions; even minimal-security missions are expected to authenticate telecommands. Section 4 explains the mechanisms: symmetric and asymmetric encryption, key management, digital signatures and message digests, integrity check values, anti-replay sequence numbers, access control, and RF jamming as an availability threat.

Section 5 compares five options:

* Physical Layer bulk encryption with TRANSEC;
* Data Link security with SDLS, showing which TC, TM and AOS services it protects or leaves unprotected (SDLS does not apply to Proximity-1);
* IPSec at the Network Layer;
* Space Packet security, with the packet header left in clear and a replay weakness;
* Application Layer security (TLS, the DTN Streamlined Bundle Security Protocol).

Section 6 analyses the effect of each option on visible fields, on emergency commanding (whether hardware-decoded emergency commands should be authenticated) and on [SLE](../ccsds/sle/index.md) return and forward services. It gives selection guidance: usually encrypt at one layer only. Annex A summarises real implementations: the ESA Copernicus Sentinel telecommand authentication (MAP-based, with a Logical Authentication Counter, a 128-bit MAC and OTAR through a custom PUS service), ISS AOS uplink 3DES, STRV-1c/d and the ESA ATV.


## Key points

- Issue 3 renames the report from 'The Application of CCSDS Protocols to Secure Systems' (issues 1 and 2, 1999 and 2006) to 'The Application of Security to CCSDS Protocols'. *(PDF p. 6)*
- The report mainly addresses security of the space-ground and ground-space data link; ground systems are assumed to use off-the-shelf solutions such as IPSec, and risk-assessment methods are out of scope. *(PDF p. 9)*
- Passive threats compromise only confidentiality and leave no trace; active threats modify information or system state, violating integrity or availability. *(PDF p. 14)*
- Security implementation points on the CCSDS stack are the Physical, Data Link, Network, Transport and Application Layers; the choice depends on agency policy, mission security and operational requirements, the CCSDS standards used and on-board capability. *(PDF p. 15)*
- Missions are grouped as high, moderate or minimal security; even minimal-security missions are expected to need authentication and integrity of all telecommand data. *(PDF p. 18)*
- Integrity of individual data units uses an Integrity Check Value (CRC, SHA-2, or a keyed hash or AES CMAC); integrity of a stream uses sequence numbers or time stamps against replay. *(PDF p. 22)*
- RF jamming threatens availability; spread spectrum and frequency hopping (TRANSEC) are the counter-techniques. *(PDF p. 23)*
- Bulk encryption at the Physical Layer encrypts the whole CLTU with acquisition and idle sequences on the uplink, and the whole CADU stream on TM or AOS. *(PDF p. 25)*
- SDLS (355.0-B) provides authentication, encryption or authenticated encryption for TM, TC and AOS frame data fields, inserting a Security Header and optional Trailer; it can be applied per Virtual Channel and is not applicable to Proximity-1. *(PDF p. 27)*
- For TC, SDLS fully protects the MAP Packet, MAP Access, VC Packet and VC Access services, authenticates some primary-header fields, and does not protect COP control frames or frames inserted through the VC Frame or Master Channel Frame services. *(PDF p. 29)*
- For TM, SDLS does not protect the VC and MC Operational Control Fields or the MC Frame Secondary Header; for AOS, it does not protect the OCF or the Insert service. *(PDF p. 29)*
- IPSec ESP adds an 8-byte clear header (4-byte SPI and 4-byte sequence number), padding, pad-length and next-header fields and an optional ICV. *(PDF p. 31)*
- Space packet security encrypts the packet data field (optionally the secondary header) and keeps the primary header in clear; without an authenticated replay counter in the header, packets are open to replay. *(PDF p. 31)*
- Confidentiality impact: Network Layer security leaves everything except the packet data field in clear; SDLS encrypts the frame data field including packet headers; bulk encryption hides everything, including ASM and EDAC. *(PDF p. 34)*
- Emergency commands acted on directly by the hardware command decoder pose a trade-off: no authentication opens a security hole, while authentication lengthens commands that must be very short. *(PDF p. 34)*
- In figures 6-1 and 6-2, frame-level SLE services (RAF, RCF, FCLTU, OCF and FSH services and others) are marked 'V' for all options, while services that extract frame contents (Return Bitstream, Return Space Packet, Forward TC VCA, Forward Space Packet, Forward Bitstream, Forward VCA) are marked 'U' when Data Link security ends at the SLE client; the 'V'/'U' legend is not explained in the text. *(PDF p. 36)*
- Bulk encryption denies all cross-support services unless keys are pre-placed at the access points, and it makes CCSDS synchronisation and channel coding ineffective. *(PDF p. 37)*
- Most missions should encrypt at only one layer; full traffic-analysis protection needs Physical Layer encryption, and Data Link security lets RAF and FCLTU extend the secure link into the ground network. *(PDF p. 39)*
- The ESA Copernicus Sentinel telecommand authentication works at the TC segmentation layer, with a Logical Authentication Counter and a 128-bit MAC in an authentication trailer, master and session keys, and Over-The-Air Rekeying through a custom PUS service. *(PDF p. 41)*
- The ISS encrypts its AOS uplink with 3DES at the VCA sublayer, using a 64-bit insert zone for cryptographic synchronisation; the ATV used 3DES encryption plus a Time Authentication field in TC packets. *(PDF p. 42)*
