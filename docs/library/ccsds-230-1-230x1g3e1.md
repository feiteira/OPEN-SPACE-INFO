## Summary

The CCSDS Green Book that explains and justifies the [TC Synchronization and Channel Coding](../ccsds/tc-coding-cltu.md) Recommended Standard (231.0-B-4). That standard is the sublayer that turns variable-length [TC](../ccsds/tc-sdlp.md) or [USLP](../ccsds/uslp.md) Transfer Frames into **CLTUs** (Communications Link Transmission Units) for the uplink, and back again on board. It is the most practical companion to the Blue Book, for spacecraft telecommand-decoder designers and for ground engineers who build CLTUs (for example behind an [SLE FCLTU](../ccsds/sle/fcltu.md) service).

Section 2 lists what the sublayer does at each end. Section 3 explains the (63,56) BCH code: 56 information bits, 7 inverted parity bits and a filler bit per 64-bit codeword, frames split seven octets at a time, 0x55 fill, and why the older 40, 48 and 56-bit codewords were dropped. Section 4 explains how the short-blocklength (128,64) and (512,256) LDPC codes were chosen. Sections 5 and 6 give the CLTU format for BCH (start sequence 0xEB90, tail C5 C5 C5 C5 C5 C5 C5 79) and for LDPC (64-bit start sequence, optional tail). Section 7 covers the Physical Layer Operations Procedures PLOP-1 and PLOP-2. Section 8 walks through the on-board CLTU reception state machine (Inactive, Search, Decode). Section 9 covers the TC pseudo-randomizer, section 10 systematic retransmission for deep-space links, and section 11 gives extensive performance data: frame rejection and undetected-error rates, and LDPC decoders such as layered BP and most-reliable-basis.

The annexes give the theory behind the randomizer and tail sequences, performance of obsolete features, a go-back-n analysis with multiple copies, BCH undetected-error calculations and, most usefully, worked hexadecimal examples of real TC Transfer Frames and CLTUs with and without randomization.


## Key points

- The report is informational; CCSDS 231.0-B-4 (July 2021) is the controlling Recommended Standard. *(PDF p. 13)*
- TC Synchronization and Channel Coding is used below the TC Space Data Link Protocol or USLP for variable-length frames; TM Synchronization and Channel Coding handles fixed-length frames. *(PDF p. 17)*
- The sending-end sublayer adds error protection, delimits data units, lets the receiver resolve bit ambiguity, and (through the PLOPs) controls acquisition and idle bit patterns. *(PDF p. 18)*
- A CLTU carrying one maximum-length (1024-octet) TC Transfer Frame has 147 BCH codewords and is 1186 octets long. *(PDF p. 24)*
- A BCH codeword is 56 information bits, 7 complemented parity bits and one filler bit (always 0); I0 is transmitted first. *(PDF p. 24)*
- Codeword lengths of 40, 48 and 56 bits were dropped in June 2000 in favour of the 64-bit codeword, which has 14% bandwidth overhead (25% for the old 40-bit option). *(PDF p. 25)*
- Frame data go into BCH codewords seven octets at a time regardless of frame boundaries; the last codeword is padded with alternating 0/1 fill (0x55), up to six octets. *(PDF p. 25)*
- Uplink uses are grouped into four modes (emergency, command/ARQ, file upload, human support); the (128,64) and (512,256) LDPC codes target the first two and can replace BCH with little impact elsewhere. *(PDF p. 28)*
- With BCH coding, the CLTU Start Sequence is 0xEB90 and the 64-bit Tail Sequence is C5 C5 C5 C5 C5 C5 C5 79. *(PDF p. 35)*
- With LDPC coding, the Start Sequence is the 64-bit pattern 0347 76C7 2728 95B0; the tail sequence is optional with the (128,64) code. *(PDF p. 38)*
- PLOP-2 is required for missions whose planning began after September 2010; PLOP-1 sends an acquisition sequence before every CLTU, costing throughput but avoiding missed CLTU boundaries. *(PDF p. 45)*
- The TC randomizer (Bit Transition Generator) is an 8-bit LFSR, h(x) = x^8 + x^6 + x^4 + x^3 + x^2 + x + 1, initialised to all ones; with BCH it randomizes the 56-bit information fields (not the parity, start or tail sequences), and with LDPC it is reset for each codeword after encoding. *(PDF p. 63)*
- Annex F gives worked hexadecimal examples of TC Transfer Frames (with FECF) and the resulting BCH- and LDPC-coded CLTUs, with and without randomization. *(PDF p. 134)*
