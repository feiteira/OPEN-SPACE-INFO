## Summary

A short Alcatel Space technical note for the Herschel/Planck payload module (PLM) test campaign. During those tests the warm units of the instruments were driven through a chain made of the instrument EGSE (I-EGSE), the spacecraft Central Checkout System (CCS), the PLM SCOE and a CDMU DFE (a front-end that stands in for the spacecraft Command and Data Management Unit). Because the DFE does not reproduce the full CDMU behaviour, the note records which PUS services the test set-up actually provides.

The core is a one-page table (section 3.1) that walks through service numbers 1 to 22 of the Herschel/Planck PUS tailoring. For each it states whether the DFE supplies it for telecommands and for telemetry, and which subservices or CCS functions cover it. Only services 1 (TC verification, subtypes 1-1 and 1-2), 9 (time management, subtypes 9-3, 9-4 and 9-5) and 17 (connection test) are simulated by the DFE. Everything else is either not used or 'not supplied': the DFE then acts as a mailbox and passes the packets on, and the CCS provides the function. On-board scheduling, OBCPs and event/action are replaced by CCS TCL test sequences.

Section 3.2 adds that every TM packet received by the DFE is wrapped into a PIPE message (PIPE trailer plus body) and forwarded to the CCS and to the instrument EGSEs on the CCS LAN. An engineer finds here a concrete example of how a ground test environment substitutes on-board PUS services with checkout-system functions.


## Key points

- The test set-up chains the instrument EGSE (I-EGSE), the CCS, the PLM SCOE / CDMU DFE and the instrument warm units; the note exists because the CDMU DFE does not completely simulate the CDMU. *(PDF p. 5)*
- Service 1 (TC verification) is provided for TM with subtypes 1-1 and 1-2. *(PDF p. 6)*
- Service 9 (time management) is supplied for TC with subtypes 9-3, 9-4 and 9-5; subtypes 9-6, 9-7 and 9-9 are not supplied. *(PDF p. 7)*
- Service 17 (test) is available for TC and TM (17-1, 17-2) by default. *(PDF p. 6)*
- 'Not Supplied' means the service is not supplied by the CDMU DFE but by the CCS, with the DFE acting as a mailbox that transfers messages. *(PDF p. 6)*
- On-board operations scheduling (11), on-board control procedures (18) and event/action (19) are covered by CCS TCL test sequences, for example time-tagged TCs are generated by a dedicated CCS test sequence. *(PDF p. 6)*
- Service 3 packets can be sent to the CDMU DFE to update instrument packets, but not to update the DFE's own packets. *(PDF p. 6)*
- Services 4, 7, 10, 13 and 22 are marked 'Not Used'; services 20 (Information Distribution CDMS) and 21 (Science Data Transfer) are TM routed by the CCS to the I-EGSE. *(PDF p. 6)*
- Any TM packet received by the CDMU DFE is put into a PIPE message (PIPE trailer plus PIPE body) and sent to the CCS and to the instrument EGSEs on the CCS LAN. *(PDF p. 7)*
