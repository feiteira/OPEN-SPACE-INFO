## Summary

The short CCSDS Recommended Standard that fixes **which cryptographic algorithms CCSDS missions use** for confidentiality, authentication and authenticated encryption. It does not say where in the stack to apply them; that is left to mission planners and to documents such as [350.0-G-3](ccsds-350-0-350x0g3.md) and the [SDLS](../ccsds/sdls.md) Blue Book ([355.0-B-2](ccsds-355-0-355x0b2.md)). Its choices are the algorithms behind SDLS baseline modes and many [CCSDS security](../ccsds/security.md) designs.

Section 2 gives the background: why civilian missions now need security, symmetric hash-based and cipher-based MACs, digital signatures and authenticated encryption (AEAD). It stresses that encryption without authentication is dangerous and highly discouraged.

The normative sections 3 and 4 make these choices:

* **Encryption**: AES for all missions, a 256-bit key for missions planned after this issue (128-bit allowed for existing implementations), and Counter Mode.
* **Authenticated encryption**: GCM with a 128-bit MAC.
* **Hash-based MAC**: HMAC (FIPS 198-1) with SHA-256 normally; SHA-224/384/512 or RIPEMD-160 by prior agreement; SHA-1 forbidden; truncation discouraged and only by agreement.
* **Cipher-based MAC**: AES-CMAC (256-bit key for new missions), with GMAC allowed when a GCM implementation is used for authentication only.
* **Digital signatures**: DSS, preferably RSA (PKCS #1 v2.1) with 4096-bit keys for new missions (2048-bit for existing), with DSA or ECDSA allowed.

Annex A discusses the threats and consequences of omitting confidentiality or authentication. It notes that all the algorithms are public domain with no known patents.


## Key points

- One symmetric encryption algorithm and one mode are recommended for all CCSDS missions; several alternative authentication/integrity algorithms are offered; how, when and where to apply them is left to mission planners. *(PDF p. 9)*
- Key management is outside the scope of the standard (see the Space Missions Key Management Concept, 350.6-G-1). *(PDF p. 9)*
- Applicable to all civilian missions needing confidentiality, authentication or authenticated confidentiality; security is encouraged especially on command links, subject to a risk analysis. *(PDF p. 10)*
- The use of encryption without authentication is highly discouraged for CCSDS missions. *(PDF p. 16)*
- All CCSDS missions shall use AES (FIPS 197, ISO/IEC 18033-3) for encryption; missions planned after issue 2 shall use 256-bit keys, while existing implementations may use 128-bit keys. *(PDF p. 17)*
- The mode of operation shall be Counter Mode; when encryption with integrity and origin authentication is needed, GCM shall be used with a 128-bit MAC. *(PDF p. 17)*
- HMAC (FIPS 198-1) with SHA (FIPS 180-4), normally SHA-256; SHA-224, SHA-384, SHA-512 or RIPEMD-160 may be used by prior agreement, and SHA-1 shall not be used. *(PDF p. 18)*
- HMAC output should not be truncated; any truncation must be agreed a priori by the communicating entities. *(PDF p. 19)*
- A cipher-based MAC shall be CMAC with AES (256-bit key for new missions; 128, 192 or 256 bits for existing ones); GMAC may replace CMAC when an authenticated-encryption implementation is used for authentication only. *(PDF p. 19)*
- Digital signatures shall follow DSS (FIPS 186-3), preferably RSA (PKCS #1 v2.1) with 4096-bit keys for new missions and 2048-bit for existing ones; DSA and ECDSA are also allowed. *(PDF p. 19)*
- Spacecraft without access to a key server can carry a pre-loaded public key cache or receive public keys by upload after launch. *(PDF p. 20)*
- If on-board security services fail and there is no bypass mode, communications may be lost entirely. *(PDF p. 22)*
- All referenced algorithms are in the public domain, and no patents are known to apply; no SANA action is required. *(PDF p. 23)*
