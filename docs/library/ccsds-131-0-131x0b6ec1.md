## Summary

Issue 6 of the CCSDS Recommended Standard for the **Synchronization and Channel Coding Sublayer** under fixed-length [TM](../ccsds/tm-sdlp.md), [AOS](../ccsds/aos.md) and [USLP](../ccsds/uslp.md) Transfer Frames. It supersedes the library's [issue 5](ccsds-131-0-131x0b5.md) and is the reference for anyone building or configuring a telemetry encoder, decoder or frame synchroniser. The rationale is in the Green Book [130.1-G-3](ccsds-130-1-130x1g3e1.md).

The sublayer now has four functions: error-control coding with frame validation, **Transfer Frame slicing**, synchronization and pseudo-randomization. The new section 3 is the main change. With Reed-Solomon, Turbo or LDPC coding, each frame may get a 32-bit Attached Sync Marker (ASM). The stream of Sync-Marked Transfer Frames is then cut into Information Blocks of the code's length, and each randomized codeword carries its own Code Sync Marker (CSM). Without slicing, one frame fills one codeword, and ASM and CSM are the same marker.

The codes themselves are unchanged from issue 5:

* the rate-1/2, K=7 convolutional code (171/133 octal) and its punctured rates;
* (255,223) and (255,239) Reed-Solomon with interleaving depths 1 to 8;
* concatenated coding;
* Turbo codes with rates 1/2 to 1/6;
* the (8160,7136) and AR4JA-family LDPC codes.

Turbo coding gains an optional row-column **channel interleaver** of depth 4 against burst errors. Section 9, 'Codeword Synchronization', tabulates the CSM patterns and the CADU. Randomization is now mandatory: the 131071-bit sequence is the default and the 255-bit one remains for legacy systems. Sections 11 and 12 give the allowed frame lengths and the managed parameters with and without slicing. Section 13 covers uplink and space-to-space use. The annexes give the ChannelAccess service, patents, LDPC circulants and the Berlekamp transformation.


## Key points

- Issue 6 (April 2026) replaces issue 5 (September 2023): it adds a channel interleaver for Turbo codewords, allows slicing with all block codes and makes the pseudo-randomizer mandatory; Editorial Correction 1 is dated May 2026. *(PDF p. 7)*
- The sublayer provides four functions: error-control coding including frame validation, Transfer Frame slicing, synchronization, and pseudo-randomizing. *(PDF p. 20)*
- Transfer Frame slicing is optional with R-S, Turbo or LDPC coding, fixed for a mission phase, and must not be used with uncoded data or convolutional coding alone. *(PDF p. 28)*
- With slicing, each M-bit Transfer Frame is prefixed with the 32-bit ASM 0x1ACFFC1D to form an SMTF; the SMTF stream is cut into k-bit Information Blocks, encoded, randomized (mandatory) and given CSMs. The ASM cannot be confused with the identical 32-bit CSM because the ASM is randomized. *(PDF p. 28)*
- Without slicing each Transfer Frame is one Information Block, there is a single level of synchronization, and the terms CSM and ASM are interchangeable. *(PDF p. 29)*
- With no coding, convolutional coding or Turbo coding the FECF is mandatory for frame validation; with R-S, concatenated or LDPC coding the decoder may validate, and with slicing a frame is invalid if any part of it lies in an invalid Information Block. *(PDF p. 30)*
- Basic convolutional code: non-systematic, r = 1/2, K = 7, G1 = 1111001 (171 octal), G2 = 1011011 (133 octal), with symbol inversion on the G2 output path; puncturing gives one of the rates 2/3, 3/4, 5/6 or 7/8. *(PDF p. 32)*
- Reed-Solomon: E = 16 gives the (255,223) code and E = 8 the (255,239) code; allowable interleaving depths are I = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8. *(PDF p. 36)*
- Turbo channel interleaver (7.3.12): a row-column interleaver of depth 4 with Nc = 4/r = 8, 12, 16 or 24 columns, external to the Turbo encoder's own bit permutation; its use is a managed parameter and it is highly recommended for tumbling spacecraft or solar scintillation. *(PDF p. 51)*
- The CADU is the CSM plus the randomized Transfer Frame, R-S codeblock, Turbo codeword or LDPC codeword, formed before any convolutional encoding. *(PDF p. 65)*
- CSM patterns: 0x1ACFFC1D (uncoded, convolutional, R-S, concatenated, rate-7/8 LDPC); 0x034776C7272895B0 (rate-1/2 Turbo and rate-1/2, 2/3, 4/5 LDPC); 96-, 128- and 192-bit markers for rate-1/3, 1/4 and 1/6 Turbo. *(PDF p. 67)*
- The CSM is never presented to the R-S, Turbo or LDPC encoder or decoder; it is attached directly to the codeword or codeblock. *(PDF p. 68)*
- The CSM synchronizes the pseudo-randomizer; the sequence starts at the first bit after the CSM, and the CSM itself is not randomized. *(PDF p. 70)*
- The pseudo-randomizer is mandatory for all combinations of CCSDS modulation and coding; the note records telemetry link problems on missions that did not use it. *(PDF p. 69)*
- The pseudo-random sequence is 131071 bits long, h(x) = x^17 + x^14 + 1, seeded with '11000111000111000'; the legacy 255-bit sequence h(x) = x^8 + x^7 + x^5 + x^3 + 1 (all-ones seed) may be used for backward compatibility but may cause spectral lines at 1/255 of the symbol rate. *(PDF p. 70)*
- No frame-length constraint applies when slicing is used, when data are uncoded or with convolutional coding alone (maximum 2048 octets for TM, 65536 for AOS and USLP); without slicing R-S frames are L = (255-2E-q)I octets, Turbo frames 223, 446, 892 or 1115 octets, rate-7/8 LDPC frames 892 octets and rate-1/2, 2/3, 4/5 LDPC frames 128, 512 or 2048 octets. *(PDF p. 73)*
- Managed parameters now include Randomizer (Long 131071 bits or Short 255 bits), Slicing (Present/Absent) for R-S, Turbo and LDPC, and Channel Interleaver (Present/Absent) for Turbo; all are fixed on a Physical Channel for a Mission Phase. *(PDF p. 77)*
- Turbo codes suit power-constrained links beyond low-Earth orbit; LDPC codes suit high-data-rate, bandwidth-limited links; either may be selected for AOS or USLP ground-to-space links. *(PDF p. 79)*
- The service (Annex A) is ChannelAccess.request(Frame) and ChannelAccess.indication(Frame, Quality Indicator, Sequence Indicator); only one user may use the service on a Physical Channel. *(PDF p. 81)*
