## Summary

The CCSDS Green Book that explains the **concept, design rationale and operation of the [Space Data Link Security (SDLS) protocol](../ccsds/sdls.md)** (CCSDS [355.0-B-2](ccsds-355-0-355x0b2.md)). SDLS adds authentication, encryption or authenticated encryption to the frames of the TC, TM, AOS and USLP data link protocols without changing those protocols. Read it next to the Blue Book to understand why SDLS looks the way it does.

Section 2 gives the motivation. SLE already protects the ground leg, so SDLS secures the remaining end-to-end link between mission control system and spacecraft, without breaking SLE frame services (FCLTU, RAF, RCF, ROCF). Hence frame headers stay in clear. Table 2-2 lists, service by service, what SDLS can protect. OCFs, Insert Zone data, VC and MC Frame services and COP-1 control (BC) frames are never protected, and the VC Frame Secondary Header is authenticated only. The section also covers Encrypt-then-MAC, the danger of encryption without authentication, the three key-management schemes (preloaded keys, OTAR, on-board key derivation) and the 32-octet overhead limit.

Section 3 explains several design choices:

* why SDLS sits between the frame functions and COP-1 (it is applied before the FOP at the sending end and after the FARM at the receiving end, so that COP retransmissions are not rejected as replays);
* how it plugs into TC, TM, AOS and USLP;
* the Security Header (16-bit SPI, optional IV, sequence number and pad length) and the Trailer (MAC);
* managed parameters and Clear Mode.

Section 4 walks through Security Associations, sending and receiving processing, sequence-counter windows and the **baseline modes**: TC authentication with AES-CMAC, and TM, AOS and USLP authenticated encryption with AES-GCM. Both use 256-bit keys and 128-bit MACs, as derived in Annex A. The other annexes map ISO security services to each protocol, reproduce the 2012 User Requirements Document and analyse error-detection performance.


## Key points

- Issue 2 (January 2024) replaces issue 1 (June 2018) and aligns the report with the SDLS Extended Procedures and the current TC, TM, AOS and USLP Blue Books. *(PDF p. 6)*
- SDLS protects the SDUs carried by the space data link protocols and selected protocol data structures, within the constraints of compatibility with the data link and SLE services. *(PDF p. 13)*
- Target topology: a mission control centre and a spacecraft linked through a ground station; SLE security protects the ground network, and SDLS protects the end-to-end data link between the mission control system and the spacecraft. *(PDF p. 16)*
- For space-to-space links, SDLS works master-slave with no negotiation: directives always flow from a predetermined Initiator to a predetermined Recipient. *(PDF p. 17)*
- TC MAP Packet and MAP Access, TM and AOS VC Packet and VCA, and USLP MAP Packet, MAP Access and MAP Octet Stream protection is mandatory; TC VC Packet and VCA and AOS Bitstream are optional; Insert, MC_OCF, VC Frame and MC Frame are not supported. *(PDF p. 18)*
- Three security services: authentication (integrity, source authentication, anti-replay), encryption (confidentiality only) and authenticated encryption (all of these). *(PDF p. 19)*
- VC Frame Secondary Header data can only be authenticated; OCF services, VC and MC Frame services, the Insert service and COP Management (BC frames) are not protected. *(PDF p. 21)*
- SLE compatibility requires FCLTU, Forward TC Frames, RAF, RCF and Return OCF to keep working, which is possible only if the full frame header is left unaltered. *(PDF p. 22)*
- Encryption without authentication gives no protection against data substitution and can give a false sense of security. *(PDF p. 23)*
- SDLS assumes Encrypt-then-MAC composition; authenticated encryption is the CCSDS-recommended way to provide both authentication and confidentiality. *(PDF p. 24)*
- Requirements: Clear and Secure modes per logical channel, coexisting clear and secure channels, compatibility with three key-management schemes (all keys preloaded, OTAR of session keys, on-board derivation from master keys), and at most 32 octets of overhead per Transfer Frame. *(PDF p. 26)*
- SDLS is applied before the COP at the sending end and after it at the receiving end; otherwise COP-1 go-back-N retransmissions would be discarded as replays and the MAC would reject frames that the FARM should handle. *(PDF p. 27)*
- The Security Header starts with a mandatory 2-octet Security Parameter Index; up to 65534 Security Associations per Master Channel are possible, with SPI values 0 and 65535 reserved. *(PDF p. 46)*
- With GCM-type algorithms that use an incrementing counter as IV, the transmitted IV also serves as the sequence number, and the separate Sequence Number field is omitted. *(PDF p. 47)*
- A Clear Mode on a protected SA is not recommended, but civilian missions may justify it; it can be realised as a 'no-op' algorithm SA, keeping header and trailer to avoid throughput changes. *(PDF p. 51)*
- Because TM, AOS and expedited TC cannot guarantee in-sequence delivery, the receiver accepts sequence numbers within a window, so gaps do not block the link. *(PDF p. 66)*
- TC baseline: authentication with AES-CMAC over VC ID and frame data, 32-bit anti-replay sequence number sent in-line, 256-bit key, 128-bit MAC. *(PDF p. 66)*
- TM baseline: authenticated encryption with GCM over VC ID and frame data, 32-bit anti-replay sequence number, 256-bit key, 128-bit MAC and 96-bit IV. *(PDF p. 67)*
- In the TM baseline, the IV is an incrementing counter, so the separate Sequence Number field is not needed; GCM needs no padding, and a single key serves both authentication and encryption. *(PDF p. 68)*
- Annex A's analysis gives MAC/key lengths of 78/107, 98/127 and 118/147 bits for medium, low and lowest risk and selects 128 bits for both; the baseline key length was later doubled to 256 bits against Grover's algorithm. *(PDF p. 78)*
- Annex D: the TC requirement of at most one undetected-error frame in 10^9 is met without a mandatory CRC (BER &lt; 10^-4 in TED mode, &lt; 10^-5 in SEC mode); ESA's SEC mode with mandatory CRC achieves below 10^-15. *(PDF p. 130)*
