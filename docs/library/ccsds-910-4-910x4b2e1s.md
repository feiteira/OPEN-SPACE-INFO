## Summary

The **Cross Support Reference Model (CSRM), Part 1** is the root document of the CCSDS [Space Link Extension (SLE)](../ccsds/sle/index.md) family. It defines the concepts and vocabulary that the SLE transfer service specifications ([RAF, RCF, ROCF](../ccsds/sle/return-services.md), [FCLTU](../ccsds/sle/fcltu.md), [FSP](../ccsds/sle/fsp.md)) and the SLE service management standards build on (for the ESA ground-station side, see [NCTRS and NIS](../scos2000/nctrs-nis.md)). The reference model assumes a single mission and a single spacecraft whose telemetry and telecommand follow the CCSDS space link standards.

Section 2 defines cross support (one agency using another agency's resources) and sets out the SLE documentation tree. It also covers the six security areas SLE must address: privacy, integrity, authentication, access control, availability and auditing. Section 3 describes the space mission data system: space element, SLE system and the Mission Data Operation System (MDOS). It describes the data units involved (SL-DUs, SLE-SDUs, data channels) and the full baseline of return, forward AOS and forward telecommand transfer services, with the online (complete and timely) and offline delivery modes. Section 4 gives the architecture model. In the functional view there are eight SLE Functional Groups, from Return Space Link Processing to Forward CLTU Generation. In the cross-support view, SLE Complexes are managed by SLE Utilization Management, and the Service Agreement, Service Package and transfer service instance concepts are defined, along with their time spans. Section 5 is the template for SLE service specifications, with its ports and service production. Annex A tabulates the Packet Telemetry, Telecommand and AOS data units and the return and forward SLE channel trees. The later SCCS service management concept ([CCSDS 902.0-G-1](../library/ccsds-902-0-902x0g1.md)) generalises this model. See also [SLE service management](../ccsds/sle/service-management.md).


## Key points

- SLE services are of two kinds. SLE transfer services move data within the ground data system or between it and the ground sources and sinks. SLE management services control the scheduling and provision of those transfer services. *(PDF p. 12)*
- The model assumes a single mission and a single spacecraft with CCSDS-compliant TM and TC, and ground users affiliated with a single mission management entity. Processing the contents of source packets is out of scope. *(PDF p. 13)*
- Cross support services on the ground are of three kinds: SLE services, ground communications services and ground domain services (for example tracking or exchanging spacecraft databases). Part 1 of the reference model covers SLE. *(PDF p. 20)*
- The SLE data transfer specifications do not define data privacy capabilities. Privacy is left to the mission applications, the underlying communication service, or both. *(PDF p. 22)*
- Authentication uses credentials carried in the transfer service data units, and access control uses the initiator-identifier and responder-identifier parameters. The algorithms are out of scope. *(PDF p. 23)*
- The baseline return SLE transfer services are RAF, Return Insert, Return MC Frame, MC-OCF, MC-FSH, VC Frame, VC-OCF, VC-FSH, Return Bitstream and Return Space Packet. *(PDF p. 32)*
- The forward AOS services are Bitstream, VCA, Space Packet, Proto Transfer Frame, Insert and Coded Transfer Frame. The forward telecommand services are TC-VCA, Forward Space Packet, Telecommand Frame and CLTU. *(PDF p. 33)*
- There are three delivery modes: complete online, timely online and offline. Return online delivery may extend beyond the end of the Space Link session, and forward online delivery may not. *(PDF p. 33)*
- Timely online return delivery gives the user two controls: a delay-notification threshold (Tdelay-notify) and a deletion threshold (Tdelete), above which the queued SLE-SDUs are discarded and the user is told how many. *(PDF p. 34)*
- SLE-FGs are of eight types in three categories. The return ones are Return Space Link Processing, Return Frame Processing and Return Frame Data Extraction. The forward AOS ones are VC Data Insertion and Space Link Processing. The forward TC ones are VC Data Insert, CLTU Generation and Space Link Processing. *(PDF p. 42)*
- The Forward TC Space Link Processing SLE-FG uses CLCWs from MC-OCF or VC-OCF channels to decide whether the physical channel is available. It generates acquisition and idle sequences according to the PLOP in effect and radiates the CLTUs. *(PDF p. 55)*
- An SLE Complex is a set of SLE-FGs under a single management authority. All SLE Complexes interface to a single SLE Utilization Management within the MDOS. *(PDF p. 55)*
- An SLE Service Package is the set of SLE transfer service instances, with their production characteristics, provided by one SLE Complex. Its online services are restricted to one Space Link Session. *(PDF p. 57)*
- The SLE Service Agreement period often covers the whole operational life of a spacecraft and may involve several service packages per day. Shorter agreements can cover only launch support, a manoeuvre or emergency operations. *(PDF p. 63)*
- Annex A maps SL-DUs to their sources. Packet Telemetry has frames, FSH, OCF, VCA_SDU and source packets. Telecommand has CLTU, TC frames, frame data units, segments and packets. AOS has coded frames, insert zone, proto frames, OCF, VCA-SDU, bitstream PDU and space packets. *(PDF p. 84)*
