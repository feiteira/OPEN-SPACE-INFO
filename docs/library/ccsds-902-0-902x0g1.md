## Summary

A CCSDS Green Book that sets out the concept for the **extensible Space Communication Cross Support Service Management (SCCS SM)**. SCCS SM covers the processes and information that a mission (user) and a ground-station network (provider) exchange to agree, configure, schedule, execute and account for space link and ground data transfer services, such as the [SLE](../ccsds/sle/index.md) services used by ESA ground stations. The report explains why the first-generation SM standard (CCSDS 910.11-B-1) was too narrow: it covered scheduling only and could not take new technologies. The new concept defines standard XML **Information Entities** with built-in extension points.

Section 2 is a summary. Section 3 describes the SCCS SM Enterprise Model: the Mission (Earth User and Space User CSSS) and the Provider CSSS, the Utilization Management and Provision Management functions, and functional and physical views, including Delta-DOR and three-way tracking examples. Section 4 gives the mission support lifecycle: Service Catalog Consultation, Service Agreement Development, and a Service Agreement Period with repeating planning/scheduling, execution and accounting stages. Section 5 describes 19 Information Entities, from Service Catalog, Service Agreement and Configuration Profile to Trajectory Prediction, Service Package Request, Schedule of Services and Accounting Report. Section 6 shows how extensibility works through Abstract Service Components, technology-specific Service Components and Functional Resources, using a telecommand mission and an AOS mission as examples. Sections 7 and 8 cover management services and the roadmap of follow-on Blue Books, including the Simple Schedule Format (CCSDS 902.1). Annex B maps the model onto the SLE Cross Support Reference Model ([CCSDS 910.4-B-2](../library/ccsds-910-4-910x4b2e1s.md)). See [SLE service management](../ccsds/sle/service-management.md).


## Key points

- SCCS SM is the set of processes for collecting and exchanging the information needed to negotiate, configure, schedule, prepare, access and report on SCCS services. The Mission and the SCCS service provider carry them out together. *(PDF p. 11)*
- The Version-1 SCCS SM standard (CCSDS 910.11-B-1) focused on requesting and scheduling services and could not gracefully accommodate new technologies. The extensible concept was developed to remove these shortcomings. *(PDF p. 21)*
- The near-term scope is the single-hop Earth-space link (the 'ABA' configuration) and the IOAG Service Catalog #1 services. The Solar System Internetwork is left for later. *(PDF p. 13)*
- The Mission consists of an Earth User CSSS and a Space User CSSS. The Provider CSSS transports Data Link Layer data units between them and tracks the spacecraft. Utilization Management (mission side) and Provision Management (provider side) exchange the service management information. *(PDF p. 22)*
- The mission support lifecycle has three phases: Service Catalog Consultation, Service Agreement Development, and the Service Agreement Period. In the last phase, planning and scheduling, Service Package execution and accounting stages repeat cyclically. *(PDF p. 42)*
- Several Service Agreements, each for a different mission phase (for example LEOP or cruise), may exist concurrently and overlap. *(PDF p. 43)*
- Almost all SCCS SM Information Entities are formatted in W3C XML. *(PDF p. 48)*
- An extensible Information Entity has fixed core parameters and abstract extension points. A valid Information Entity has one extension for every extension point, and provider-unique capabilities can be added as extensions. *(PDF p. 66)*
- Abstract Service Components (ASCs) are the extension points of the Service Agreement and Configuration Profile. They are specialised into technology-specific Service Components, whose management parameters are represented by Functional Resources. *(PDF p. 67)*
- There are six basic ESLT configurations: SLS, SLS data delivery, retrieval data delivery, forward offline data delivery, SLS radiometric and retrieval radiometric. *(PDF p. 68)*
- The layered model used for the ASCs comprises aperture, physical channel, channel synchronization and coding, and space data link protocols. Data stores, such as the Offline Frame Buffer of SLE RAF/RCF, buffer data between sessions. *(PDF p. 68)*
- Standard SCCS Management Services were expected around 2020. Until then, the standard Information Entities can be carried by provider-specific management services. *(PDF p. 101)*
- Streaming, low-latency management services are expected to be built on the CCSDS CSTS Specification Framework. *(PDF p. 102)*
- The roadmap orders the follow-on Blue Books: Simple Schedule Format first, then Planning Data Formats, Trajectory Prediction, Service Request and Service Package, Service Agreement and Configuration Profile, Space Link Event Sequence, Service Catalog, Service Accounting, Management Services, and a Best Practices Magenta Book. *(PDF p. 103)*
- Table 8-1 estimated the Simple Schedule Format Blue Book for late 2014 and the Management Services (Automation) Blue Book for late 2020. *(PDF p. 107)*
- The SCCS SM Enterprise Model evolves from the SLE Cross Support Reference Model. The CSRM SLE Complex became the SCCS Complex (now the Provider CSSS), and the MDOS corresponds to the Earth User CSSS. *(PDF p. 111)*
