    ST[13] Large packet transfer¶

    A space packet may be up to 65 542 octets long, but real spacecraft impose much smaller limits on the packets they exchange with the ground.1 Some requests (a large memory load, a file) and some reports (a big table dump) do not fit. The large packet transfer service type, ST[13] of the Packet Utilization Standard, splits such a "large packet" into smaller parts, carries each part in an ordinary TM or TC packet, and rebuilds the large packet at the other end. The services that produce and consume the large packets need not know that this happened.2 Abbreviations are expanded in the glossary.

    At a glance

    Item Value
    Service type 134
    Subservices (PUS-C) large packet downlink, large packet uplink; at least one, at most one of each5
    Messages (PUS-C) TM[13,1]-[13,3] downlink parts, TC[13,9]-[13,11] uplink parts, TM[13,16] uplink abortion report - all "minimum"18
    Size limit packets up to the CCSDS maximum (about 65 KB); no retransmission23
    Accessible from any other service (like event reporting)6
    PUS-A (2003) clause 16 "Large data transfer service", subtypes 1-16, with acknowledgement, sliding window and retransmission24
    Solar Orbiter service 13 used as a file transfer: uplink to CSW partitions (subtypes 9-11, 13-16 and 128; no 12), downlink from the SSMM (subtypes 129-153)34

    Why the service exists¶

    Ground-to-space protocols usually cap the TM and TC packet lengths below the CCSDS maximum, so a large request can exceed the mission's maximum telecommand packet length and a large report the maximum telemetry packet length.2 Solar Orbiter is a concrete case: a telecommand packet data field holds at most 242 octets (236 of application data) and a telemetry packet at most 4 112 octets.3536 By principle, the sources and destinations of large packets are not supposed to know about these link constraints.2

    From PUS-A to PUS-C¶

    PUS-A had a complete transfer protocol (described below). During the PUS-C revision the working group weighed standardising PUS-A service 13 with or without its "extended packets", a new service 24 limited to 65 KB packets with or without retransmission, both, or leaving the problem to the lower layers.22 The final standard kept number 13 for what the 2015 status presentation calls a simplification of PUS-A large data transfer that only uploads and downloads CCSDS packets of at most 65 KB, without retransmission.23 Recovering lost parts is left to the space link (for example COP-1 on the uplink); bulk file movement belongs to ST[23] file management and CFDP.

    Inference

    The pointers to COP-1, ST[23] and CFDP are this wiki's reading of where the removed functions now live; clause 6.13 itself does not name them.

    Service layout¶

    • Each large packet transfer service contains at least one of the two subservices and at most one of each.5
    • Each provider is hosted by exactly one application process, and an application process hosts at most one provider; when both subservices exist, the on-board downlink sending entity and the on-board uplink receiving entity live in the same application process.5
    • The foundation model makes large packet transfer, like event reporting, accessible to every other service.6

    Each subservice has two entities, one on board and one on the ground:

    Subservice Sending entity Receiving entity
    Large packet downlink on board: splits the large packet, wraps each part in a "downlink part report", downlinks them ground: collects the part reports, rebuilds the packet, delivers it to its destination
    Large packet uplink ground: splits the large packet, wraps each part in an "uplink part request", uplinks them on board: collects the part requests, rebuilds the packet, delivers it to its destination

    Source: §6.13.1.2-6.13.1.3.23

    Large packet downlink subservice¶

    Configuration¶

    Item Meaning
    Maximum concurrent downlinks how many large packets can be in transit at once
    Downlink maximum part size size of each part; bounded by the packet data field of a maximum-length TM packet
    Downlink reception timeout longest wait allowed between two successive part reports at the receiving end
    Resources resources given to the sending entity, declared with the spacecraft architecture

    Sources: §6.13.3.1-6.13.3.2.7

    Sending: splitting into part reports¶

    For each large packet the on-board sending entity assigns a unique large message transaction identifier, splits the packet into parts, numbers the parts with a part sequence number starting at 1, and wraps each part in one report: TM[13,1] for the first part, TM[13,2] for intermediate parts and TM[13,3] for the last part. All parts have the downlink maximum part size except the last, which may be shorter. Part reports go out in increasing sequence number at the highest frequency the operational constraints allow, to a ground application process declared in the space-to-ground architecture.8 The standard assumes large packets are not duplicated on board and leaves the hand-over between the source service and ST[13] out of scope.8

    Receiving: reconstruction on the ground¶

    The ground receiving entity starts a downlink operation on the first part, restarts a reception timer after every successful first or intermediate part, adds each part to the reconstruction, and ends the operation with the last part. If the timer reaches the reception timeout the operation is aborted. When all parts arrived, the large packet is generated for routing to its destination; the receiving entity does not check the large packet's checksum. If the operation was aborted or completed with parts missing, the ground monitoring and control system is notified of the abortion and the missing parts, and the packet and its parts are discarded.9

    Large packet uplink subservice¶

    Configuration¶

    The uplink mirrors the downlink: a declared maximum number of concurrent uplinks, an uplink maximum part size (the user data space of a maximum-length TC packet), an uplink reception timeout between two part requests, and declared on-board resources.11

    Sending: uplink part requests¶

    The ground sending entity assigns a transaction identifier, numbers the parts from 1, wraps them in TC[13,9] (first), TC[13,10] (intermediate) and TC[13,11] (last) and sends them in increasing order to the on-board application process that hosts the receiving entity.12

    Receiving: on-board reconstruction and abortion¶

    The on-board receiving entity starts the uplink operation on the first part, restarts its timer after the first and each intermediate part, ends on the last part, and aborts on timeout or when it detects a discontinuity in the sequence. A successful uplink generates the large packet for routing to its destination (again without checking its checksum). An aborted uplink produces one large packet uplink abortion notification with the reason, reported in a TM[13,16] large packet uplink abortion report, and the packet and its parts are discarded.1314 The observables are the number of on-going uplinks and their transaction identifiers.15

    stateDiagram-v2
  state "idle" as IDLE
  state "receiving (timer running)" as RX
  [*] --> IDLE
  IDLE --> RX : TC[13,9] first part
  RX --> RX : TC[13,10] next part in sequence (restart timer)
  RX --> IDLE : TC[13,11] last part - rebuild and route the large packet
  RX --> IDLE : timeout - TM[13,16], discard
  RX --> IDLE : sequence discontinuity - TM[13,16], discard

    One state machine runs per transaction identifier, up to the declared number of concurrent uplinks.1113

    Inference

    The standard does not say what the receiving entity does with a TC[13,10] or TC[13,11] whose transaction identifier is unknown, nor how a duplicated part is treated; a repeated sequence number is presumably a "discontinuity".

    Message types and data fields (PUS-C)¶

    Message Name Fields
    TM[13,1] first downlink part report large message transaction identifier (unsigned integer), part sequence number (unsigned integer), part (fixed octet-string)
    TM[13,2] intermediate downlink part report same as TM[13,1]
    TM[13,3] last downlink part report same, but the part is a fixed octet-string of deduced size (from the packet length)
    TC[13,9] uplink the first part transaction identifier, part sequence number, part (fixed octet-string)
    TC[13,10] uplink an intermediate part same as TC[13,9]
    TC[13,11] uplink the last part same, last part of deduced size
    TM[13,16] large packet uplink abortion report transaction identifier (unsigned integer), failure reason (enumerated)

    Sources: §8.13.2, Figures 8-140 to 8-146.1617 The widths of the identifier and sequence number fields, and the values of the failure reason, are not stated in the available sources; they are left to the mission. Every message type is "minimum" for its subservice in Annex C.18

    Worked example (illustrative values)

    A mission whose largest TM packet leaves 1 000 octets for the part (the downlink maximum part size P) has to downlink a 9 500-octet large packet. It needs \(n = \lceil 9500 / 1000 \rceil = 10\) parts: TM[13,1] with sequence number 1, TM[13,2] with 2 to 9, and TM[13,3] with 10 carrying the remaining 500 octets. The receiving entity knows the last part's size from the TM packet length.816

    The 2024 Rev.1 draft¶

    A comparison by this wiki of clause 6.13 and of §8.13 in the Rev.1 DIR1 draft with the 2016 issue found no change other than renumbered figures (8.13-1 to 8.13-7); Annex C lists the same message types with the same "minimum" applicability.192021

    PUS-A (ECSS-E-70-41A, 2003)¶

    The PUS-A large data transfer service was a supporting service, used by the ground or other services to move large service data units in a controlled way, avoiding service-specific solutions. Its examples were reporting a command schedule bigger than one packet and loading a large memory area with size-limited telecommands.24 It aimed at three properties: knowing when a transfer is complete, re-transmitting only the lost or erroneous parts, and delivering the parts in order and without duplicates.24

    The service data unit¶

    The unit passed to the service was a TM or TC packet or an extended packet, whose data field could exceed the 65 535-octet limit; an extended packet had the same structure but its packet length field set to 0.26

    Field Type Meaning
    Unit Type enumerated 0 = standard packet, 1 = extended packet
    Packet Header 6 octets standard primary header (length 0 if extended)
    Data Field Header deduced TM or TC data field header
    Packet Data any source data of a large report or application data of a large request

    A Large Data Unit ID distinguished overlapping transfers from or to the same application process. Parts had a fixed part size, smaller than the maximum data field, except possibly the last.2526

    The protocol¶

    • Sliding window (optional): the sender may run ahead of the last acknowledged part by at most "window size" parts; several window sizes may be used, for example a large one for memory management. Without a window, parts are sent at the highest rate allowed.27
    • The receiver tracks the last successfully received part (no gaps before it) and acknowledges it at the window boundary, or only at the end without a window; the final acknowledgement is sent only once the destination has the whole unit.27
    • Acknowledgement timer at the sender after the last part of a window or of the unit, and a reception timer at the receiver after each first or intermediate part; expiry on either side fails the transfer.27
    • Erroneous or missing parts form the set of failed parts, reported to the sender (at the window boundary when a window is used) and re-transmitted with the same sequence number.27
    • Either end, or the ground, may abort at any time.27

    (13,4) TM Downlink Abort Report sender (on board) aborted

    Solar Orbiter¶

    Solar Orbiter (see How Solar Orbiter uses PUS) uses service 13 to transfer files. The uplink goes from the ground to the Central Software (CSW) of the on-board computer, which stores files in "partitions" of the OBC mass memory or the SafeGuard Memory (SGM). The downlink goes from the Solid State Mass Memory (SSMM) to the ground, with structures specified in the SSMM software TM/TC ICD (SOL.A.TAS.ICD.00001, not in this library).3837

    Uplink: files into CSW partitions¶

    An uplinked file is split into a first part carrying the SDU header (file characteristics) and the first data, intermediate parts carrying data, and a last part carrying the last data and a checksum (the ICD's overview calls it a CRC). The ICD states that the reception of all parts can be confirmed at the end of a transfer and that retransmission of the relevant parts can be requested after an unsuccessful one, without saying here which side does what.38 Each file has an attribute:39

    Attribute Meaning
    1 OBCP (on-board control procedure)
    2 TC sequence, delayed
    3 TC sequence, immediate
    4, 5 unused
    6 other
    7 FAT, internal software use only

    Typical uploads are TC files, profiles and ephemerides, and OBCPs; typical downloads are SSMM data files such as science files.39

    The rules: the SDU length of TC(13,9) (header plus data) fixes the length of every TC(13,10) SDU; the last part is shorter; and if the data and the 4-octet checksum do not fit, the checksum may go into another TC(13,11). When the attribute is "TC sequence - immediate", the CSW executes the file as a TC sequence once TC(13,11) completes, creating a TC(134,1) with sequence identifier 1 and an execution rate of 1 Hz.404142

    Message Content (sizes in octets) Purpose
    TC(13,13) Abort Reception of Uplinked Data unit ID (1) aborts a session or confirms an on-board autonomous abort reported by TM(13,16)
    TM(13,14) Uplink Reception Acknowledgement Report unit ID (1), sequence number (2) all parts up to that number received; also reports completion of upload and storage
    TM(13,15) Unsuccessfully Received Parts Report unit ID (1), N (2), N x sequence number (2) sent after TC(13,11) when parts are missing
    TM(13,16) Reception Abort Report unit ID (1), reason (4): 0 file manager storage error, 1 checksum error, 2 file transfer time-out session aborted
    TC(13,128) Change File Transfer Time-out value time-out (4), in seconds private subtype

    Sources: §5.14.4-5.14.8.434445

    Worked example (derived from the ICD limits)

    With the full 236 octets of application data, TC(13,9) has 3 octets of unit ID and sequence number, 13 of SDU header and 220 of file data, so its SDU is 233 octets and every TC(13,10) carries 233 octets of file data. A 10 000-octet file then needs 1 first part (220 octets), 41 intermediate parts (9 553 octets) and 1 last part with the remaining 227 octets plus the 4-octet checksum: 43 telecommands with sequence numbers 1 to 43. This arithmetic is this wiki's, assuming maximum-length commands.354042

    Compared with PUS-C, Solar Orbiter keeps the PUS-A uplink half: acknowledgement (13,14), the failed-parts report (13,15) and abort on both sides, with a file header added inside the first part.3431 It does not, however, define PUS-A's TC(13,12) Accept Repeated Part, which PUS-A Table 30 pairs with (13,15); neither the ICD service table nor the MIB has a subtype 12.343353 How the parts listed in a TM(13,15) are re-sent is not stated in the available sources. The ICD service table also lists subtype 128 as a TM ("Change Timeout value"), whereas §5.14.8 and the MIB (ZCD00D80) define it as a telecommand.344553

    Downlink: SSMM file transfer sessions¶

    The downlink subtypes are all private and implemented only in the SSMM software: start, suspend, resume, terminate and abort a file transfer session (TC(13,129) and 13,131-134), change session parameters, end-of-file-transfer timeout, downlink virtual channel and retransmission limit (13,135-138), report the parameters (13,139/140), and control low-level file transfers (enable/disable, start, suspend, resume, abort, repeat parts, confirm reception, request the end-of-transfer report: 13,141-148), with reports 13,130 and 13,149-153.4634

    In the 2025 MIB the SSMM commands (APID 492) show the mechanism: an LLFT is appended or replaced by time window or data volume with a packet store index (ZSM00075, ZSMX0075, ZSMX1075, ZSM00105), and TC(13,146) "SSMM Repeat LLFT Parts" asks for blocks between a start and an end ID - a selective repeat. The reports are SSMM LLFT start and end-of-file-transfer reports, a retransmission-limit report and an EOFT timeout on APID 490, an FTS parameters report on APID 483 and TM(13,152) "SSMM FDU Layout" carrying the session ID, an FDU counter and packet store data words on APID 493.5354 With the ICD's allocation, 490 is the SSMM (PID 30) with category 10, "file transfer, exclusive for service 13 telemetry", and 493 is category 13, listed as spare.47

    Inference: FDUs and naming differences

    "FDU" is not expanded in the available Solar Orbiter sources; "file data unit" is the ICD title of TM(13,153). The MIB, however, has no TM(13,153): it uses subtype 153 for the TC "SSMM Reset FTS Queue" and carries the FDUs in TM(13,152), which the ICD names "Low-level file transfer abort report". Two other MIB names differ from the ICD titles: subtype 135 is "SSMM Append LLFT" in the MIB but "Change file transfer session parameters" in the ICD, and subtype 142 is "SSMM Replace LLFT" in the MIB but "Start low-level file transfer" in the ICD.46345354 The SSMM ICD (RD9) would settle this.

    On the ground the downlinked files become TM files in the mission file archive (FARC), served by EDDS as plain concatenations of TM packets; extracting the packets into the packet archive is not part of the baseline. Event packets with SPID 4000 record each transfer: transaction ID, packet store ID, status (ACTIVE, COMPLETED, INCOMPLETE, INJECTED, ABORTED and others), FDUs received and expected, retransmissions, times and file name.4849

    The TC(13,9) definition ZCD00D09 fixes the sequence number to 0x0001 and makes the partition and file attribute selectable through text aliases; the file content is a single variable-length octet string (PTC 7, PFC 0), and TC(13,10)/(13,11) have no separate checksum field, so the checksum must be appended to the octet string by whoever builds the commands.55 The ranges allow session IDs 1-255 and sequence numbers 2-65 535 for later parts.55 The reports TM(13,14)-(13,16) are SPIDs 14166-14168 on APID 170, the DMS with category 10, and TM(13,16) decodes the reason through the calibration LDT_FMS_STOR / LDT_CHECKSUM / LDT_TIME_OUT.54

    The s13a, s13p and s13t tables¶

    The 2025 MIB release contains three files that are not MIB tables of the SCOS-2000 import ICD: s13a.dat (7 rows), s13p.dat (11 rows) and s13t.dat (12 rows). They are absent from the April 2020 release. No document in this library mentions them; the column names below are therefore generic.5657

    Inference: what the s13 tables appear to contain

    • s13a = file attributes. COL3 (always equal to COL4) gives the numeric attribute: OBCP 1, delayed TC 2 and immediate TC 3 agree with the ICD's attribute table and with the MIB alias set CCDT0082TX, and payload context 4 agrees with CCDT0082TX.3955 The mnemonics PLAIN, OBCP, DEL, IMM and PAYLOAD match the five values the Solar Orbiter DDID gives for the command-history field s13ObFileAttribute (PLAIN, OBCP, TC_DELAYED, TC_IMMEDIATE, PAYLOAD_CONTEXT).50 The codes for 0, 5 and 6 do not agree: s13a says 0 PLAIN, 5 INVALID, 6 OTHER; CCDT0082TX says 0 FT_INVALID, 5 FT_PS, 6 FT_PLAIN; the ICD says 4-5 unused and 6 other.
    • s13p = destination partitions. COL3 equals the partition values of the TC(13,9) alias set CCDT0192TX, and the descriptions name the OBC mass memory (OMM) sides A and B and the SGM EEPROM.55 The values read naturally in hexadecimal: 0xA1-0xA4 for OMM side A, 0xB1-0xB4 for side B, 0xC1-0xC3 for the SGM.
    • s13t = ground file types. Each row maps a file type to a format tag (COL4), optionally a default attribute (COL6, an s13a COL1 value) and a default partition (COL7, an s13p COL1 value): OBCP files go to OBCA, TC sequence files (CSF types 0-2) to TCFA, the backup MTL (mission timeline) to SYSA.
    • The one-letter codes (s13a COL2 N/O/D/I, s13p COL2 M/B/N, s13t COL2 C/W) and the negative codes in s13t COL3 are not explained by any available source.
    • Taken together, the tables look like configuration for a ground tool that turns files (OBCPs, command sequences, SCOS-2000 command stack files) into service 13 uplinks.

    Service 13 elsewhere on the ground¶

    The SCOS-2000 import ICD states that SCOS-2000 has no specific support for service 13; a mission-specific uplink implementation exists from Rosetta, and other missions need their own implementation to generate service 13 data units and process the reports.52 The Rosetta, Mars Express and Venus Express data delivery ICD shows the trace this leaves in the command history: a column flags commands encapsulated in a service 13 packet, and commands of an aborted transfer are shown as "ABORTED".51 For Solar Orbiter, the EDDS packet TC report carries s13ObFileId (the file a command was uplinked in) and s13ObFileAttribute.50

    SQL used 
    -- solo-2025-09-16.sqlite (MIB__20250916M25925PFMS001_SOL)
SELECT CCF_CNAME, CCF_DESCR, CCF_STYPE, CCF_APID, CCF_NPARS
  FROM ccf WHERE CCF_TYPE = '13' ORDER BY CAST(CCF_STYPE AS INT);
SELECT PID_TYPE, PID_STYPE, PID_APID, PID_SPID, PID_DESCR, PID_TPSD
  FROM pid WHERE PID_TYPE = '13';
SELECT c.CDF_ELTYPE, c.CDF_DESCR, c.CDF_ELLEN, c.CDF_BIT, c.CDF_PNAME, c.CDF_VALUE,
       p.CPC_PTC, p.CPC_PFC, p.CPC_CATEG, p.CPC_PAFREF, p.CPC_PRFREF
  FROM cdf c LEFT JOIN cpc p ON p.CPC_NAME = c.CDF_PNAME
 WHERE c.CDF_CNAME IN ('ZCD00D09','ZCD00D0A','ZCD00D0B','ZSM00075','ZSM00125')
 ORDER BY c.CDF_CNAME, CAST(c.CDF_BIT AS INT);
SELECT * FROM pas WHERE PAS_NUMBR IN ('CCDT0192TX','CCDT0082TX');
SELECT * FROM prv WHERE PRV_NUMBR IN ('VCD00176','VCD00248');
SELECT l.PLF_NAME, l.PLF_OFFBY, p.PCF_DESCR, p.PCF_PTC, p.PCF_PFC, p.PCF_CURTX
  FROM plf l JOIN pcf p ON p.PCF_NAME = l.PLF_NAME
 WHERE l.PLF_SPID IN ('14166','14168','80012','80014','80018');
SELECT * FROM txp WHERE TXP_NUMBR = 'CCDT0027TM';
SELECT * FROM s13a; SELECT * FROM s13p; SELECT * FROM s13t;
