    ECSS Overview Standards

    ECSS: the European space standards system¶

    The European Cooperation for Space Standardization (ECSS) is the body that writes the standards European space projects apply to management, engineering, product assurance and sustainability.12 It is a cooperative effort of the European Space Agency (ESA), national space agencies and European industry associations, and its documents are published by the ESA Requirements and Standards Section at ESTEC in Noordwijk.2 For the subject of this wiki, ECSS matters in three ways: its communications standards (the E-50 series) select and adapt the CCSDS space link protocols; its ground systems and operations standards (the E-70 series) define the Packet Utilization Standard (PUS), the operability requirements a spacecraft must meet, and the monitoring and control data a supplier hands to the operator; and these documents are what a mission control system such as SCOS-2000 and its Mission Information Base are built to serve.

    This page explains how ECSS documents are organised, named and produced, and gives a linked map of the documents relevant to telemetry and telecommand. ECSS publishes its own map of the whole system as the ECSS Trees, a set of charts that this wiki holds in its November 2025 edition; each chart carries its own "as of" date, from June 2024 to November 2025.4957 The detailed pages are:

    Page What it covers

    ECSS-E-ST-70C The parent ground systems and operations standard: operations and ground segment engineering processes and their documents

    What ECSS documents are¶

    ECSS standards open with a near-identical foreword. It says that requirements are stated as what shall be accomplished rather than how the work is organised, and that this lets existing organisations and methods be reused and evolve without rewriting the standards. Its first sentence, which calls each document one of a series meant to be applied together, has changed over time:

    Documents in the library Areas named in the opening sentence
    Issues of 2008 to 2016 (for example ECSS-E-ST-70C, ECSS-E-ST-50-04C, ECSS-E-ST-70-41C) management, engineering and product assurance
    Issues of 2021 to 2023 (ECSS-E-ST-50C Rev.1, ECSS-E-AS-50-22C to -26C) management, engineering, product assurance and sustainability
    Issues from December 2024 (ECSS-E-ST-50C Rev.2, ECSS-E-AS-50-21C Rev.2, the PUS Rev.1 draft, ECSS-E-ST-70-11C Rev.1) sentence omitted

    Sources: the forewords, PDF p. 2 of each.5823594 Each document is prepared by a dedicated working group (for the adoption notices, the ECSS Space Communications Working Group), reviewed by the ECSS Executive Secretariat and approved by the ECSS Technical Authority.2359

    ECSS standards are written to be tailored: each one states that it may be tailored to the characteristics and constraints of a project in conformance with ECSS-S-ST-00.56 The PUS describes itself as a "menu" from which a mission selects services and service levels for each on-board application process.7 ECSS-E-ST-70-11C Rev.1 goes further and says that every mission needs to tailor it, choosing the applicable requirements and adding mission-specific ones.6

    Branches, disciplines and the system documents¶

    The 2021 to 2023 forewords name four areas: management, engineering, product assurance and sustainability.2 The ECSS Trees of November 2025 show the full structure: two system documents at the top, ECSS-S-ST-00 (system description) and ECSS-S-ST-00-01 (glossary of terms), and below them five branches, each divided into numbered disciplines. The letter after "ECSS-" names the branch, and the Trees file each document under the discipline whose number matches the leading digit of the document number: ECSS-E-ST-50C and ECSS-E-ST-50-04C under E-50, ECSS-E-ST-31C and ECSS-E-ST-32C Rev.1 under E-30 (mechanical engineering).4985

    Inference

    None of the forewords in the library mentions the industrialization branch, and the 2008 to 2016 forewords do not mention sustainability either. That the branches were added over time is this wiki's reading of the forewords and the Trees; the dates at which ECSS created the U and I branches are not stated in the available sources.

    Document types¶

    Type Infix Status Example
    Standard -ST- Normative: "shall" requirements (in recent issues each with a unique identifier) ECSS-E-ST-50C Rev.2, ECSS-E-ST-70-41C
    Adoption notice -AS- Normative: applies another body's standard "with the following modifications"; in E-50 the six CCSDS notices, elsewhere ISO standards (the adoption of IEC 61025 on fault tree analysis is numbered ECSS-Q-ST-40-12C, with an -ST- infix) ECSS-E-AS-50-21C Rev.2 to ECSS-E-AS-50-26C; ECSS-E-AS-11C (ISO 16290, technology readiness levels) and ECSS-U-AS-10C Rev.2 (ISO 24113) in the Trees
    Handbook -HB- Informative guidance; not part of the standards, which prevail in any conflict ECSS-E-HB-50A
    Technical memorandum -TM- Listed with the handbooks; the Trees distinguish a handbook that is "published" from a memorandum that is "made available" ECSS-E-TM-10-20A (product data exchange), ECSS-E-TM-10-23A (space system data repository); none in the library
    Draft for public review DIR suffix A draft circulated for public review before publication ECSS-E-ST-70-41C Rev.1 DIR1

    Sources for the rows: the adoption-notice wording; the handbook disclaimer; the Trees charts of the engineering, sustainability, product assurance and handbook series; the DIR change log.11125255545613 What distinguishes a technical memorandum from a handbook beyond that legend is not stated in the available sources.

    Status in the Trees. The legend of each standards chart distinguishes four states: published; affected by the update of another document (M); ongoing (or planned) update of an existing document (U); new document in production (or planned) (N). The handbook charts use "HB published" and "TM made available" with the same U and N marks.525356 In the engineering chart of October 2025, for example, ECSS-E-ST-70-41C is published and its Rev.1 is marked U, and ECSS-E-ST-40-02C (machine learning software engineering) is marked N.52

    Normative words. ECSS-E-ST-70-11C Rev.1 spells out the convention shared by the series: "shall" expresses a requirement, "should" a recommendation (which tailoring turns into a requirement or removes), "may" and "need not" express permission, and "can" is descriptive only. Present and past tense state facts.14

    Requirement identifiers. In the recent documents of the library every requirement carries a unique label that combines the document number and a running number, for example ECSS-E-ST-50_0970031 (the in-sequence telecommand delivery service of ECSS-E-ST-50C, 2021), ECSS-E-ST-70-11_1670115 (essential telecommands without on-board software, Rev.1 of 2025) or ECSS-E-AS-50-25_1560001 (the clause that applies CCSDS 232.0-B-4, 2023).151611 Labels survive revisions: ECSS-E-ST-50_0970031 is unchanged in Rev.2 of 2024.66 The PUS change log lists "naming each requirement to facilitate tailoring, traceability" among the ECSS drafting rules applied in issue C.18 The 2008 to 2011 issues in the library (ECSS-E-ST-50-01C to -05C, ECSS-E-ST-70C, -70-01C, -70-31C, -70-32C) carry no such labels: their requirements are identified only by clause number and letter (for example 5.1b of ECSS-E-ST-50-03C).37

    Inference

    The seven digits after the underscore appear to start with a three-digit code per document: 097 for ECSS-E-ST-50, 167 for ECSS-E-ST-70-11, and 152 to 156 for the adoption notices -21 to -25 (ECSS-E-AS-50-21_1520001 up to ECSS-E-AS-50-25_1560001).151679 The sources do not explain the numbering.

    The C issues of 2008 were produced by applying the new ECSS template, renumbering the requirements and checking consistency with CCSDS and other ECSS standards.202223 For ECSS-E-ST-70C the change was larger: it merged the two parts of ECSS-E-70A (principles and requirements; document requirements definitions) into one document and replaced the phase-based approach with a process approach that separates operations engineering from ground segment engineering, each with its own customer–supplier relationship.71 ECSS-E-ST-50C Rev.2 changes little: its change log says it was done mainly to stop naming the issues of the adopted CCSDS books, so that the standard need not change when CCSDS updates them, and otherwise lists only the foreword, the deletion of twelve definitions, one typo correction and the bibliography.65

    Inference

    The A issues are numbered without the ST infix (ECSS-E-70-41A, ECSS-E-70-11A) and the C issues with it (ECSS-E-ST-70-41C, ECSS-E-ST-70-11C). The change logs link the 2008 C issues to the new ECSS template; that the ST infix itself was introduced by that template is this wiki's reading of the numbering, not a statement in the sources. Documents first published after 2008 also start at letter C: ECSS-E-ST-70-01C of 2010 (A and B "never issued") and the adoption notices of 2021, whose change logs call issue C the "First issue".7274 The letter therefore seems to mark the generation of the ECSS document system rather than count the issues of each document; the sources do not say so.

    How a standard is produced¶

    The PUS gives the most complete record in the library of an ECSS standard's life. In January 2011 the working group for issue C had 190 change requests against issue A. A first draft for public review (DIR1) was reviewed publicly for four months in 2014 and produced 620 discrepancy reports; the status presentation of October 2015 planned delivery of the final draft to ECSS for December 2015, and the standard was published on 15 April 2016.2418 The disposition statistics show 464 review reports from agencies, 156 from industry and 44 from the working group itself.25 The revision now under way follows named steps: Draft for Review (DFR), Draft for public review (DIR, with the public review of ECSS-E-ST-70-41C Rev.1 running from 12 December 2024 to 28 February 2025), draft with implemented review reports, Technical Authority vote for publication, and publication.13 The rules behind these steps are ECSS documents themselves: ECSS-D-00-01C and ECSS-D-00-02A give the drafting rules and templates for standards and handbooks, and ECSS-D-00-11A the criteria for accepting a draft for public review and for releasing a handbook.57 In the Trees of October 2025 the PUS revision is still marked as an ongoing update; its outcome is not stated in the available sources.52

    ECSS and CCSDS¶

    The Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems was formed in 1982 by the major space agencies to standardise space data systems for cross support.26 Its Recommended Standards (Blue Books) are not binding on its members: an agency may adopt them, endorse them or write a related standard of its own.27

    ESA historically did the last of these. CCSDS recommendations were modified for European needs and republished in the ESA PSS series (Procedures, Specifications and Standards), including or excluding options according to European preferences; all PSS documents have since been withdrawn in favour of ECSS standards or CCSDS Recommended Standards.28 ECSS continued the practice for the core telemetry and telecommand standards (ECSS-E-ST-50-01, -03 and -04 of 2008), with two structural differences from CCSDS: the PUS is an ECSS application-layer protocol with no CCSDS equivalent, and the three CCSDS telecommand books were combined into the single ECSS-E-ST-50-04.29 The handbook announced that this practice was being phased out in favour of direct reuse through adoption notices, which may remove options but otherwise apply the CCSDS text, and that ECSS expected to adopt all new communications-related CCSDS recommendations.2930

    That is what happened. ECSS-E-ST-50C lists six adoption notices, ECSS-E-AS-50-21 to -26, which replace ECSS-E-ST-50-01C, -03C and -04C.31 The 2021 Rev.1 named the CCSDS issue each notice was based on; Rev.2 of December 2024 names only the book (for example "CCSDS 131.0-B"), and each notice states which issue applies, so that a new CCSDS issue requires a new revision of the notice but not of ECSS-E-ST-50C.6575

    The PSS documents themselves were "prepared by" ESA boards and approved by the ESA Inspector General: the Standards Approval Board (STAB) for Space Data Communications for PSS-04-106, -107 and -151, and the Committee for Operations and EGSE Standards (COES) for PSS-07-101.81 PSS-04-106 of January 1988 says it is derived directly from the CCSDS Packet Telemetry Blue Book 102.0-B-2 of January 1987, and PSS-04-107 that its first issue (June 1990) was derived directly from the CCSDS Telecommand recommendations.8283 Their full story is on the history page.

    The division of labour, in one paragraph (this wiki's summary of the cited scopes): ECSS-E-ST-50 says how to engineer the end-to-end communication system and what it must achieve (for example that commands are delivered in sequence and that every frame identifies its spacecraft); the adoption notices say which protocol does it; ECSS-E-ST-70-11 says what the spacecraft must let the operator see and do; ECSS-E-ST-70-41 says which standard services and packets do that; and ECSS-E-ST-70-31 says what data must be handed over so that a ground system can drive those services, which in ESA's world ends up as the SCOS-2000 MIB.154547

    How Solar Orbiter used the series¶

    The Solar Orbiter TM/TC and packet structure ICD shows how a mission of the 2010s cited ECSS. Its applicable documents include ECSS-E-70-41A (the PUS of 2003), and its reference documents include ECSS-E-ST-50-03C for the TM transfer frame and ECSS-E-ST-50-04C for the telecommand link, both of July 2008 and both since superseded by the adoption notices first issued on 1 March 2021.483174 The SCOS-2000 Database Import ICD (issue 7.0, 2017) in turn references the older ESA PSS standards, the Packet Utilisation Standard ESA PSS-07-101 of May 1994 and the ESA Packet Telecommand Standard, and, as RD-20, ECSS-E-70-41A with the remark that it "will be replaced by ECSS-E-ST-70-41C".3334 Standards therefore live on in missions and ground systems long after ECSS supersedes them, which is why this wiki covers the superseded documents too.
