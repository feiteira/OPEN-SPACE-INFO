    ECSS-E-ST-70-32C: PLUTO test and operations procedure language¶

    ECSS-E-ST-70-32C ("Space engineering – Test and operations procedure language", 31 July 2008) does two things. It lists the capabilities that any language for automated test and operations procedures has to provide, and it defines a reference language that provides them, the Procedure Language for Users in Test and Operations (PLUTO).12 The standard calls the procedure the principal means by which the end user controls the space system, both in functional testing before launch and in operations after launch.1 Its main promise is reuse: a procedure written once for satellite checkout should carry over to mission operations, and the same language should be used from one mission to the next.19

    PLUTO is not a stand-alone programming language. It works on top of the space system model (SSM) of ECSS-E-ST-70-31C: a procedure refers to system elements, reporting data, activities and events that the SSM already defines, and it only adds the procedural script.13 This page covers the whole standard, then compares PLUTO with what ESA ground systems actually ran: the Tcl-based TOPE language of the SCOS-2000 EGSE, the command sequences of the SCOS-2000 MIB, and the MOIS procedure tools.

    Why a common procedure language¶

    The standard places procedures in the life of a space system. The space system consists of a space segment, a ground segment and a launch service segment, whose elements are used at different times: the electrical ground support equipment (EGSE) during development, the launch service segment before and during launch, and the mission control system and ground stations during operations.8

    • Testing. The standard does not prescribe at which integration level automated procedures are used (that is a decision of each mission's verification approach), but notes that they are generally used from subsystem level upwards. Reusing test procedures across integration levels needs standardised EGSE functions; reusing them in operations needs harmonised requirements for EGSE and mission control systems.9
    • Operations. ECSS-E-ST-70 makes procedures the primary mechanism for mission operations and names two kinds of flight control procedure (FCP): nominal procedures, the building blocks of the timelines and schedules of the flight operations plan (FOP), and contingency procedures, which reconfigure the space system after pre-identified anomalies. FCPs were traditionally run by hand; pressure to reduce routine manpower pushes towards automating their execution.10

    In this standard the EGSE and the mission control system together are the EMCS (EGSE and mission control system). The procedure language and its development and execution environments are part of the EMCS and have direct access to its other monitoring and control functions.11

    FOP and MOP

    ECSS-E-ST-70-32C speaks of the flight operations plan (FOP) of ECSS-E-ST-70. The 2008 issue of ECSS-E-ST-70C, published the same day, calls the corresponding document the mission operations plan (MOP) and does not use the abbreviation FOP.1016 ESOC's own plans used the FOP name: its INTEGRAL plan of 2003 is a "Flight Operations Plan" whose mission rules state that the FOP provides the timelines and the nominal and contingency flight procedures (see ECSS-E-ST-70C).107

    The space system model seen by a procedure¶

    The SSM of ECSS-E-ST-70-31 captures mission knowledge for assembly, integration and verification (AIV) and for operations. Four kinds of SSM object matter to a procedure:1213

    SSM object Meaning Examples given by the standard
    System element A functional element of the space system, from the functional decomposition of the space segment (ECSS-S-ST-00) or of the ground segment (ECSS-E-ST-70) Power subsystem, AOCS, star tracker, optical head; EGSE, MCS, ground station, baseband equipment, telemetry decoder
    Reporting data Data used to assess the functioning of the space system: parameters (the lowest level of elementary information with a meaning for monitoring) and compound parameters (records of parameters, arrays and sub-records interpreted together, for example a whole telemetry packet or an anomaly report) Temperature, Source Count, Background Count Report, Telemetry Lock Status, Go NoGo Status
    Activity A space system monitoring and control function: procedures, telecommands to the space or ground segment, and any EMCS function (a printer request, an e-mail, an ftp transfer); missions may add their own activity types Switch On
    Event The occurrence of a condition or set of conditions during a test session or mission phase, used to trigger a monitoring and control function; associated with system elements, reporting data or activities Undervoltage (of the power subsystem), Switch On Failure (of the star tracker's Switch On activity)

    Reporting data can have several representations during its life: an on-board measurement has an encoded value in telemetry and a raw or engineering value on a ground display.12 For any given configuration of the space system (a stand-alone unit test, a partly integrated spacecraft, a ground segment validated against a simulator) only a subset of the SSM is in use, but the model itself is independent of the configuration.13

    The procedure language refers to system elements, activities, reporting data and events but does not define them; it defines only the procedural script.13 The SSM supplies the common definition of every activity:15

    Activity data in the SSM Procedure-specific data in the SSM
    Name, description, type (procedure, telecommand or operating system call), associated system element Default execution mode (manual or automatic)
    Version number and configuration history; validation status (testing status) Activation mode (permanently active, or explicitly initiated)
    Validity period (earliest and latest date of execution); mean, minimum and maximum duration The procedural script
    Arguments, each with name, description, engineering units, data type and default value; allowed combinations of argument values
    Planning attributes such as the resource profile (on-board power, downlink bandwidth)

    ECSS-E-ST-70-31C turns this into data requirements: every procedure has an initiation mode (background or foreground) and earliest and latest validity dates; a ground procedure adds a script "written according to" PLUTO and a default execution mode (automatic or manual); an onboard procedure adds a script compliant with ECSS-E-ST-70-01, a "preloaded on board" flag and a high, medium or low priority.80 Arguments therefore live outside the script: a procedure receives them at initiation and cannot change them.21

    Requirements on any procedure language (clause 5)¶

    Clause 5 is the normative heart of the standard. It is written for any language, and PLUTO is the one that satisfies it.

    Procedure structure (§5.1)¶

    Req. Capability
    a, b Build a high-level, goal-oriented activity (a procedure) from elementary activities (telecommands, operating system calls); a procedure may call other procedures
    c, d, e Define self-contained sub-goals, each reached by one activity call or a sequence of them; activities can take arguments passed at initiation
    f A step construct encapsulates complex logic (waiting for a time or a condition, conditional branching)
    g Sub-goals can run in series or in parallel
    h, i, j Preconditions (to be fulfilled before start) and confirmation conditions (whether the goal was met) for procedures, and also for steps
    k–n Contingency actions besides the nominal flow: for a procedure, on space system anomalies, on failure of the nominal path or on other events or conditions; for a step, on failure of its nominal path or on other events or conditions
    o, p A contingency action should be taken at the level where the failure occurs, and should either fix the anomaly or report it and return control to the nominal flow
    q If the anomaly cannot be fixed, one of these actions shall be performed: raise an event to flag it at a higher level; reach the goal another way and terminate; abort
    r While a contingency action runs, the body of the procedure or step is suspended

    Source: §5.1.17

    Language constructs (§5.2)¶

    The 24 requirements of §5.2 list what a procedure must be able to do:18

    • Initiate activities (a–d): request any space system activity; request it and continue at once; or request it and wait for confirmation, then test the result to decide what to do next.
    • Acquire data (e): execution or confirmation status of an activity; initiation, start, termination or completion time of its last execution; the value, validity status, overall or detailed monitoring status (limit, delta, expected-status and status-consistency checks) and sampling time of reporting data; the time of the last occurrence of an event.
    • Replace missing functions (f): when a space system function is not available (for example in an early test phase), set an activity's confirmation status, or a parameter's value, validity status, monitoring statuses or sampling time.
    • Snapshot (g): make a local copy of reporting data so that several properties used by successive statements are sampled at the same instant, avoiding the asynchronous update problem.
    • Generic EMCS access (h): request any operation and read any property of any space system object.
    • Flow control (i): if-then-else, multi-way branching on a parameter or variable, loops (fixed count, while a condition holds, until a condition becomes true), and waits until an absolute time, for an interval, until a condition is true or until an event occurs.
    • Variables and events (j–m): typed local variables, assignment, and local events declared in the procedure or step and raised by it (for example to trigger a contingency action).
    • Expressions and units (n–s): the Annex C functions (should), expressions over constants, parameters from the EMCS, activity arguments and local variables; engineering units on constants, free mixing of compatible units and automatic conversion between them.
    • Comments and users (t–w): comments; messages for user acknowledgement; messages to the procedure execution log; input from the user.
    • Pre- and confirmation conditions (x): any combination of waiting until a time, for an interval, until a condition or until an event, testing a condition, or asking the user for the outcome.

    Language specification (§5.3)¶

    A conforming language shall be formally specified in ISO EBNF (ISO/IEC 14977). PLUTO, with the engineering units and functions of the annexes, should be used for automating procedures in development and exploitation. Using PLUTO ensures conformance with every requirement of the standard and eases the transfer of procedures from functional testing to operations.19

    The structure of a PLUTO procedure¶

    A procedure has five bodies, in a fixed order; only the main body is mandatory:2044

    Body Keyword pair Presence Purpose Execution status while active
    Declaration declare … end declare optional Declares the procedure's local events (procedure level: events only) –
    Preconditions preconditions … end preconditions optional Holds the procedure until, or runs it only if, initial conditions are met "preconditions"
    Main main … end main mandatory Achieves the goal through activities and steps, in sequence or in parallel "executing"
    Watchdog watchdog … end watchdog optional One or more watchdog steps, all initiated in parallel, each watching for one contingency "executing"
    Confirmation confirmation … end confirmation optional Decides whether the goal was achieved "confirmation"

    Preconditions and confirmation bodies accept any combination of: wait until an absolute time, wait for an interval, wait until a condition becomes true, a Boolean if test, and a decision requested from the user; the keyword then chains several conditions.22254563 If an if expression is false, or a wait in the preconditions times out, the procedure (or step) is skipped and its confirmation status becomes "aborted"; a raise-event action after a timeout is not available in a preconditions body, because the watchdog has not started yet.4546 In the confirmation body a false condition or an expired wait gives "not confirmed".63

    The procedure main body may contain only seven statement types: set procedure context, initiate in parallel, initiate and confirm step, initiate and confirm activity, initiate activity, inform user, and log. All computation (assignments, loops, waits) therefore happens inside steps.2349

    The procedure watchdog body detects and reacts to system-level events such as anomalies, keeps procedure-level invariants true for the whole run, and handles failures that a step's own watchdog could not handle. It need not fix everything itself: it can suspend or abort the procedure while another procedure handles the anomaly. Concurrent watchdog steps watch independent conditions; a triggered watchdog step suspends the main body while the others keep monitoring, so contingencies that interact must be handled within one watchdog step. Error handling after a procedure has ended belongs to the caller.24

    Steps¶

    A step has the same five-body structure, with these differences:2651

    • Its preconditions body is optional for a main-body step but mandatory for a watchdog step, where it is a single wait statement that defines the contingency being watched.2628
    • Watchdog steps have no watchdog body of their own.26
    • A step name is unique within its procedure and can be used to refer to the step.26
    • Step-level declarations add local variables (with an engineering value, a validity status and a sampling time, visible in the step, its lower-level steps and its watchdog steps) and enumerated sets, plus local events that only the step's own watchdog body can use. Before its first assignment a variable's validity status is "not available" according to §A.3.9.14, but "invalid" according to §A.1.7.2.2752
    • A step main body accepts the full statement set (below); a procedure main body only seven.2953
    • The step watchdog body handles failures in the step's main body and keeps step-level invariants; error handling after the step ends belongs to the caller.30

    Behaviour: execution and confirmation status¶

    Every procedure, step and activity carries two statuses that the caller can monitor:31

    • the execution status: "not initiated" (steps and activities), "preconditions", "routing" (telecommands only), "executing", "confirmation" or "completed";3168
    • the confirmation status: "not available" until completion, then "confirmed", "not confirmed" or "aborted".31

    Procedure¶

    stateDiagram-v2
  [*] --> Preconditions: initiate
  Preconditions --> Executing: preconditions OK
  Preconditions --> Completed: preconditions not OK (aborted)
  state Executing {
    Main --> Watchdog: contingency detected (main suspended)
    Watchdog --> Main: resume
  }
  Executing --> Confirmation: main body completed, or watchdog "terminate"
  Executing --> Completed: abort (aborted)
  Confirmation --> Completed: confirmation completed (confirmed / not confirmed)

    Execution states of a procedure, after Figure A-2.31

    1. Preconditions. On initiation the preconditions body runs ("preconditions", "not available"). If they hold, the watchdog body starts (all watchdog-step preconditions are initiated) and then the main body; if not, the procedure completes as "aborted". Without user preconditions both bodies start immediately.31
    2. Main body. Statements run in sequence ("executing", "not available"). Each initiate and confirm statement ends with a continuation test whose outcome is: continue; restart the step or activity from its preconditions; raise a local event for a watchdog step; or abort the procedure.31
    3. Watchdog. If no contingency occurs, the watchdog body ends when the main body ends. If one occurs, the main body is suspended: the atomic statement in progress completes, but its follow-on action is not taken. Every statement except initiate-and-confirm step, initiate activity, initiate-and-confirm activity and initiate in parallel is atomic; inside an initiate-in-parallel, all branches are suspended once their current atomic statements end. The watchdog step then returns one of four continuation actions: resume (main body continues where it stopped and the watchdog step is restarted), terminate (the goal was reached another way; main and watchdog bodies end and confirmation starts), raise event (pass the problem to another watchdog step) or abort.31
    4. Two watchdogs at once. A second watchdog step triggered while the first runs executes in parallel; control returns to the main body only when both have finished; each is restarted when it completes; an "abort" from either aborts the procedure at once.31 If the returned actions differ, the user is asked.62
    5. Confirmation. When every step and activity initiated by the main body has completed (including the remaining branches of an "until one completes" parallel statement), the watchdog body ends and the confirmation body runs, giving "confirmed" or "not confirmed".31
    6. Implicit confirmation. Without a confirmation body: after a watchdog "terminate" the procedure takes the watchdog step's confirmation status; otherwise it is "confirmed" only if every activity and step initiated by the main body was confirmed, and "not confirmed" in all other cases. If an initiated step or activity raised an event in its continuation test, the watchdog step that caught it decides.3163

    For a procedure, what happens after it ends is left to the caller; for steps and activities the continuation test inside the calling statement decides.32

  Executing --> Confirmation: execution completed / terminated
  Executing --> ContinuationTest: abort
  Confirmation --> ContinuationTest
  ContinuationTest --> Precondition: restart
  ContinuationTest --> Completed: complete

    Execution states of a step (Figure A-3) and of an activity (Figure A-4) combined; the "Routing" state applies to activities only.3233

    A step runs its preconditions, main and watchdog bodies exactly as a procedure does, then its confirmation body, and finally the continuation test of the statement that initiated it, which maps the confirmation status ("confirmed", "not confirmed" or "aborted") to a continuation action; "restart" sends it back to "preconditions", anything else completes it. Missing preconditions or confirmation use the same defaults as a procedure.32

    An activity is executed by the EMCS, not by the procedure. The EMCS reports progress notifications for the state transitions and for type-specific sub-states, such as the routing stages of a telecommand ("released from EMCS", "reception by ground station", "reception on-board"). Initiate activity does not wait; initiate and confirm activity waits and applies a continuation test.33

    Parallel execution¶

    in parallel until all complete (the default) or in parallel until one completes starts all of its steps and activities at once.50 With "until one completes", the first to finish lets the calling body move on; the others keep running and their continuation actions still apply, except "continue", which then does nothing. With "until all complete", each branch's action is handled as it arrives: "abort" aborts the parent and all other branches at once; "restart" restarts that branch (resetting it to "preconditions" and "not available") and the parallel statement cannot end until it finishes again; "raise event" is raised at once and suspends the main body through a watchdog step.34

    The language¶

    Lexical conventions¶

    • Notation. Each construct is a grammar rule (a non-terminal symbol) defined from non-terminal and terminal symbols, drawn as a railroad diagram (main line mandatory, branch lines optional, return lines repetition, rectangles for non-terminals, rounded boxes for terminals) and repeated in ISO EBNF in clause A.4.3669
    • Case. Only engineering units are case-sensitive; everything else, including keywords, is not. Spaces inside multi-word keywords count as single blanks.3738
    • Comments run from /* to */.37
    • Keywords may be several words ("not confirmed", "execution status", "is contained in"). If an SSM object is named with a keyword, the implementation resolves the ambiguity by grammar precedence, but the standard warns against names that confuse readers.38
    • Identifiers are one or more words separated by spaces; the first word starts with a letter, each word is letters and digits. Switch on Gyro5 in Fine Mode is one identifier. Identifiers are unique in their context (a step name in its procedure); objects elsewhere are reached by an object reference path.39

    Object references¶

    Objects are reached with of chains, read from the specific to the general: Temperature of Catalyst Bed of Thruster X of Thruster Main Branch. A reference may start with an explicit object type (variable, predefined value set, activity statement, step, argument, system element, reporting data, parameter, record, array, activity, event).48 When the type is omitted and a name is not unique in the SSM, the 14 precedence levels resolve it, from highest to lowest: variable, local reporting data, local event, activity statement, step, argument, system element, reporting data, parameter, record, array, activity, predefined value set, system event.48 Inside expressions a bare identifier resolves first to a local variable (searched upwards through the step hierarchy), then to a procedure argument, a function name, and finally an object name.65

    Activity calls, arguments and directives¶

    An activity call names the activity and supplies either explicit arguments or a predefined value set from the operations database, plus optional directives.61 Arguments are simple (an expression, or a reference to an activity, parameter or reporting data, system element or event), records (recursive groups of arguments, as in ECSS-E-70-41) or arrays. Either all arguments are named or none are, in which case their order defines them.61 Directives are specific to an activity type rather than to one activity; for telecommands the standard lists the MAP ID, AD or BD transmission mode, and the verification packets to request.61 Its examples of simple arguments are also PUS-flavoured: a parameter reference for a ST[12] request, a system element (APID) for a ST[14] request, and an activity call as the argument of a ST[11] TC[11,4] insertion into the on-board schedule, where each scheduled command is a record with the telecommand, its release time, sub-schedule and interlock fields.61

    A worked example¶

    Illustrative PLUTO procedure

    The following procedure was constructed for this page from the grammar of clause A.4; it is not taken from the standard or from a mission. All object names are invented.

    procedure
  declare
    event Heater Overcurrent described by "Line current above limit"
  end declare
  preconditions
    wait until Bus Voltage of Power Subsystem > 27 V timeout 2 min
  end preconditions
  main
    initiate and confirm step Warm Up
      declare
        variable Target of type real with units degC
      end declare
      main
        Target := 20 degC;
        initiate and confirm Switch On Heater with arguments Line := 3 end with
          in case not confirmed : restart max times 2; end case;
        wait until Temperature of Camera > Target timeout 30 min;
        log "Camera at ", to string (Temperature of Camera);
      end main
    end step;
    initiate and confirm Switch Off Heater with arguments Line := 3 end with;
  end main
  watchdog
    initiate and confirm step Overcurrent Guard
      preconditions
        wait until Current of Heater Line 3 > 1.5 A
      end preconditions
      main
        initiate and confirm Switch Off Heater with arguments Line := 3 end with;
        inform user "Heater line 3 switched off by watchdog";
      end main
    end step
      in case confirmed : terminate; end case;
  end watchdog
  confirmation
    if Temperature of Camera >= 20 degC
  end confirmation
end procedure

    How it runs, following clause A.2:

    1. The procedure enters "preconditions" and waits up to 2 minutes for the bus voltage. On a timeout it completes as "aborted", because no watchdog exists yet to catch an event.46
    2. When the voltage condition holds, the watchdog step Overcurrent Guard starts waiting on its single wait precondition, then the main body starts ("executing").31
    3. In Warm Up, if the heater command is "not confirmed" the explicit couplet restarts it up to twice; "aborted" falls back to the main-body default "abort"; "confirmed" takes the default "continue".6435
    4. If the line current exceeds 1.5 A, the main body is suspended after its current atomic statement, the watchdog step switches the heater off and, when confirmed, returns "terminate": the main and watchdog bodies end and the confirmation body runs.3135
    5. The confirmation body sets "confirmed" only if the camera reached 20 degC; otherwise the procedure ends "not confirmed", and what happens next is up to its caller.6332

    PLUTO and ESA's SCOS-2000 world¶

    No PLUTO in SCOS-2000; TOPE instead¶

    None of the SCOS-2000 documents in this wiki's library describes a PLUTO interpreter.     Command sequences in the MIB¶

    What the SCOS-2000 MIB itself can store is far simpler than PLUTO: command sequences, ordered lists of commands and nested sequences with formal parameters, defined in the tables csf, css, sdf and csp (see sequences). A nested sequence is expanded like a macro when the sequence is loaded; elements carry release and execution delta times (relative to the previous command or to the sequence start), grouping and blocking flags, interlock scope and stage, and flags to override PTV checks or disable CEV checks.959798

    MOIS and the procedure tools¶

    Procedures at ESOC are prepared outside the MIB. In 2000 ESA presented a "Winfops / MOIS" system built on MS-Access for procedure preparation, with simple automation in tabular form, among the elements offered to Herschel/Planck.87 By 2023 RHEA described ASTRAL-Prepare as the latest evolution of MOIS (used on more than 100 spacecraft), with editors for OBCP scripts, mission planning rules, PLUTO scripts, Writer procedures and synthetic SCOS-2000 scripts.101102 The same paper lists the executable "operations languages" that such tools export (OBCPs in OL, automation procedures in Tcl/TOPE, SPELL and others) and notes that MOIS has generated them for years through its Writer editor; some ESOC SCOS-2000 missions automated routine operations with Writer procedures and the MOIS Scheduler, starting contingency recovery procedures automatically on known events.103104 ESOC's OPEN preparation environment planned to re-engineer MOIS Writer, its editor for flight operation procedures, as an extension, and the 2025 EGS-CC migration plan keeps MOIS or ProToS for the flight control procedures of missions that stay on a SCOS-2000 MIB, with converters for MOIS and ProToS procedures for missions that move fully to EGS-CC.105106 The tools are described on ancillary systems and EGS-CC and operations preparation.

    PLUTO in practice

    The available sources show PLUTO scripts as one of several supported procedure formats in a commercial preparation tool, not as the language of ESOC's SCOS-2000 operations. Whether any ESA mission executes PLUTO procedures operationally is not stated in the available sources.
