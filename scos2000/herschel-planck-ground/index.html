    Herschel/Planck: SCOS-2000 in a science mission ground segment¶

    Herschel (still called FIRST in the documents of 2000) and Planck were two ESA science satellites developed together, with one industrial prime, shared spacecraft units and a common approach to their ground segment. They are the best documented case in this wiki's library of SCOS-2000 used across a whole programme: as the core of the instrument electrical ground support equipment (EGSE) in the instrument teams' laboratories, as the base of the spacecraft Central Checkout System in industry, and as the mission control system at ESOC. The thread that ties these uses together is the SCOS-2000 Mission Information Base. The same ASCII tables had to pass from the instrument teams to the industrial system database, to the checkout system, to the operations centre and into the Herschel science system.1091101123065

    This page reconstructs that story from the documents that the Herschel Science Archive kept in its SPIRE instrument folder, from three Herschel documents on ESA's COSMOS web site (the EGSE integration plan, the common EGSE document overview and the operations interface requirements) and from related papers. It covers the decision of April 2000, the operations interface requirements, the system database and naming rules, the MIB tailoring for the Herschel Common Science System (HCSS), the ingestion of SCOS-2000 command history and out-of-limits files, SPIRE's database configuration control, and the experience of the attitude control subsystem team. A second set of documents, copied on 12 November 2026, adds the programme's interface control documents (the Packet Structure and Space/Ground ICDs, the instrument test router, the EGSE interface requirements, the CCS external interfaces and the TC History ICD), the HPSDB user manual, one HPSDB release note, a printed HIFI MIB and a 2015 retrospective by the prime; this page uses them where they show how the MIB moved between the parties, and Herschel/Planck interface documents reads them bit by bit.125138147154158 The Herschel and Planck mission page covers the missions themselves.

    At a glance

    • April 2000: at an ESOC meeting all FIRST/Planck instrument groups state their intention to use SCOS-2000 as the basis of their EGSE and real-time analysis; ESA offers the software free of charge under licence, with paid support.614
    • 2001-2003: the Herschel EGSE for instrument level tests is assembled from SCOS-2000, gateways, a router, test control and a MIB editor; the operations interface requirements make the SCOS-2000 Database Import ICD the format of the satellite database.2536
    • 2002-2006: a naming convention and the Herschel/Planck System Database (HPSDB) constrain what instrument MIBs may contain so that they survive the "smooth transition" from instrument to spacecraft level.404447
    • 2003-2008: the HCSS ingests MIBs, telecommand history and out-of-limits files from SCOS-2000 into its own archive.6591
    • 2002-2008: the checkout system reads HPSDB "bridge files" in an extended ICD 5.1 format and returns "update files" listing what each test validated; the HPSDB delivers validated models with release notes, change records and checksums (the library holds one such note, for a delivery to ASED in April 2008).134135149151
    • 2015: the prime presents the HPSDB concept, adapted as a System Reference Data Base for a later programme, as validated by Herschel/Planck.158160
    • Archive: the Herschel MIB releases are listed in the ESA Herschel Science Archive, but this wiki could not copy them (see below).

    The decision of April 2000¶

    The meeting¶

    On 18-19 April 2000 ESOC hosted a meeting on "SCOS 2000 and FIRST/PLANCK" with the project (ESTEC), ESOC and the instrument groups and their partners (TESRE/CNR, OAT, MPE, RAL, LAL, LABEN, IAS, SRON and KUL).2 The agenda had eleven items:1

    5 Options for SCOS 2000 systems J-F. Kaufeler

    The background came from the project's evaluation slides. SCOS-2000 version 0.2 had been evaluated on 3-5 November 1999 by the "RTA CWG" as a "framework" for a common real-time analysis (RTA) system of the FIRST and Planck instruments; the expansion of CWG is not stated in the available sources. Since then the instrument groups had considered a wider role, as an EGSE kernel, and PACS had collected the missing RTA functions and expected EGSE features in a "consolidated" delta URD (user requirements document) dated 4 April 2000, which "cannot be considered consolidated at present".7120

    What ESOC presented¶

    The ESOC presentation set out the state of SCOS-2000:89

    • Use. Past: SOHO (telemetry only, 1995), Huygens, TeamSat and MTP for Eumetsat (1997). Present: Integral and MSG (successful SVTs), Rosetta (architectural design phase), ERS (first delivery end 1999, migration from SCOS-1), and PROBA (MCS and EGSE). Future: Smart-1, Envisat and XMM (MOC and SOC), both migrating from SCOS-1, Mars Express, the FIRST/Planck MOC and Cryosat and GOCE (listed as "Earth Core Explorer"); "First/Planck EGSE + PIs ??" closed the list.
    • Name. SCOS-2 up to release R1.4 (until 1998), SCOS-2000 from R2.0 (1999), with a new telecommand chain, architectural consolidation, a user interface fully based on ILOG, systematic use of ObjectStore and CORBA for distribution and external interfaces.
    • Releases. R2.0 in September 1999 (Integral SVTs), R2.1 in March 2000 (MSG SVTs), R2.2 in September 2000 as the end of the nominal project. R3.0 (mid-2001) was to port SCOS-2000 to PCs and Linux, separate applications from the operating system and replace ObjectStore; R4.0 (first quarter 2002) was to allow several SCOS-2000 versions on one machine, for constellations, and add higher-level monitoring.

    The EGSE elements came from PROBA, where SCOS had served both as checkout facility and mission control system (EMCS). They were a router for TM reception, TC transmission and a SCOS gateway; a procedure automation tool based on the free Tcl/Tk; and database editors based on Oracle.10 The router gives location and data transparency, client management with several clients per connection, and message delivery by asynchronous call-back or synchronous polling. The router on a Windows NT PC (Pentium III) handled 1 Mbit/s of TM input with recording (parallel DMA card), 4 kbit/s of TC (RS422 card), at under 15% CPU, with about 2% CPU per distributed virtual channel. The TCP/IP gateway needed no COTS. The TM/TC front end extracted CCSDS packets from a data stream and built the TC stream; with the ground-space protocol it needed a commercial TC encoder shell (about 10 kEUR).11 The Test Procedure Environment (TPE) linked a SCOE system to the SCOS kernel through parameter and command interface servers, with Tcl/Tk procedures able to get parameters, send commands and read the database.12

    ESOC's offer had five software items. Option 1 gave all of them "as is"; option 2 added support and service by TOS-G for items 1-3 and support by TOS-O for items 4-5, with users treated as external customers:13

    # Item Basis
    1 SCOS-2000 ESOC product
    2 Router From PROBA
    3 Tcl/Tk procedure automation tool From PROBA
    4 Database editors MS Access, from Integral
    5 WINFOPS / MOIS MS Access, for procedure preparation and simple tabular automation

    Licence, costs and support¶

    All software was free of charge but needed a formal, non-exclusive licence stating its purpose; the user had to procure and pay for all hardware and COTS licences.14 The licence text handed out on 19 April 2000 lists the items (SCOS-2000; the PROBA EGSE with router, automation tool and Oracle database; the Integral MS Access database; Winfops/MOIS) and four licence types: documentation only, a time-limited trial, the run-time version, or the source code. ESA keeps ownership, so the software cannot be sold on, and points the licensee to its COTS suppliers at preferential conditions.21 This matches ESA's general practice for "operational software": ESA holds the rights and may grant free, non-exclusive licences to Member State bodies for peaceful use in space research and technology.23

    Support covered the initial phase (installation, training, consultancy). Contracts were to be of "Fixed Unit Time" (FUP, for example labour) or firm fixed price (FFP) nature.1622 The minutes clarify that a "user" for service means a single point of contact that may represent a community, and that the first-level help desk is included in the 35 kEUR per year.5

    The project's position¶

    The project stated its limits clearly. ESA's science directorate expects industry to provide the EGSE as part of the spacecraft contract, with a "hands-off" approach, while commonality between flight control and checkout systems is pursued within project constraints (also a goal for Rosetta and Mars Express). Selecting SCOS-2000 as instrument EGSE kernel was "entirely a PI decision". The project could give no technical support, no hardware or software, no money for direct costs (hardware, licences, delta URD developments, support, training) or indirect costs, and no acceptance of responsibility; its coordination of the evaluation ended with the termination of the RTA CWG.1819 The benefits of commonality "have not been quantified".20

    What was agreed¶

    The minutes record the discussion:3456

    • ESOC called SCOS-2000 stable, mature and reliable, and committed to support it for flight operations and EGSE development. SCOS-2000 is the baseline for MOC operations, and the invitation to tender (ITT) was to say so for the core of the flight control system.
    • The COTS available for Solaris would also be available on Linux (Windows NT under investigation), and ObjectStore would probably be replaced by a cheaper object database.
    • From PROBA: SCOS-2 was the basis; Oracle served as database, extended to the SCOE equipment; the TPE read Oracle at compile time; SCOEs were addressed by APIDs outside the spacecraft range. SCOS-2000 concentrates on an interface to a database that can be any product; the common project database was to come from the prime, and for ESOC the options were Oracle, MS Access (the Integral tool, compatible with SCOS-2000) or custom solutions.
    • ESOC keeps "one product only". Changes are either infrastructure enhancements (ESA funded) or project-specific add-ons, funded by the customer and built either by the customer or by ESOC.
    • The instrument teams worried that no coherent strategy existed for the interface to the prime's checkout equipment, and needed a core option by May 2001 for DPU testing. The three FIRST instruments had agreed a common EGSE, with requirements frozen around June 2000. Interfacing with FINDAS would use the DDS, with the ESOC infrastructure ready between end 2001 and mid-2002.
    • Conclusion: all PI groups "unanimously stated their intention" to use SCOS-2000 as the basis for EGSE and RTA, to be reflected in the ITT interface requirements for database and telemetry.

    The Herschel EGSE for instrument level tests¶

    The decision materialised in the Herschel EGSE for instrument level tests (EGSE-ILT). Its integration plan (SRON-U/HIFI/PL/2001-1, issue 1 of 6 November 2001, identical to draft 4 of 1 July 2001) lists the blocks and who delivered them:2425

    Block Delivered by
    SCOS-2000 ESA
    SCOS gateway ESTEC
    Test-equipment interface (TEI) HIFI
    TM-TC router HIFI
    Test control PACS
    CDMS simulator SPIRE
    OBSM ESA
    MIB editor ESA

    The router, TEI, CDMS simulator and test control tests all start the same way: connect the subsystems and import (or load) the test MIB into SCOS-2000. They then check TM packet reception and content in SCOS-2000 and TC packet transmission to a test client, test equipment or instrument simulator; the last test checks the link between test control and the HCSS.26 On-board software maintenance (OBSM) was still an open item.26

    The operations interface requirements¶

    The Herschel/Planck Operations Interface Requirements Document (OIRD, SCI-PT-RS-07360, issue 2.2, dated "31 September 2003" on its cover, while its change record dates issue 2.2 to 22 May 2003) lists the requirements on spacecraft and payloads needed for operations, and on the deliverables needed at the MOC. It was prepared at ESOC, approved by the project (SCI-PT) and ESOC, and agreed by the prime (ASPI), the project scientists and the instrument PIs; the ESTEC project office controls it.272829 Section 3 derives from the ECSS Packet Utilisation Standard (draft 5.3 of April 2001), and the SCOS-2000 Database Import ICD is an applicable document: it became one in issue 2.0 (July 2001) and was updated to its issue 5.1 (October 2001) in issue 2.2. Requirements marked "P" apply to spacecraft and payloads, "PO" to the spacecraft with possible relevance to payloads, unmarked ones to the spacecraft only.2928

    Operations concept¶

    In routine operations the ground station contact lasts a few hours per day. In that time the ground checks health, dumps the previous day's stored telemetry, uplinks the next day's schedule and corrects problems; science runs autonomously from the schedule. Packet telemetry is expected to ease the ordering of the daily dump, for example status first, then events and command verification reports, then observation data. Operations would use "the same telemetry and telecommand database used in the EGSE".30 Every telecommand is a packet addressed by APID and identified by APID and sequence control, with its function given by PUS type and subtype and its required acknowledgement in a header field. Telemetry is collected in housekeeping, science, telecommand report, event and memory dump packets and stored in the solid-state mass memory until erased by ground.3031

    Packet rules that shape the MIB¶

    Several OIRD requirements decide what the MIB tables can express. APIDs are allocated by the project in the Packet Structure ICD (PSICD; see the APID structure); telemetry from a subsystem uses the same APID as its telecommands where possible; the location of a housekeeping parameter must follow from APID, type, subtype and structure ID (SID); SIDs are unique within an APID and few per APID; housekeeping subcommutation is forbidden and supercommutation allowed only with regular sampling; a parameter has the same structure in every packet; all packets carry a time field (except time and idle packets); and Herschel instrument packets carry a common observation identifier.33 For verification, each telecommand controls its own verification level through the acknowledgement field, acceptance and execution reports carry the failure reason, and reports indicate the command source (ground, mission timeline, OBCP).34 Telemetry must let the ground assess status without the command history, echo the inputs of autonomous processes, use contiguous bits for each parameter and keep the contributing parameters of any synthetic parameter in one packet.35

    Comments, answers and the SPID problem¶

    The note also preserves the e-mail exchanges behind it. The HSC asked that the naming convention allow underscores in short descriptions, because they store readable names such as DPU_START_AUT_FUN for the mnemonic PC004380, and because the CUS scripting language cannot use + or -. It pointed out that the leading characters I, O and Q were reserved by the instrument centres for HIFI, PACS and SPIRE EGSE-ILT parameters, while H, P and S lead instrument-related mnemonics.76 ESOC's SCOS team answered on 5 December 2002 and raised document change requests: non-mandatory fields without default are implicitly null; separators are always present (DCR 286); PLF offsets count from the end of the SCOS-2000 packet header (DCR 309); bit 0 is the most significant bit (DCR 308, whose own wording says "most significant byte"). Two odd structures stay: soft and hard limits as separate ocp records, inherited from previous control systems, and the PID_VALID flag that selects which record of a key is valid; ESOC had no intention to change either.77

    The best documented problem is SPID uniqueness. In September 2002 PACS reported that all its telecommand verification packets had SPID 10006, because SCOS-2000 did not allow a range of SPIDs for packet type 1. That contradicted the ICD rule that the SPID uniquely defines a packet structure, and SCOS-2000 used PCF_VALID to decide which plf location applied, which PACS called an abuse of a field meant for the validity of a value. ESOC replied that the verifier "listens" only to a predefined list of SPIDs for report-based verification. Rosetta had already allowed any SPID for service 1 packets and Cryosat a range of SPIDs, and the useful Rosetta and Cryosat changes to the verifier and the on-board queue model were planned for S2K R3.1: a function reads all SPIDs of service type 1 from pid.dat and adds them to the TM cache filter.78 See command verification in the MIB.

    Telecommand history and out-of-limits ingestion¶

    The design (TC History Ingestion TN)¶

    The HCSS needed to know what was actually sent. Requirement HCSS-UR-3.1-1111 asks that the HCSS associate each telecommand in the TC history with the instrument or spacecraft request in the observation schedule, and use case UCF-759 asks that failed, missing and additional commands be flagged.82 The TC History Ingestion Technical Note (SPIRE-ICS-DOC-002314, issue 0.2, 18 March 2003; issue 0.1 of 7 September 2001, updated when the code went into the HCSS) describes the process for the instrument level test phase, version 0.1.x of the HCSS, over a TCP/IP interface to SCOS-2000 that a CORBA interface was to replace.798081 Among its references is the SCOS-2000 Command History Data Provision Services ICD (S2K-MCS-ICD-0008-TOS-GCI, issue 1.2, August 2000).81

    The assumptions show how SCOS-2000 data meet the science schedule:82

    • the TC history file (THF) is an ASCII file produced by SCOS-2000 and contains values only for editable command parameters;
    • commands in the HCSS schedule carry a 32-bit sequence identifier (TCID); manual commands have a null or default TCID;
    • the uplinked schedule also sets observation and building block identifiers (OBSID, BBID), which relate manual commands to observations;
    • TC history times are UT, schedule times TAI, and in instrument level tests only relative times are used, so time conversion is needed;
    • on-board generated event-driven TCs are out of scope; telecommands are the lowest level of command generation and become persistent when the schedule is exported to the MOC.

    The process has two components linked by their own TCP/IP connection: a THF server, which asks SCOS-2000 for the history of a time window, receives the file name and fetches the file from the directory where SCOS-2000 wrote it; and a TC history ingestor, which validates the file (first line, time window), creates one TCHistory object per command, links it to its TC in the database by TCID, flags missing histories and missing TCs, updates the success flags of observations and block executions, commits, and waits for the next period.83

    sequenceDiagram
  participant I as TC history ingestor (HCSS)
  participant F as THF server
  participant S as SCOS-2000
  participant D as HCSS database
  loop every configured period
    I->>F: request TC history for a time window
    F->>S: connect, ask for the window
    S-->>F: file name of the THF written to its directory
    F->>F: read the THF from the shared directory
    F-->>I: THF
    I->>I: validate first line and time window

    The design decisions, in short:868788

    Decision Content
    Interface TCP/IP to SCOS-2000 for now, CORBA later; the THF server must run on the SCOS-2000 host or see its directory
    Availability On a lost or failed connection, wait and retry, logging each event
    Missing TCs A history with a TCID unknown to the database is logged and stored anyway (an error condition, so that no data are lost)
    Manual commands TCID 0; the observation and building block are guessed from the time stamp; the history is stored without association
    Success A failed TC is logged; a building block is marked uplinked when all its TCs succeeded, an observation executed when all its TCs succeeded
    Size and rate A THF holds 0 to many TCs, typically a day; one THF per day is foreseen; one commit per file

    The software lives in the Java package nl.esa.herschel.tchingest (api, bin, doc, server, test, util). The FileIngestion interface (getFile, checkFile, processFile, initStore, commitData) is shared by the TC history and out-of-limits ingestion, with abstract classes for file details, ingestion (including roll-back on abort) and the file server.8485

    Operating it (TCH/OOL user manual)¶

    The User Manual for Telecommand History and Out-of-Limits ingestion (SPIRE-ICS-DOC-001852, issue 2.3, 13 November 2008) covers both ingestors. Its change record shows the history: a combined version in October 2003, integrated system test (IST) support in April 2004, a command-line retrieval mode in May 2004, time-out and delete properties in February 2007, and support for "DDS OOL files" in November 2008.8990 Each ingestion has a file retriever that asks SCOS-2000 for a THF or an out-of-limits file (ORF) and receives its name, a file server used during instrument level tests (ILT mode) to serve files periodically over TCP/IP, and an ingestor that works either from the server (ILT mode) or from files named on the command line (IST mode). The OOL ingestor also reads DDS files from the MOC in the routine phase.91 Every ingestor validates the file and the time window, creates objects, skips objects already in the database, makes the others persistent, links them where possible and commits. The TC history ingestor checks the header line, links histories to TCs, updates success flags and looks for gaps in TC IDs; the OOL ingestor attaches each OOLPacket to the building block closest in time.92

    The THF format

    The record names in these error messages (header, command data, bit pattern and parameter data records) are those of the THF's own ICD, the TC History ICD (SPIRE-ICS-DOC-000900, issue 1.0, 30 October 2002, prepared by M. J. Graham), which is now in the library: an ASCII file named THF_yymmdd_cccc.DAT, made of a header record and fixed-length records that end with a new-line but have no field delimiters, each command record (C) followed by a bit pattern record (B) and zero or more parameter records (P); the bit pattern is not applicable to ILT, where it is available through SCOS-2000.154155156 The record layouts are on the interface documents page. The format of the out-of-limits files (ORF) is still not stated in the available sources.9791

    SPIRE database management and configuration control¶

    SPIRE's note on database management and CUS configuration control (SPIRE-RAL-NOT-002829, issue 1.0, 19 February 2007) shows the HCSS side of a test campaign. It applies to all SPIRE test environments: ILT, AVM, IST and the HSC.99100 The HCSS stored its data in a Versant object database (version 7.0.1.1 or later) with a Java 1.5 runtime.101 Creating a test database takes these steps:102104

    Step What it does
    Allocate and initialise Create the database and its instrument model registries (HIFI, PACS, SPIRE)
    Public access and index Allow access; index TM source packets by time
    First configuration Create mission configuration config1; every later change to definitions or calibration tables needs a new configuration
    Ingest the MIB Point hcss.mib.datadir at the MIB directory and run the ingestion with a label; semantic problems are written to mibchecker.log, serious ones stop the ingestion, minor ones do not (example label SPIRE_MIB_2.2.G1)
    Load into the CUS Load the labelled MIB into the Common Uplink System GUI
    Calibration tables and CUS definitions Upload calibration tables and the building blocks, procedures and modes from CVS
    Observation identifiers Set the initial OBSID (in hexadecimal) for the database
    Instrument model Bind MIB and CUS definitions to the configuration; list the models (for example SPIRE PFM5)

    The ingestion settings name the files that complement the MIB: the table definitions, a CUS script, a command list and the command durations file required by the HCSS note.10370 The configuration control rationale is to know, later, "which version of which script was used to produce a particular set of SPIRE telemetry data". Definitions live in the ESA CVS repository, one folder per campaign (CUS and TestControl for ILT, CUS and CCS templates for IST); a single FM folder was to replace them because separate folders proved hard to maintain. Verified definitions are committed and tagged LABEL_CONFIGN (for example AVM_TEST_CONFIG6). Once a test starts, changes are tracked only by the database's automatic versions (CVS 1.2 becomes 1.2.1 when ingested) and checked back into CVS (1.3) after the test, because managing hundreds of definitions by check-in and check-out "can become highly unstable". A new mission configuration freezes a snapshot. Test control scripts and CCS templates have no database versioning, so their working folders are themselves CVS checkouts.105106 A calibration table TestSetup.txt with keys configuration, env (moc, ilt, hsc, ist, par), test_type (ft, pr), instrument (prime, redundant), condition (warm, cool, cold) and dbg_level lets the same CUS definitions run in every environment.107108

    The ACMS database experience (NLR)¶

    Authors from NLR, Dutch Space, Alcatel Alenia Space (France and Italy) and ESTEC reported the database experience of the attitude control and measurement system (ACMS), developed under Dutch Space responsibility, at SpaceOps 2006 (AIAA-2006-590; NLR report NLR-TP-2007-240, April 2007). The ACMS Test Equipment used the SCOS-2000-based Central Checkout System, ensuring "commonality between tools and data used during subsystem and system level testing and the operational phase".109 The team built its own ACMS operations database in the DOORS requirements tool because the TM definition document could no longer be kept consistent by hand, the TM/TC interface was more complex and extensive than for other ACMS subsystems that Dutch Space had developed, SCOS-2000 in the test equipment needed every TM/TC detail including operational identifiers, and the HPSDB was not mature enough at the time. Dutch Space then supplied its data to the HPSDB as HPSDB-compatible XML.111112

    The SCOS-2000 MS Access database "did not fit": it supports one mission only (not the commonality of two satellites with common data), has no configuration control at object level, and cannot relate application software TM/TC to the software specification.112 The DOORS database has modules that mirror the MIB: command characteristics (ccf), TC packet structures (cdf), parameters (pcf, cpc), conversions (caf, cap, cca, ccs, mcf), enumerations (txf, txp, paf, pas), packet identification criteria (pic), packet identifiers (pid, tpcf), TM packet structures (plf), alphanumeric displays, HPSDB boxes and a change log.113114 Operational identifiers are built from HPSDB box numbers: the ACMS mode parameters are AESMG002 on Herschel and AESMG003 on Planck.114 Every object carries its spacecraft (Herschel, Planck or common), software (ASW or BSW) and a VersionFrom/VersionTo range, from which specific SCOS-2000 or HPSDB files are generated; exports, not DOORS baselines, are kept under CVS, and the HPSDB-generated *.dat files are compared with those used in ACMS AIT by a ccsdiff script.115116

    The paper's recommendations: integrate the database with specifications and ICDs, make test specifications use database identifiers, keep requirement, software, test and operational identifiers independent but traceable, version every object, and use inheritance and instantiation to avoid duplication. It closes with a plea to standardise "a database design/interface for definition of operations and test related information", for all programmes.117 ESA's 2007 Bulletin likewise notes that common tools for checkout and operations preparation promote a seamless transition from manufacture to operations, as "exploited recently by Herschel and Planck".118

    Lessons¶

    • One database, many consumers. The OIRD demanded a single satellite database for engineering, AIV and operations. In practice the instrument EGSE, the HPSDB, the CCS, the MOC and the HCSS each read the SCOS-2000 tables with their own rules, and each documented them: the naming convention, the Alcatel limits, the HPSDB constraints and the HCSS tailoring note.364764
    • The ICD needed interpreting. The HCSS questions of 2002 produced ESOC change requests on separators, PLF offsets and bit numbering, and exposed the SPID limitation of the verifier.7778 The history of the import ICD follows how such points entered later issues.
    • Mission-level conventions are local. Leading characters, identifier lengths and description character sets were set by one programme for its own smooth transition. A modern MIB such as Solar Orbiter's follows different conventions (see mission tailoring of the MIB).123
    • Configuration control was explicit. The one delivery documented in the library travelled as a zip with a release note listing its inputs, change records, open non-conformances and an MD5 checksum, and test results flowed back as update files naming each validated command and parameter.151148135
    • The model outlived the missions. In 2015 the prime presented the HPSDB, adapted as a System Reference Data Base for a later programme, as a validated concept and planned to map it onto the EGS-CC data model.158159160
