    Time correlation in SCOS-2000¶

    A spacecraft keeps its own clock, the on-board time (OBT). The ground keeps UTC. The two are different clocks with different epochs, rates and drifts, and the spacecraft cannot simply be told "the time": every packet it sends is stamped with OBT, and every time-tagged command it executes waits for an OBT. Time correlation is the ground function that measures the relation between the two clocks and converts between them. In SCOS-2000 it is needed mainly by the telemetry packetiser, to time-stamp packets in UTC, and by the releaser, to encode time-tagged commands in OBT.4

    This page explains the mechanism end to end: what the spacecraft sends, which delays the ground removes, how pairs of times become coefficients, how the coefficients are monitored, and how the SCOS-2000 software (the "TCO" subsystem) is built. It then shows what three generations of ESA missions did with it: Rosetta, Mars Express and Venus Express, and Solar Orbiter, whose MIB still defines the internal time correlation packets. The on-board side of the story (the ST[09] time management service) and the time code formats (CCSDS 301.0-B-4) have their own pages.

    The documents¶

    The main source is the SCOS-2000 Time Correlation Technical Note, EGOS-MCS-S2K-TN-1030, version 1.0, dated 2018-10-31 and prepared by the SCOS-2000 GIMUS team. Its change log says it was created for S2K-7699: chapters 1 to 3 are mostly new, chapter 4 is taken from the EUD4S2K software user manual, section 5.2 from a Gaia time correlation document, and the rest refers to the TM Packetiser and Packet Displays ADD (EGOS-MCS-S2K-ADD-0017).12 Read with the body numbering, chapter 4 is "Time Correlation Mechanisms" and section 5.2 "Time Correlation Packets"; with the (different) numbering of the table of contents they would be the packetiser chapter and "Methods of Time Correlation", so the attribution is not fully certain (see the note below). Neither the EUD4S2K manual nor ADD-0017 is in this wiki's library.

    Section numbers in TN-1030

    The table of contents of TN-1030 numbers its chapters one lower than the headings in the body (for example "3 Time Correlation Mechanisms" in the contents is "4 TIME CORRELATION MECHANISMS" in the text).3 This page cites the numbers printed in the body.

    Other sources fill in context: the SCOS-2000 Database Import ICD issue 7.0 (how parameters are correlated), the Rosetta/Mars Express/Venus Express Data Delivery Interface Document (the earlier, manual time correlation of those missions), the Solar Orbiter Data Delivery ICD, the Solar Orbiter packet structure ICD, and the public Solar Orbiter MIB.

    Why the ground needs it¶

    The standards ask for it at every level:

    • ECSS-E-ST-50C requires the space link to provide a time correlation capability that lets the on-board time be correlated with the time kept on the ground.31
    • ECSS-E-ST-70-11C (§5.4 "Datation and synchronization") requires the space segment to provide timing information so that the ground can maintain the OBT-to-UTC correlation better than a mission constant <TIME_CORREL_ACCUR> over a period <TIME_CORREL_PERIOD>; to let the ground set the OBT by telecommand; to keep the OBT monotonic and free of wrap-around for the mission lifetime; to limit the on-board time system's contribution to <ONBOARD_STABILITY> ms per day; and to document every on-board delay that contributes to the correlation. For GNSS-disciplined clocks it adds an on-board GNSS/OBT correlation reported in housekeeping telemetry.2930
    • The PUS time management service supplies the raw material: time reports that sample the spacecraft time reference at a precisely defined instant, so that the ground "can perform a correlation" between spacecraft time and UTC.25

    SCOS-2000 itself treated time correlation as mission-specific code until release 5.2; from R5.2 a generic implementation supporting both free-running and synchronised clocks is part of the kernel.34 Earlier still, Earth Explorer missions contributed pieces: GOCE is listed as having contributed a "Time Correlator" to the Earth Explorer mission family kernel built on top of SCOS-2000.35

    Two clocks and their time codes¶

    On-board time. The OBT is usually a binary counter of seconds and sub-seconds, encoded in the CCSDS Unsegmented time Code (CUC): a T-field of coarse octets (seconds) and fine octets (binary fractions), with an optional P-field that says how many of each there are and whether the epoch is the CCSDS epoch of 1958 January 1 TAI (level 1, code 0b001) or an agency-defined epoch (level 2, code 0b010).23 A clock synchronised to an external reference may instead use the CCSDS Day Segmented code (CDS: day count, milliseconds of day, optional sub-milliseconds), which is UTC-based and therefore subject to leap seconds.24 PUS reflects this split: CUC suits spacecraft time measurement, while CDS is typically used for an on-board time already synchronised with a ground reference such as TAI or UTC.25

    Ground time. Inside SCOS-2000, times are held in the Unix-like TDSsunTime structure (4 octets of seconds and 4 of microseconds) or TDSsunTime_pico (plus 4 octets of picoseconds), counted from an epoch given by the MISC variable TCO_ESA_EPOCH; if that variable cannot be read, a DEFAULT_ESA_EPOCH hard-coded in the software is used.1011

    Which telemetry is correlated. In the MIB, each absolute-time parameter (PTC 9) carries two fields in pcf: PCF_CORR (Y, the default, applies the time correlation when the raw value is interpreted; N extracts the time as is) and PCF_OBTID, which selects one of the mission's on-board clocks. A mission with N clocks numbers them 1 to N; when PCF_OBTID is empty the MISCconfig variable TCO_DEFAULT_OBT_ID applies, and an uncorrelated parameter without an OBT ID takes its epoch from TCO_ESA_EPOCH. Relative times (PTC 10) must set PCF_CORR = N.32 SCOS-2000 supports CUC formats with 1 to 4 coarse and 0 to 3 fine octets (PFC 3 to 18), CDS without and with microseconds (PFC 1 and 2; the PFC 2 row adds that CDS is supported only for command parameters, not monitoring parameters), and an 8-octet Unix time (PFC 30); the self-describing form with a P-field (PFC 0) is not supported.33

    The Solar Orbiter MIB shows how this is used in practice. Of its 126,745 monitoring parameters, 3,796 are absolute times. The 3,793 on-board times in CUC form (2,116 with 4 coarse octets, PFC 15, and 1,677 with 4 coarse and 2 fine octets, PFC 17) all leave PCF_CORR empty and therefore take the default Y. The three exceptions explicitly set N: two Unix-format times (PFC 30), "Period From" and "Period To" of the ground-generated time correlation report, and one CDS time with microseconds, "GS Acceptance Time" of the ground station response packet, which the ICD's remark on PFC 2 would not allow for a monitoring parameter. No parameter sets PCF_OBTID, so the mission uses a single default clock.5133

    The measurement: what the spacecraft sends¶

    The spacecraft samples its clock when a particular telemetry frame starts to leave. In TN-1030's words the trigger is the transmission of a frame whose frame counter is a multiple of N, where N is a power of 2 between 1 and 256; the sampled time is "latched" and sent to the ground in a time packet carried by a later frame.6 The ground, knowing N, can work out which frame triggered the sample.

    PUS defines the same rule precisely. The time reporting subservice samples the spacecraft time reference for each virtual channel 0 frame whose frame count modulo the time report generation rate is zero, and it samples at the leading edge of the first bit of that frame's attached synchronisation marker. The rate is one of 1, 2, 4, ... 256 and can be changed by TC[9,1].2627 Time reports are spacecraft time packets: APID 0, no secondary header, message type TM[9,2] (CUC) or TM[9,3] (CDS).28

    Solar Orbiter's time packet

    Solar Orbiter's packet structure ICD defines the Standard Spacecraft Time Source Packet as an APID 0 packet with no data field header and 8 data octets: an S-field whose bits 4 to 7 give the generation frequency, a P-field fixed at 0b00101110 (CUC, agency-defined epoch, 4 coarse and 2 fine octets; the ICD states the octet counts, and the epoch code 0b010 is decoded here with CCSDS 301.0-B-423) and a 6-octet T-field holding the spacecraft elapsed time. The T-field relates to the leading edge of the first ASM bit of the VC 0 frame with frame count 0.36 The MIB defines it as SPID 9999 "Time Report" (type 9, subtype 2, APID 0) with the parameters NCD09021 (S-field, offset 6), NCD09022 (P-field, offset 7) and NCD09023 "OBCHP_OBT" (PTC 9, PFC 17, offset 8).51 The decoding is worked through on the ST[09] page.

    The delays: from Earth reception back to the latch¶

    The frame that triggered the sample reaches the ground station some time later and is stamped there with its Earth Reception Time (ERT). The ERT arrives at the MCS in the header with which the network interface (NCTRS/NIS) delivers the frame.6 Between the on-board trigger and the ERT stamp lie four delays, defined in TN-1030 §6.1:13

    # Instant or interval Definition
    a Frame Transmission Time (FTT) When the Transfer Frame Generator (TFG) on board starts transmitting the frame; for time correlation frames the TFG signals this instant to request the OBT sample
    b OBT Latching Time (OLT) When the OBT corresponding to that FTT is sampled and stored in the on-board register
    c Frame Radiation Time When the leading edge of the first bit of the ASM preceding the frame is at the phase centre of the on-board antenna
    d Frame Reception Time When the same edge is at the phase centre of the ground antenna (definition taken from SLE)
    e Earth Reception Time (ERT) The UTC time stamp that the ground station equipment gives the frame; for SLE service providers it must equal the frame reception time
    f On-board frame radiation delay From the latch request to the start of radiation: frame radiation time minus FTT
    g OBT latching delay From the latch request to the actual latch: OLT minus FTT
    h Propagation delay One-way light time (OWLT) from on-board antenna to ground antenna
    i Ground delay ERT minus frame reception time; zero by definition for SLE providers, which correct the time stamp themselves
    j UTC Time of Transmission (UTT) The UTC corresponding to a given OBT; the name assumes a zero latching delay and is, TN-1030 notes, misleading but common 
    sequenceDiagram
    participant TFG as On-board TFG and clock
    participant ANT as On-board antenna
    participant GS as Ground antenna and station
    participant MCS as SCOS-2000 packetiser
    Note over TFG: FTT: frame k starts (k mod N = 0), latch requested
    Note over TFG: OLT = FTT + OBT latching delay (OBT sampled)
    TFG->>ANT: on-board frame radiation delay
    ANT->>GS: propagation delay (OWLT)
    Note over GS: frame reception time, then ERT stamp after the ground delay
    GS->>MCS: frame k with ERT (via NCTRS/NIS)
    TFG->>GS: a later frame carries the time packet with the latched OBT
    GS->>MCS: time packet
    Note over MCS: match packet to frame k, compute OLT, build a time couple

    The arithmetic is simple:613

    \[ \mathrm{FTT} = \mathrm{ERT} - \text{ground delay} - \text{propagation delay} - \text{on-board frame radiation delay} \]
    \[ \mathrm{OLT} = \mathrm{FTT} + \text{OBT latching delay} \]

    The pair (OBT from the time packet, OLT in UTC) describes one physical event, the latching of the clock, seen by two clocks. It is called a time couple, and SCOS-2000 stores each one in a telemetry packet of its own.6 Where the delays come from is mission configuration: the propagationDelay API returns a light-time delay, an on-board delay and a station delay for a given UTC and an optional auxiliary identifier (for example a ground station), and its default implementation simply returns a value set with setPropagationDelay.9 For Rosetta and Mars Express, Flight Dynamics supplied the propagation delay routine, to be embedded in the R(ME)MCS time correlation component, computing the downlink delay as a function of UTC at reception from per-station One-Way Light Time (OWLT) files.46

    Matching the time packet to its frame¶

    Because the time packet arrives after the frame it refers to, the packetiser remembers the frame time of every candidate frame and opens an interval in which a time packet is expected. A packet outside the interval is ignored and no couple is made.6 Formally, the candidate FTT must satisfy16

    \[ \mathrm{FTT}_{report} - \mathit{FarLimit} \le \mathrm{FTT}_{obt} \le \mathrm{FTT}_{report} - \mathit{CloseLimit} \]

    where \(\mathrm{FTT}_{report}\) is the FTT of the frame carrying the time report and the two limits are the MISCdynamic variables TCO_FAR_FTT_PLAUSIBILITY_LIMIT and TCO_CLOSE_FTT_PLAUSIBILITY_LIMIT. Whether the check is applied at all is a MISC variable set per OBT from the Time Correlation application.16

    A time couple (illustrative numbers)

    Quantity Value
    ERT of the triggering VC 0 frame 12:10:00.250 000 UTC
    Ground delay 0.000 120 s
    Propagation delay 480.000 000 s
    On-board frame radiation delay 0.000 050 s
    OBT latching delay 0.000 010 s
    FTT 12:02:00.249 830 UTC
    OLT 12:02:00.249 840 UTC
    OBT in the time packet 811 339 200.500 000 s

    The formulas are TN-1030's; the numbers are invented by this wiki.13

    From couples to coefficients¶

    SCOS-2000 fits a straight line through the couples. Two algorithms exist and can be switched at run time:714

    Clock Algorithm (TN-1030 name) Couples needed Coefficients
    Free-running Least squares ("free running clock") A buffer of recent couples Gradient and offset of the best-fit line
    Synchronised to an external source such as GPS Difference ("synchronised on-board clock") The last couple Offset only; gradient fixed to 1

    To keep resolution, the conversion is written relative to the last couple used in the calculation, \((\mathrm{OBT}_N, \mathrm{UTC}_N)\):712

    \[ \mathrm{UTC}(\mathrm{OBT}) = \mathrm{UTC}_N + \mathit{Gradient}\,(\mathrm{OBT} - \mathrm{OBT}_N) + \mathit{Display\ Offset} \]

    The older absolute form \(\mathrm{UTC} = \mathit{Gradient}\cdot\mathrm{OBT} + \mathit{Offset}\) is kept in the coefficient packet but marked obsolete; the two offsets are related by \(\mathit{Offset} = \mathrm{UTC}_N - \mathit{Gradient}\cdot\mathrm{OBT}_N + \mathit{Display\ Offset}\). TN-1030 Figure 4 shows the two coordinate systems.712

    Inference: sign conventions

    TN-1030 prints the relative relation with a plus sign before the gradient term in the description of the Gradient field, and with a minus sign in the description of the Display Offset field; the difference algorithm is printed as \(\mathrm{UTC}_N + \mathrm{OBT} - \mathrm{OBT}_N - \mathit{Offset}\).712 Only the plus form is consistent with a gradient near 1, so this page uses it. The sign of the offset in the difference algorithm cannot be settled from the available sources.

    A least-squares correlation (illustrative numbers)

    Four couples taken every 15 minutes; OBT is shown relative to 811 339 200 s, UTC relative to 12:00:00 on the day of the pass:

    Couple OBT − 811 339 200 (s) OLT (s after 12:00:00)
    1 0 0.250 010
    2 900 900.250 210
    3 1 800 1 800.250 455
    4 2 700 2 700.250 675

    An ordinary least-squares fit gives Gradient = 1.000 000 249 (the on-board clock runs about 0.25 ppm slow against UTC) and, relative to couple 4 (\(\mathrm{OBT}_N\) = 2 700, \(\mathrm{UTC}_N\) = 2 700.250 675), a Display Offset of −0.000 001 5 s. The residuals are 8.5, −15.5, 5.5 and 1.5 µs. One hour later a couple with OBT 6 300 and OLT 6 300.251 875 is received; the coefficients predict 6 300.251 570, so its deviation is +0.000 305 s. The numbers are constructed by this wiki to illustrate TN-1030's equations.7

    Monitoring the correlation: deviation, accuracy, validity¶

    Every new couple tests the coefficients in force. The deviation is the UTC of the couple (its OLT) minus the UTC obtained by converting the couple's OBT with the current coefficients. Two configurable limits, the tighter accuracy limit and the looser validity limit, classify it:64

    Condition Status
    deviation ≤ accuracy valid and accurate
    accuracy ≤ deviation ≤ validity valid but inaccurate
    validity ≤ deviation invalid (and inaccurate)

    In the engine the limits are the MISCdynamic variables TCO_ACCURACY_INTERVAL and TCO_VALIDITY_INTERVAL; in the coefficient packet "inaccurate" means the user-controlled soft threshold was exceeded and "invalid" the hard one.1612 The validity limit is meant to catch coefficients that no longer apply at all, for example after a jump of the on-board clock.14

    stateDiagram-v2
    VA: Valid, accurate
    VI: Valid, inaccurate
    II: Invalid, inaccurate
    VA --> VI: (1) couple deviation above accuracy limit
    VA --> II: (2) deviation above validity limit, or reset
    VI --> II: (3) deviation above validity limit, or reset
    II --> VA: (4) correlation performed, new coefficients committed
    VI --> VA: (5) correlation performed, new coefficients committed

    The diagram is TN-1030 Figure 6.14 A status change is filed by setCorrelationStatus, which also raises an alarm or an information message depending on the direction of the transition.16

    Synchronisation status. For a clock disciplined by an external source, the expected offset between OBT and UTC is known: GPS time, for instance, ignores leap seconds, so it differs from UTC by a known number of seconds. If the computed offset is within the accuracy limit of the expected value, the coefficients are synchronised; otherwise unsynchronised. Unlike accuracy and validity, synchronisation is assessed only when new coefficients are generated, not on each couple. Coefficients can therefore be valid and accurate yet desynchronised: usable for conversion, while the clock has lost its time source.614 The server's checkSynchronised computes the expected TAI/UTC difference (TCO_TAI_UTC_DIFFERENCE) minus the current correction and compares its absolute value with the accuracy interval.20

    stateDiagram-v2
    S: Synchronised
    U: Unsynchronised
    S --> U: new correlation, abs(offset - expected) above accuracy
    U --> S: new correlation, abs(offset - expected) below accuracy

    Automatic and manual operation¶

    Manual mode. The operator asks for a calculation. In the simplest case it uses the live buffer, as the automatic mode does, but the result is an intermediate set of coefficients that become active, and whose packet is archived, only when confirmed (confirmTCOparameters). The operator may instead give an end time (the configured number of couples before it is used) or an end and a start time (all couples in the archive between them); such calculations use a dedicated buffer so that the live one is untouched, and in difference mode only the last couple before the end time matters. When confirming, the operator can set the validity time, which becomes the filing time of the coefficient packet; in automatic mode it is "now". Validity and accuracy are always checked in manual mode.1916

    Automatic mode (TCO_AUTO_UPDATE) adds two rules, applied only while the MISCdynamic flag TCO_SUSPEND_VALIDITY_CHECK is false:16

    1. Auto-update. If the deviation is above the accuracy interval but not above the validity interval, the engine asks the TCO server to recompute the coefficients from the latest couples and to store a new coefficient packet.
    2. Auto-reset. If the deviation exceeds the validity interval for N consecutive couples (TCO_AUTORESET_INVALID_DEVIATIONS), the couples are still evaluated but kept out of the buffer, treated as rogues; after the N-th, the server resets the correlation (resetTCOparameters): the status becomes invalid, the buffer is emptied, the validity check is suspended, and as soon as two new couples are in the buffer the coefficients are recomputed automatically.
    flowchart TD
    A[Time packet passes plausibility check] --> B[createTimeCouple: couple stored by TCO server]
    B --> C[Convert couple OBT with current coefficients]
    C --> D{deviation vs limits}
    D -- "below accuracy" --> E[valid and accurate, no action]
    D -- "accuracy to validity" --> F[status change filed; auto mode: recalculate and commit]
    D -- "above validity" --> G[count consecutive invalid couples]
    G -- "fewer than N" --> H[couple not buffered, treated as rogue]
    G -- "N reached" --> I[reset: invalid, buffer emptied, checks suspended]
    I --> J[two new couples buffered, recalculate, resume checks]

    Inference: three statements of the update trigger

    Sections 4 and 6.2 of TN-1030, and the summary paragraph that closes §6.3.2, say the automatic recalculation happens whenever the deviation exceeds half the accuracy limit, and the sequence diagram (Figure 9) is annotated "If ½ accuracy limit is exceeded"; step (6) of the engine description in §6.3.2 uses the accuracy interval itself; and the overview in §3.1 speaks of the deviation exceeding the thresholds "for the validity".46141617 The available sources do not say which threshold the released software applies, or whether it is configurable. This page follows step (6), the most detailed statement.

    The MISC variables named in TN-1030 are listed below. TN-1030 notes that each is duplicated per on-board clock, the server using the suffix _n for the n-th OBT.1620

    Software architecture¶

    The time correlation function has three parts: an engine inside the telemetry packetiser, a client library used by every application that converts times, and a TCO server that owns the calculations and the time correlation packets. There is no access to the server other than through the client library; the operators' Time Correlation application also goes through the library.5

    The TCO engine in the packetiser¶

    The engine has two jobs: detect the frames whose transmission triggers an on-board sample, and turn the subsequent time packets into couples. The engine class is assisted by a rule class and a clock class, and a configuration class ties them together; a mission normally replaces only the configuration class:15

    Class Purpose
    TPKTMtcoTimePktRule Rule recognising trigger frames: virtual channel IDs, data stream types and sampling frequency (TN-1030's example: real-time VC 0 data with VCFC % 32 = 0). Can be subclassed
    TPKTMtcoObt Describes an on-board clock: its time format and where its time lies in the time packet. Can be subclassed, for example when each clock has its own time packet
    TPKTMtcoConfig Singleton that creates rules and clocks and maps them: each clock has exactly one rule, a rule may serve several clocks
    TPKTMtcoEngine Singleton that applies the rules to every frame (processTF) and every time packet (processSrcPkt)

    For each time packet that matches a rule, the engine performs seven steps for every clock mapped to the rule: (1) plausibility check of the candidate FTT; (2) if it fails, nothing else happens; (3) extract the OBT in the configured format (CUC or CDS) and call createTimeCouple, the server storing the couple packet; (4) convert the OBT with obtUtt locally, without the server; (5) compare with the OLT and file any status change; (6) apply the auto-update rule; (7) apply the auto-reset rule.16 Figure 9 of TN-1030 shows the calls crossing from the engine through TCOconcreteInstance and TCOclientCache to the CORBA TCOserverInterface and TCOserverImpl, which sends packets to PDSpktSource.17

    An engine can also be created in offline mode: frame detection and couple creation work, but everything that depends on the current coefficients (counting deviations, automatic reset) is disabled. TN-1030 expects it to be used on historical telemetry, for example to regenerate more accurate couples with better propagation delays.15

    The client library¶

    TCOfunction is the abstract interface for conversions; TCOconcreteInstance is the SCOS-2000 implementation, a singleton used by the classes that take part in correlation, while ordinary users of conversions hold a TCOfunction obtained through TCOfactory::createTCO. A mission with a different strategy writes its own TCOfunction subclass and makes the factory return it.18 TCOconfig creates one TCOobt per on-board clock (format and location of the OBT in time packets), each with a TCOtimeFormat; the library provides TCOtimeFormatCUC (parameterised by the PFC) and TCOtimeFormatCDS, and missions may add formats.418 For each monitored clock the instance creates a TCOclientCache, which keeps the last and the last "next valid" coefficients with their validity intervals, so that a conversion for a time inside the interval of the last coefficients is answered without asking the server, a measure TN-1030 calls necessary for performance.18

    The public operations, all keyed by the OBT identifier obtId, are:918

    Operation What it does
    instance(), destroyInstance(), isTcoServerAvailable() Singleton handling; server availability
    obtUtt OBT to ground time with the current coefficients; false if none are set
    obtUttAccuracy Same, optionally for a reference UTC (refUtt), returning the accuracy flag of the coefficients used
    obtUttValid Uses the OBT to find the applicable coefficients; if they are invalid, uses the next valid ones and returns false
    uttObt UTC to OBT (listed in the class diagrams)
    propagationDelay, setPropagationDelay Delay model; the default returns the value set
    createTimeCouple Creates a couple packet from OBT, FTT, ERT, filing time, light time, on-board and station delays, auxiliary ID and deviation; updateBuffer controls use in the live buffer
    setCorrelationStatus Files validity, accuracy and synchronisation status with a message severity
    calculateTCOparameters, confirmTCOparameters, resetTCOcalculation Calculation life cycle, optionally with time limits or a validity time
    checkTAIsynchronised Synchronisation status of the current coefficients
    getTCOcoefficients, setTcoCoefficients, injectTCOcoefficients, deleteTimeCouple(s), updateTCOtimes Listed in the class diagram of the client side

    The TCO server¶

    The server is a resident process with a CORBA interface (TCOserverInterface); like the client, it creates one TCOserverImpl per on-board clock and delegates to it.19 It produces and distributes the couple packets, calculates and confirms current or historical coefficients, deletes coefficients that are no longer valid, injects coefficients and retrieves them, and it converts times for historical data, which needs coefficients from the archive.19 Notable operations of TCOserverImpl:20

    • initialiseTCOparameters(cacheInit) restores at start-up the current and last valid coefficients and their status, from the cache and history files or from MISCconfig variables.
    • obtUttReference finds, through TCOtmPktIF, the coefficients valid at a reference UTC, searching by filing or creation time, backwards only if the earliest known coefficients precede the current time; it converts even with invalid coefficients (returning false) and returns zero when none are found. obtUttValid looks forward for the next valid coefficients instead.
    • calculateTCOparameters computes new coefficients from the buffer filled by createTimeCouple, marks them valid and accurate, and stores them in MISCconfig variables and a local cache; confirmTCOparameters activates manually calculated ones and archives their packet.
    • createTimeCouple(..., inPast) with a filing time in the past creates the packet without touching the live buffer, for reprocessing.
    • deleteTimeCouple(from, to) asks the PARC to delete couple packets; findEarliestTime returns the earliest valid OBT and UTC coefficients retrievable from PARC.

    Historical look-ups are keyed on OBT, which fails when the OBT is reset (for instance in simulations) and many coefficient packets cover similar OBT ranges. The server therefore deletes, whenever it creates a coefficient packet, all archived coefficient packets relating to an OBT within the period in which the previous coefficients were valid.19 TCOtmPktIF talks to the CPD, the Client Packet Distributor,22 using filters built from SPIDs held in MISCconfig variables; TCOcalculation is the abstract calculator with the two specialisations for the difference and least-squares methods.21

    Time-stamping telemetry¶

    Every frame and source packet that SCOS-2000 files or distributes has a time stamp that is the prime filing key of the archive. The packetiser offers three policies, one of which the mission selects:8

    1. the time when the SCOS-2000 packet is filed (useful when replaying old frames in tests);
    2. the FTT of the frame that carried the packet header, computed from the ERT minus the ground, propagation and on-board processing delays;
    3. the on-board generation time, converted from the OBT in the packet with the TCO API.

    The Solar Orbiter DDID shows the result in the SCOS-2000 packet header: a 2-bit Timestamp field (0 = packet reception "PR", 1 = frame transmission "FT", 2 = correlated SCET "PG") and a 2-bit Time Quality field (0 good, 1 inaccurate, 2 bad), meaningful when Timestamp = 2. The filing time (the EDDS "generation time") is then the correlated on-board generation time; reception time stamping is used only when the user explicitly selects it, for example to bypass time correlation.37 Superseded TCO coefficient packets are filed on the internal stream 65535, which external users should ignore.37

    The Rosetta/Mars Express/Venus Express DDID gives the time quality algorithm of that generation:43

    Time quality Condition Time stamp
    0 good OBT valid (top bit of the OBT not set) OBT converted with the applicable correlation
    1 inaccurate OBT invalid, packet received on VC 0 ERT of the VC 0 frame minus propagation, on-board and ground delays
    2 bad OBT invalid, packet received on VC 1 ERT of the VC 1 frame minus the same delays

    A plausibility check followed: a correlated VC 0 time stamp may be no more than a configurable number of seconds (usually 2) ahead of, nor (usually 15) behind, the corrected reception time of its frame; for VC 1 (playback) only the "ahead" check applies. With autocorrection enabled a failure sets the quality to bad and replaces the time stamp with the corrected frame time; otherwise only a warning is logged.43 Because stored data are dumped later, a packet is time-stamped with the correlation valid for its OBT, not with the current one.41

    The TCO subsystem keeps its products as SCOS-2000 telemetry packets, so they are archived, distributed and retrievable like any other telemetry.10

    Testing without a spacecraft¶

    TN-1030 explains how to exercise the chain without real data: in the TM-TC Test Tool's simulation tab, the TM Packet Injector can add packets by SPID, and three SPIDs matter, time report 1000005, time couple 1000098 and time coefficients 1000099.4

    Inference

    These SPIDs are those of TN-1030's test or reference configuration. A mission chooses its own: Solar Orbiter uses 9999 for the time report and 66840 to 66842 for the ground products (next section).451 The server reads the SPIDs from MISCconfig variables.21

    Rosetta, Mars Express and Venus Express: manual correlation¶

    The common DDID of the three planetary missions documents the time correlation of the generation before TN-1030, which was part of the mission control systems rather than the kernel.4134

    • Sampling. Following the ESA Packet Telemetry Standard, the spacecraft elapsed time clock is sampled at the first ASM bit of the VC 0 frame with frame count 0 and sent in the standard spacecraft time source packet before 255 more VC 0 frames have been counted; intervals of 128 down to 1 frame may be chosen instead. The ground stamps the reception of the same bit with a CDS-coded UTC and extracts the CUC time sample. On Rosetta, Mars Express and Venus Express the rate is selectable among every 256, 128, 64, 32 or 16 VC 0 frames.41
    • Delays. The packetiser takes the ERT of each "zeroth" VC 0 frame and removes three corrections: the station delay (a fixed, measured part per station plus a bit-rate-dependent part common to all stations), the propagation delay from the Flight Dynamics OWLT file, and the on-board processing delay (fixed plus bit-rate-dependent). With the next time source packet it creates a "Time Telemetry" packet (type 190, subtype 0, APID 0) holding the spacecraft time and its generation UTC.41
    • Coefficients. The Flight Control Team triggered each correlation manually, choosing the time range of Time Telemetry packets for a least-squares fit, then accepted or rejected the result and gave a validity start time. A correlation stayed valid until the next coefficient packet. The gradient was nominally 1, and the DDID warns users to treat any packet with a gradient substantially different from 1 with caution.41

    The Time Correlation Coefficient Packet was an MCS-generated packet (APID 1966, type 190, subtype 40), not a spacecraft packet, stored big-endian after the SCOS-2000 TM header.4542

    The generation time is CUC without P-field, 4 coarse and 2 fine octets; in the DDS header of a delivered coefficient packet, the SCET field holds the time from which the correlation became valid, and the ground station ID of these packets is 0 because the couples can come from several stations.4244 Flight Dynamics extracted these "Time Coefficient History packets", with the telemetry, from the MCS data archive system (DAS) using its own SCOS-2000/ORATOS Data Access (SoDA) software.47 The mission papers state the achieved result: the Mars Express and Venus Express control systems time-stamp telemetry by converting OBT to UTC with an accuracy of better than 2 ms.4849 Downstream, the Venus Express science operations centre converted the time correlation packets into SPICE spacecraft clock (SCLK) kernels for the instrument teams.50

    Solar Orbiter: the kernel implementation on a current mission¶

    The Solar Orbiter DDID tells external users that the MCS files internal packets on APID 1966 that they should normally ignore, with one exception, the time correlation packets, which are available from the data dissemination system (EDDS):38

    Inference: one lineage, two dialects

    The Solar Orbiter couple packet is TN-1030's layout shifted by 16 octets but without the OBT latching delay field, so from the ground station delay onwards its offsets are 8 octets smaller, and it appends the two frame counters, the OBT size and the raw 6-octet OBT. The coefficient packet does not follow TN-1030 Table 2 at all (no version, algorithm or synchronisation fields; standard deviation and error added); it is closer to the Rosetta-era packet (gradient, offset, standard deviation). The constant 16-octet shift matches PID_DFHSIZE = 16 of these packets, suggesting the MIB counts a 16-octet header before the data, as for a PUS packet; but the releaser's ground station response packet (SPID 66850, same APID and the same PID_DFHSIZE) has offsets starting at 0, so the MIB is not consistent on this point (see NCTRS, NIS). The "nsec" unit of the light time delay contradicts TN-1030's seconds. All of this is deduced from the MIB and is not stated by any public document.511112

    Both MIB releases (2020-04-16 and 2025-09-16) define the three packets with the same parameter counts and sizes.5152 The DDID also explains where the data end up for the science ground segment: time packets and coefficient packets are ordinary telemetry in the RAPID files forwarded to it.40

    Accuracy: what the sources say¶

    TN-1030 gives no numbers: accuracy and validity limits are configuration. The error sources are nevertheless visible in the equations. Every delay subtracted from the ERT, the time-tagging accuracy of the station, the latching delay and the on-board sampling accuracy all add to the deviation, and PUS asks the mission to declare the sampling accuracy and the accuracy of the time difference between the reference frame's transmission and the sampled time.1326 ECSS-E-ST-70-11C asks for a correlation better than <TIME_CORREL_ACCUR> over <TIME_CORREL_PERIOD>, with an on-board contribution below <ONBOARD_STABILITY> ms per day, all mission constants.2930 For Mars Express and Venus Express the published figure is better than 2 ms.4849

    Inference: the error budget

    The propagation delay dominates in size (minutes for interplanetary missions) but is predicted precisely by Flight Dynamics, so its error is small; ground and on-board delays are small but must be calibrated per station and per bit rate, as the Rosetta-era corrections show.41 The least-squares fit averages random errors over the buffer, while the difference algorithm takes each couple at face value. This qualitative reading is the wiki's, not TN-1030's.

    Open points¶

    • TN-1030 does not say whether the Table 1 and Table 2 layouts (taken, per its change log, from a Gaia document) are the kernel default or Gaia-specific, and it gives neither the size of the couple buffer nor the configuration of the least-squares window.
    • The update threshold (accuracy limit or half of it) is stated inconsistently (see above).
    • The signs in the relative and difference formulas are printed inconsistently.
    • The Solar Orbiter packets' exact semantics depend on the non-public Packetiser ICD BC-ESC-IF-22002 and the BepiColombo time correlation note BC-ESC-TN-05004.
