    NCTRS, NIS and the ground station interface¶

    A SCOS-2000 mission control system (MCS) never talks to an antenna directly. Between the ESA ground stations (ESTRACK) and the MCS at ESOC sits a generic gateway that receives the telemetry the stations deliver, forwards telecommands to the right station, collects tracking measurements and passes files in both directions. The documents call it the NCTRS, the NIS or, in the more recent ones, "NCTRS/NIS" (see Names on how the two relate), and the modern interface between it and the stations is CCSDS Space Link Extension (SLE). This page gathers what the public documents say about this layer: its roles, how three missions ran it (INTEGRAL in 2003, Herschel and Planck in 2011, and CHEOPS, whose paper describes passes through an INTA station without naming the NCTRS, the NIS or ESTRACK), how SCOS-2000 addresses it (command routing, throw events, release verification, ground station responses), how ESTRACK books the passes that the gateway then serves, the tracking data files of the station modem, the IFMS, and the flight dynamics flows that ride on it. The detailed NCTRS and NIS interface control documents are not public; the page is explicit about the resulting gaps.

    Names¶

    NCTRS Network Controller & TM Router System GSAW 2003 (ESOC)5
    NCTRS Network Command, Telemetry & Ranging System GSAW 2003 (ESOC)6
    NCTRS Network Command and Telemetry Routing System (in "NCTRS / SLE") GSAW 2003 (ESOC)8
    NCTRS Network Control and Telemetry Routing System 4S 2024 (DLR)13

    All the NCTRS expansions describe the same ESOC infrastructure component. The two names coexisted from at least 2003: the Rosetta/Mars Express FDS-MCS ICD of that year uses both, the GSAW 2005 slide lists an Aeolus contribution as "Network Interface System Automatic Schedule Execution (NCTRS)", and the MCS interface control document is titled "NIS/NCTRS".313424357 The time correlation note of 2018 speaks only of the "NIS header".52

    Inference

    No available source says explicitly whether the NIS is a subsystem of the NCTRS, a renamed NCTRS or its successor. The usage above (NIS alone in the most recent SCOS-2000 note, the pair "NCTRS/NIS" in the EDDS ICD of 2025) suggests that NIS is the current name of the same gateway.5249

    The Mars Express and Venus Express papers describe the functions. The ground station equipment performs basic checks on the telemetry and stamps it with the Earth Reception Time (ERT); the NCTRS passes housekeeping and science telemetry to the relevant MCS and gathers telecommands from the MCS to forward them to the relevant station. It also acquires and locally stores tracking data (antenna angles, range and Doppler measurements), made available to Flight Dynamics through the File Transfer System (FTS) and to authorised PIs through the DDS, and it transfers files generated at the operations centre, such as antenna pointing information and station schedules, to the stations.4142

    ESOC's 2003 overview shows the context of that time. The stations listed were Kiruna, Redu, Perth, Villafranca, Kourou, New Norcia, Cebreros and the NASA DSN; the wide-area network was ESANET over X.25 with "migration to TCP/IP planned", alongside SLE; the LEOP configuration shows prime and backup NCTRS machines next to the prime and backup SCOS-2000 servers. Under the NCTRS interface the slides list TMP4, TCE, MKII, MPTS MKIII and STC II, apparently the station equipment it interfaced at the time (the slide does not expand them).346 The command releaser was the SCOS-2000 component that interfaced the external command destination, the NCTRS.7

    Mission-dedicated instances

    The Rosetta and Mars Express FDS-MCS ICD lists, per mission, a prime and a backup "Network Control" node next to the prime and backup control systems, and names the FTS nodes of the NIS as NIA and NIB.3839 The DDID's TC history of the same missions names the release stage "Release from SCOS-2000 to the NCTRS".54 Herschel and Planck each had one NCTRS per MCS chain (A and B), started on the machines NCTRS-A and NCTRS-B (see Herschel and Planck).73

    The SLE user and provider¶

    In SLE terms the NCTRS/NIS is the SLE service user at ESOC and the station's TM/TC baseband system (TMTCS, "TM and TC BaseBand System") is the SLE service provider. The import ICD says so when it distinguishes the two kinds of throw event: those destined for the SLE service provider (TMTCS) and those executed by the SLE service user (NIS).488 The EDDS ICD names the release stage of a telecommand "Release from SCOS-2000 to the SLE User (NCTRS/NIS)".49 The Rosetta/Mars Express/Venus Express DDS packet header records, for each packet, the SLE service used, as one of twelve codes: rows SLC, MC, VC and "Bad Frames" combined with the columns real time, real time with flow control and off-line.55 The DDID does not expand SLC, MC and VC; their possible mapping onto the SLE return services is discussed, as an inference, on GDDS and the DDIDs.

    ESOC's 2019 overview of its control-system generations explains why this boundary is the standardised one. The MCS is the only ground system that manages all interactions with the space assets, through the ground station network. Because control centres and stations are not necessarily run by the same organisation, the TM/TC exchange between them was standardised by CCSDS through SLE, giving access to spacecraft through the international network of stations that support it; standards between the other ground systems (mission planning, flight dynamics, payload data processing) were, by contrast, "still lagging behind".6667

    Running the gateway: three missions¶

    The public interface documents say little about the gateway as a running system. Three operations documents fill part of the gap from the mission side.

    INTEGRAL (2003): the NCTRS as part of the MOC¶

    INTEGRAL's Flight Operations Plan, Volume 1 (issue 2, 21 February 2003), lists the NCTRS with the IMCS (the INTEGRAL Monitoring and Control System) and the flight dynamics system as MOC hardware and software under ESOC's standard configuration control, every modification needing the approval of the Spacecraft Operations Manager (SOM).6872 Its operational ground segment was the routine-phase stations Redu, Goldstone and Villafranca (backup), Perth for the launch and early orbit phase (LEOP), the MOC at ESOC and the communication facilities. The ground stations' task was the satellite-to-ground interface; the communications' task was data routing within the operational ground segment and to the science ground segment, including the transmission of satellite TM and auxiliary data to the INTEGRAL Science Data Centre (ISDC).6970

    The FOP shows the three layers of configuration control that a pass crossed:72

    Layer Controlled by Rule in the FOP
    ESA stations ESOC (TOS-G), as a resource shared between missions The INTEGRAL SOM approves modifications that could affect INTEGRAL
    DSN station (Goldstone) NASA NASA is expected to consult the SOM; INTEGRAL may have to adapt to JPL/Goldstone changes
    Communications (MOC to stations, MOC to ISOC and ISDC) TOS-ON, under the MOC's responsibility Shared resources; modifications that could affect INTEGRAL need SOM approval

    The mission rules make the gateway's chain a launch criterion. The countdown was to stop if the IMCS (in particular TM reception and processing and TC generation and release), flight dynamics and communications ("in particular OPSLAN") were not available in hot redundancy, or if the MOC could not send TCs to, and receive and process data from, Redu or Villafranca. Missing tracking at Redu, Villafranca and Goldstone only made the ground segment "not fully operational". After launch, losing contact with a ground station for more than 24 hours was one of the conditions for a spacecraft emergency.71 The GSAW 2003 slide that uses INTEGRAL as its example of an "S2K based MCS" is where the NCTRS is expanded as "Network Controller & TM Router System" (see Names).5

    Herschel and Planck (2011): one NCTRS per mission and chain¶

    The Herschel/Planck flight operations plan (PT-HMOC-OPS-FOP-6001-OPS-OAH, FOP issue 3.1) contains a ground segment procedure, C_GSP_MCS_NCTRS "Herschel/Planck NCTRS Setup" (version 1, created on 11 August 2011), whose objective is starting or stopping NCTRS-A and B.73 Its constraint shows how the gateway was deployed for two missions operated side by side: there must only ever be one instance per mission running, that is only one Herschel NCTRS and one Planck NCTRS per MCS chain (A and B). An "OVERVIEW" task must never be started on NCTRS-A or B, "because it would allow to start another instance on a machine of the other mission".73 The procedure has no spacecraft configuration, command sequences or referenced displays: it is purely a ground operation.73

    The flowchart redraws the six steps of the procedure.74 In step 3 the stop command is run "3 times" because the clean-up is sometimes incomplete, and the operator checks the process list for tasks with "Herschel" or "Planck" in their names. If the NCTRS does not start, it is probably still running and the operator goes back to stopping it; if it cannot create its windows, the display permission was forgotten and the procedure starts again.74 The procedure does not expand DCR; ESOC's 2003 LEOP set-up slide expands it as "dedicated Control Room".4

    Credentials and host names not reproduced

    The procedure prints the NCTRS machine names, the operating-system account and the NCTRS MMI account with their passwords. This wiki does not reproduce them. The procedure is also summarised on Herschel/Planck interface documents.

    Inference

    Three points follow from the procedure but are not stated in it. The NCTRS of that time was a set of operating-system processes started per mission by scripts, with its own X-based user interface (the "NCTRS MMI") and its own user login, separate from the SCOS-2000 client. "Chain A and B" suggests that each NCTRS machine served one of the two redundant MCS chains of both missions. And the warning about OVERVIEW suggests a multi-mission start-up mode that the Herschel/Planck configuration deliberately avoided.7374

    By February 2014 the NIS user interface was among the ESOC GUIs re-implemented on the Java and Eclipse-based EGOS User Desktop (EUD), next to those of SCOS-2000, SIMSAT and the FARC (see EUD).75

    CHEOPS: a pass without ESTRACK (2025)¶

    CHEOPS, operated by its consortium with a SCOS-2000 5.4.2 MCS at INTA's Torrejón centre (CEIT), communicates through the INTA ground station there; its paper mentions neither ESTRACK nor the NCTRS or NIS, but it describes every step of a pass at the MCS-station boundary:767877

    1. Booking. INTA's CEIT G/S Planner (written in C#) takes pass requests from CHEOPS and the other missions of the station, resolves conflicts and configures the control system of each station with the passes, their start and end times, configurations and mode-change times. From the flight dynamics visibility file it produces GSAvailability, an XML list of the CHEOPS passes of the next three weeks with station, start time and duration.77
    2. Pass preparation. For each pass the SCOS-2000 CommandBuilder generates TC stacks that begin and end "the MCS-G/S communication" and start and stop the downlink of recorded TM, and places them in a pass folder.79
    3. During the pass. A Python script checks the SCOS-2000 tasks, loads the stack that opens the MCS-station connection into an Auto Stack, reads MISC variables to verify that the ground commanding capability is available, switches to AD mode and uplinks. Real-time housekeeping TM goes straight from the station to the MCS. At the end the stack that closes the connection is sent.7980
    4. After the pass. The recorded TM, which the station wrote to a binary file, is fetched by FTP; the SCOS-2000 TM Processor splits it into one file per virtual channel (real-time housekeeping, recorded housekeeping, science), and gaps are found from jumps in the virtual channel frame counter.81
    5. Tracking. Two-way Doppler files from the station go to flight dynamics, where PREPRO converts them into ESA's NAPEOS tracking data format for orbit determination.77

    Inference

    The CHEOPS stacks that "begin and end the MCS-G/S communication" occupy the place that the Solar Orbiter MIB gives to the NIS throw events OPEN SESSION, OPEN LINK and CLOSE LINK (below). The paper does not describe their content, their command type or the protocol between the MCS and the INTA station, and it mentions SLE only for the simulator.7982

    Time stamps¶

    The ERT of each frame reaches the MCS in the header with which the NIS delivers the frame, and the time correlation works backwards from it.52 When the station is an SLE provider, the ERT is required to equal the frame reception time, so the ground delay is zero by definition.53

    Command routing¶

    In the MIB, the optional dst table routes commands by APID. Any APID not listed goes to the default destination NCTRS, which may only be used for spacecraft commands; EGSE.SCOE (non-spacecraft commands) and EGSE.TC.<address> (spacecraft commands sent through a SCOE and TC front end) are the alternatives, used by SCOS-2000-based EGSE.46 The Solar Orbiter MIB has a single dst record, routing APID 1372 to EGSE.TC.0x8800, which is exactly the example Destination ID printed in the ICD; every other command uses the NCTRS default.6346 The April 2020 release has the same single record. The instrument databases in the library carry a dst table with no rows: the SWA database (2021 ASCII set, 198 commands; v6.5.0, 201 commands) and the STIX database v2.26.38 (100 commands), so in an MCS importing them every instrument command would take the NCTRS default.6465

    Inference

    An empty dst in an instrument database is what the ICD's default implies for flight use: instrument commands travel through the spacecraft like any other telecommand. Whether the instrument teams' own EGSE set-ups used a different routing is not stated in the available sources.4665

    The SCOS-2000 EGSE of the Herschel/Planck era exposes the same switch as the MISCconfig variable EGSE_MODE: 0, the releaser connects only to the NCTRS; 1 (the default), to both the NCTRS and the EGSE router; 2, only to the EGSE router. When the NCTRS was not running the desktop raised a connection alarm, and an NCTRS simulator could be started from three processes, TCSIM, ADMSIM and CLCWSIM.5051

    Release and ground verification¶

    A telecommand passes through three ground-related verification stages before any on-board stage, reported in the TC history as follows (EDDS ICD, version 24.0):49

    Stage Meaning
    R Release from SCOS-2000 to the SLE user (NCTRS/NIS). Fails if PTV was not OK at release, if no TCP/IP connection to the NCTRS existed, or if the NCTRS accepted and then rejected the command, for example because its station connection was down; in that case an administrative message from the NCTRS is logged
    G Ground station reception (first uplink verification stage)
    T Uplink, also called radiation (second stage); for a throw event this is its execution stage
    O On-board reception (third stage, AD mode only)

    The Solar Orbiter DDID adds that the uplink time in the SCOS-2000 TC packet starts as the release time and is then updated with the time stamp copied from the station's radiation response message, and the execution time of an immediate command becomes uplink time plus propagation delay.59 See Command verification in the MIB for the stages after uplink.

    CHEOPS shows the same chain in routine use at a non-ESA station. Its automation commands in AD mode, which detects missing TCs and retransmits them, and "several steps of acknowledgement" are enabled to trace the reception and the transmission of each TC by the ground station and its reception and, for immediate TCs, execution on board; with this mode SCOS-2000 verifies "the correct and complete uplink".80

    Inference

    The two station steps of the CHEOPS paper look like the G and T stages above, and the on-board steps like the O stage plus the PUS acceptance and execution reports (see COP-1 and ST[01]). The paper does not name the stages, so the correspondence is this wiki's.4980

    Ground station responses as telemetry¶

    SCOS-2000 files the station responses it receives as an internal telemetry packet, which the Solar Orbiter MIB defines as SPID 66850 "TM Packet used by the Releaser G/S response" (APID 1966, type 165, subtype 1, 34 octets):63

    Octet Parameter Type Values
    0 GS Packet Size unsigned, 32 bits
    4 GS Packet Type enumerated, 16 bits 0 BDseq, 1 BDseq_response, 3 LinkStatus_PCM, 4 COP1directive, 5 ThrowEvnt, 6 ThrowEvnt_Resp, 7 CLTU, 8 CLTU_Resp, 9 SDID_MKII_RQM, 10 SDID_MKII_TRM, 20 FSPPkt, 21 FSPPkt_Resp, 22 FSPdirective, 23 FSPdir_Resp
    6 GS Spacecraft ID unsigned, 16 bits
    8 GS Acceptance Time absolute time, CDS with microseconds (PTC 9, PFC 2), not correlated
    16 spare 3 octets
    19 GS CLTU ID unsigned, 32 bits
    23 GS Acknowledgement enumerated, 8 bits 0 Confirm_Accept, 1 ConfirmTrnsmit, 3 Failure_accept, 4 FailureTrnsmit, 9 Nis_Rejected
    24 GS Acknowledgement unsigned, 8 bits (second parameter of the same name)
    25 spare 1 octet
    26 GS Next TC ID unsigned, 32 bits
    30 spare 4 octets

    The values come from the textual calibrations CYGT2202TM and CYGT2207TM. The octets are the PLF_OFFBY values as stored; they start at 0 although PID_DFHSIZE of this packet is 16, whereas the time correlation packets on the same APID start at octet 16 (see Time correlation). Whether they count from the start of the packet data is not documented.63

    Inference

    The packet looks like a copy of the response messages of the NCTRS/NIS TC interface: the packet types name the data units that flow on it (BD sequences, COP-1 directives, CLTUs, throw events, link status, FSP packets and directives, and two "SDID MKII" data units from the older station equipment), and the acknowledgement separates acceptance by the station from transmission, with a separate code for rejection by the NIS itself. This matches the "G" and "T" stages above. The interpretation is the wiki's; the message format is defined in the non-public NIS/NCTRS Volume 2 ICD.634943

    Throw events¶

    A throw event is a ground control directive that travels on the command path but is not uplinked. SLE defines it for the forward CLTU service: the user invokes CLTU-THROW-EVENT to have the provider forward an event requiring management action to SLE complex management, for example a change of uplink bit rate after the spacecraft has been commanded to the new rate; the event is asynchronous with the radiation of CLTUs, and the return only says whether the invocation was accepted.60 Since SCOS-2000 R5.0 the MIB can define two kinds, by ccf field CCF_CTYPE:4548

    CCF_CTYPE Kind Executed by Content
    T SLE throw event Sent to the SLE service provider (station equipment), not uplinked Event ID and following fields as in Table 2-14 of the NIS/NCTRS Volume 2 ICD
    N NIS throw event Executed by the NIS, not forwarded to the station Event ID and following fields as in Table 3.2 of the NIS Throw Events ICD

    Throw events cannot be blocked with other entries of a sequence or command source.44

    Booking the pass: ESTRACK scheduling and cross support¶

    The service agreement (sagr) and service package (spack) attributes in those identifiers take values that are, in the words of the SLE books, to be agreed between the user and the provider;61 agreements and packages are the objects of SLE service management (see SLE service management). Before the NIS can bind anything, the pass has to be booked. A SpaceOps 2025 paper by authors at ESOC describes that side, as seen from the ESTRACK Scheduling Office (ESO).8384

    Capacity and partners. In twenty years the number of ESA spacecraft in flight tripled while ESTRACK capacity stayed the same, and deep-space demand now exhausts the network at peak periods. ESTRACK therefore books six external providers and in turn serves five external partners, acting as user, provider and broker.838486

    From standing orders to a booking. Each project starts with a mission agreement listing the available services and the interfaces to implement them, often with standing orders (planning requirements and constraints) that are translated into the configuration of the ESTRACK Planning System (EPS). The EPS solver combines all users' standing orders into one consistent booking, typically 4 weeks ahead for the low Earth orbit network and up to 12 months for deep-space antennas. Users then request additions, modifications and deletions, and providers sometimes propose supports themselves.85 Every change request is checked in four steps:85

    Check Content
    1. Validity Format, identifiers and privileges are correct
    2. Conflict The request is blocked until all conflicts are resolved
    3. Technicality The service is available to the user at the requested terminal, and the visibility suits the activity
    4. Availability (external provider) The request is forwarded to the provider

    The ESTRACK Management System web Portal (EMSP) handles all these exchanges, for people through the web and for systems through REST or files. Requests and supports can change on either side without notice (LEOP, maintenance, failures), so ESTRACK closes the loop with the provider's Simple Schedule Format (SSF) list of everything it has agreed to support rather than with per-request confirmations; new interfaces since 2017 use the SSF and the draft SMURF request format, and the core is shielded from each partner's variant by XML and XSL transforms.868788 Across six agencies and five commercial entities, the same minimal data set proved sufficient: spacecraft, ground station or terminal, beginning and end of track, a service name or comment, plus service parameters and the user and provider identifiers.89

    From a booking to an executed pass. ESTRACK network operations are fully automated. Each service type is linked to a scheduling template that lists the automated tasks to run on the target systems, and the templates turn every allocation into an executable timeline. A published catalogue of "user scheduled events", basic actions at absolute times such as starting or stopping the uplink or a recording, covers occultations and link changes inside a support; it has become a de facto standard for ESTRACK users and providers. External providers receive the same information (service type, parameters, events) and may execute it automatically or manually.91 The paper's advice is to keep only tested configurations reachable through the interface and to be able to set up a service from instructions read over a voice loop when automation fails.9092

    The ESTRACK paper does not mention the NIS, the NCTRS or the MCS, and the NIS/NCTRS ICDs are not public.     Tracking data: the IFMS-OCC interface¶

    The IFMS (Intermediate Frequency & Modem System, also "IF and Modem System") is the station modem that produces the radiometric measurements (Doppler and ranging themselves are described on RF and ranging). Its interface with data clients such as the NCTRS is "for historical reasons" called the IFMS-to-OCC (Operations Control Centre) interface, and issue 11.4.0 of its ICD (2006) is public through the Mars Express radio science archive.1615

    Protocol and services¶

    The interface is entirely FTP over TCP/IP; the IFMS is a file server, and files are read-only for users. The server supports at least USER, PASS, QUIT, PORT, MODE (stream only), TYPE (ASCII and image only), STRU (file only), CWD, PWD, LIST and RETR; no restart, no experimental commands. It offers four services: catalogue access by directory listing, off-line data set retrieval, "pseudo-on-line" access (instead of streaming, short data sets are closed at configurable durations, so retrieving them frequently approximates a stream; technically identical to off-line access) and file management, which is performed only by the IFMS (creation by its acquisition processes; deletion automatically or on request through its STC and DCP interfaces).1718 External users use one read-only account; they can neither modify nor delete data sets or logs.30

    Data sets¶

    Data sets are stored under <root>/station/spacecraft/, open-loop data under a separate root. File names have eight underscore-separated fields:1920

    Field Characters Example Meaning
    Station ID 4 PER1 From the IFMS configuration
    Spacecraft ID 4 CLU3 From the configuration
    Year 4 2002 Year of DAP start
    Day of year 3 108 Day of DAP start
    Data set kind 2 OP Free mission choice, e.g. OP, TS, CL
    DAP type 2 RG D1, D2 (Doppler 1 and 2), G1, G2 (AGC), ME (meteo), OL (open loop), RG (ranging)
    DAP start time 6 145513 hhmmss
    Data set sequence ID 4 0001 Extended to 5 digits beyond 9999

    The example PER1_CLU3_2002_108_OP_RG_145513_0001 is the ICD's: operational ranging, which the ICD's own narrative describes as a "Perth_1, Cluster_3" DAP.2021 A DAP (data acquisition procedure) lasts at most 20 hours and a data set at least 100 samples at 0.1 s, so a long DAP can reach sequence IDs of about 7200; a configuration change closes the data set and opens the next with the same DAP start time.21

    Ranging measurements taken during ambiguity resolution carry an offset known only once the ambiguity is resolved, so the IFMS keeps uncorrected copies in a raw/ subdirectory with a .raw extension and a header flag; issue 11.1.0 notes that the ranging data were not yet corrected, only raw files being available. Every closed ASCII data set also exists gzipped, except open-loop data.2122

    Content¶

    Every data set starts with a header: station, spacecraft, kind, DAP type, reference time tag of sample 0, first and last sample times, requestor (DCP or STC) and request ID, why it was opened (DAP_Started, Conf_Change, Max_Size_Reached, Tone_Lost), total samples, sample period, the use of the internal reference oscillator, the 70 or 230 MHz uplink carrier base, the actual carrier and tone indicators, the source of the "EPD" value (configuration or Doppler prediction; the ICD does not expand the term), the ranging correction flag, the sequence ID and the active configuration tables (modulator, frequency plan, tracking, demodulators). Time tags use YYYYMMDD.hhmmss.mmm.2324

    DAP type Fields per sample
    Doppler 1 and 2 sample number and time, 17.5 MHz NCO clock count, unwrapped carrier phase (turns), spurious carrier flag, accumulated one-way delta delay (s)
    AGC 1 and 2 carrier level (dBm), polarization angle, incoherent AGC gain, input power on channels A and B, carrier lock status
    Meteo humidity (%), pressure (hPa), temperature (°C)
    Ranging round-trip delay modulo the code ambiguity, current code (1 to 24), ambiguity-resolved, spurious carrier and tone and previous-correlation flags, estimated Doppler, and DSP status (lock, tone and code levels, phase error, tone loop SNR, modulation index)
    Open loop binary records of complex RHC and LHC samples at 1, 2, 4 or 12 bits

    The table condenses ICD Tables 1 to 4 and §6.7.252627

    The Doppler data set gives the delta delay computed from the carrier phase. With the count \(C\) of the 17.5 MHz clock and the carrier replica phase \(\Phi\) at the DAP start (index 0) and at sample \(N\):25

    \[ \Delta t = \frac{C_N - C_0}{17.5\times 10^{6}}, \qquad \Delta\Phi_{D} = (\Phi_N - \Phi_0) - \Delta t \cdot f_{in}, \qquad \Delta\tau = -\frac{\Delta\Phi_{D}}{k\, f_{down}} \]

    where \(f_{in}\) is the nominal (Doppler-free) carrier offset at the IFMS input relative to 70 MHz, \(f_{down}\) the downlink carrier frequency, and \(k = 2\) for a coherent transponder (two-way: the value is half the two-way delay) or \(k = 1\) for a non-coherent one.

    Open-loop records have a 44-octet header of eleven big-endian 32-bit words followed by 136 blocks of 6 octets:28

    The samples derive from the IFMS's 17.5 Msps complex baseband stream, itself filtered and decimated from the 280 Msps 8-bit output of the common front end; a 6-octet block holds 12, 6, 3 or 1 samples for 1, 2, 4 or 12-bit quantization; a new data set opens every minute; the filter centre frequency is the sp_centre value times \(17.5\times10^6/2^{32}\) Hz (about 4.075 mHz).28

    Support logs. Six ASCII support-log files (D1SupportLog, D2SupportLog, G1SupportLog, G2SupportLog, MESupportLog, RGSupportLog) record open, close and delete events with reasons, durations and sample counts, as a backup monitoring mechanism only, because FTP cannot lock them.29

    How the MCS uses them¶

    For Rosetta and Mars Express the IFMS files did not originate in the MCS, which only delivered them unchanged: the MCS, via the NIS, obtained ranging, Doppler 1 and 2, meteo and signal strength files from the IFMS and the antenna angle files from the station computer, stored them in the FARC and passed them to Flight Dynamics; NASA/DSN tracking files came instead through a dedicated flight dynamics gateway.31 High-priority files had to go straight to their destination, sent twice by the source when they also needed archiving; the FTS was point to point, so a file for two destinations was injected twice.3637

    Sources: the FDS-MCS ICD file tables and the DDID data sources.313256 In the opposite direction, Flight Dynamics delivered Spacecraft Trajectory Data Messages (STDMs) for ESA stations, archived by the MCS and transferred "via the NIS (also passive regarding data file contents)" to the station computers; for the DSN it delivered CCSDS ephemeris messages through its gateway. Rosetta's MPTS files were the one exception to FTS transfer, fetched by Flight Dynamics with manual FTP from the two Rosetta "Network Control" nodes.334038 The one-way light time files used for time correlation are identified by a Flight Dynamics ground station ID that, the ICD warns, does not correspond to the NCTRS ground station ID.34

    The CHEOPS paper of 2025 shows the radiometric side at a non-ESA station without any of this machinery: the station's tracking data files with two-way Doppler measurements go to the flight dynamics system, whose PREPRO module converts the frequency shifts (Hz) into radial velocities (km/s) in the NAPEOS tracking data format for orbit determination by BAHN, twice a day after each session of passes. The paper does not say how the files travel from the station.77

    Ground station identity and monitoring in the MCS¶

    Every SCOS-2000 TM packet carries the numerical ID of the station that received its frame. Solar Orbiter's list: 21 Kourou, 22 Perth, 23 New Norcia, 24 Cebreros, 25 Malargüe, 30 Maspalomas, 201 reference test station (ESOC), 202 NDIU Lite and 203 NDIU IFMS.58 The Rosetta/Mars Express/Venus Express list is on GDDS and the DDIDs.

    The Solar Orbiter MIB also defines station monitoring telemetry, packets "LMS_NNO", "LMS_CEB" and "LMS_MLG" for New Norcia, Cebreros and Malargüe, each in a low and a high frequency variant:63

    SPID APID Description Parameters tpcf size
    66029, 66030 2048, 2049 LMS_NNO low / high frequency 156 / 16 716 / 69
    66025, 66026 2060, 2061 LMS_CEB low / high frequency 152 / 6 700 / 66
    66027, 66028 2064, 2065 LMS_MLG low / high frequency 150 / 6 691 / 66

    The New Norcia low-frequency packet carries 78 64-bit real values and 78 3-bit enumerated "VALIDITY" parameters. The real values include weather station temperature, wind speed, rain, hail, humidity and pressure, antenna actual and predicted azimuth and elevation and their rates, round-trip delay, S- and X-band uplink EIRP, amplifier output powers, and for two downlink chains the AGC carrier level, estimated SNR and frame counters ("Rece F", "Good F").63

    Inference

    APIDs 2048 to 2065 do not fit the 11-bit APID field of a space packet (maximum 2047),62 so these packets cannot come from the spacecraft: they are ground-generated pseudo-packets that bring station monitoring into the SCOS-2000 parameter world, where they can be displayed, limit-checked and archived like spacecraft telemetry. LMS is not expanded in the available sources, and how the data reach the MCS (through the NIS or another station monitoring interface) is not stated.63

    SCOS-2000 descendants: DLR's SLE switchboard¶

    DLR's German Space Operations Center runs GECCOS, a monitoring and control system branched off from SCOS-2000. GECCOS talks to the stations with the NCTRS protocol, and a separate SLE Switch Board (SSB) translates between that bespoke protocol and standard SLE, giving GECCOS access to the worldwide station networks that offer SLE.1114 In DLR's 2021 demonstration of a mobile, compact control centre for the BIROS satellite, the first campaign used the NCTRS protocol to the ground station, with the SSB part of the ground data system; the second was to integrate the SSB into the MCS for a standards-compliant antenna interface.1012 The 2024 paper on integrating small satellites places GECCOS with the SSB at the heart of the GSOC multi-mission environment, alongside the MIB and procedures, before a future move to EGS-CC.14

    Inference

    That a SCOS-2000 descendant still speaks "the NCTRS protocol" in 2021–2024 and needs a protocol bridge to reach SLE stations suggests that the MCS-side NCTRS interface outlived the NCTRS software and that the NIS keeps it towards the MCS while speaking SLE towards the stations. The available sources do not describe the protocol itself.1114
