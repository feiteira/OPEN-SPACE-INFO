    Herschel/Planck interface documents: packets, ground links and the CCS¶

    Herschel and Planck shared one prime contractor, one set of packet rules for both spacecraft and their instruments, and one mission operations centre at ESOC.1213 This page reads the set of Herschel/Planck interface control documents that the library now holds, from the on-board packet layer to the operator's workstation at ESOC. The central document is the Packet Structure Interface Control Document (PS-ICD, SCI-PT-ICD-07527), which fixes every telemetry and telecommand packet, service, application process identifier and parameter type of both spacecraft, and also the MIL-STD-1553B data bus protocol between the central computer and the instruments.1276 Around it sit the Space/Ground ICD for the radio link and the transfer frames, the documents of the test systems (the instrument teams' EGSE router, the PIPE protocol, the Central Checkout System and the Herschel/Planck System Database), the HIFI instrument's telecommand and database documents, and the ground segment interface documents that carried telemetry and command history to the science centres.91144209

    The programme-level story (the decision of 2000 to build on SCOS-2000, the HPSDB naming convention, the HCSS tailoring of the MIB) is told on Herschel/Planck: SCOS-2000 in a science mission ground segment, and the missions themselves on Herschel and Planck. This page goes one level down, into the bits, bytes and message flows. Where the documents disagree with each other, the page says so; a summary of the discrepancies is at the end.

    At a glance

    • Packets: TC packets of at most 248 octets (228 when stored in the mission timeline), TM packets of at most 1024 octets, PUS version number 0, a 3-bit command source field inside the TC sequence count, and a 16-bit CRC on every packet.1719
    • APIDs: one base APID per user; its four least significant bits separate essential housekeeping, routine housekeeping and science, and prime from redundant units.67
    • Link: X-band up and down, 1115-octet transfer frames, Reed-Solomon (255,223) with interleave 5 plus convolutional coding, six virtual channels in use, COP-1 with AD and BD.10495100
    • Bus: a 1 s frame of 64 subframes of 24 message slots; TM packets are pulled by the bus controller with a request/confirmation handshake.83
    • Test systems: a TCP router keyed on APID for instrument tests, the PIPE protocol (10-byte header, sync word 0xFADE) between the CCS and every SCOE, and MIB extensions (cur, grp, lgf, delta limits) that later entered the standard SCOS-2000 import ICD.137148177247

    The Packet Structure ICD¶

    Status and history¶

    The PS-ICD complements the ESA Packet Telemetry and Packet Telecommand standards (PSS-04-106 and PSS-04-107) with "all the mission specific details" of the data transfer between ground and on-board applications. Being tailored for Herschel/Planck, it states that it supersedes the Packet Utilisation Standard ECSS-E-70-41A, which it lists only as a reference document. Requirement 1000-GEN makes it exhaustive: the space and ground segment shall support all and only the structures and services it defines.12 The document is agreed by the ESA project manager, the ESOC representative and the prime contractor, and then controlled by the prime contractor.12 Requirements carry identifiers (for example 3000-TC, 5050-TCV) and point to their parent requirement in the Operations Interface Requirements Document (OIRD, SCI-PT-RS-07360) with tags such as [P:SCI-PT-RS-07360#3.1-PACK-14.12

    Operations scenario¶

    The PS-ICD's scenario explains why its services look the way they do. Most telecommands are stored on board and released at a defined time by the on-board scheduling service, from a Mission Timeline (MTL): a linear sequence of time-tagged commands, loaded during each daily communication period, that controls the satellites for up to 48 hours. Function management and on-board control procedures (OBCPs) handle longer activities, and OBCPs "shall be kept simple".1315 Every telecommand is verified by a TC verification report at acceptance (1010-GEN); progress and completion may be reported by verification packets or by housekeeping.13 The Command and Data Management System (CDMS) routes all packets and is the controller of the on-board serial data bus.14

    Telecommand packet¶

    All telecommand packets follow PSS-04-107 with the following mission rules (3000-TC, 3010-TC):17

    The packet ends with a 16-bit Packet Error Control field (see the CRC).17

    Field Bits Values Meaning
    Version 3 0b000 Required for all commands
    Type 1 1 Telecommand
    Data field header flag 1 1 Only TC(2,3), the Standard Distribute CPDU command, has no data field header, so its type and subtype are not in the packet
    APID 11 Appendix 3 Application or unit addressed
    Sequence flags 2 0b11 All telecommands are stand-alone packets
    Source part 3 see below Generator of the command
    Sequence part 11 0 to 2047 Incremented by the source for each command to that APID; wraps to 0; never checked for monotony by the receiver
    Packet length 16 odd, ≤ 241 Data field length minus one; the data field is a whole number of 16-bit words
    CCSDS secondary header flag 1 0 "Non-CCSDS defined" secondary header
    PUS version 3 0 Only version 0 allowed
    Ack 4 bit 3 to bit 0 Requests for success reports; failure reports are always generated
    Type, subtype 8 + 8 Appendix 2 Unique across all application processes
    Spare 8 0 Pads the header to two words

    The 3-bit source part is the mission's way to trace who released a command, which matters because one instrument can receive commands from ground, the timeline, OBCPs and FDIR:17

    Source Meaning
    000 Ground, highest priority, all sources
    001 FDIR telecommands of the CDMU
    010 Mission Time Line (maintained by ground, executed by the CDMU)
    011 On-board control procedures (executed by the CDMU)
    100 Ground, low priority, all sources
    101 FDIR telecommands of the ACC
    110 CDMU, other on-board sources
    111 ACMS, other on-board sources

    When an acknowledgement is requested, the verification report must copy the full sequence control word as the identifier of the command.17 The Ack bits use the CCSDS numbering, so the acceptance request is the last bit of the field (---1). Ground sets it nominally; when it is cleared, the application still performs the acceptance checks but does not send the TM(1,1) success report.18

    Length limits. The packet data field holds at most 242 octets, so a TC packet is at most 248 octets. Because almost all commands are executed from the MTL, they are wrapped as a variable octet string inside TC(11,4), which leaves 228 octets for the embedded packet and 216 octets for its application data.1743 Commands stored as event/action actions are limited to 232 octets (220 of application data), and an OBCP load segment carries at most 232 octets of code.5755

    PUS version 0

    ECSS-E-70-41A (2003) tells its users to set the TC and TM PUS version number to 1.245 The PS-ICD keeps version 0, the value of the earlier ESA PUS, in both headers.1720 See PUS-A vs PUS-C and PUS packet structures.

    On-board verification of telemetry¶

    Some TM packets are consumed on board, for example by the event/action service, so the CDMU and the ACC check them before use. Requirement 4020-TM lists the checks with failure codes that match the TC acceptance codes: 2 for the packet error control, 1 for the length, 0 for an illegal APID, 3 for the type and 4 for the subtype; science packets of service 21 are exempt. A failed packet produces a type 5 event with the code and at least 4 octets of the faulty packet, its data are not used on board, and the packet goes to ground on its usual virtual channel, or on VC4 if its APID, type or subtype cannot be identified.21

    Conventions and field alignment¶

    Bit 0 is the first transmitted bit and the most significant bit of an unsigned field; for a signed field, bit 0 is the sign.61 The alignment rules of Appendix 1 shape every layout on this page:61

    1. A parameter of 16 bits or more is word aligned (its LSB on a word boundary).
    2. A parameter shorter than 16 bits never crosses a word boundary.
    3. Short parameters are right-adjusted in their 16-bit word, with padding in the most significant bits.
    4. Several short parameters in one word are right-adjusted together.

    A consequence appears in the housekeeping report: 8-bit parameters are grouped by pairs in a word, so an odd sequence of them gets a spare octet before its last member.31 Type and subtype numbers are written as two numbers, TC(1,1) or TM(1,2), and the numbering of types is chosen to pair TCs with their reports, so it is not contiguous.62

    Parameter types¶

    Appendix 6 defines the only parameter types allowed in packets. The parameter code is a pair (PTC, PFC) of two 8-bit numbers (the 8-bit widths date from issue 3).724 Compare the PUS parameter types and MIB parameter types.

    PTC Type PFC values and lengths Notes
    1 Boolean PFC 0: 1 bit 0 false, 1 true
    2 Enumerated PFC 1-8, 12, 16, 32: that many bits Discrete, unordered values such as error codes
    3 Unsigned integer PFC 0-11: 4 to 15 bits; 12: 2 octets; 13: 3 octets; 14: 4 octets
    4 Signed integer Same lengths as PTC 3
    5 Real PFC 1: 32-bit IEEE 754 single; PFC 2: 64-bit IEEE double PFC 1 is also the internal format of the DSP21020; PFC 3 (MIL-STD-1750A) removed in issue 6.0
    6 Bit string PFC 1, 2, 4, 8 bits
    7 Octet string PFC 0 variable (with length), PFC > 0 fixed Cannot be edited by the operator; a TC or TM packet carried as a variable octet string uses its own packet length instead of a length field
    8 Character string PFC 0 variable, PFC > 0 fixed On board only for the PROM software version identifier; SCOE/EGSE may use it freely
    9 Absolute time PFC 17: 6 octets, 4 coarse + 2 fine CUC without P-field, synchronised to TAI
    10 Relative time – Not used
    11 Deduced PFC 0 Type taken from a preceding field; used for OBCP parameters in TM(18,13), which are 32 bits except 64 bits for double reals

    Sources: Appendix 6 §A6-2 to A6-3.10.727311

    A unit that cannot provide a time synchronised with the Central Time Reference (CTR) sets the most significant bit of its time field to 1; the rest of the field then has a user-specific meaning.73

    Packet error control¶

    The 16-bit packet check sequence is a CRC with generator \(G(x) = x^{16} + x^{12} + x^5 + 1\) (CCITT V.41), encoder and decoder preset to all ones, computed over the whole packet except the last 16 bits. The same algorithm checks memory blocks in service 6. For blocks shorter than 32768 bits it detects all odd numbers of bit errors, all double errors and all single bursts of up to 16 bits.70 Appendix 4 gives test vectors (hexadecimal, real values from the source):71

    Data Packet check sequence
    00 00 1D 0F
    00 00 00 CC 9C
    AB CD EF 01 04 A2
    14 56 F8 9A 00 01 7F D5

    Checked by this wiki

    A bit-wise implementation of this CRC (polynomial 0x1021, initial value 0xFFFF, no reflection, no final XOR) reproduces all four vectors. The check is this wiki's, run with Python on 2026-11-12; the worked example further down uses the same implementation.

    APID assignment¶

    Appendix 3 gives every user a base APID in the most significant bits and uses the four least significant bits to tell packet categories and prime or redundant units apart, so part of the packet identification needs no decoding of the data field header. The project office manages the allocation, and one range is reserved for test equipment.67

    APID (dec) Hex TC packets TM packets

    Time packet and idle packet¶

    The Standard Spacecraft Time Source Packet carries spacecraft elapsed time to ground for correlation with UTC. It has no data field header, APID 0 and an 8-octet data field (packet length field 7). Its time refers to the leading edge of the first bit of the attached sync marker of the VC0 transfer frame whose VC frame count is 0 modulo 64. Its sequence count wraps from \(2^{14}-1\) to 0 and must never "short-cycle".74

    The P-field value 0b00101110 reads, in the CCSDS rules, as no extension, time code identification 010 (agency-defined epoch, level 2), \(3 + 1 = 4\) octets of seconds and 2 octets of fraction.74246 The same P-field byte reappears in the time broadcast on the on-board bus (see below), and the EGSE interface specification gives the EGSE CUC time a TAI epoch of 1958 January 1.85150 See Time code formats and Time correlation in SCOS-2000.

    The idle packet fills a frame when not enough source packets are available: APID 0x7FF, no data field header, sequence flags 11, sequence count fixed at 0x0000, free length and a pseudo-random data field.75

    Service 2: device commands¶

    TC(2,1) carries 1 to 8 pulse command addresses, TC(2,2) 1 to 8 register address/data pairs.26 The CPDU command TC(2,3) carries pairs of an 8-bit output line identifier and an octet with 5 spare bits and a 3-bit duration \(D\); the pulse lasts \(13\ \text{ms} \times 2^{D}\), from 13 ms (\(D=0\)) to 1664 ms (\(D=7\)). The "Standard" version, without data field header, is executed directly by the TC decoder with 1 to 120 pulse instructions; the "Mission specific" version goes through the on-board software and carries 1 to 8.27

    Service 3: housekeeping and diagnostics¶

    Each unit sends one nominal HK packet at a default period, plus essential HK packets on the base APID sized for the worst link conditions.28 All periods are powers of two of the unit's basic sampling period: the packet period is \(2^{n}\) times it (\(n \ge 0\)), and a super-commutated parameter appears \(2^{k}\) times consecutively at the end of the report and is sampled correspondingly faster, which gives it a reporting period shorter than the basic sampling period.28 A Structure Identifier (SID) implicitly defines all sampling and structure of a packet.28

    TC(3,1) and TC(3,2) define a report with an HK packet ID (16 bits), SID (16), sampling interval (16), segment ID (8), N (8) and N parameter IDs (16 bits each). Long definitions are split into segments 1 to K (at most 254) and closed by a segment 0xFF with N = 0; a single-packet definition uses segment 0. One TC carries at most 114 parameter IDs. For the CDMS and ACMS a dedicated USD-ID turns a "parameter" into a RAM area (start address MSB, LSB, length), at most 28 per TC.2930 Redefinition requires the old entry to be cleared, keeps the APID and real-time VC implied by the HK packet ID, and disables the packet meanwhile; TC(3,3) and TC(3,4) clear definitions and disable generation as TC(14,2) would; TC(3,9) and TC(3,11) ask for TM(3,10) and TM(3,12).2830 The reports TM(3,25) and TM(3,26) contain a 16-bit SID, the parameters and an optional spare octet for word alignment.31

    Service 5: event reporting¶

    Three subtypes route events by severity: TM(5,1) for information and warnings, TM(5,2) exception reports when an on-board recovery procedure exists (the CDMS may modify the MTL or start an OBCP), and TM(5,4) error/alarm reports when only ground can intervene.32

    An optional variable Parameters B field follows. The design goal is that the event ID alone determines the layout.33 Routine, especially cyclic, software functions shall not need TC verification reports, and events that only show normal software progress need ESA and prime approval.32

    Service 6: memory management¶

    The service is a last resort, used only when a memory location cannot be reached by nominal means. Addresses count in Smallest Addressable Units (SAU) of 8, 16, 32 or 48 bits, defined per unit; the memory ID and start address together give the absolute address, and their combination may extend the 16-bit address for the CDMS and ACMS.34 TC(6,2) loads (memory ID 16, start address 16, spare 8, N 8, data, optional pad octet, CRC 16 over the unpadded data), with at most 228 octets of data; the unit writes, reads back and compares before reporting TM(1,7) or TM(1,8). TC(6,5) and TC(6,9) dump and check (memory ID, start, N), TC(6,11) aborts a dump, and TM(6,6) and TM(6,10) return data (at most 1016 octets per packet) or a CRC; a star-tracker dump has its own data layout.3536

    Service 8: function management¶

    TC(8,1) starts a function (FID 8 bits, spare 8, SID 16, parameters), TC(8,2) stops it, TC(8,4) performs an activity (FID, activity ID, SID, parameters), TC(8,5) asks for the TM(8,6) status report.37 Starting an active function or stopping a stopped one is rejected with TM(1,2), and an activity on a stopped function is rejected too. The split between TC(8,1) and TC(8,4) supports an "Arm-Fire" pattern, and critical functions should terminate by themselves.3738 The design goal again is one fixed parameter set per FID and activity, so the SID can be "don't care".38 Table A-2.1 also lists TM(8,7) SREM data, TM(8,8) VMC data and TM(8,9) mass memory dump reports, each for a single user.64

It verifies a user with TC(9,6), on which the CDMS sends TC(9,7) to the user; at the next sync signal the CDMS reports TM(9,8) (the CTR) and the user TM(9,9) (its own clock).     Service 12: on-board monitoring¶

    The CDMS keeps a monitoring list of check definitions per parameter, and reports every checking status transition, both out of and back into range. The default number of consecutive samples for a transition is 3, and the basic monitoring period is a negative power of two of the basic sampling period.45 TC(12,5) adds or modifies entries with, for each one: parameter ID, monitoring ID, monitoring status, monitoring interval (1 to 65535), repetition number, a 16-bit check value, a 16-bit mask, a check type and two event IDs.46 The parameter is right-aligned and ANDed with the mask before comparison. The check types are bit values: 1 equal, 2 greater, 4 less, 8 not equal, 16 greater or equal, 32 less or equal, each with an expected result of FALSE. A transition to OUT_OF_RANGE generates TM(5,2) with Event-ID-1, the return generates TM(5,1) with Event-ID-2, and monitoring event IDs are above 32767.4748 Compare the ground-side monitoring checks in the MIB.

    Service 18: on-board control procedures¶

    OBCPs run in the CDMS, the ACC and possibly instrument computers; each has a procedure ID from 1 to 65535 (0 in TC(18,4) stops all). TC(18,1) loads code in segments (ID, segment 1 to 254 then 0xFF, N ≤ 232, code); the loaded procedure is "stopped". Further commands delete, start, stop, suspend, resume, pass parameters, list, report status (TM(18,13), with procedure status 0 stopped, 1 running, 2 suspended) and dump (TC(18,14), TM(18,15)). Commands released by an OBCP get a fresh sequence count and CRC at release, and TM(1,9) reports these final values.545556 See ECSS-E-ST-70-01C on-board control procedures.

    Service 19: event/action¶

    The CDMS (optionally the ACMS) holds a detection list of {APID, event ID, action TC, enabled}. When a TM(5,1), (5,2) or (5,4) matches an enabled entry, the stored TC is sent. TC(19,1) adds an entry with its action disabled; replacing an entry needs its action to be disabled first. TC(19,4) and TC(19,5) enable and disable actions, TC(19,6) asks for TM(19,7) with one TC per packet. Editing the list is classified as hazardous.5758

    Service 21: science data¶

    The satellite data bus (Appendix 9)¶

    Appendix 9 is a self-contained specification of the physical, data link and transfer layers of the MIL-STD-1553B bus between the CDMS and its remote terminals (RTs), with its own numbering.76 Transformer-coupled stubs and a 75 Ω TSP cable are used; the CDMS, a redundant unit, is the bus controller (BC); RT-to-RT transfers are not foreseen; broadcast is allowed; message repetition on error is allowed.7778

    Timing. The Satellite Data Bus Protocol is cyclic and synchronised with the central on-board time: a 1-second frame of 64 subframes, each of 24 message slots. Subframes 1, 17, 33 and 49 are reserved for telecommands (33 also carries the time), the other 60 for telemetry; a TM packet must fit in one subframe. Slot 1 (150 µs) carries the synchronisation mode command (without data word in subframe 1, with data word elsewhere), slots 2 to 4 command and acquisition, slots 5 to 20 (750 µs each) data transfer, 21 to 23 packet control such as polling, and slot 24 is a regulation slot.8384 The performance requirements are: at least 350 kbps of packet throughput for all users, 50 TM and 12 TC packets per second, one time synchronisation per second, timing accuracy better than 100 µs and every user served at least twice a second.82 The BC switches between predefined bus profiles on ground command without interrupting service.84

    Inference

    Sixteen transmit subaddresses (11 to 26) of 32 16-bit words each hold \(16 \times 64 = 1024\) octets, exactly the maximum TM packet, and four receive subaddresses (11 to 14) hold 256 octets, enough for a 248-octet TC packet. The PS-ICD does not state this reasoning; it is this wiki's arithmetic from the subaddress table and the packet limits.7982

    Telecommand delivery. In one subframe the BC writes the packet from SA 11R onwards and then a two-word Packet Transfer Descriptor to SA 27R; at the next sync the RT checks the reserved bits and the 8-bit packet count, stores a new packet, and copies the descriptor to SA 27T as its confirmation, which the BC reads no earlier than three subframes later.86

    Telemetry retrieval. The RT announces a packet in its two-word TM Packet Transfer Request at SA 10T; the BC polls it at least one subframe ahead, reads the packet from SA 11T to 26T and returns a TM Packet Transfer Confirmation to SA 10R in the same subframe, with the flow control set to 11. A transmission error discards the packet and leads to one retry. In burst mode one RT sends fixed 1024-octet science packets in consecutive subframes, without retry.878889

    Time distribution. In subframe 33 the BC broadcasts (RT address 31, SA 8, 4 data words) the time valid at the start of the next frame: a reserved octet, the P-field 0b00101110, 4 octets of coarse and 2 of fine time; RTs copy it to SA 8T at the next frame sync.85

    Bus FDIR. The BC collects the message error, busy, subsystem and terminal flags of RT status words, transmission errors, no-response time-outs and loop-back failures. Bus medium A is the default; on a no-response, transmission or loop-back error the BC tests medium B with dedicated messages and switches all traffic to B only if B works; it never switches back automatically. Ground commands select the medium and enable or disable the automatic reconfiguration.8081

    An instrument OBCP in detail¶

    SPIRE's internal FDIR raises four TM(5,2) events: 0xC000 (DRCU power or temperature anomaly), 0xC010 (DPU power anomaly, switch SPIRE off), 0xC100 (operations anomaly, disable SPIRE commands) and 0xC110 (resume at the next subschedule), on APID 0x0500 or 0x0501.127 The OBCP DB_OBCP_H_SPIRE_DRCU_OFF (ID 0x1102, time-out 600 s, to be confirmed) shows the pattern that the note applies everywhere:128129

    1. Stop any conflicting OBCP (here the resume OBCP 0x1107) with TC(18,4).
    2. Disable, with TC(19,5), the EAT entries of the triggering event and of the event that would undo the recovery (0xC000 and 0xC110 on both APIDs).
    3. Disable the SPIRE command and meta subschedules with TC(11,2).
    4. Switch off the nominal and redundant power lines with the PCDU function TC(8,4,112,3) (unit codes 0x0033 and 0x0034, LCL 51 and 52).
    5. Report a TM(5,4) with event ID 0x1000 "SPIRE DRCU Switched OFF".
    6. Re-enable the EAT entries that triggered it with TC(19,4).

    OBCP parameters such as the subschedule IDs live in CDMS datapool words DID_OBCP_32_OFFSET_nnn; the same identifier carries different parameters on the two spacecraft, for example offset 000 is SPIRE_SUBS_ID_CMD on Herschel and HFI_SUBS_ID_CMD on Planck.130 The note leaves open which acknowledgements OBCP-generated commands should request, whether TM(1,9) is wanted for each, and suggests at most one TC per second per OBCP.131

    PIPE: the CCS protocol for SCOEs and instrument stations¶

    The EGSE Interface Requirement Specification defines how the Central Checkout System (CCS), the special check-out equipment (SCOEs), the TM/TC front end (DFE) and the instrument stations (IS) exchange data on 100 Mbit/s Ethernet with TCP/IP, on separate SCOE/DFE and IS LANs.144146 The PIPE protocol carries packets unchanged behind a header and adds remote commanding (RC) and remote monitoring (RM) of the test equipment itself, with acknowledgements, TC echo and a keep-alive.145 The CCS is the client and every SCOE, DFE or IS a server that accepts connections automatically, one socket per connection. The CCS expects a message at least every 60 seconds; an inconsistent length or synchronisation word in the header, or a message that cannot be read in 5 seconds, drops the connection with an alarm, while an unknown message ID or illegal VCID only raises an alarm.147

    Bytes are big-endian. The body is a TM or TC source packet of at most 1024 or 248 bytes.148149

    Message ID Kind (Type, subtype) Direction

    The TC history file¶

    The TC History ICD (SPIRE-ICS-DOC-000900, ICD #5 of the Herschel ground segment list) fixes the format of the TC History File (THF) that the HCSS ingests in ILT (from SCOS-2000), IST (from the CCS) and operations (from the MOC, through the DDID). It quotes as its own requirements the TC history requirements of the IRD issue 2.0 of December 2001 (its text points to AD2, the design description, but the requirement numbers and the applicable document AD3 are the IRD's). There the HSC pulls the TC history from the MOC; IRD issue 2.6 (above) has the MOC push it.224225214 Files are named THF_yymmdd_cccc.DAT (start date and a daily cyclic counter); about one file a day is expected.226227 The file is ASCII, with a header and fixed-length records, each ended by a new-line, fields left-justified and blank-padded, and no delimiters.228

    The real-time science interface (RTSI)¶

    The HPMCS Science Ground Segment Real-Time Telemetry ICD describes how real-time telemetry (VC0, VC4 and VC1 of normal passes) reached the instrument workstations at ESOC, IW@MOC for Planck and ICC@MOC for Herschel. The HP MCS RTSI ran on the HP Security Data Server (HPSDS) outside the ESOC operations firewall; the MCS TM packetiser connected to it as a client, and the PI applications connected to it as clients in turn, through CORBA over TCP/IP.233235 The RTSI performs no time or quality checks, provides no time correlation, replay or archive; clients detect gaps with the source sequence counter and must reconnect themselves after a link failure.234236

    The interface is the SCOS-2000 External Interface (EXIF) TM packet provision service (TMP_PRO_002 in the CORBA naming service). Clients register with registerTMpackets() and a filter by APID or, from SCOS-2000 3.1, by SPID; each packet arrives in a TMpacketNotifyData callback whose m_pktBodyRawData holds the source packet.237239 Up to 10 clients are supported.235

    Data item Mean source packet size Maximum rate
    VC0 TM 22 octets < 28 per second
    VC4 TM 300 octets < 10 per second
    VC1 TM 800 octets < 49 per second

    The ICD pessimistically assumes up to 1000 octets of overhead per transfer and sizes every transfer at 1800 octets; at 87 transfers a second one unfiltered client then needs about 1.2 Mbit/s and ten clients 12 Mbit/s, on one of two instances (Herschel and Planck).238 A template client in C++ with omniORB is part of the HPMCS deliveries.240 See SCOS-2000 architecture for EXIF.

    Two flight operations procedures¶

    The library holds two procedures of the Herschel/Planck flight operations plan (PT-HMOC-OPS-FOP-6001-OPS-OAH), both written by F. Keck and both in the FOP's spreadsheet layout with objectives, constraints, referenced displays, configuration control and numbered steps with branches.241243

    • C_GSP_MCS_SCOS (version 4 of April 2009, FOP issue 3.0) logs into a Herschel or Planck workstation, starts or stops an HPMCS client, connects the client to MCS chain A, B or C, and switches the workstation between missions by logging in again. Users log into the client with one of nine operational accounts, for example FCT or OBSM.241242
    • C_GSP_MCS_NCTRS (version 1 of August 2011, FOP issue 3.1) starts and stops the NCTRS A and B. Its constraint is that only one NCTRS instance per mission runs on each MCS chain. The operator first stops the mission's NCTRS (running the command three times, because cleanup is sometimes incomplete), never starts the "OVERVIEW" task, because it would allow a second instance to be started on a machine of the other mission, then starts the NCTRS for Herschel or Planck and logs into its MMI.243244
