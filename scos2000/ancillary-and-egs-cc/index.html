    Ancillary systems, MICONYS and the move to EGS-CC¶

    SCOS-2000 never worked alone. At the European Space Operations Centre (ESOC) it sits in a family of reusable ground software, branded EGOS from 2003 and presented in 2005 as the "ESOC software infrastructure". Around the SCOS-2000 kernel are a network router (NCTRS), data dissemination (GDDS, TDRS, EDDS), archives, data analysis (MUST, then ARES), tools that manage tailoring data and procedures (DABYS, MOIS, ProToS, FOPPER), automation (MATIS) and a desktop framework (EUD). ESOC's 2025 paper calls SCOS-2000 and its complementary systems the MICONYS suite of generic products, and announces its replacement by a system built on the European Ground Systems Common Core (EGS-CC).11487787

    This page follows that story from the 2005 infrastructure picture to the 2025 migration plan. It explains what each ancillary system does as far as the sources say, shows the tool set of one ESOC mission (INTEGRAL, 2003) and the selection made by a consortium-run mission outside ESOC (CHEOPS, 2025), describes the EGOS User Desktop that became the common face of these systems, then explains how EGS-CC differs from SCOS-2000, how ESOC extends and adapts it (EGOS-CC), and what happens to the MIB on the way. It ends with three neighbouring developments: a GSOC concept for small satellites on EGS-CC, the CCSDS Mission Product Distribution services compared with EDDS, and the web framework that turns payload requests into command request files. The tools that prepare the tailoring data (databases, procedures, displays) have their own page, operations preparation.

    At a glance

    • 2005: SCOS-2000, NCTRS, GDDS, TDRS/WebRM and reused mission tools form the ESOC software infrastructure; new mission control systems are specified as a "delta" against it, and mission families add a kernel layer.1416
    • 2013-2014: the EGOS User Desktop (EUD), a Java and Eclipse RCP framework, enters mission operation and becomes the replacement GUI of SCOS-2000, SIMSAT, NIS and FARC.118122
    • 2017-2019: the EGOS-CC project designs ESOC's EGS-CC based products and builds a pilot Juice MCS; GMV runs SCOS-era TOPE procedures on EGS-CC.5455
    • 2019: ESOC compares its four control-system generations; M4C, built on EGS-CC, is to provide features equivalent to "SCOS-2000 and its complementary systems".127
    • 2020: ARES, an evolution of MUST, is ESOC's off-line analysis and multi-mission storage system, with a Python library, novelty detection and a dependency finder.77
    • April 2024: ESOC reconfirms EGS-CC as the basis of its next MCS; Swarm is the pilot migration, Ariel the first mission to launch on it.57
    • 2033 at the earliest: predicted end of life of SCOS, after a transition of at least 7 years for more than 40 missions.3

    The ESOC software infrastructure (2005)¶

    Reuse as a strategy¶

    At the 2005 Ground System Architectures Workshop (GSAW, 1-3 March 2005), ESOC described its approach as maximum reuse of experience, procedures, software and operations concept. Reuse cuts cost, because a new mission develops and maintains only a small amount of code, and cuts risk, because the reused software has already been validated by earlier missions.12 The reuse rests on two bases. The first is design: mission control components built expressly for reuse, mission needs designed in a generic way, and harmonised spacecraft design in everything that affects the ground segment. The second is standards: CCSDS packet TM/TC (see Space Packet), the ECSS Packet Utilisation Standard, Space Link Extension and XML.13

    The components¶

    The 2005 presentation lists the infrastructure as follows:14

    Component Role in 2005
    SCOS-2000 (Spacecraft Control and Operations System) Distributed client-server system with the generic services for TM reception and processing, TC uplink and verification, data archiving, display and retrieval
    NCTRS (Network Control and TM/TC Router System) Interface between the mission control system and the ground station network, using SLE for non-ESA stations; see NCTRS and NIS
    GDDS (Generic Data Disposition System) Data distribution to external users; see GDDS
    TDRS (Telemetry Data Retrieval Services) and WebRM Web-based live telemetry, command verification, telemetry retrieval and statistics for external users such as payload owners; see TDRS
    File Transfer System Reused from XMM
    Mission Planning System Reused from Envisat
    Database editors Reused from Cryosat

    The last three are examples of software that started as a mission-specific need but was designed generically enough to be reused. The ground segment scenario slide of the same talk places the operational database editor, the MCS (SCOS-2000), NCTRS and the file transfer system on the operations LAN, the GDDS behind firewalls on a relay LAN, and external users beyond the Internet or ISDN.15 The redrawing below follows the component labels of that slide.

    The delta approach and the requirement sets¶

    The delta approach means that MCS requirements are no longer written in full but only as differences from the infrastructure, typically SCOS-2000. The talk lists the benefits: a slim requirements document, more detail from an early phase, clear identification of new developments, and better cost and risk estimates. The approach carries through the life cycle. Design is a delta as well, with interfaces kept untouched where possible. Testing focuses on mission-specific features, because several missions test the common software at the same time. In maintenance, the SCOS-2000 kernel is maintained under separate arrangements, so each mission maintains only its own part.16 The page on SCOS-2000 shows the resulting layering.

    Missions with similar profiles need similar features that are not generic enough for SCOS-2000. These are grouped into a family kernel, a software layer on top of SCOS-2000, so the saving applies twice.17 In 2005 ESOC named three families: Earth Explorer (low Earth orbit, short visibility periods), planetary (long propagation delay, on-board autonomy) and observatory (long visibility, proposal-based observations).18 The 2007 ESA Bulletin names four families in use at ESOC, with slightly different labels: interplanetary, Earth observation, observatory, and navigation (typically a constellation in medium Earth orbit).29

    For the Earth Explorer family the talk sorts requirements into five sets:19

    Set Meaning
    (a) SCOS-2000 requirements, fully reusable by any Earth Explorer mission
    (b) Earth Explorer kernel requirements, common to all missions of the family
    © Mission-specific requirements, used by only one mission
    (d) Requirements implemented by one mission but common to others: candidates for moving into the family kernel
    (e) Requirements implemented in the family kernel but common to other families: candidates for moving into SCOS-2000

    The heritage slide gives 680 kernel requirements and 35, 42 and 115 mission-specific requirements for Cryosat, GOCE and Aeolus, 872 in total for the family.20 (At definition phase the counts had been 406, 129 and 57 kernel requirements; the SCOS-2000 page reproduces that table.)

    The missions also contributed functions to the kernel. Several were marked as already moved, or as candidates for moving, into SCOS-2000:2122

The development workspace was a single, highly configurable system with a separate installation per mission, strong configuration control, isolation of kernel requirements and a requirement review at each delivery.23 The conclusions stress that ground segment teams should take part in the design of the satellite TM/TC, because harmonised TM/TC data types, structures and services across similar satellites save cost in the MCS.24

    The FCT was supported by ESOC units for ground system control, station equipment and control, communications, data processing, flight dynamics and computer systems.113 The ESOC organisation chart in the same volume places "Mission Control Systems" (TOS-GC), "Mission Support" (TOS-GM), "Flight Dynamics" (TOS-GF) and "Station Systems" (TOS-GS) under Ground Systems Engineering (TOS-G), and the ground facilities operations, the flight operations and the Villafranca and Redu stations under Mission Operations (TOS-O).114 How the unit codes in the text (TOS-GMC, TOS-GMS, TOS-GFO) relate to the divisions of the chart is not stated in the available sources.

    ESOC's own GSAW 2003 slide of INTEGRAL as an "S2K based MCS" shows the same elements around the IMCS (the IOS, the NCTRS, the timeline generator MPS, the FDS, the on-board software maintenance system, PAS and ISDS), with two differences of naming: PAS is the "Offline Evaluation System" on the slide but the "Offline Performance Analysis System" in the FOP, and where the FOP names MADDS the slide shows an operations archive with a long history file and mission archive CD-ROMs (see other missions).110112 The FOP itself never names SCOS-2000; the identification comes from that slide and from the R2.0 release plan of 2000 (see SCOS-2000).

    Inference

    The INTEGRAL list maps onto the later EGOS vocabulary: MPS onto mission planning, MADDS onto the generic data disposition and archive products, IOS onto the SIMSAT simulator infrastructure and NCTRS onto NCTRS/SLE; OBSMS has no counterpart in the 2003 EGOS list. The FOP describes the elements as INTEGRAL systems and does not say which of them were generic ESOC products.11227

    EGOS: the name for the whole¶

    In 2003 ESOC presented a new framework, EGOS, as a family of products following the SCOS-2000 approach. The list then covered ground systems test and validation (GSTV), a multi-mission configuration control manager (MCCM), ground systems monitoring and control (GSMC), NCTRS/SLE, the SIMSAT simulator infrastructure, generic file transfer (GFTS) and data disposition (GDDS), operations preparation (OPS), advanced data analysis (ADAS), automated procedure execution (APES), an archive (EVA), EGSE, mission planning (MPS), a TM and TC baseband system (TMTCS) and flight dynamics (FDS).2627 In 2007 the ESA Bulletin described EGOS as an extensive suite of applications, middleware and lower-level components covering mission control, simulator infrastructure and ground station "back-end" software, with SIMSAT and SCOS-2000 as cornerstones.28

    MICONYS: the mission control product suite¶

    ESOC's 2025 paper defines MICONYS ("MIssion COntrol SYStem" in its acronym list, "MIssion CONtrol sYSstem" in its abstract) as SCOS-2000 plus the complementary systems that form a suite of generic products. These products "have significantly evolved over time" and are a mature baseline, but SCOS-2000 shows signs of obsolescence that "can no longer be ignored".1 The earlier EGOS-CC paper expands MICONYS as "Mission Control System infrastructure".33 The suite is not listed in one place in the sources.     Mission Information Base or Database

    The two SpaceOps 2025 papers, by ESOC (#477) and by Starion with ESA (#141), expand MIB as "Mission Information Database".2103 The DLR paper and the EGOS-CC paper use "Mission Information Base".6433 Both refer to the same SCOS-2000 tailoring data described on the MIB.

    MUST and the data products¶

    The EDDS requirements (issue 18.0, 2018) describe the three "current" dissemination subsystems on which the EDDS requirements are based: TDRS (parameter extraction from fixed-length packets in the SCOS-2000 history files, results as MVDA or spreadsheet files), WebRM (remote monitoring through CORBA-based injection and provision servers, with SCOS-2000-like displays), and the generic DDS (web and FTP front ends serving continuous, file and catalogue data). These "do not perform any analysis". A fourth, prototype subsystem, MUST, gave input to EDDS: its server keeps a parameter archive filled by scheduled historic packet requests from the MCS, and its clients analyse those parameters. EDDS was required to offer a similar TM parameter interface so that "MUST like clients" could be supported.85

    MUST outlived the prototype stage. In 2025 the CHEOPS team uses MUST and its web interface, WebMUST, for telemetry visualisation and trend analysis "as alternatives to SCOS-2000, which is limited in certain functionalities". The tool was first implemented at ESOC and later transferred to INTA and the University of Bern "due to maintenance considerations"; the operations and instrument teams now run and maintain it with support from the company Solenix. Its database is updated with RAPID files generated by SCOS-2000, and periodic trend analyses use the Python MUSTLink API, which authenticates against the server and splits large requests into pages to avoid overloading it.138

    EUD: the common user interface (2014)¶

    At GSAW 2014 ESOC presented the EGOS User Desktop (EUD), a framework for building rich-client graphical user interfaces and an integration platform for GUI components, aimed primarily at ground data systems. ESA called it the basis of its new generation of user interfaces at ESOC; it had been in mission operation since 2013.118 It belonged to a wider modernisation of the ground data systems infrastructure, motivated by obsolescence of existing implementations, removal of dependencies on COTS products and cost reduction through reuse, with four objectives: state-of-the-art open-source technology, platform independence (Linux and Windows), shared implementations across systems and domains, and a harmonised look and feel.119 Obsolescence and cost are also the reasons ESOC later gives for moving to EGS-CC (see below).

    EUD is written in Java on the Eclipse Rich Client Platform (RCP; the expansion is the CHEOPS paper's),133 uses only open-source software and runs on several operating systems.     ARES and data analytics (GSAW 2020)¶

    In March 2020 ESOC presented the state of its data analytics infrastructure. It maps ESOC's systems onto four "big data" layers: a raw layer at full fidelity in file and folder structures, a processing layer for distributed computation, an optimised layer of structured data organised by use case, and a low-latency speed layer.75 At ESOC, storage and infrastructure is ARES. Data accessibility (preparation, processing, retrieval, enhancement) is provided by PyARES, the novelty detection system, the dependency finder and long-term data preservation (LTDP) products and collections.76

    ARES¶

    ARES (Analysis and Reporting System) is an off-line data analysis system. It evolved from the Mission Utility Support tools (MUST) and is built on the EGOS User Desktop framework. It mainly stores spacecraft housekeeping data in engineering format, supports generic storage of files, parameters and events, and is optimised for fast retrieval.77 The architecture slide shows an ARES client with display and database access layers, an ARES server with a backend that reads import files from a file system, the EDDS response file, and below them EDDS, the MCS and the DARC and PARC archives, on an ARES LAN and an operations LAN.77 The demonstration slides show graphical, alphanumeric and table displays.78

    Inference

    The slide labels suggest that ARES is fed by EDDS response files, which EDDS produces from the PARC and DARC archives of the MCS. The EDDS design document supports this reading: its appendix C discusses the "response format compatibility for ARES" when EDDS serves telemetry from EGS-CC instead of SCOS-2000.90 The data path is not described in prose in the available sources.

    The multi-mission shared storage answers growing volumes: Gaia, for example, receives around 150 million unique housekeeping TM samples per day. Its goals are to remove limits on data type and content (store all data rather than a subset, mine across missions), to scale with stable retrieval performance, to offer high availability and redundancy, to enable long-term data preservation, and to give all missions a homogeneous service.79

    PyARES, novelty detection and the dependency finder¶

    PyARES is a Python library for ARES. It offers metadata and data retrieval, processing with user-defined functions and machine-learning jobs (random forest). It aims to "bring the processing to the data" with PySpark and hides technologies such as Spark, HBase and Protobuf.80

    Novelty detection looks for unusual behaviour, which "is often the signature of an anomaly in the way to happen". "Deep novelty" uses neural networks when a parameter's behaviour depends on other parameters; the slide judges deep learning promising but in need of more effort.81 The dependency finder looks for parameters whose joint distribution is very different from the one expected if they were related only by chance. It supports training, anomaly investigation and operations preparation. A prototype by the Artificial Intelligence and Operations Innovation team was validated by the Mars Express flight control team.82

    LTDP products and collections combine diverse data types, preserve them with semantic context and metadata, and federate data sources. A prototype used ARES (a Hadoop database cluster), Elastic Search and a REST API, with a generic schema for multi-mission products, different data types and instructions to preserve and recreate products.83 The "examples of potential future work" were live TM stream processing, a federation with a central data portal for correlation with science data, migration of legacy missions to Hadoop, operational AI tools for operators, and a curated "Anomaly Detection ESA Dataset" for benchmarking.84

    Why SCOS-2000 has to be replaced¶

    ESOC's EGOS-CC paper gives the reason plainly: the SCOS-2000-based generation "is expected to face significant obsolescence problems in the next decade", with maintenance and evolution costs becoming excessive. EGS-CC is the opportunity to modernise the infrastructure and its processes.35 The ICALEPCS paper of 2019, by M. Pecchioli, one of its two authors, puts it in one sentence: the generation in operation "has been successfully used for all categories of missions, including many commercial ones operated outside ESOC", but its implementation is expected "to face obsolescence in the coming years".126 Obsolescence had already been the first motivation of the EUD modernisation of 2014, together with removing COTS dependencies and cutting cost through reuse.119 The 2025 paper quantifies the transition. ESOC predicts at least 7 years from the moment EGS-CC first enters operations (in 2026) to the end of life of SCOS, so no earlier than 2033. One driver is the number of missions at ESOC, above 40.3

    EGS-CC in brief¶

    The European Ground Systems Common Core is an initiative led by ESA with European industry. It answers a fragmented landscape: many different monitoring and control (M&C) systems in use, often different ones for assembly, integration and test (AIT) and for operations, with little synergy across missions and phases. Its kernel makes no implementation or interface assumption about the controlled system, so the same core can serve space segment and ground segment applications.3546 The sources describe the following building blocks:

    Concept Meaning in the sources
    Kernel Core M&C, data handling and application support; generic and agnostic of the application domain68
    Reference Implementation (RI) Components that adapt the kernel to an application, for example a CCSDS/PUS mission; it may be modified or replaced68
    Monitoring and Control Model (MCM) A model of the monitored and controlled space system, reflecting its functional decomposition; a hierarchy of Monitoring and Control Elements (MCE)58
    MCE aspects Activities, events, parameters and nested elements5869
    Tailoring Data Model (TDM) Defines the data model of the controlled system and of EGS-CC itself, allows customising the packetisation layer, and holds consistency rules69
    Conceptual Data Model (CDM) The EGS-CC data model that teams used to the "SCOS-2000 MIB ICD" must learn39
    Technology Distributed OSGi application in the Apache Karaf runtime, written in Java; "no C++, no CORBA, no SQL"726239
    Software Development Environment (SDE) The prescribed tools and procedures for developing and integrating components39

    The MCM separates the abstract M&C view from the processing of the data units exchanged with a particular controlled system.     What changes for ESOC¶

    ESOC lists the expected benefits beyond replacing obsolete software:36

    • Heterogeneous controlled systems. SCOS-2000 was designed for spacecraft that exchange TM/TC with the control system according to PUS. EGS-CC adds an abstraction layer for any system, ground segment included.
    • Systems of systems. Simpler tailoring, validation and deployment for composites of units, ground station networks, control centres, mission families and constellations.
    • Off-the-shelf reuse. A component and interface based design in which applications are built without modifying the infrastructure ("binary compatibility").
    • Simpler operations concepts. Today's operations work at the level of single telecommands and parameters; the abstraction layer allows higher-level interactions that are easier to automate.
    • Cross-fertilisation. Space segment and ground station teams use different tools and life cycles today; a common data model is to harmonise them.

    The paper is equally clear about what the new generation must match: the functional scope of the current systems (thousands of user and software requirements), their usability, performance, the teams' expertise and their operational maturity.37 Instead of a "flat specification", ESOC built a hierarchical functional model of the whole infrastructure and used it for a functional gap analysis: which functions EGS-CC supports natively, which need extensions, and which can be dropped as conflicting with EGS-CC concepts. The result is a "delta specification" again, now against EGS-CC. Reusing an existing application instead of a new extension was chosen only when it clearly minimised total cost without harming long-term maintainability.38

    The same paper calls the change disruptive, in seven respects:39

    Area What changes
    Terminology A newly agreed EGS-CC vocabulary that all teams must adopt
    Engineering culture EGS-CC is reused as a "black box" third-party product; SCOS-2000-based MCSs assumed full freedom to modify the infrastructure
    Tools and processes Model-based engineering, component and interface based design, layered validation and automated V&V in a prescribed SDE
    Technology A modern stack without C++, CORBA or SQL, which changes the expertise needed
    Data model Teams know the "SCOS-2000 MIB ICD" in detail; the EGS-CC CDM defines similar data types with a largely different approach and semantics, and definitions are expected earlier in the spacecraft development
    External interfaces Few interfaces are standardised; EGS-CC supports many natively, but differently
    Approach to operations Model-based operations through the MCM, already practice for ESA ground station operations, a "very significant change" for spacecraft operations

    EGOS-CC: ESOC's EGS-CC based infrastructure¶

    Decomposition¶

    ESOC named its EGS-CC based generation of applications EGOS-CC.34 Both ESOC papers decompose it into the same layers:4047

    Layer Content
    Development Environment Tools for developing, integrating and validating EGS-CC components and applications; in 2019 based on the EGS-CC SDE
    Preparation Environment Definition and management of tailoring data: M&C model, TM/TC packets, automation procedures, displays. A framework with common services (import, compare, merge, consistency checks, versions and baselines, product generation and export) and a library of editors per data type
    Platform Baseline Operating system and third-party stack: two Linux distributions for the back end and Windows for the front end (undated paper); SLES12 (2019 paper)
    EGS-CC The main M&C functions, adopted without modification
    Extension Components Additional features built with exactly the same approach as EGS-CC components
    Adapter Components Bridges to other ground systems (mission planning, flight dynamics, ancillary applications), proprietary where no standard exists
    Ancillary Applications Planning, schedule execution and automation at ground segment level, dissemination, analysis and reporting, on-call notification, long-term storage. Largely legacy, not built with EGS-CC practices

    Two application domains sit on top. Mission control systems must support many dozens of users in parallel (especially in LEOP), "off-line operations" with very short passes or very long propagation delays, real-time and deferred data to reconstruct the spacecraft state, end-to-end verification of complex operations, automation across ground and space, and access to the full mission data set. Ground station M&C needs fully automated planning, scheduling and execution that can re-plan on events, shared tailoring data across equivalent units, and both local and remote operation over very limited bandwidth.4148

    Products: MICONYS-CC, M4C, GSMC-CC and OPEN¶

    The undated paper calls the products MICONYS-CC (the EGS-CC based mission control system infrastructure) and GSMC-CC (ground station monitoring and control). Each consists of an off-line preparation application, the Operations Preparation ENvironment (OPEN); a core execution application based on EGS-CC plus "EGS-CC like" EGOS-CC components; and several legacy EGOS applications for dissemination, analysis and reporting. MICONYS-CC is the basis for full mission control systems, which add mission-specific extensions.34

    The 2019 SESP paper calls the mission product M4C (Mission Control Core based on Common Core) and the ground station product "Ground Station Monitoring and Control System based on Common Core". M4C and GSMC-CC have a functional scope "very similar" to SCOS-2000 and to STC2 (ESOC's ground station product), respectively. M4C can be extended for a given MCS, while GSMC-CC is not mission-specific because ground stations are shared.4946 Cross-domain applications (CDAs) such as data distribution and long-term archiving stay outside the EGS-CC run-time and are being adapted to work with both M4C and GSMC-CC and with the current S2K and GSMC infrastructure; a separate ESOC initiative turns them into centrally managed multi-mission services.49 EGS-CC itself offers little support for preparing tailoring data, so OPEN is a full framework, completed by OPEN-M for mission operations and OPEN-S for ground stations. All three were to be available to the EGS-CC community under the ESA Software Community License.50

    The ICALEPCS paper of the same year describes M4C from the system-design side and sets it against MSSS and SCOS-2000 (the full comparison is on SCOS-2000). Its main points for M4C are:129130128

    • Deployment. "Conceptually centralised": only the user interaction layer runs remotely, in installation-free thin clients, while the central functions sit on separate scalable tiers (software-defined networks, cloud computing, cluster storage). Together with the strictly layered organisation, for example of system sessions, this allows multi-mission deployments in which missions share most mission control functions. SCOS-2000 MCSs, by contrast, run on dedicated physical machines.
    • Scalability. From dedicated machines with local storage to fully shared multi-mission virtual data centres.
    • Components. Each component is self-standing, with its design model, external interfaces, test environment and procedures, configuration data and tailoring data.
    • Extensibility. Mission-specific functions, or changes to the behaviour of common functions, without source-code changes to the infrastructure, which the paper calls the design focus of M4C. User-defined scripts, processing routines and displays are credited to the recent generations in general.
    • Operations. Any control action ("activity") can be triggered from the ground or on board, on delta or absolute times, events or procedures; file based operations are supported, as in SCOS-2000.
    • Preparation. OPEN, an expandable Eclipse-based framework in which editors for each data type can be developed and integrated quickly, used in a distributed but centrally managed way. The paper presents OPEN as the latest step of preparation support without tying it to one generation.
    • Maintainability. Portable technologies with few direct COTS dependencies, widely used languages with a large pool of experts, modules visible only through service interfaces, extension and customisation hooks, and automated production, deployment and validation of the operational systems. The paper names the last three of these five measures as a significant improvement over SCOS-2000.

    Functional extensions¶

    ESOC identified two kinds of genuine extensions (as opposed to "ground system adapters" that only connect EGS-CC to legacy systems such as mission planning or flight dynamics): Operations Services, high-level functions for executing operations, and M&C Adaptation, interfaces to the controlled system.42

    Operations services:43

    • File based operations (FbO), in three levels: file transfer management (initiate, suspend, resume uploads and downloads), file store management (create folders, delete files, keep a ground image of the on-board store and synchronise folders), and file utilisation (operations specific to a file type, such as activating a loaded "TC file" and verifying the commands it contains). See PUS ST[23] file management.
    • On-board software management: patch upload and verification before activation, assuming memory management through PUS service 6 (ST[06]) but with a life cycle of memory images that does not depend on one spacecraft.
    • Automated schedule execution: the mission planning system produces schedules for the spacecraft (PUS service 11, ST[11]) or for the MCS; EGS-CC only time-tags control activities, so a service is needed that starts activities "at the same level of abstraction as the human operator would do".

    M&C adaptation, on top of the PUS-over-SLE components and SCOE adapters of the EGS-CC reference implementation:4344

    • File based reception of TM frames, for short-pass, high-rate missions (notably Earth observation) and for replaying AIT data. A manifest file describes each TM file so that different formats and data types can be injected without reconfiguration. The component plays the same role as the SLE component.
    • File exchange with the space segment, wrapping ESA's generic implementation of CFDP: CFDP PDUs to and from TM/TC packets, files to and from the EGS-CC file store, and M&C of the CFDP engine.
    • Cross Support Transfer Services (CSTS), the CCSDS successor of SLE, which adds monitoring data from the ground station provider.
    • ESA ground station M&C interface, for equipment that follows the "CORBA M&C ICD".
    • Ground system M&C adapters, so that control centre systems become elements of the controlled system and are operated like any other.

    Programmatics to 2019¶

    The EGOS-CC project proceeds incrementally: system engineering, progressive development, integration and validation, and progressive deployment into real missions. Phase B delivered the high-level design and specification; development of the extensions was planned to start in the second quarter of 2017.45 By March 2019 development of OPEN and the extensions had started (in 2017), OPEN was delivered monthly to end users for evaluation, and the first EGOS-CC components were due in the second quarter of 2019.54 The Juice MCS (J-MCS) was the first target. A pilot J-MCS was to show stability, reliability, maturity and functional scope before a commitment in September 2019; a pilot GSMC-CC was to lead to a decision at the end of 2019.54 ESOC acted as an EGS-CC integrator, sat on the EGS-CC engineering team, integrators' team and steering board, and, when problems blocked progress, patched EGS-CC itself while raising the corresponding software problem reports (SPRs). Users saw mock-ups and test sessions that ran the EGS-CC reference test facility (RTF) simulator and the BepiColombo simulator side by side.5433

    Juice and Ariel

    The 2019 paper names Juice as the first target MCS. The 2025 paper names Swarm (a flying mission) as the first migration and Ariel as the first mission to launch on EGS-CC. The outcome of the 2019 Juice decision is not stated in the available sources.547

    Towards EGS-CC at ESOC (2025)¶

    April 2024 and the Swarm pilot¶

    In April 2024 ESOC reconfirmed that its next-generation mission control system will be based on EGS-CC. The agreed way forward was to prepare the system first for Swarm, a constellation of three satellites launched in 2013 to study Earth's magnetic field. Swarm demands precision and reliability, its scope tests performance and scalability, and because it is flying with its S2K system still available, the transition can proceed "at no risk" as confidence grows.56

    The paper lists the expected benefits: shared modernisation cost for pre- and post-launch systems (EGSE and MCS) in Europe; a common data model across pre- and post-launch phases; exchange of ancillary applications (procedure preparation, scheduled automation, data analysis) across organisations; one code base for many ESOC missions; one solution for both mission control and ground station M&C; native automation; common operations and tailoring data across missions; installation-free web access; and the automatic testing approach of EGS-CC.4

    Challenges and recent work¶

    The hardest technical problem named is the archive. It must store telemetry frames, packets, parameters, events, log messages, alarm states and control activities, balance storage speed and volume against retrieval performance, absorb peaks and support many filter combinations, while keeping the total cost of ownership of data down. Working in a large community brought valuable insight but also process overhead compared with what ESOC's users knew from SCOS. Many community issues concerned the user interface, so more customisation capabilities were added while keeping common parts common.9

    Work in 2024-2025 focused on Swarm's key scenarios: commanding chain enhancements including pre-release checks, playback telemetry processing, a user interface for SLE links, performance of user interface and back end, retrieval and display of historical data, a refurbished time correlation, on-board queue modelling, a smaller memory footprint and usability. These went partly into EGS-CC itself and partly into the MCS-CC layer.10 Next come file based operations, on-board software maintenance, security, and PUS-C for operational use, including the Generic Operational Interface Requirements that future ESOC missions are to support. Validation is expected to take most of 2026, leading to a fully operational solution in 2027 in line with Ariel's 2029 launch.11

    EDDS on EGS-CC¶

    EDDS is one of the cross-domain applications that must work with both generations. Its latest design document in the library (published as "v24"; the title page reads version 25.0 of 7 March 2025, the page footers version 24.0 of 10 January 2025) adds an EDDS-CC architecture. A new "ARC standalone" Java library inside the EDDS server reads batch data (TM, TC, parameters, messages) from the EGS-CC HBase archive; unlike SCOS, EGS-CC stores all processed data in its archive. A second data provider calls EGS-CC REST end points maintained by the EGS-CC team.8889 Two mapping questions follow:

    • Domains and partitions. SCOS uses domains and data partitions. EGS-CC segregates data per mission in one static dataspace, and per session (live, playback and others) in dynamic datapartitions. EDDS maps the dataspace to a domain and keeps datapartitions as partitions. There is no default partition in EGS-CC, so every EDDS-CC request must name both.91
    • Telemetry fields. An EGS-CC TM report lacks several fields that S2K reports carry, which could affect ARES. Available fields include APID, packet ID, generation and Earth-received times, time quality, source sequence counter, PUS type and subtype, spacecraft and virtual channel IDs, route, body and datapartition. Missing are, among others, the database version, ground station, SLE service ID, SPID, PI1/PI2, packet length and mnemonic. It was agreed to map the SPID to the packet ID.90

    Note

    Appendix D of the EDDS design document first says that datapartitions are kept as datapartitions, then summarises the mapping as "domain and dataspace". The first reading matches the rest of the appendix.91

    Running SCOS-era procedures on EGS-CC: TOPE¶

    ESA uses two flavours of SCOS: SCOS-2000 for operations at ESOC, and SCOS-EGSE at ESTEC, which includes TOPE (test procedure, preparation and execution) for automation procedures. Repositories of TOPE procedures and engineers who want to keep using TOPE exist; migrating them to the EGS-CC automation language was expected to be "far from trivial". With ESA, GMV therefore built adapters that migrate existing MIB databases to EGS-CC and run TOPE scripts on top of it. They were deployed with a development version of EGS-CC in the Avionics Test Bench (ATB) at ESTEC, one of the earliest EGS-CC deployments.55

    TOPE extends Tcl with AIV commands. They follow four patterns: synchronous calls (fetch a parameter, write the system log, patch database commands), asynchronous subscriptions to TM packets and parameters that appear as Tcl variable updates, synchronous calls with asynchronous progress (tcsend to the spacecraft or a SCOE, callasync for child sequences), and local helper commands (waits, logging, conversions).56 In the ATB Real-Time Bench, TOPE lives in the TSEQ subsystem: one TOPE interpreter per test sequence, the TSVR server coordinating parallel instances, TEXEC (monitoring GUI, log viewer, debugger) and TPREP (editor and consistency checker). TSEQ talks to SCOS-2000 through EXIF, the CORBA-based external interface module of the SCOS-2000 kernel, which injects events, parameters or commands and provides telemetry, packets, events and command history.5657

    The TOPE Adaptation Layer (TAL), developed in ERFEC (the EGSE reference facility in the EGS-CC context), replaces EXIF. It implements CORBA servers compliant with the existing TSEQ/SCOS-2000 IDL files, so TSEQ needs no change, and translates in both directions. On the EGS-CC side it consumes the M&C access API (activity, parameter, event control; parameter and event injection; MCM access, name translation, monitoring and element registration), M&C definition management, messaging and system configuration.5960

    At start-up TAL receives, through EGS-CC configuration, the path of the ASCII MIB files and of a file mapping MIB definitions to MCM definitions. It loads the MIB, generates the EGS-CC CDM that EGS-CC loads into the MCM, and on request extracts or injects MCM parameters, activities and events. Two constraints follow: the ASCII MIB must always be provided (the ATB used the EagleEye mission's MIB), and TM/TC defined only in the MCM cannot be written back to ASCII MIB files; TAL logs a warning instead.61 TAL was designed in UML 2 with Enterprise Architect along the EGS-CC modelling guidelines, written in Java with JacORB, built and checked with the EGS-CC reference SDE (Jenkins, Nexus, SonarQube), packaged as OSGi bundles running inside an EGS-CC session, and validated with the EGS-CC Component Test Framework managed in the Test Management System.6263 GMV concludes that the approach can serve long term or as a staged migration.63

    Beyond ESOC: small satellites on EGS-CC at GSOC¶

    The German Space Operations Center (GSOC) runs GECCOS, a SCOS-2000-based MCS, at the heart of its multi-mission environment. GECCOS connects through the NCTRS protocol to an SLE switch board and from there to a worldwide station network, and processes PUS TM/TC, so the spacecraft must follow CCSDS and ECSS. Two elements make this work: the MIB, in which "each single TM/TC" is defined, and the procedures. ProToS prepares and runs TC stacks through the GECCOS API and automates activities; SATMON gives real-time and historical TM access, also from outside the control centre.6566 It has become an "industry standard" that the manufacturer delivers a MIB and procedures.66

    CubeL, a 3U CubeSat launched in early 2021 and GSOC's first small satellite, broke these assumptions. It uses the CubeSat Space Protocol (CSP) through a manufacturer toolkit of nodes talking over ZMQ, a command-line terminal (CSP-Term), and a UHF station. There is no MIB: commands are functions of a C API, one operator input may trigger several CSP packets, and the TM structure is defined in JSON. Integrating it needed a patched modem firmware, a new tool to command over GSOC's S-band link, an auxiliary MIB for telemetry, new off-line processing, mission planning changes and a custom automation solution.67

    DLR proposes to solve such cases with an EGS-CC reference implementation for CSP. An operator invokes an activity; it passes through the M&C access and MCM components to an activity processor, a CSP component that calls the manufacturer's CSP API. The return value of the API call becomes the activity result, and tailoring the TDM can add the CSP packet format for TM processing.70 The component is a Maven project with three modules: csp-library (Java bindings to the C library generated with SWIG plus JNI glue, packaged as native code in a Karaf bundle), csp-terminal (a CSP node whose CspActivityProcessingService implements a TDM activity and reports through EGS-CC's IActivityReporter), and csp-feature (deployment as a Karaf feature).71 In an experimental pass in January 2024 GSOC commanded CubeL through this prototype: pings to the on-board computer and TM requests to subsystems.73

    The consequence for the MIB is notable. Today GSOC requires a MIB, which a CSP manufacturer "inherently cannot comply to". With EGS-CC the requirement could shift to exposing the manufacturer's API as EGS-CC activities and defining the TM structures in the TDM.70 The ground station interface stays open: SLE and the CORTEX equipment at compatible stations rely on CCSDS 401.0, 131.0 and 231.0 and on the TM and TC data link protocols, so a CSP mission might instead route the RF signal to a project-specific software modem at the operations centre.74

    Mission products delivery: the CCSDS MPD services¶

    The standard¶

    The CCSDS Spacecraft Monitoring and Control (SM&C) working group is standardising Mission Product Distribution (MPD) services within the CCSDS Mission Operations (MO) framework, whose Message Abstraction Layer (MAL) describes services independently of implementation and transport, with machine-readable specifications in the SANA registry.93 Cross-agency exchange of mission products had relied on bespoke, mission-specific interfaces, "often duplicative, fragile, and hard to scale".92 MPD defines three services:9495

    Service Operations Pattern
    Product Retrieval listProducts (metadata filtered by type, dates, source, attributes); getProducts (full products through a progress interaction); deliverProductFiles (transfer to a URI with per-file status) Pull, ad hoc
    Order Management listStandingOrders; submitStandingOrder (filter, delivery method, target, validity); cancelStandingOrder Subscription set-up
    Product Order Delivery notifyProductDelivery (notification of a file transfer: metadata, file name, URI, status); deliverProducts (products through publish-subscribe) Push

    The information model separates ProductType (name, description, attribute definitions with type and unit), Product (an instance with creation and content dates, source, attribute values, file metadata and body) and StandingOrder (a long-lived subscription with filter, delivery method, validity and target). Filters are ProductFilter (type, domain, sources, attributes), TimeWindow and AttributeFilter in three variants: ValueRange, ValueSet and StringPattern.96 Delivery can be by service message or by file transfer to a nominated URI, with or without notification.95

    MPD compared with EDDS¶

    The paper maps EDDS onto MPD:97

    EDDS concept MPD equivalent
    batchRequest() / streamRequest() submitStandingOrder()
    getResponse() notifyProductDelivery(), deliverProducts()
    getJobs(), getStatus() listStandingOrders()
    getCatalogue() listProducts() with ProductFilter
    FileServerDelivery, ServerDelivery Delivery methods FILETRANSFER, SERVICE_COMPLETE
    File metadata embedded in responses File metadata in the ProductMetadata structure

    It describes EDDS as using a SOAP/WSDL interface with an XML schema for data requests, job submission and tracking, catalogue queries and user management, plus a REST service. EDDS supports push and pull like MPD and server-to-server transfers. It has no advanced metadata search, does not normally forward live delivery notifications (job status is polled), and uses login credentials that yield tokens, where MPD assumes MAL access control. An EDDS job corresponds to an MPD standing order. Some EDDS product types (PktEv, PktTc, PktTm) and their filters are specific to the data models of SCOS and EGS-CC and may need compromises.9798 The paper also maps NASA AMMOS DataDrive, which is pull-oriented with CLI-only subscriptions, and lists gaps on both sides.99

    ESA and NASA JPL each built an independent prototype provider against a shared test harness on ESA's Java MO stack, with local file:// transfers only and no connection to EDDS or DataDrive. The tests, derived from the draft standard, exposed naming inconsistencies, misused nullable attributes and inappropriate field types, which were fixed in the data model.100 The specification the paper works from is the Red Book CCSDS 522.2-R-2, "Mission Operations - Mission Data Product Distribution Services" (July 2024). At the time of writing (2025) the standard was in the final stage of CCSDS agency review, a Prototype Report (Yellow Book) was in preparation, and Blue Book publication was expected in Q3-Q4 2025.101 Whether it was published is not stated in the available sources.

    Payload command requests: the CRF framework¶

    At ESOC, payload requests belong to a broader concept, the Command Request File (CRF), which carries telecommands, sequences, automation procedures and mission events, collectively called "occurrences". Payload teams of BepiColombo and JUICE submit Payload Operations Requests (POR) and Payload Direct Operations Requests (PDOR), subtypes of CRF in XML, which must agree with the mission's MIB.102105 Earlier missions let each user devise their own way of creating and delivering CRFs, which caused many iterations with the flight control team because of MIB and ICD incompatibilities.102

    Under ESA's EFC2 frame contract, the Starion service team at ESAC built a centralised web framework. For BepiColombo (launched October 2018, 7.2-year cruise, nine planetary flybys) it is called SPOT Cruise; by the time of the paper it had produced more than about 1 500 payload operations requests since launch, for near-Earth commissioning, six-monthly instrument check-outs and flyby science.104106 Its features: COSMOS LDAP access control, a POR/PDOR editor, MIB compatibility checks, e-mail notifications, full traceability, MIB update handling and conflict resolution, flight dynamics and science events, product generation with direct submission to the MOC and delivery and acknowledgement monitoring, 3D flyby visualisation, timeline and resource simulation, search, and a MOIS-compatible procedure builder.106 SPOT JUICE was adapted in 2021 without 3D, procedure builder and simulation, supported ground tests, commissioning, check-outs and the Lunar-Earth gravity assist of August 2024, and produced more than about 700 requests; HERA and ARIEL showed interest.107105

    Capability Purpose
    CRF editing Create and edit accurate, complete CRFs
    Management of MIB updates Handle MIB changes and resolve conflicts
    MIB compatibility validation Every CRF compatible with the MIB
    ICD compliance verification CRF content conforms to the operations planning ICDs
    CRF generation and delivery Creation, submission, delivery and acknowledgement monitoring

    The capabilities above are those summarised by the paper.108

    Inference

    The CRF is also the input of the EGOS-CC Command Request Management component, which converts CRFs into EGS-CC stacks or ground schedule increments.52 A framework such as SPOT therefore stays useful across the move to EGS-CC, provided its MIB checks are applied to whatever tailoring data the MCS-CC uses. The sources do not discuss this link.
