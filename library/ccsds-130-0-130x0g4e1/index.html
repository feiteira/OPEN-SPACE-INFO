    Summary¶

    A CCSDS Green Book (informational, non-normative) that gives an architectural tour of every space communications protocol CCSDS recommends and shows how they combine in a mission data system. It is the natural starting point before reading any of the individual Blue Books: it contains no protocol specifications, only the map.

    Section 2 recounts the history, from early fixed-format TDM telemetry through the 1980s Packet Telemetry, Telecommand and AOS standards, which were later restructured into the Space Packet Protocol, the TM, TC and AOS Space Data Link Protocols and the TM and TC synchronization and channel coding books. It also covers SCPS, CFDP, DTN (LTP and Bundle Protocol), data compression, Proximity-1, USLP and SDLS. It then places all of them in a five-layer reference model (Physical, Data Link with its two sublayers, Network, Transport, Application).

    Section 3 is the most useful part for a telemetry and telecommand engineer. It explains Virtual Channels, Master Channels (MCID = TFVN + SCID) and MAP channels, and tabulates the identifier ranges of each data link protocol (Table 3-1), the services each one offers (Table 3-2) and the coding, randomisation and synchronisation options of each coding standard (Table 3-3). It also explains where COP-1 retransmission applies and how Encapsulation Packets carry IP or DTN traffic. Section 4 gives example end-to-end protocol stacks: Space Packet or Encapsulation Packet forwarding, IP over CCSDS and BP-based routing. A 61-entry reference list catalogues the CCSDS books as they stood in 2023.

    Key points¶

    • The report is a top-level overview only: protocol specifications are in CCSDS Blue Books and their rationale is in accompanying Green Books. (PDF p. 9)
    • Packet Telemetry, Telecommand and AOS were restructured into the Space Packet Protocol, the TM, TC and AOS Space Data Link Protocols, and TM and TC Synchronization and Channel Coding. (PDF p. 16)
    • CCSDS has retired all SCPS protocols except SCPS-TP. (PDF p. 17)
    • USLP was released in 2018 and is envisioned as the data link layer protocol for all future robotic and crewed missions. (PDF p. 18)
    • The CCSDS Data Link Layer has two sublayers: the Data Link Protocol Sublayer (Transfer Frames) and the Synchronization and Channel Coding Sublayer. (PDF p. 20)
    • SDLS can add authentication and confidentiality to TM, AOS, TC and USLP Transfer Frames; its use is optional, and no security requirements exist for Proximity-1. (PDF p. 20)
    • TM, AOS and USLP use fixed-length Transfer Frames for robust synchronisation; TC, Proximity-1 and USLP use variable-length frames for telecommand. (PDF p. 25)
    • A Master Channel is the set of frames with the same MCID (TFVN + SCID) on a Physical Channel and consists of one or more Virtual Channels. (PDF p. 25)
    • Only TC and USLP (COP-1) and Proximity-1 and USLP (COP-P) have link-level retransmission; TM and AOS rely on higher layers for complete delivery. (PDF p. 25)
    • Identifier ranges (Table 3-1): TFVN is version 1 for TM and TC, 2 for AOS, 3 for Proximity-1 and 4 for USLP; SCID 0-1023 (TM, TC, Prox-1), 0-255 (AOS), 0-65535 (USLP); VCID 0-7 (TM), 0-63 (TC, AOS, USLP); MAP ID 0-63 (TC), 0-15 (USLP). (PDF p. 28)
    • Table 3-2 maps data types to services: VC Packet / MAP Packet services for packets, VC/MAP Access services for private data, FSH/OCF/Insert services for short fixed-length data, Bitstream/Octet Stream for streams, and VC/MC Frame services for frames. (PDF p. 29)
    • Table 3-3: TM coding options are convolutional, Reed-Solomon, concatenated, turbo, LDPC, SCCC and DVB-S2 with a 32-bit or longer ASM; TC uses BCH (optionally with FECF) or LDPC with a 16-bit Start Sequence; Proximity-1 uses a 24-bit ASM. (PDF p. 31)
    • The TM pseudo-noise randomisation sequence differs from the one used by TC and Proximity-1. (PDF p. 31)
    • Handling of APIDs along managed data paths is set by management agreement and is not formally part of the Space Packet Protocol; SPP gives no reliability, ordering or duplicate-suppression guarantees. (PDF p. 35)

    Notes

    The slug's 'e1' is Editorial Change 1 (February 2024), recorded in the Document Control table; page footers still read 'April 2023'. PDF pages 1 and 2 are duplicate cover pages. The extraction has 44 PDF pages (registry: 43). Section 4.1 announces 'five examples' of protocol configurations, but only three subsections (4.2 to 4.4) follow, and 4.3 calls itself 'the fourth example' (apparently a leftover from an earlier issue). In section 2.1 the text cites 'The Application of CCSDS Protocols to Secure Systems' as reference [37], whose list title is 'The Application of Security to CCSDS Protocols'. Figure 4-5 comes out garbled in the extraction; the other figures are reduced to scattered labels. No individual authors are named. Section 1.4 is a plain 'References' list, catalogued here as kind 'reference'.
