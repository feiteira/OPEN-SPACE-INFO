    Summary¶

    The CCSDS Green Book that explains the concept and rationale of the three classic Space Data Link Protocols: the TC, TM and AOS Space Data Link Protocols, plus the COP-1 retransmission procedure that goes with TC. Proximity-1 is left to its own report. It is short and readable, and it is the best place to learn why the link layer looks as it does before tackling the Blue Books.

    Section 2 sets out the design goals (several qualities of service with simple on-board algorithms, upward compatibility with older TDM formats) and the usual protocol pairings: TC up with TM down, TC with AOS, or AOS both ways. It also places the Data Link Protocol Sublayer above the TM and TC synchronization and channel coding sublayer. Section 3 takes the user's view. It covers unidirectional services, the Sequence-Controlled (Type-A) and Expedited (Type-B) TC services, the absence of delivery guarantees in TM and AOS, the asynchronous, synchronous and periodic timing models, and a table of every service (packet, encapsulation, VC/MAP access, FSH, OCF, insert, bitstream, frame).

    Section 4 takes the implementer's view. It covers variable-length TC frames versus fixed-length TM/AOS frames, Virtual Channel multiplexing for priority, the identifier hierarchy (TFVN, SCID, MCID, GVCID, Physical Channel Name, MAP ID), how COP-1 works (FOP-1, FARM-1, CLCW, go-back-n), and how packets are segmented (TC MAPs) or packed with a First Header Pointer (TM/AOS). A short FAQ compares the protocols with HDLC, mentions SDLS security, managed parameters in SANA, and COP-1 for deep-space and blind commanding.

    Key points¶

    • Only TC-SDLP, TM-SDLP and AOS-SDLP are covered; Proximity-1 has its own rationale report (CCSDS 210.0-G-2). (PDF p. 8)
    • Only one Space Data Link Protocol may be used on a given Physical Channel; TC usually gets its feedback through TM or AOS on the reverse Physical Channel. (PDF p. 14)
    • The three common pairings are TC forward with TM return (used by most missions), TC with AOS, and AOS in both directions (for example for piloted missions or high-rate two-way links). (PDF p. 14)
    • The TC Type-A (Sequence-Controlled) service uses go-back-n ARQ to give complete, in-sequence delivery at a single SAP; Type-B (Expedited) sends each SDU once, typically for recovery operations or when a higher layer retransmits. (PDF p. 20)
    • TM and AOS give no completeness guarantee and no delivery confirmation, but preserve the order of SDUs at a single SAP. (PDF p. 21)
    • CCSDS recommends no standard Virtual Channel multiplexing algorithm, except that SLE Forward Space Packet (912.3-B-2) imposes its own algorithm when it is used. (PDF p. 22)
    • TM and AOS offer asynchronous (queue, each SDU sent once), synchronous (single buffer, an SDU may be sent several times or not at all) and periodic (constant rate) service types. (PDF p. 23)
    • TM and AOS frame length is fixed on a Physical Channel during a Mission Phase and must be known to the receiver through management. (PDF p. 32)
    • TFVN values: TC and TM are version 1 (binary '00'), AOS is version 2 ('01'); TC and TM frames are told apart by management. SCIDs are 10 bits for TFVN 1 and 8 bits for AOS. (PDF p. 35)
    • A spacecraft using TC forward and AOS return needs two SCIDs; MCID = TFVN + SCID, and MCID + VCID = GVCID. (PDF p. 35)
    • COP-1 is a pair of per-VC procedures: FOP-1 at the sender and FARM-1 at the receiver, with status returned in CLCWs; FARM-1 rejects Type-A frames after the first sequence error until the expected sequence number arrives. (PDF p. 37)
    • In TM and AOS, packets are concatenated into frames and may span frames; the First Header Pointer marks the first packet header, and an Idle Packet fills frames when no data is available. (PDF p. 39)
    • For deep-space commanding, performance improves by lowering the COP-1 timer or by multiple frame transmission in the coding sublayer; for blind commanding the Expedited service can be used. (PDF p. 41)
