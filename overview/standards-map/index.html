    The standards landscape¶

    The chain described in How it all fits together is specified by documents from several publishers. The space agencies' CCSDS writes the space link and ground cross-support protocols. ECSS writes the European engineering standards, adopts CCSDS books into its own system and owns the Packet Utilization Standard. ISO publishes many CCSDS Recommended Standards as ISO standards. ESA and ESOC write the interface documents of their own ground software, such as the SCOS-2000 Database Import ICD that defines the MIB, and every mission adds its own interface control documents on top.1649

    Each family has its own numbering, its own way of issuing revisions and its own idea of what is binding. This matters in practice because a document names the editions it was written against, and those editions stay in force for whoever applies it. The Solar Orbiter packet ICD applies the PUS of 2003; the SCOS-2000 ICD issue of 2017 still points at the ESA PUS of 1994 and at the CCSDS time code issue of 1990.1211 This page explains how to read each family's document numbers, how the families depend on each other, and which edition of every document this wiki holds.

    CCSDS: colours, numbers and editions¶

    Book colours¶

    CCSDS publishes in coloured "books". The colour is also a letter inside the document number.

    Colour Letter Category Wording on the cover or in citations Example in this wiki
    Blue B Recommended Standard: prescriptive interfaces, protocols and encodings "Recommendation for Space Data System Standards" CCSDS 133.0-B-2 Space Packet Protocol
    Magenta M Recommended Practice: consensus "best" or "state of the art" ways to apply standards "Recommendation for Space Data System Practices" CCSDS 914.0-M-2 SLE API core; CCSDS 320.0-M-7, which was a Blue Book until issue 6
    Green G Informational Report: overviews, rationale, performance data, tutorials "Report Concerning Space Data System Standards" CCSDS 130.0-G-4 overview of the protocols
    Orange not shown in the sources Experimental Specification: research results, not a standard not in the library none
    Yellow Y CCSDS Record: charters and procedures "CCSDS Record" CCSDS A02.1-Y-4 Organization and Processes, cited in the forewords
    Silver -S suffix Historical: superseded or obsolete documents "(Historical)"; the cover of a historical copy says it is "no longer current" SCPS-NP 713.0-B-1-S; Forward Space Packet 912.3-B-3-S (September 2022); held as historical copies: 910.4-B-2, 912.3-B-3, 135.0-B-4
    Red R Draft Recommendation, circulated before approval "Draft Recommendation" CCSDS 910.11-R-1 (SLE service management, 2006) and 727.0-R-5 (CFDP, 2001), both cited as drafts; the library's 902.4-B-1 is a "proposed draft" whose cover is already laid out as a Blue Book dated September 2026

    Sources: the ECSS communications handbook §3.1.2, the reference lists of CCSDS 130.0-G-4 and 910.3-G-3, the normative references of ECSS-E-70-41A and the covers and document control pages of the books themselves.122275926121120123

    A book can change colour between issues: the spacecraft identifier procedures were Blue Books 320.0-B-1 to -B-6 (1993 to 2013) and became the Recommended Practice 320.0-M-7 in November 2017, which also introduced the frequency band as a qualifier of the identifier and moved the request form to the SANA web site.121 A book can also change number: the XTCE Green Book 660.2-G-2 (February 2021) lists as its own first issue a 660.0-G-1 of July 2006.122

    A Green Book usually accompanies a Blue Book: the specification of a protocol is in the Blue Book and its rationale in the Green Book.19 In this library, CCSDS 130.1-G-3 and 230.1-G-3 summarise the concept and rationale of TM and TC synchronization and channel coding, 130.2-G-3 does the same for the TM, TC and AOS data link protocols, 660.1-G-2 describes the XTCE elements, and 910.3-G-3 describes the SLE services.22109248 The Green Books added to the library in November 2026 follow the same pattern: 350.5-G-2 is the summary of concept and rationale of the Space Data Link Security protocol, 130.3-G-1 an informational report on the Space Packet Protocols, 350.0-G-3 a report on the application of security to CCSDS protocols, 660.2-G-2 an informational report on XTCE and 902.0-G-1 the concept of extensible cross support service management.128

    CCSDS does not publish this range table in the available sources; it is this wiki's grouping of the numbers that appear in the reference lists and on the books held. The retired Space Link Identifiers Blue Book, whose registries SANA replaced, is named in CCSDS 130.0-G-4 without its number; the library's historical copy shows that it is 135.0 (issue 4, October 2009).23120 The second part after the dot (133.0, 133.1) separates sibling books on the same subject, for example the Space Packet and Encapsulation Packet protocols.

    Issues, editorial changes and corrigenda¶

    Every CCSDS book in this library opens with a Document Control table that lists each issue and its status ("Original issue, superseded", "Current issue"), followed by two kinds of later amendment:2932

    • An Editorial Change (EC) corrects presentation without touching the technical content. CCSDS 133.0-B-2 has two: EC 1 (October 2020) adds a missing arrowhead to figure 2-1 and makes clear that a link uses either the Encapsulation Packet or the Space Packet protocol; EC 2 (September 2024) removes duplicated table-of-contents entries and changes the page size to A4.29 The same A4 change appears in several other editorial changes of 2024, for example those of 231.0-B-4, 727.0-B-5 and 232.0-B-4.313432
    • A Technical Corrigendum (Cor.) changes technical text within an issue. CCSDS 232.0-B-4 Cor. 1 (October 2023) expands the discussion of the order of processing between the TC data link protocol, COP-1 and SDLS. CCSDS 232.1-B-2 Cor. 1 (April 2019) adds support for USLP and clarifies the FARM-1 window widths. CCSDS 914.0-M-2 Cor. 1 (August 2016) moves the hash algorithm reference to CCSDS 913.1-B.323337 The cover of 232.0-B-4 then states that the "current issue includes all updates through Technical Corrigendum 1".27 The cover of CCSDS 320.0-M-7 carries the same kind of note for its Cor. 1 (July 2019), which adds USLP frames and redefines the frequency bins with ITU frequency ranges; CCSDS 231.0-B-4 Cor. 1 (July 2026) revises the CLTU tail sequence of 5.2.4.2 and adds a note and probability details to it.121124
    • Not every amendment called editorial is purely presentational: the Editorial Change 1 of CCSDS 732.0-B-5 (October 2025) corrects a note and the issue year but also, in the words of its document control, "upgrades section 4.1.2.6 to Reed-Solomon (15,11) code".126 The first amendment of CCSDS 131.0-B-6 (May 2026) is called an "Editorial Correction" and fixes numbering in the table of contents, a table and four figures.125

    Each new issue lists its substantive changes. CCSDS 131.0-B-5 (September 2023) revises the pseudo-randomiser to avoid spectral spikes on high-rate links; CCSDS 133.0-B-2 (June 2020) removes network-type terminology from the Space Packet Protocol; CCSDS 133.1-B-3 renames the "Encapsulation Service" to the "Encapsulation Packet Protocol" and drops the Space Packet from its formats; CCSDS 732.1-B-3 (June 2024) adds VC Packet and VC Access services to USLP and allows more than one frame per CLTU; the 2023 SLE issues add support for USLP frames.2829303536 For two books the library also holds a newer issue: CCSDS 732.0-B-5 (October 2025) extends the AOS Spacecraft Identifier to 10 bits and the M_PDU First Header Pointer to 16 bits for frames of up to 65 536 octets, and CCSDS 131.0-B-6 (April 2026) adds an interleaver for turbo codewords, allows slicing for all block codes and makes the pseudo-randomizer mandatory.126125 The TM coding and AOS pages describe what changed.

    Inference

    CCSDS does not explain its file naming in the available sources. The reading in the last column is this wiki's, and it matches the document control table of every copy listed in the version matrix: x replaces the dot, the colour letter and issue follow, then e<n> for the latest editorial change and c<n> for the latest corrigendum, and a final s marks a historical (silver) copy. The two copies of 2025 and 2026 issues were served from a different directory of the CCSDS site and write the editorial amendment as ec1; whether that is a new convention is not stated in the available sources.

    Review cycle, status and binding force¶

    The Blue Books carry a standard statement of intent, quoted here from the Space Packet Protocol. Participation in CCSDS is voluntary and its recommendations bind no agency. An agency that endorses one agrees to keep its own related standards in accord with it and to tell the other members about them. Each Recommended Standard is reviewed within five years of issue to decide whether it stays, changes or is retired; a new issue does not make existing implementations non-compliant, although members are encouraged to plan new work against the newer issue.16 Books are approved by the CCSDS Management Council under the procedures of CCSDS A02.1-Y-4.1720 Retired books keep their number with an -S suffix, for example the historical SLE Forward Space Packet book 912.3-B-3-S of September 2022.222

    Adoption notices in practice¶

    An adoption notice is a short document that lists, clause by clause, what ECSS changes in a CCSDS book; everything else applies unchanged.47 ECSS-E-AS-50-25C Rev.1 (13 January 2023) adopts CCSDS 232.0-B-4 and, among other changes, makes the TC frame's Frame Error Control Field mandatory instead of optional and keeps only the 1,017-octet maximum for the frame data field.48 ECSS-E-AS-50-26C (1 March 2021) adopts COP-1 (CCSDS 232.1-B-2) and adds an "FDU aborted indication" that FARM-1 sets when it accepts a Type-BD frame but has no buffer for it.51 It notes that the CCSDS corrigendum published in the meantime does not affect the adoption.49 The other four notices are summarised on the ECSS-E-ST-50C page: for example ECSS-E-AS-50-24C Rev.1 allows only one TC frame per CLTU and always decodes BCH in single-error-correction mode, and ECSS-E-AS-50-23C Rev.1 forbids the AOS Replay Flag value '1'.146147 Six notices together replace the three 2008 ECSS link standards:4350

    ISO adoption of CCSDS books¶

    CCSDS describes its own process as producing recommendations that are "subsequently adopted by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) for use by any space organization".6 The ECSS handbook adds that many CCSDS Recommended Standards are published as ISO standards.1 This wiki's library holds the CCSDS editions only. The ISO numbers that correspond to the books on this page, and which CCSDS issue each ISO edition reproduces, are not stated in the available sources. The traffic also runs the other way into ECSS: the ECSS Trees list adoption notices of ISO and IEC standards outside the E-50 discipline, such as ECSS-E-AS-11C (ISO 16290, technology readiness levels), ECSS-U-AS-10C Rev.2 (ISO 24113, space debris mitigation) and ECSS-Q-ST-40-12C (fault tree analysis, an adoption of IEC 61025).130133132
