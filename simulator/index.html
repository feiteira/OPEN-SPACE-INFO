    Solar Orbiter simulator¶

    This page flies Solar Orbiter along its real trajectory and lets you operate it the way a SCOS-2000 operator would. The positions of the spacecraft, Earth, Venus and Mercury come from JPL Horizons.1 Every telemetry packet, parameter, calibration curve, limit, synthetic parameter and telecommand is taken from the public Solar Orbiter MIB release of 16 September 2025.2 What the spacecraft does (temperatures, solar-array steering, wheel speeds, power, data volume) is a set of simple models written for this wiki and labelled as such below.

    A teaching simulator

    This is an independent educational tool. It is not affiliated with ESA or the Solar Orbiter project, it is not an operational simulator, and its spacecraft behaviour is approximate. The MIB definitions and the trajectory are real; the numbers they carry are produced by the models described below.

    What is real and what is modelled¶

    The simulator generates five housekeeping reports: AOCS Dynamics FAST (SPID 29500) and AOCS Dynamics SLOW (29516) on APID 178, and PLF Summary Thermal (33501), PLF Comms Summary (33580) and PLF SADE HK (33582) on APID 210. It also produces the TM(1,1) and TM(1,7) verification reports (14181, 14182) and the TM(17,2) connection-test report (14178).2 It models 274 of the 479 distinct parameters in those packets. The rest keep their real position in the packet but are left at zero and shown as "not simulated". Most of them are octet-string containers, the fields of the redundant SADE B, and AOCS quantities without a model. Every name links to its entry in the MIB Explorer.

    How to use it¶

    • Time. The clock runs at the chosen speed, from real time up to five days per second. Go to jumps to any perihelion, aphelion, latitude extreme, solar conjunction or gravity assist found in the Horizons data, or to today's date. The URL keeps a shareable state, for example #t=2027-02-07T08:00&speed=600&view=craft.
    • Passes. Telemetry reaches the ground only while a ground station tracks the spacecraft. When there is no pass, Next pass ▸ jumps to the next acquisition of signal (AOS). Malargüe is the default station; New Norcia and Cebreros can be added under Failures & log.
    • Light time. The 3D view and the state panel show where the spacecraft is now. Telemetry generated on board arrives one light time later, and a telecommand takes the same time to arrive. Its TM(1,1) acceptance report therefore appears about two light times after release.
    • Charts. The first tab plots 38 real MIB parameters in 17 charts, grouped into solar arrays, AOCS, thermal and communications. Choose 6 h, 1 day or 7 days of on-board history, or Live TM for what has reached the ground. Hover a chart to read values. Amber and red lines are the MIB soft and hard limits, and shading marks wheel off-loading or ground-station passes. ☆ in the Telemetry tab adds any other numeric parameter. The URL can open a view directly, for example #t=2027-02-07T08:00&win=7d&grp=aocs.
    • Tabs. Telemetry is an alphanumeric display of a packet, with raw and engineering values and limit status. Mission profile has 2020-2030 charts; click one to jump to that date. Packets decodes headers and hex dumps. Commanding sends telecommands and loads command sequences, and keeps the command history. Failures & log has failure injection, ground stations and the event log.
    • Sequences. Commanding → Sequences lists the 27 command sequences of the MIB whose commands the simulator models. Fill in the formal parameters, check the expanded list of commands and their release times, then Load and release. The ground releases each command at its time while the pass lasts, and Stop sequence cancels the rest. The URL can open a sequence directly, for example #seq=ADMF011E.

    Things to try¶

    1. See the light time. During a pass, send ZCD01101 Perform Connection Test. The TM(17,2) report and the TM(1,1)/TM(1,7) verification reports arrive about two one-way light times later; the command verification stages fill in the history.
    2. Trip a real limit. Go to a perihelion minus about 20 days and send ZCSD0003 Disable SA Steering. The MIB expects NCFT2PZ0 SASteeringEnabled to read TRUE, so it raises a soft alarm at once. As the spacecraft nears the Sun, NRU02263 SA PY Wing passes its soft (160 °C) and hard (165 °C) limits.3 ZCSD0004 re-enables steering.
    3. Break a thermistor. Inject Battery thermistor open circuit. The raw value goes to full scale (32767). That lies outside calibration curve COMB000001 (raw 376 to 30353), and the parameter's PCF_INTER flag is F, so the engineering value becomes invalid instead of being extrapolated.113
    4. Overheat the battery. Inject Battery heater stuck on. The battery thermistors cross their soft (37 °C) and hard (40 °C) limits about two and three hours later. Under Charts → Thermal the battery line climbs through the amber and red limit lines.
    5. Lose the link. Send ZCSD1013 TWTA Power Down with TWTA_A. The TM(1,7) report for that command still arrives, but nothing after it. ZCSD1012 brings the transmitter back. Its TM(1,1) report is lost, because it is generated while the TWTA is still off.
    6. Stop a housekeeping report. Send ZCF00306 Disable HK Para Rpt Gen with SID 2. PLF Summary Thermal stops arriving one light time after the command executes; ZCF00305 restarts it.
    7. Run a procedure sequence. Open ADMF011E Def HK Rep Freq AOCS, generated from procedure DM-FCP-011. Enter SID 1 and a collection interval of 16, then load it during a pass. It releases four commands one second apart: disable the report, set its interval, request a summary, and enable it again. When they have executed, AOCS Dynamics FAST arrives every 2 s instead of every 4 s (16 cycles of ⅛ s). The Packets tab shows the new rate. ASYS001L nests ADMF011B three times and releases 12 commands over 19 seconds.4
    8. Watch the wheels and the arrays. Go to a perihelion and open Charts with 7 days. Solar radiation pressure winds the reaction wheels up until an automatic off-loading (shaded) resets them, with a jump in the attitude error each time. The wing temperatures show the steering law tilting the arrays in small steps to stay near 130 °C.
    9. Visit a solar conjunction. Go to 2025-08-15. Seen from Earth, the spacecraft is almost behind the Sun (Sun-Earth-probe angle 0.0° in the Horizons data), and the simulated link is down.

    How the telemetry chain works¶

    On board, the model produces engineering values. The simulator converts each to raw form with the inverse of the parameter's MIB calibration. It writes the value into the packet at the PLF_OFFBY/PLF_OFFBI position, with the width that the PTC/PFC pair implies (see parameter types). Then it adds the 6-octet space packet header and the 10-octet PUS-A data field header. That header holds the service type and subtype, a destination ID of 0 (ground) and a CUC time stamp of 4 coarse and 2 fine octets.67 The time is counted from 00:00 on 1 January 2000, the Solar Orbiter zero time reference.8

    On the ground, the packet is identified the way SCOS-2000 does it. APID, type, subtype and the SID read at octet 16 (the pic rule for TM(3,25)) select one pid row and so one SPID.102 Each parameter is then decoded and calibrated. Next come the validity condition (PCF_VALID/PCF_VALPAR) and the monitoring checks (OCP). A check applies only when its relevance parameter (OCP_RLCHK) has the value OCP_VALPAR.1213 Finally, the synthetic parameters are evaluated from their published OL source. For example, the source of NCASX003 SpacecraftSun Dist AU is (NCAT6M70 * 6.6845871226706E-12);, which converts the on-board ephemeris distance from metres to au.2

    Telecommands follow the reverse path. The NOM_TC header comes from tcp/pcdf. The parameters come from cdf/cpc. Textual values are encoded through pas, numerically calibrated ones through cca/ccs, and prf/prv range-check every value before release. Fixed areas such as the function identifier 482A of ZCAD482A are raw hexadecimal values.142 The packet ends with the 16-bit packet error control.9 The TM(1,1) and TM(1,7) reports carry the packet ID and sequence control of the command (NCD01011, NCD01012), which is how the ground matches them to the command history.215

    Inference

    To enable, disable or re-time a housekeeping report, the simulator maps telecommand APID 188 to telemetry APID 178 (AOCS) and telecommand APID 220 to telemetry APID 210 (platform). The mapping is deduced from the subsystem names in the MIB; the MIB does not state it.

    The models and their assumptions¶

    Topic What the simulator does Basis
    Interpolation and time Cubic Hermite interpolation of the daily Horizons positions and velocities; TDB = UTC + 69.184 s Horizons file header1; leap-second offset assumed constant
    Attitude +X points to the Sun (heat shield); +Z towards the projection of ecliptic north; the roll of the real spacecraft is not modelled. Body rates come from the rotation of this frame; the quaternion is relative to EME2000, scalar last assumption
    Ground stations Malargüe (baseline), New Norcia and Cebreros (backups); a station tracks above 10° elevation, at most 9 hours per pass centred on culmination stations and 9-hour passes: Red Book §7.1.121; 10° mask: assumption
    Downlink rate 150 kbit/s × (1 au / Earth distance)², capped at 1 Mbit/s; no link when the Sun-Earth-probe angle is below 2°; telemetry lost when the HGA points more than 1° away from Earth 150 kbit/s at 1 au: Red Book and Müller et al.1623; cap, conjunction and beam limits: assumptions
    Mass memory 549 Gbit; in-situ data 12 kbit/s from the Earth gravity assist on; remote-sensing data 70 kbit/s more in three 10-day windows per orbit (perihelion and both latitude extremes) capacity: Red Book §5.3.617; windows: Red Book §7.2.322; rates: assumptions
    Solar flux 1361 W/m² × (1 au / r)² IAU 2015 nominal solar constant25; the Red Book quotes up to 12.7 solar constants at perihelion20
    Solar arrays Wing temperature 325 K × (flux factor × cos tilt)^¼; on-board steering tilts the arrays about their long axis so the wings stay below 130 °C (at most 85°, 0.2°/s) tilting about the long axis for temperature control: Red Book §5.6 and Müller et al. §32024; numbers: assumptions
    Power Available power 974 W at 630 W/m² for a cold array, scaled with flux, cos tilt and −0.45 %/K; load 760 W platform + up to 160 W heaters (919 W far from the Sun) + 180 W payload 974 W, 919 W at 630 W/m², 180 W: Red Book §5.3.6 and §5.5.21719; split and coefficient: assumptions
    Thermistors Each NRU… temperature follows a first-order lag (time constants of 10 min to 3 h) towards an equilibrium placed inside its MIB soft limits, rising with the logarithm of the solar flux; the HGA also heats when it faces the Sun. Each sensor has a fixed offset of up to ±5 % of its limit half-range, so that sensors with the same limits do not read alike limits: MIB3; everything else assumption (real temperatures are not in the sources)
    Wheels Four wheels in a pyramid absorb a solar-radiation-pressure torque of 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ N m at 1 au scaled with flux; above 2,500 rpm, or on ZCAD482A, a 20-minute off-loading returns them to ±955 rpm four wheels and thruster off-loading: Red Book §5.3.617; torque drives off-loading frequency: Red Book §5.4.118; numbers: assumptions
    HGA Azimuth = atan2(Y, X) and elevation = −(angle from +Z) of the Earth direction in the body frame, which puts Earth inside the MIB command ranges (−179.5…179.5°, −180…0°); mechanism speed 0.05°/s ranges: MIB prf/prv VCD00348, VCD003492; convention: inference; speed: assumption
    Transmitter TWTA A in use; 180 s warm-up after power-up; EPC-1 helix current 0.35 mA and anode voltage 3300 V when on limits of NRU02141/NRU02142: MIB3; values: assumptions
    Equipment status Side A in use: RIU A on and in use, RIU B on but not in use, SADE A on, SADE B off, TWTA A in use. These status parameters drive the validity and relevance checks assumption
    Report rates AOCS FAST every 4 s, AOCS SLOW every 32 s, thermal and comms every 16 s, SADE every 32 s. ZCA00382 and ZCF00382 (TC(3,130)) change them; their interval counts cycles of 8 Hz interval unit: the description of formal parameter XF316A01 of sequence AACF316A, "Coll Interval (8Hz Cyc)"4; rates: assumption (the MIB does not give them)
    Command sequences The ground expands nested sequences and computes each release time from CSS_RELTYPE and CSS_RELTIME. A counts from the start of the sequence the element belongs to, R from the release of the previous command. Element values come from SDF as raw values, engineering values, formal parameters or command defaults. The simulator dispatches every command automatically, although the MIB flags most of them for manual dispatch (CSS_MANDISP). Sequences that allow either kind of time tag (CSF_IFTT B) are released ground time-tagged. A sequence stops at the first command that cannot be uplinked release-time rules, value sources and time-tag modes: ICD 7.05; automatic dispatch and the stop rule: simplifications
    Chart history Every housekeeping parameter is sampled every 2 minutes (kept for 25 hours) and every 15 minutes (kept for 7 days), through the same quantisation, calibration and validity checks as the packets. After a jump the simulator starts 7 days earlier in equilibrium, with the wheels at their bias speeds and no failures, and runs the models forward simulator design, not a property of the spacecraft
    On-board time Seconds since 2000-01-01 00:00 counted in UTC, with no time correlation epoch: ICD §5.108; no correlation: simplification (see time correlation)

    The heat shield is the spacecraft's defining feature. Its front reaches about 500 °C at perihelion, and the remote-sensing instruments look through feed-throughs in it.24 None of the five packets carries a heat-shield temperature, so the simulator does not show one.

    Limitations¶

    • Daily positions smooth out the geometry within a few hours of a gravity assist. The closest-approach times and distances listed under Go to come from separate 5-minute files.1
    • After 2026-11-03 the Horizons trajectory is a prediction, not a fit to tracking data.1
    • Not modelled: safe modes and on-board FDIR, eclipses during flybys, manoeuvres, instrument operations, the roll of the spacecraft, on-board time-tagged commands (PUS service 11), command interlocks, and the transfer-frame, CLTU and COP-1 layers. For those, see the downlink and uplink walkthroughs.
    • Real-time telemetry generated outside a pass is discarded, so the Telemetry tab and Live TM charts show only what arrived. On the real spacecraft, housekeeping is stored on board and dumped later. The history under Charts plays that role here, but the simulator does not model the dump as packets.

    Observations from the data¶

    • The Horizons trajectory reaches the latitudes that Müller et al. planned in 2020 on the same days. The heliographic latitude first reaches −23.5° on 2027-01-28 and −33.0° on 2029-07-24. The paper lists 24° first reached on 28 January 2027 and 33° on 24 July 2029.123
    • The refined Earth gravity assist is closest at 04:31 UTC on 2021-11-27, 6,835 km from Earth's centre. Horizons' object notes give about 454 km altitude at 04:30:31 UTC.1
    • Calibration curve COMB000001, used by most platform thermistors, is not monotonic near its hot end. Raw 817 gives 99.99 °C, 869 gives 90.00 °C and 939 gives 94.98 °C. Whether this is intended is not stated in the available sources.2
    • NCFT2PY0 SADE INUSE Profile type is expected to read NOMINAL only while NCFT2PZ0 SASteeringEnabled is
    • This is a relevance-checked status consistency check of the kind described on the monitoring checks page.3

    Rebuilding the data¶

    tools/build_simulator.py reads the MIB SQLite copy and the Horizons responses. It writes docs/simulator/data/mib.json, the 8 packets with their 501 parameters, 138 limit sets, 50 calibration curves 16 telecommands and the 27 command sequences built only from them, and docs/simulator/data/ephem.json, 3,935 daily samples and 113 derived events. The simulator code is in docs/assets/javascripts/sim/ under the wiki's MIT licence. The 3D views use three.js r160, loaded from unpkg. The Horizons responses are kept unchanged, with a manifest of every query, in the library entry.
