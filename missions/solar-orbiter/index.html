    EDDS MIB Mission PUS SCOS-2000 Solar Orbiter

    Solar Orbiter¶

    Solar Orbiter is the ESA mission whose ground data systems are documented best in the public record that this wiki draws on. Its Mission Operations Centre (MOC) is ESOC and its Science Operations Centre (SOC) is ESAC.1 A set of interface documents, a training presentation and more than a hundred releases of the operational Mission Information Base were posted on the document server of the SWA/PAS team at IRAP (solarorbiter.irap.omp.eu), from where this wiki copied them.6769 Together they describe the whole chain: how on-board applications format telemetry (TM) and telecommand (TC) packets, how the packets travel in frames, how the SCOS-2000-based Mission Control System (MCS) archives them, how external users get them through the EDDS, and how the science planning interface constrains instrument commanding.

    This page is the entry point. The data-driven pages are:

    • The Solar Orbiter MIB - row counts, subsystems, APIDs, calibrations, synthetic parameters, commands and the 2020-to-2025 evolution of the real database.
    • How Solar Orbiter uses PUS - every standard and private service found in the MIB, mapped to the PUS pages.
    • Solar Orbiter SWA instrument database - the two copied SWA instrument database versions (6.3.4 and 6.5.0), their release notes and how they relate to the spacecraft MIB.
    • Solar Orbiter STIX instrument database - the same analysis for the STIX instrument database 2.26.38.
    • Solar Orbiter instrument interfaces: SWA DPU ICD and EID-A - what ESA required of every instrument in 2007 and how one instrument, SWA, defined its packets and services.
    • The Solar Orbiter simulator, which flies the spacecraft along its JPL Horizons trajectory and generates, decodes and checks real MIB housekeeping packets and telecommands.
    • The MIB explorer, where every parameter, packet, command and sequence of the September 2025 release can be browsed, with separate views of the SWA 6.5.0 and STIX instrument databases.

    Who does what: the ground segment¶

    The documents name three groups of actors. The Operational Ground Segment (OGS) at ESOC runs the MCS, its long-term archive (LTA) and the Data Dissemination System (DDS). The Science Ground Segment (SGS) with the SOC at ESAC plans science operations. The instrument teams, led by Principal Investigators (PIs), design their instrument operations and need their data back.301 The DDS gives the SGS and the PIs controlled access to the archive, while a second, push-based path forwards every archived packet to the SGS as RAPID files.31 In the other direction, the SOC turns the MOC's planning products into a planning skeleton for the instrument teams, the E-FECS, and collects their Instrument Operations Requests (IORs), which it forwards to the MOC as Payload Operations Requests (PORs).21

    The split goes back to the instrument requirements of 2007. Issue 1 of the Experiment Interface Document Part A (EID-A) already placed the MOC and the communications infrastructure under ESA/ESOC, archived housekeeping and raw payload data at the MOC, and had the SOC generate the payload operations requests that the MOC implements and distribute the data to the PIs. It listed the MOC tasks relevant to science: overall mission planning, supplying each PI in near real time with the raw data of their instrument plus spacecraft housekeeping and auxiliary data, giving the SOC a subset, checking a set of payload parameters for out-of-limit values in near real time, and notifying payload anomalies.72 Its baseline ground station was New Norcia, with Cebreros as backup;73 the DDID of 2019 lists six ESA stations (see the table above).41

    The space-ground interface: SOL.S.ASTR.TN.00079¶

    What it is. SOL.S.ASTR.TN.00079 issue 7 is the Solar Orbiter TM-TC and Packet Structure Interface Control Document, written by the prime contractor Airbus Defence and Space Ltd (copyright 2014) in response to ESA's Statement of Work and Operations Interface Requirements Document (OIRD), on the basis of ESA's Solar Orbiter Generic Frame and Packet Structure (SO-ESC-RS-05002).1012 It is the baseline definition of all application-level data structures exchanged between the on-board computer, the solid-state mass memory, the 10 payload instruments and the star trackers, and of the same structures in their ground peer entities.10 It was exported from the PUS module of the prime's DOORS requirements database, which is why the text carries object identifiers such as PUS-31.11 Its 396 pages cover the TC and TM packet and frame layers (chapters 3 and 4), every tailored PUS service (chapter 5) and annexes on time and idle packets, the CRC, APID and source/destination allocation, failure and event identifiers, housekeeping structure identifiers and the on-board parameter identifier.13

    Its rules on PUS are strict: changing the definition of a standard service, or using it in a non-standard way, is forbidden; any user-specific packet must be defined as a private service in a range assigned to the user, and private services are documented in each unit's own TM/TC ICD rather than here.10 The services themselves are analysed on How Solar Orbiter uses PUS.

    Application process identifiers¶

    Solar Orbiter gives structure to the 11-bit APID: its 7 most significant bits are a process ID (PID) that names the on-board application, and its 4 least significant bits are a packet category (CAT). For telecommands the category is always 12; for telemetry it says what kind of packet the process is emitting.1419

    CAT TM meaning (Annex 8)

    5 Functional cyclic: private TM(3,25) produced on ground request
    6 Functional non-cyclic: private report packets on ground request

    The category table and the process ID table are Figures 13.1-1 and 13.1-2 of the ICD.26 The process IDs are allocated as follows: 0 time; 2 the OBC hardware (CPDU high-priority commands, HPTM); 10 to 16 the Central Software (CSW) applications - 10 data management, 11 AOCS, 12 payload, 13 platform, 14 system control, 15 OBCP, 16 thermal; 30 to 32 the SSMM; 40 and 41 star trackers A and B; 49 payload broadcast; then instrument ranges EPD 50-56 and 100, EUI 57-62, MAG 63-66, METIS 67-71, PHI 72-74, RPW 75-81, SOLOHI 82-84, SPICE 85-89 and 101-104, STIX 90-94, SWA 95-99; 112 to 127 EGSE.26 For example the AOCS application (PID 11) sends its events on APID 11 × 16 + 7 = 183, and every command for it goes to APID 11 × 16 + 12 = 188; both appear in the real MIB (see the APID catalogue).64

    Telecommand path¶

    A TC packet carries the PUS-A data field header with PUS version 1, the acknowledgement flags (only acceptance and completion are used), service type, subtype and an 8-bit source ID. The sequence count is kept per APID and source ID but is not checked on board, and the packet is protected by a 16-bit CRC.141564 The bit-level layout and the way SCOS-2000 builds it from the MIB are on PUS packet structures. Reserved ground source IDs distinguish the mission timeline (110), TC sequences (111), recovery actions (112), the back-up mission timeline (113) and direct commands (120); the CSW uses 11 (AOCS), 14 (system control) and 15 (OBCP) for commands it generates itself.27

    Packets travel in TC segments whose header carries 2 sequence flags, always 0b11 because Solar Orbiter never segments packets, and a 6-bit MAP ID: 0 for CPDU commands, TC(2,3), and 1 for all other commands.16 To raise the uplink throughput, several complete packets are aggregated in one segment; each packet's start is found from the length of the previous one.17 In the MIB, the MAP ID 0 path is used by the 125 HPC-1 commands: they have the header HPC1_TC without a secondary header, go to APID 44 (process 2, category 12) and are flagged high priority (CCF_HIPRI = Y) with CCF_MAPID = 0, the field that SCOS-2000 uses to force the MAP ID of high-priority commands. The TC(2,3) commands, which the ICD's MAP ID text names as the CPDU commands, are in the MIB ordinary PUS commands to the CSW on APID 172, mostly named "HPC-3 ..." (see service 2).6466

    The CPDU (Command Pulse Distribution Unit) inside the OBC can switch vital units on and off without the CSW. Commands sent to it from the ground are HPC-1 commands; the reconfiguration module can use the same lines (HPC-2), and the CSW can write CPDU registers to produce the same pulses (HPC-3).16 The segment is the data field of a TC transfer frame (CCSDS 232.0) with version 0b00, the usual bypass and control command flags giving AD, BD and BC frames under COP-1, spacecraft ID 650 and a maximum length of 256 octets, so that only the low 8 bits of the 10-bit frame length field are ever used.18 The frames are then encoded into CLTUs (see TC synchronization and channel coding); the ICD lists ECSS-E-ST-50-04C as a reference for this layer.12

    Telemetry path¶

    All on-board TM uses packet version 0b000; the data field header flag is 1 except in the standard time packet. APID 0 is reserved for the time packet and APID 2047 (all ones) for idle packets.19 The grouping flags may mark the first, continuation and last packets of a multi-packet answer (memory dumps, report of service definitions) but each packet stays a self-standing PUS packet; the source sequence count is kept per APID and destination ID and the ground must alarm on every discontinuity.19 The 10-octet data field header holds the PUS version (1), service type and subtype, the destination ID and a 6-octet CUC time; its exact octet layout is a figure without text in the ICD and is reconstructed on PUS packet structures.20 The destination ID is the command's source ID copied back for solicited TM, the receiving process for packets meant for another on-board process, and 0b00000000 (ground) otherwise, always for categories 2, 3, 4, 8 and 9; the time packet has no data field header and therefore no destination ID.28

    Packets are carried in TM transfer frames of exactly 1115 octets (CCSDS 132.0): version 0b00, spacecraft ID 650, an operational control field with a CLCW in every frame, a frame secondary header in every frame, a synchronisation flag of 0 with packet order flag 0 and segment length identifier 0b11, and a first header pointer of all ones when no packet starts in the frame, or 0b11111111110 in idle frames on VC7. A frame error control word is present in every frame.21 Packets longer than the 1099-octet frame data field are spread over several frames.19

    2 VC0 Real-time TM, and the time packet when due VC4

The ICD lists VC5 before VC0, but its conditions ("when ready and no VC4 or VC0 available" for VC5, "no VC4 available" for VC0) put VC0 above VC5, which is the order used in the table. Only VC2 and VC3 may carry compressed packets; VC0, VC1, VC4 and VC5 never do.22 On the ground, the MCS stream identifiers 1000 to 1003 correspond to the on-line VC0 (real-time non-science), VC1 (playback non-science), VC2 (science) and VC3 (file transfer), which suggests how the VCs are used in flight.41

    Time, idle packets and the CRC¶

    The standard spacecraft time packet has APID 0, no data field header and 8 octets of data. Its time refers to the leading edge of the first bit of the attached synchronisation marker of the VC0 frame whose virtual channel frame count is 0; ground uses it to correlate on-board time with UTC.23 See Time correlation in SCOS-2000 and CCSDS time codes.

    Idle packets have APID 2047, no data field header and random filler data.24 The packet error control uses the CCITT polynomial \(x^{16}+x^{12}+x^5+1\) with the register preset to all ones, computed over the whole packet except the CRC itself. The ICD gives test vectors, for example 00 00 gives 0x1D0F and AB CD EF 01 gives 0x04A2 (real values from the source).25

    Data delivery: the DDID and the EDDS¶

    What it is. The Solar Orbiter Data Delivery Interface Control Document (DDID), SOL-ESC-IF-05011 issue 1 revision 2 of 26 June 2019, prepared by the Solar Orbiter data systems manager's team in ESA's operations directorate (OPS-GDS), is the mission-specific complement to the generic EDDS External User ICD (EGOS-GEN-EDDS-ICD-1001); the two are meant to be read together.29 It specifies the request and delivery mechanisms and the data products of the Solar Orbiter DDS, which is an instance of the generic EGOS Data Dissemination System, for all mission phases.29

    Services and access control¶

    The DDS offers an HTTPS web interface and an EDDS client application for requesting TM packets and parameters, TC history, spacecraft and MCS events, auxiliary files and catalogue information. Requests are XML documents, either batch requests that return a complete data set as a file, or stream requests for near-real-time data; they can be saved, scheduled, or dropped as XML files on the server via SFTP. Responses are pushed to the user's SFTP server or kept for download.3137 Users can also subscribe to auxiliary files as they arrive in the file archive.31 The DDS server runs in cold redundancy - only one of the prime and backup servers is up at a time, and a switch-over needs the user to point the client at the other machine - with an availability requirement of at least 95% over a month.3335

    Each account can be restricted by APID, SPID, parameter name, file name or file type, by data volume over a period, by a relative time range (for example only the last 12 months) and by an access period; accounts may create sub-users within their own rights.323851 During cruise, most users were expected to fetch only their own payload data plus some housekeeping, while near the Sun the SGS would retrieve the full data set and redistribute it.38

    Where the data come from¶

    The DDS reads the MCS long-term archive: packets, events and TC data from the packet archive (PARC), auxiliary files and TM files from the file archive (FARC), and parameters from the parameter archive (DARC). Spacecraft TM is available as soon as it is stored, normally within seconds of reception at the ground station.36 See Mission archives: PARC, DARC and FARC.

    Data type Archive Notes
    TM, TC and event packets, packet statistics PARC includes MCS-generated packets such as time correlation packets
    TM parameters, parameter statistics DARC
    Command history, TM gap and other reports PARC
    Auxiliary files, TM files, archive catalogues FARC

    The table condenses §6.2 of the DDID.37 Among the auxiliary file types is the MIB itself (archive file type MIB), alongside flight dynamics products (attitude, orbit, events, one-way light time, HGA blockage) and planning files.49

    The SCOS-2000 packet as delivered¶

    Raw packet requests return the SCOS-2000 packet (also called the TDEV packet): a common header, a TM-, TC- or event-specific header, then the packet data. For spacecraft TM the packet data is the complete, unstripped source packet starting with the CCSDS version number; all DDS data are big-endian and times are 4 octets of seconds since 1 January 1970 (leap seconds not counted) plus 4 octets of microseconds.3940 The EDDS request and data formats page covers the generic formats; the Solar Orbiter values are these:

    The field list and values are from §7.1.1 of the DDID.41 Two fields tie every archived packet to a MIB release: the filing key is the SPID and DB Rel / DB Iss record the VDF_RELEASE and VDF_ISSUE of the vdf table in force when the packet was archived.41 For the September 2025 MIB those are 25925 and 733.64

    The TM header and the RouteID codes are from §7.1.2.42 The TC header instead holds the uplink, execution and last-update times, MCS request identifiers, the PUS APID, sequence count, type, subtype and acknowledgement flags, an uplink flag that records whether the command passed pre-transmission verification (PTV), and the command source; its "command details" body is produced by overlaying a C++ class on 32-bit aligned memory, so fields are padded to 32-bit boundaries.4344 The MCS also archives, for each command, a second TC packet with SPID 200 holding its static PTV and verification details from the MIB; external users ignore it. Released commands are filed with SPID 100.4041

    Inference

    The common header adds up to 480 bits (60 octets) and the TM header to 128 bits (16 octets), 76 octets in all. The SCOS-2000 ICD gives 76 octets as the TM packet header size of SCOS-2000 packet header version 4,65 while the DDID states version 5 for Solar Orbiter. The equality of the sizes is this wiki's arithmetic; whether version 5 has the same size is not stated in the available sources.

    When frames are missing while the MCS assembles a long packet, it files the part built so far as a "bad" source packet (data unit type 1, qualifier 1) and the following frames as individual good frames, so users can still recover the data.45

    TM files, the command history and RAPID files¶

    Telemetry downlinked through Solar Orbiter's service 13 file transfer arrives as TM files: plain concatenations of TM packets, stored in the FARC as file type "DLPP Files" and named <PS_ID>_<file_reconstruction_timestamp>, for example 13_2014.336.13.54.07.057. Their transaction history is recorded in event packets with SPID 4000 (transaction ID, packet store ID, status, FDUs received and expected, retransmissions, times, file name, injection kind and time).50 The command history report carries additional "Science Kernel" custom fields (in the XML response, CustomField elements with name, type and value), among them the service 13 file ID and file attribute (PLAIN, OBCP, TC_DELAYED, TC_IMMEDIATE or PAYLOAD_CONTEXT) of commands uplinked inside files.48

    The nominal OGS-to-SGS path is not the DDS but RAPID (Raw Archive Packet Interceptor Dumper) files: the MCS writes every SCOS-2000 packet it archives into one of three open files (TM, TC, events), closes them every 50 seconds and lets the Generic File Transfer System (GFTS) forward them to the SGS: one GFTS instance moves them from the MCS long-term archive across the operational LAN (OPSLAN) firewall to the DDS server on the relay LAN, and a second one across the ESOC corporate firewall and the SGS firewall. Packets appear in reception order, so re-dumped or recalled data arrive out of generation order, and a TC packet is re-sent whole every time its verification status changes. Two weeks of RAPID files are kept for recovery.5234 Files are named PARC_<Type>_<FirstPacketTime>__<LastPacketTime>.53

    ST, ET 8 + 8 First and last time of a delete range (not used)
    AT 8 Action time: microseconds since 1970 when the packet was inserted or updated

    The EDDS/DDID training presentation¶

    EDDS / DDID - Presentation & Demo is an 80-slide ESA training deck first given on 28 September 2017 by the BepiColombo mission data systems manager and the EDDS technical officer, updated for Solar Orbiter in January 2019; its file name gives the reference SOL-ESC-PRS-22100 issue 1 revision 0 of 2019-01-30, which is not printed on the slides.54 It explains to external users how the DDID complements the EDDS External User ICD (then issue 15.0 of February 2018), that EDDS gives controlled access to mission data for users without MCS access by reading PARC, DARC, FARC and the file system, and that it keeps the interfaces of its predecessors GDDS and TDRS.5556 The deck still gives the DDID reference as BC-ESC-IF-05011, issue 1.0 of 2013-09-10, with issue 1.2 expected in mid-2019; the issue in the library is SOL-ESC-IF-05011 issue 1 revision 2 of 26 June 2019.5529 It also stresses what EDDS is not: a data warehouse or an analysis tool.57 See TDRS and GDDS.

    Planning: the E-FECS ICD¶

    What it is. SOL-SGS-ICD-0006 issue 3.0 (5 October 2021), issued at ESAC, defines the E-FECS (Enhanced Flight Events and Communication Skeleton), the XML "planning skeleton" through which the SOC tells the instrument teams the global planning constraints before they prepare their IORs in short-term planning (STP).21 The MOC provides the FECS (from its flight control team) and the PTEL (from flight dynamics); after long-term planning (LTP) the SOC produces the E-FECS, mostly by adding windows to those inputs. It is used in cruise and in the nominal and extended mission phases.2

    flowchart LR
  MOC["MOC<br/>FECS (FCT) + PTEL (FD)"] --> LTP["SOC long-term planning<br/>(~6 months)"]
  LTP --> EF["E-FECS<br/>EFECS_Mnn_Vvv.xml<br/>delivered at T-20 weeks"]
  EF --> IT["Instrument teams<br/>short-term planning (~1 week)"]
  IT -- "IORs" --> SOC["SOC"]
  SOC -- "PORs" --> MOC2["MOC<br/>commanding"]

    The flow follows §1 and §3 of the ICD.238 Several of its rules touch TM/TC directly:

    • Time format. Every event time uses the CCSDS ASCII time code B day-of-year form YYYY-DDDThh:mm:ssZ, with no sub-seconds and a mandatory Z; because the legacy planning systems ignore leap seconds, a window containing one may last a second longer.4 See CCSDS time codes.
    • Passes. Any low-latency data written to SpaceWire before the start of a PASS event is downlinked in that pass (special cases aside). Pass events carry the station, the frame-level downlink rate, the modulation, whether ranging is on, and a "MIB code" for the transponder configuration.5 The transponder's parameters and commands in the MIB are described on RF and ranging.
    • Routing maintenance. During ENG_WIN windows TM and TC routing to and from the instruments is suspended or not guaranteed because of SSMM/SpaceWire maintenance, so no planned instrument TCs may be submitted.6
    • TAC windows. For turn-around calibrations the SOC re-routes all of an instrument's science APIDs (other than low-latency data) to a small shared store for prompt downlink; instrument teams wait 15 seconds after the window starts for the routing commands to complete.7
    • Packet store resizing. Resizing SSMM packet stores loses data unless the stores are empty, and needs a short window without SSMM memory operations; how to plan this was still an open issue.9

    E-FECS files are delivered through GFTS at about T-20 weeks for each six-month period and are named EFECS_M[nn]_V[vv].xml, with B instead of V for a backup plan.8

    On-board inter-instrument communication¶

    What it is. SOL-SGS-TN-0007 issue 2.0 (7 October 2016), issued at ESAC for the Science Ground Segment, records how the instrument teams agreed to exchange real-time information on board so that they can react autonomously to solar events, for example by triggering coordinated burst modes.58 Instruments never talk to each other directly. Each sends an inter-instrument communication (IIC) housekeeping packet TM(3,25) to the OBC, with category 5, its own process ID (different from the one used for its other HK) and a structure ID; the OBC copies the parameters into its data pool within a nominal 250 ms.59 Up to 8 times a second the OBC assembles a TC(20,128) carrying 36 octets of platform data and up to 200 octets of instrument data and distributes it; TC(20,1) and TC(20,2) start and stop distribution per instrument, with a period from one CSW cycle (125 ms) to 65 535 cycles (about 8191 s; 0 requests a one-shot distribution). The nominal maximum end-to-end delay is 500 ms. The same TC(20,128) also acts as the OBC heartbeat that lets instruments go safe if the spacecraft enters Survival Mode.5960

    EID-A: what ESA asked of the instruments in 2007¶

    What it is. The Solar Orbiter Experiment Interface Document - Part A, SOL-EST-IF-0050 issue 1 revision 0 of 9 October 2007, was prepared at ESTEC by the Solar Orbiter team. The EID is first released with the instrument Announcement of Opportunity; Part A states the technical and programmatic requirements that every PI must meet, Part B is each PI's response and becomes the sole binding agreement between the project office and that PI, and both are placed under configuration control once signed.70 The copy in the library was taken from the host explorers.larc.nasa.gov.91 It was written before the payload was selected: its resource table lists potential instruments named VIM, EUS, COR, HI/WFC and DPD next to SWA, RPW, MAG, EPD, EUI and STIX, and it allocates the payload 180 kg, 180 W and 100 kbps in all, against maximum data rates of, for example, 14 000 bps for SWA and 200 bps for STIX.71

    Its TM/TC-relevant requirements are few but basic:

    Topic EID-A issue 1 requirement Where it ended up
    Data handling the spacecraft Data Management System provides instrument command and control, command timeline management, packetised science data acquisition, mass memory storage and transmission, and health monitoring of instruments that are off; the bus is SpaceWire (ECSS-E-50-12) and each instrument uses a dual cold-redundant SpaceWire interface for TM and TC packets74 the SWA DPU talks to the OBC over SpaceWire, with TC, TM and time-code services in its EXOS layer84
    Links and time SpaceWire link interface compliant up to the packet and network levels; two independent lines per instrument, addressed and routed separately; every instrument shall receive SpaceWire time codes; digital telemetry "TBW"75 in the SWA DPU, time-code management is implemented in the DPM FPGA and reported through EXOS84
    Fault handling no automatic reconfiguration after a failure; go to a safe mode, send TM records of the fault to the DMS, recover only by ground telecommand76 the inter-instrument TC(20,128) doubles as the OBC heartbeat that lets instruments go safe when the spacecraft enters Survival Mode59
    On-board software ECSS-E-40; boot software in PROM with access to all functions; an event report before any forced processor reset and on overload, arithmetic overflow, illegal instruction, data bus error, EDAC-detected memory corruption and checksum error; a single telecommand to enable software, after checking the upload; all ground communication with software functions through dedicated TC and TM source packets77 the SWA DPU boot software resides in PROM and cannot be patched84
    EGSE the instrument station exchanges TM with the Central Checkout Equipment over a TCP/IP LAN at source packet level, using an EGSE protocol to be defined by the contractor; the CCE commands the instrument and monitors its housekeeping78 see SCOS-2000 as EGSE

    The PUS is not mentioned in issue 1, and its operations chapter is mostly placeholders: mission operations, inputs to the satellite database, on-board software maintenance, inputs to the planning system and command request handling are all "TBW".79 In the library, the PUS tailoring is covered by the later packet structure ICD and planning by the E-FECS ICD; how instrument definitions enter the satellite database is visible only in the data. The DPU ICD cites issue 5 of the EID-A, which is not in the library.82

    The SWA DPU Communications ICD¶

    What it is. SO-SWA-DPU_CD-IC-003 issue 8.2 (7 August 2019) is the communications interface control document of the SWA Data Processing Unit, written by the Italian DPU team under an ASI contract in the format of Annex E of ECSS-E-ST-40C. Over 403 pages it gives the DPU software architecture, the APID allocation of every SWA telemetry type, the format and error codes of every telecommand the DPU accepts (PUS services 3, 5, 6, 9, 17, 20, 21 and the private services 200 to 204), the housekeeping, asynchronous, inter-instrument and science packets, the internal protocols with the four sensors, and the code tables for events and for the failure reports TM(1,2) ("TARF") and TM(1,8) ("TECF").808185 It references ECSS-E-70-41A (PUS-A) and SOL.S.ASTR.TN.00079 issue 7, the packet structure ICD described above.82 Its change record runs from issue 1 (27 June 2014) to 8.2, and the SWA IDB release notes follow most of its issues; the comparison of the ICD, the IDB and the MIB is on Solar Orbiter SWA instrument database, and the document itself is presented on Solar Orbiter instrument interfaces.8387
