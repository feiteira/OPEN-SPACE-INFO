    Solar Orbiter instrument interfaces: SWA DPU ICD and EID-A¶

    A science instrument reaches the ground through a chain of interface documents. ESA's Experiment Interface Document (EID) Part A states what every Solar Orbiter instrument must comply with, and each Principal Investigator answers with a Part B.2 The spacecraft prime contractor's TM/TC and packet structure ICD, SOL.S.ASTR.TN.00079, fixes the packet formats, APIDs and PUS services on board (see Solar Orbiter).53 Each instrument then documents its own packets and commands: for SWA (the "Solar Wind Analyzer" in the EID-A acronym list) this is the DPU Communications Interface Control Document SO-SWA-DPU_CD-IC-003, and the same content is delivered in machine form as the SWA instrument database (IDB), which ends up in the spacecraft MIB.151648 This page covers the three round-2 sources of that chain - EID-A issue 1.0, the SWA ICD issue 8.2 and the SWA IDB v6.5.0 with its release notes - and checks the ICD against the data. The database itself is analysed on Solar Orbiter SWA instrument database; the STIX equivalent is STIX instrument database.

    The Experiment Interface Document, Part A (2007)¶

    What the document is¶

    The EID-A was to be released with the instrument Announcement of Opportunity; its background section notes that ESA's Science Programme Committee was to decide in November 2007 whether and how to proceed with the mission.2 It exists to make the PIs design, build and verify their instruments within the spacecraft's constraints and the prime contractor build a spacecraft into which they can be integrated. Part A holds the technical and programmatic requirements common to all PIs; Part B, written by each PI, becomes the only binding agreement between the ESA project office and that PI, and both parts become applicable documents to the prime contractor.2 Once signed they are under configuration control: changes go through an Engineering Change Request, deviations through a Request for Waiver, each numbered for example ECR/XYZ/0100, and the receiving party's board must decide within 4 weeks.12 The copy in the library is issue 1.0, signed on 9 October 2007; the SWA ICD lists the EID-A as applicable document AD[2] under the reference SOL-EST-RCD-0050 at issue 5, which is not in the library.118

    The 2007 instrument list shows how early this issue is: its resource table (Table 3) lists eleven potential instruments with mass, power and data rate, among them SWA (then "Solar Wind Plasma Analyzer", 16.5 kg nominal, 14 W, 14 000 bit/s) and STIX (4.4 kg, 4 W, 200 bit/s), against a payload allocation of 180 kg, 180 W and 100 kbit/s that the listed total of 106 100 bit/s exceeds.4 The flown payload named in the packet ICD (EPD, MAG, RPW, SWA, SPICE, PHI, EUI, METIS, STIX, SoloHI) differs from that 2007 list.54

    Data handling requirements¶

    The data handling chapter is short. The on-board data handling system - the Data Management System (DMS), combining the functions of a Command and Data Management Unit and the attitude control computer - is the instruments' gateway to ground and provides instrument command and control, command timeline management, packetized science data acquisition, mass-memory storage and transmission, and health and safety monitoring while instruments are off.5 The bus architecture is SpaceWire to ECSS-E-50-12, and each instrument must use a dual cold-redundant SpaceWire interface "for transmission of TM and reception of TC packets".5 The detailed requirements are:8

    Clause Requirement
    4.8.2.1 A SpaceWire link interface (cables and connectors, LVDS drivers, codec, FIFO) compliant up to the packet and network levels of the SpaceWire standard
    4.8.2.1 (1) Instruments receive SpaceWire time codes according to ECSS-E-50-12
    4.8.2.3 (2) Two independent SpaceWire lines per instrument, addressed and routed separately to the spacecraft data handling system
    4.8.2.2, 4.8.2.4 Monitoring and synchronisation interfaces "TBD"; digital telemetry "TBW"

    A SpaceWire Remote Terminal Controller ASIC under ESA development could implement the remote terminal, but it is a PI-provided item (targets: about 50 MIPS, under 500 mW and 100 g).5

    Software and fault-handling requirements that shape TM/TC¶

    The requirements of section 4.9.1 that most directly shape telemetry and telecommands are:9

    • all on-board software complies with ECSS-E-40, with separate memory areas for code, fixed constants and variable parameters, a minimum boot software in PROM from which every function, including EEPROM updates, is reachable, and modules that can be changed without moving others;
    • software resource usage (memory, CPU, I/O) should be telemetered, uploaded software must be checkable before it is enabled, and enabling should take a single telecommand;
    • any communication between ground and an on-board software function uses telecommand and telemetry source packets designed for the purpose;
    • an event report is generated before a forced processor reset, and on processor overload, unexpected arithmetic overflow, illegal instruction, data bus error, memory corruption detected by EDAC, and checksum error.

    Fault tolerance relies on redundancy; after a failure an instrument must not reconfigure itself to continue but go to a safe mode, send telemetry records of the fault to the DMS and wait for a ground telecommand to recover; and instrument computers must reset and resume from a defined mode after power cycling.67 The SWA ICD's safe mode, boot software and event list (below) can be compared with these clauses.

    Operations, database and ground equipment¶

    The operational chapter assigns the MOC and communications to ESA/ESOC, archiving at the MOC, and the SOC to produce payload operations requests and distribute data to the PIs; the MOC supplies raw instrument data with housekeeping and auxiliary data and performs near-real-time out-of-limit checks on a set of payload parameters.310 The PI defines the instrument modes, supplies a user manual, inputs to the database and to the Flight Operations Plan, and maintains the software to end of mission.1011 Most operational subsections are still empty: "Inputs to Satellite Database", on-board software maintenance, command request handling and the mission products are all "TBW" in issue 1.0.10 On-board software is delivered with each model, either resident in non-volatile memory or loadable through the spacecraft telecommand uplink.13 The instrument EGSE must integrate into the system EGSE; its Instrument Station talks to the Central Checkout Equipment (CCE) over a TCP/IP LAN, exchanging telemetry "at Source Packet level", and the CCE commands the instrument and monitors its housekeeping.13

    What EID-A issue 1.0 does not specify¶

    The document's normative references are the mission requirements document, engineering standards of the ECSS-E-10, -20 and -30 series, SpaceWire (ECSS-E-50-12), software (ECSS-E-40), product assurance standards (ECSS-Q, ESA PSS) and a materials handbook; the PUS (ECSS-E-70-41) is not among them, and the document does not mention it anywhere.14 Issue 1.0 therefore says nothing about packet structure, APIDs, PUS services, time formats, verification or the database format: those come from the spacecraft packet ICD, whose PUS services, Annex 8 APID scheme and Annex 9 failure and event IDs the SWA ICD and IDB follow (see How Solar Orbiter uses PUS).5458 Whether the later issue 5 cited by SWA adds such requirements is not stated in the available sources.

    The SWA DPU Communications ICD (issue 8.2, 2019)¶

    Purpose and history¶

    SWA and its DPU¶

    SWA has four units: two Electron Analyser Systems (EAS1, EAS2) for electron velocity distributions and moments, a Proton Alpha Sensor (PAS) for the major solar wind ions, and a Heavy Ion Sensor (HIS) for charge states of oxygen and iron and minor ions; all are connected to the DPU by SpaceWire at 10 Mbit/s.19 The DPU provides power, control, temporary storage, communication and computation; its hardware is a rigid-flex backplane with six SpaceWire splitters, two Data Processing Modules (each a communication and science processing board plus a CPU board) and two Power Conditioning and Distribution Modules.19 Its software provides TC reception, science and HK telemetry, sensor control (all sensors are switched by the DPU except HIS, powered by a dedicated spacecraft line, although Table 7 also lists DPU commands TC(200,153)/(200,154) to switch HIS on and off), buffering of 5 minutes of high-resolution EAS and PAS data for triggered transmission, moment calculation and compression, time synchronisation, and inter-instrument exchange through PUS service 20.20

    Packet formats¶

    The ICD refers to the packet ICD for the packet formats and to the SpaceWire application protocol for the transport, and draws each packet as a grid of 16-bit words.22 Every telecommand starts with the same five words - version, type, data field header flag, PID 95 and category; sequence flags and count; packet length; secondary header flag, PUS version 1, ack flags and service type; subtype and source ID - and ends with the packet error control word.23 The SWA IDB's TC header NOM_TC gives the exact bit positions:48

    The ICD names the fields VN, TY, DF, PID, PKT, SF, PSC, PL, SHF, PVN, ACK, ST, SS, SI and PEC; the IDB fixes the source ID to 0x78 (120), the value the packet ICD reserves for ground direct commands, and asks for acceptance and completion reports (CCF_ACK = 9) on all 201 commands.234857 The IDB treats PID and PKT as one 11-bit APID element, 95 x 16 + 12 = 1532.4853

    Telemetry uses a 10-octet data field header drawn as secondary header flag, PUS version 001, four spare bits and the service type; subtype and destination ID; and three words of on-board time (OBRT).3940 The field widths follow from the printed values (3-bit 001, 4-bit 0000) in a 16-bit word, and agree with the 10-octet data field header and the 6-octet CUC time (4 octets coarse, 2 fine) of the packet ICD and with the 16-octet header length (PID_DFHSIZE = 16) of every SWA packet in the IDB.5648

    PUS manager: services 3, 5, 6, 9, 17, 21¶

    • Service 3. TC(3,5)/(3,6) enable or disable one HK packet by its SID (1-15; types HK or DIAG), TC(3,129) changes its period in 125 ms time slices, which must be a multiple of 8 (one second); bad SIDs and rates fail execution with codes 0xA8D9 and 0xA8DA.24
    • Service 5. TC(5,5)/(5,6) enable or disable a list of event IDs, given in decimal from 43500 (EvntOk) to 43564 (DPUSwWarning), for example 43526 RpwTriggerOccurred, 43547 dpuSafeState, 43550 thrustFire.25
    • Service 6. Load, dump and check by absolute address, and abort dump TC(6,129).     DPU manager: states, configuration and macros¶

    The DPU manager runs the instrument's state machine. Each mode command starts a DPU "CCSDS macro" - DPUDiagM, DPUStbyM, DPUOps or DPUSafeM - and fails acceptance with 0xA7FF when the current state does not allow it or 0xA805 while a safe transition is running.30313243

A macro holds at most 16 TCs of at most 64 octets each, drawn from a list of TC instances (for example 0x0E SetE1Off, an instance of TC(201,132), or 0x26 DisTcProc, TC(200,165) with value 7), and its content can be modified with service 6.43 The step list is not fully consistent with the TC tables: it maps PasPwrOffFast (0x14) to TC(200,154), which Table 7 names "Switch off HIS", and maps both SetPas (0x13) and PasPwrOff (0x1C) to TC(203,138), which Table 6 names "PAS FastPower OFF"; which mapping is intended is not stated in the available sources.433536

    Sensor, EAS, PAS and HIS managers¶

    The sensor manager puts all sensors into burst mode at the next 4-second boundary (TC(200,160), durations in 0.125 s steps), puts both EAS into science mode, sets the product cadence (TC(200,162): EAS 10 s, 100 s or 400 s; PAS and HIS nominal or low), shifts science timing against the SCET by -299 to +299 (TC(200,163)), stops macros, and enables or disables commanding of individual sensors (TC(200,165)).33 The EAS managers hold 43 commands each, mostly register, table and high-voltage settings (dither, notch, deflection and hemisphere tables, MCP high voltage, eight engineering modes 2-9, strahl selection, pre-amplifiers); the PAS manager's 33 commands cover calibration and engineering modes, high-voltage ramps, sequence and configuration tables, start and stop science and stand-by.3435 HIS commands TC(204,x) are passed through: the DPU routes them unchanged and HIS validates them itself.3642

    Asynchronous reports, events and the S20 contribution¶

    Reports produced on request are TM(200,248) debug data, (200,249) monitoring configuration, (200,250) configuration parameters (a count followed by identifier, length and data per parameter, for example 0x0002 RebootCnt), (200,251) macro content, (200,252) FSW start-up report, (200,253) error log, and (201,200), (202,200), (203,200) EAS1, EAS2 and PAS parameter dumps; the ICD gives all of them PID 99 and category 9.3922 Section 13 lists the events with their auxiliary data, codes from 0xA9ED (TcAcqError) upwards.44 In the IDB the SWA event IDs run from 43501 to 43999 (0xA9ED-0xABDF) in all four severities: SWA numbers its events inside its 43000-43999 block (Table 14.3-1 allocates these ranges for both failure and event IDs) rather than by the severity ranges that the packet ICD defines for the central software (and makes optional for others).4859

    Every 4 seconds the DPU sends the TM(3,25) with PID 95, category 5 and SID 255 for distribution to other instruments: the heartbeat (coarse OBRT), the PAS proton density and the three solar wind velocity components, then spares, with a packet length field of 0x1A.40 The bit layout and its agreement with SOL-SGS-TN-0007 and the IDB are on SWA.62

    Science telemetry and sensor protocols¶

    Science products use service 21. An EAS 3D product, generated every 100 s in normal mode, is one TM(21,3) with SID, SID counter, SCET, the EAS mode, validity flags for the whole set, each of the 16 elevations and each energy, and the compression information, followed by TM(21,6) packets (grouping flags first, continuation, last) carrying up to 2 045 words of compressed data.41 PIDs 96, 97 and 98 distinguish normal science, optional burst or RPW-frozen data and core burst; HIS science packets are passed through unchanged.41 The IDB has 96 such packets (54 TM(21,3) and 42 TM(21,6) on the science APIDs 1532, 1548, 1564, 1580 and 1596).48 Table 10 of the ICD lists the six HIS science products (pulse-height analysis, alpha and heavy matrix rates, alpha and heavy velocity distributions, rates) as TM(21,5), whereas the IDB defines no TM(21,5) packet and carries HIS science as TM(21,3) and TM(21,6) with SSIDs 128-134 and 191 (for example SPID 58808 SWA_TM_SCI_HIS_PHA_NORM, TM(21,6) on APID 1548).4148

    Inside SWA the DPU talks to EAS and PAS with a simple register protocol over SpaceWire, not with PUS packets. Each EAS command starts with a header octet:42

    followed by a 16-bit address, a 16-bit length and data in 24-bit units, the EAS internal bus width.42 A command that could damage the instrument is sent as an enable packet followed immediately by a go packet with identical address and data; any other packet in between clears the enable and the go is rejected.42 HIS, in contrast, speaks CCSDS: the DPU forwards TCs to it and its TM to the spacecraft, and on receiving a HIS TM(5,3) it forwards the packet and powers HIS off at once.42

    Inference

    The documents show a division of labour: ESA's EID-A sets the physical interface and the behaviour (safe mode, event reports), the prime contractor's packet ICD sets the PUS layer, the instrument team's ICD maps the instrument onto it, and Planetek's IDB transcribes the ICD into MIB tables that the spacecraft MIB absorbs. The release notes' habit of citing ICD issues, and the near-identity of IDB v6.5.0 and the 2025 MIB, support this; the formal delivery route from IDB to MIB is not described in the available sources.
