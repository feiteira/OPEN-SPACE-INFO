    ECSS-E-70-41A ECSS-E-ST-70-41C MIB PUS Solar Orbiter

    How Solar Orbiter uses PUS¶

    The Packet Utilization Standard (PUS) defines services - families of requests and reports identified by a service type and subtype - that a mission picks and tailors. Solar Orbiter's tailoring is written down in its space-ground packet structure ICD, SOL.S.ASTR.TN.00079,1 and implemented, packet by packet, in its SCOS-2000 Mission Information Base. This page reads the two side by side: the pid table gives every telemetry (TM) packet definition with its type and subtype, and the ccf table every telecommand (TC). All counts are from this wiki's queries on the September 2025 release (and, where stated, the April 2020 release), shown in the "SQL used" blocks.3738

    Which PUS: A or C?¶

    Solar Orbiter uses PUS-A, ECSS-E-70-41A of 30 January 2003. The question can be settled from both the documents and the MIB:

    Evidence What it shows
    The packet structure ICD's applicable document AD1 is ECSS-E-70-41A, and all application-level structures are "according to" it the standard is PUS-A34
    TC and TM data field headers carry PUS version 1 (0b001) PUS-A requires version 1; PUS-C requires version 2562125
    The MIB TC header NOM_TC has a fixed 1-bit "Secondary Header Flag" = 0 at bit 48 and a fixed 3-bit "PUS Version" = 1 at bit 49 the PUS-A TC data field header layout, not PUS-C's 4-bit version field3722
    The TC source ID is 8 bits (DFH_SRC at bit 72) and the TM destination ID 8 bits PUS-A field sizes; PUS-C uses 16-bit source and destination IDs37726
    PID_DFHSIZE = 16 for 11 138 of the 11 150 packets: 6 octets of primary header + 10 octets of data field header the ICD's 10-octet TM data field header with optional PUS-A fields376
    Services 4, 7 and 10 are not used; 16 and 20-22 have Solar Orbiter's own meanings consistent with PUS-A's service list, where 7, 10 and 16 are "not used" and 20-22 do not exist224

    The header is taken from the MIB's pcdf table and agrees with the ICD.375 The packet layouts, including the TM header reconstruction, are on PUS packet structures; the differences between the issues are on PUS-A vs PUS-C.

    The ICD forbids non-standard use of standard services and requires user-specific packets to be private services; services are marked mandatory, optional or reserved for a user, and for optional ones the minimum capability sets of ECSS-E-70-41A apply.41 PUS-A itself reserves service types 0-127 for the standard and leaves 128-255 to missions, and within a standard service leaves subtypes 128-255 to missions.23 Solar Orbiter uses both freedoms heavily, as the counts below show.

    How the MIB sees a PUS packet¶

    SCOS-2000 identifies a TM packet by APID, type and subtype plus up to two extra identification fields whose position and width are given per type/subtype (and optionally per APID) in pic; each distinct key gets its own SPID.2930 The Solar Orbiter PIC rules for the standard reports are:37

    Report 1st field (octet, bits) Meaning of the field
    TM(1,2), TM(1,8) 20, 16 failure code, after the 4 octets of TC packet ID and sequence control
    TM(3,25), TM(3,26) 16, 8 structure ID (SID)
    TM(5,1) to TM(5,4) 16, 16 event ID; on virtual APIDs a 2nd field at octet 18 (19 for TM(5,1) on APID 1974)
    TM(6,6) 16, 16 memory ID; on virtual APIDs a 2nd 32-bit field at octet 18
    TM(21,3), TM(21,5), TM(21,6) 16, 8 science structure ID (SSID)
    TM(12,9) on APIDs 1986-1990 19, 8 with a 2nd 8-bit field deep inside the packet

    Octet 16 is the first octet after the 6-octet primary header and the 10-octet data field header. The consequence is that one PUS report type becomes many database packets: every event ID and every failure code has its own SPID, with its own parameter layout. That is why services 1 and 5 account for 8 965 of the 11 150 packet definitions.37

    On the TC side, each command's CCF_ACK sets the acknowledgement flags. The ICD allows only 0000, 0001 (acceptance), 1000 (completion) and 1001 (both);8 the MIB uses 9 (1001) for 5 225 commands, 1 for 78 and 0 for 21 (130 have no value: commands without a PUS header).37 Verification is covered on Command verification in the MIB.

    SQL used (PUS version and identification) 
    -- solo-2025-09-16.sqlite (MIB__20250916M25925PFMS001_SOL)
SELECT * FROM pcdf WHERE PCDF_TCNAME = 'NOM_TC' ORDER BY CAST(PCDF_BIT AS INT);
SELECT PID_DFHSIZE, PID_TIME, count(*) FROM pid GROUP BY 1, 2;
SELECT * FROM pic ORDER BY CAST(PIC_TYPE AS INT), CAST(PIC_STYPE AS INT);
SELECT CCF_ACK, count(*) FROM ccf GROUP BY 1;

    Service by service¶

    Service 1: TC verification¶

    Every on-board unit implements acceptance success and failure (1,1 and 1,2) and execution success and failure (1,7 and 1,8); start and progress reports are not used.18 The CSW applies static acceptance checks to every TC independently of the acknowledgement flags: header constants, a packet length between 5 and 241, the length against the received octets, the CRC, the process ID and the category 12.8 In the MIB, service 1 reports sit on 31 category-1 APIDs: 29 of them have exactly one 1,1 and one 1,7 packet, while the 274 failure-code packets of type 1,2 and 1 477 of type 1,8 are spread over all 31 (SOLOHI's APIDs 1329 and 1345 have failure reports only).37 Failure codes follow Annex 9: standard codes 0 to 7 (illegal APID, length, CRC, type, subtype, application data, segment flags, MAP ID), then ranges per user - SSMM 1000-1999, star trackers 2000-2999, OBCPs 4000-9999, CSW 10000-39999, EPD 40000s, MAG 41000s, RPW 42000s, SWA 43000s, SPICE 44000s, PHI 45000s, EUI 46000s, METIS 47000s, STIX 48000s, SoloHi 49000s.9 The units' codes in the MIB agree: SSMM failure codes run from 1000 to 1090, star tracker codes 2500-2655, EPD 40000-40717, MAG 41100-41822, RPW 42000-42005, SWA 43000-43265, SPICE 44000-44099, PHI 45011-45827, EUI 46700-46800, METIS 47000-47719, STIX 48000-48463 and SOLOHI 49000-49004.37 The CSW (processes 10-16) is the exception: besides codes in its 10000-39999 range it defines codes 256-277 for its own acceptance checks (for example 256 "Illegal CCSDS packet version number") and 175 distinct codes between 509 and 4881, which Annex 9 assigns to the reserved standard range, to spares, to the SSMM, to the star trackers and to OBCPs.379

    Service 2: device command distribution¶

    TC(2,3) carries CPDU commands, TC(2,128) MIL-STD-1553 commands with the TM(2,129) answer, and TC(2,130) a SpaceWire packet.1 Service 2 is by far the largest TC service: 1 257 TC(2,128) bus commands (for example "DEU A Arm Pinpuller") and 105 TC(2,3) CPDU commands, all to the data management APID 172, plus one TC(2,130).37 Apart from the generic "Distribute CPDU commands", the MIB names the TC(2,3) commands "HPC-3 ..." (for example "HPC-3 IMU 1 Channel 1 On"), the pulses that the CSW produces by writing CPDU registers. The ground-to-CPDU HPC-1 commands are a separate set: 125 commands to APID 44 (process 2) with the HPC1_TC header, no secondary header and CCF_MAPID = 0, none of which shares a description with a TC(2,3) command.3712 See ST[02] Device access.

    Service 3: housekeeping and diagnostic reporting¶

    The routine HK report TM(3,25) is mandatory for all units; the CSW also implements the PUS-A definition, clearing, enabling, disabling and reporting subtypes 1-12, a diagnostic report TM(3,26), and private subtypes 128-139 (report definitions in summary form, update periods, collection intervals, one-shot request, adding parameters to a definition, snapshot anomaly report).1 The MIB has 288 TM(3,25) definitions on 62 APIDs and 89 TM(3,26) on 14 APIDs, and 345 service 3 commands.37

    Structure IDs follow a pattern per category. For the CSW, Annex 10 allocates SIDs 1-127 to TM(3,25) and 128-255 to TM(3,26), in blocks (1-10 general status, 11-30 mode, 31-60 equipment, 61-90 specific requests, 101-104 ESOC, 105-127 ancillary data; 128-137, 138-157, ... for diagnostics).11 In the MIB the data management application, for instance, reports 15 SIDs between 1 and 16 on its HPTM APID 162 (category 2), the same reports as SIDs 77-92 on its routine HK APID 164 (category 4), SIDs 153-156 on its functional cyclic APID 165 (category 5) and diagnostic SIDs 193-194 on APID 168 (category 8); the AOCS follows the same pattern (HPTM SIDs between 1 and 21, routine 77-97, functional cyclic 153-159 and 191-192, diagnostic 193-223, 228-229 and 251-255). Every one of the 68 CSW HPTM TM(3,25) definitions has a routine HK counterpart on the APID two higher with a SID 76 higher, and in 67 cases the two share the same description.37

    Inference

    The MIB's CSW SIDs do not follow the Annex 10 blocks: the routine HK SIDs 77-102 run across the "specific data request" (61-90), spare (91-100) and ESOC (101-104) blocks, and the category 5 TM(3,25) SIDs 153-159 and 188-192 fall in the range that Annex 10 reserves for TM(3,26). The offset of 76 between HPTM and routine HK SIDs is this wiki's observation from the data; the rule behind it is not stated in the available sources.

    Service 5: event reporting¶

    Events are the largest TM service: 7 156 definitions on 50 APIDs - 1 374 normal/progress (5,1), 1 159 low severity (5,2), 2 911 medium severity (5,3) and 1 698 high severity (5,4) reports, plus the CSW's private event log subtypes 130, 132 and 134.371 Annex 9 encodes the severity in the two most significant bits of the 16-bit event ID (5,1 from 0x0000, 5,2 from 0x4000, 5,3 from 0x8000, 5,4 from 0xC000) and splits each quarter into boot software, general and specific application, unexpected error, monitoring, OBCP and ESOC ranges.10 All 2 308 CSW event definitions (processes 10-16) obey the severity rule; the instruments' events follow it less consistently (5 499 of all 7 142 events of subtypes 1-4 obey it).37 The busiest event APIDs are the thermal control events (APID 263, 699 definitions), the three EUI event APIDs (620 each) and the AOCS events (APID 183, 436).37

    Service 6: memory management¶

    Load (6,2), dump (6,⅚,6) and check (6,9/6,10) by absolute address are mandatory for all units; private subtypes add pre-loaded RAM patches, abort dump, enabling and disabling the SSMM EEPROM patch, and load with mask.1 The MIB has 289 TM(6,6) dump and 20 TM(6,10) check report definitions and 221 commands; dumps are identified by a 16-bit memory ID at octet 16.37

    Service 8: function management¶

    The CSW's TC(8,1) "Perform Function" is used by 64 commands (for example "1553 Failure Recovery"); private subtypes 140-144 enable or disable functions and their auto-reset and report their status in TM(8,145).371

    Service 9: time management¶

    The OBC keeps the on-board time as CUC with 4 octets of seconds and 2 of sub-seconds from a zero reference of 1 January 2000; TM(9,2) reports it (without data field header, on APID 0), TC(9,1) changes its rate, TC(9,128) sets the OBT, and TC(9,130)/(9,131) start and stop time synchronisation of a user, which makes the CSW send TC(9,129) "accept time update" packets to that user.1314 The 2020 MIB defined 19 service 9 commands, including four TC(9,128) variants and eleven TC(9,129) commands addressed to the SSMM and the instruments; the 2025 release keeps only TC(9,1), two TC(9,130) and one TC(9,131).3837

    Inference

    Since the ICD says that TC(9,129) is generated by the CSW, not sent from the ground, its removal from the ground command database is consistent with its definition; why the TC(9,128) "Set OBT" commands were also removed is not stated in the available sources.

    Time correlation itself is done on ground with the MCS packets TM(164,1) to TM(164,3) on APID 1966, described on Solar Orbiter and Time correlation in SCOS-2000.31

    Service 11: on-board operations scheduling¶

    The CSW implements the PUS-A time-based schedule almost completely: enable, disable and reset release, insert, delete (also over a time period), time-shift, detailed and summary reports (9-17) and schedule status 18/19.1 The MIB adds a private TC(11,132) "Insert Backup MTL Telecommands" (two commands to APID 172), which Table 5.1-1 of the ICD does not list.371 The insert command ZCD00B04 (explorer) is dissected on PUS packet structures and in the TC worked example.

    Service 12: on-board monitoring¶

    Enable, disable, change maximum report delay, clear, add, delete, modify and report the monitoring list, and report the out-of-limit list, with the check transition report TM(12,12).1 The 254 commands are dominated by TC(12,5) "add parameter" (114 variants by parameter type) and TC(12,7) "modify checking information" (83).37 The 32 TM definitions include 19 TM(12,9) monitoring list reports, 13 of them on the virtual APIDs 1986-1990 that the ground uses to decode each CSW application's list (see The Solar Orbiter MIB).37 Ground and on-board checks are compared on Monitoring checks.

    Service 13: large data transfer¶

    Solar Orbiter's service 13 uplinks files from the ground to the CSW (first, intermediate and last parts, abort, acknowledgement, unsuccessful part and abort reports, TC(13,9) to TM(13,16)) and downlinks files from the SSMM with a private protocol of file transfer sessions and "low-level file transfer" (LLFT) subtypes 128-153.115 Each uplinked file has an attribute: 1 OBCP, 2 TC sequence for delayed execution, 3 TC sequence for immediate execution, 6 other and 7 internal, with 4 and 5 unused.15 The MIB has 28 service 13 commands on APIDs 172 and 492 (SSMM) and 9 report definitions.37 Downlinked files reach users as "DLPP" TM files in the file archive.32 The MIB's mission file s13a.dat lists the attribute values 0 PLAIN, 1 OBCP, 2 DELAYED TC, 3 IMMEDIATE TC, 4 PAYLOAD CONTEXT, 5 INVALID and 6 OTHER, so it gives meanings to 0, 4 and 5, which the ICD leaves undefined or marks unused, and has no entry for the ICD's 7.3715 Service 13 is a PUS-A large data transfer, not the PUS-C ST[13] or ST[23].

    Service 14: packet forwarding control¶

    Twelve commands enable and disable the forwarding of TM source packets, housekeeping, diagnostic and event packets, and report the forwarding status (TM(14,8), (14,12), (14,129), (14,131)).137

    Service 15: on-board storage and retrieval¶

    The CSW and the SSMM both implement service 15, with the SSMM adding creation, resizing, deletion, renaming, listing and copying of packet stores, cyclic/non-cyclic modes, default virtual channel and priority, and the CSW adding SID-based storage selection and the storage routing table - subtypes 128 to 156.1 All 47 commands go to APIDs 172 and 492.37 The E-FECS notes that resizing SSMM packet stores loses data unless the stores are empty.36 See ST[15].

    Service 16: on-board traffic management¶

    A Solar Orbiter service in a type number that PUS-A marks "not used": five commands set the MIL-STD-1553 bus configuration and switch buses or remote-terminal channels between nominal and redundant; the ICD says they are not meant for nominal flight operations but for investigation or recovery after bus FDIR.162437

    Service 17: test¶

    TC(17,1) and the TM(17,2) connection test report are mandatory for all units; the CSW adds TC(17,3) to request a connection test of another unit, and TC(17,128) is a test command of maximum length.1 18 units have a TC(17,1) in the MIB and 17 have a TM(17,2).37

    Service 18: on-board control procedures¶

    Suspend, resume, communicate parameters, report the OBCP list and private load-and-start, stop-and-delete and OBCP HK subtypes; TM(18,144) carries OBCP telemetry.1 The 38 TC(18,140) "Load and start OBCP" variants account for most of the 46 commands, which grew from 32 in 2020.3738 How the ICD's OBCP description lines up with the OBCP standard is on ECSS-E-ST-70-01C.

    Service 19: event-action¶

    Add, delete and clear event detection entries, enable and disable actions, and report the list or a single entry (TC(19,130)/TM(19,131)).1 The 48 commands are on three APIDs, with 25 TC(19,1) "Add Event" variants.37

    Service 20: information distribution¶

    Not PUS-C's parameter management: Solar Orbiter's service 20 distributes inter-instrument data and the OBC heartbeat. TC(20,1) and TC(20,2) start and stop distribution to a user, and the single TC(20,128) carries all the data.1734 The MIB has the CSW's TC(20,1) per instrument (ten "Start Information Distribution ..." commands to APID 172, plus a generic "... for User" one) and one TC(20,2), and five TC(20,128) definitions on APID 796 - process 49, "payload broadcast" in Annex 8 - for EPD, MAG, PHI and STIX.3720 The instruments' contributions arrive as category 5 TM(3,25) packets, compared with the 2016 agreement below.

    Inference

    The differing process IDs and SIDs suggest that the IIC agreements were revised after issue 2.0 of the note; neither the revision nor its reason is in the available sources.

    Service 21: science data transfer¶

    A Solar Orbiter service for moving instrument science to the SSMM: TC(21,1) and TC(21,2) start and stop the transfer, TC(21,128) resets the output buffer, and the data come in TM(21,3) (fixed length with SSID), TM(21,4) (fixed, no SSID), TM(21,5) (variable stream with SSID) or TM(21,6) (variable with structure and SSID).18 The MIB has 105 TM(21,3), 72 TM(21,5) and 151 TM(21,6) definitions on 45 science APIDs (category 12), and 6 TM(21,7) definitions for SPICE, a subtype that the ICD does not define.37

    Service 22: context saving¶

    Instruments save their operational context as a file in the OBC mass memory (OMM), where the ground can also reach it with service 133, and get it back on request: the ground asks the CSW to save a context (22,5), the CSW requests it from the instrument (22,1), which answers with a context report (22,2) within 5 seconds; on a retrieval request (22,4) the CSW sends the context back in up to 18 TC(22,3) segments.19 Only STIX's context report remains in 2025; PHI's TM(22,2) on APID 1163 was removed after 2020.3738

    Inference

    Several Solar Orbiter private services do jobs that PUS-C later standardised: 139 data-pool management resembles PUS-C ST[20], 133 file management ST[23], 134 TC sequencer ST[21], and 142 functional monitoring the functional monitoring subservice of PUS-C ST[12].28 The similarity is functional; the packet layouts are Solar Orbiter's own and are not claimed to match PUS-C. Type 251 is used both by ground packets (on APIDs 1964 and 1966, process 122 in the EGSE range) and, according to the ICD, by SPICE; since SPICE's process IDs are 85-89 and 101-104, the two uses do not collide in practice (and SPICE's MIB entries use only type 255).

 GROUP BY p;

  FROM pid WHERE PID_TYPE = '5' AND PID_STYPE IN ('1','2','3','4')
   AND (CAST(PID_APID AS INT) >> 4) BETWEEN 10 AND 16 GROUP BY 1;
