    Missions¶

    The standards and systems described elsewhere in this wiki - CCSDS packets and frames, the PUS, SCOS-2000 and its Mission Information Base - only become concrete in a mission. This section gathers what the public record says about individual ESA missions: which documents exist, which data could be copied, and what they reveal about how each mission used the standards. Every statement is tied to a document in the library, and every library entry records where the file was copied from and when.

    Three missions are documented in depth, because their interface documents (and, for one of them, their real database) are publicly posted:

    • Solar Orbiter - the only mission whose operational MIB is publicly available as data; this wiki analyses two releases with SQL and lets you browse every parameter, packet and command in the MIB explorer. The databases of two of its instruments are also publicly posted, analysed on SWA and STIX, and the instrument-level interface documents are on Solar Orbiter instrument interfaces.
    • Herschel and Planck - a joint project that put SCOS-2000 into the MOC, the central checkout and the instrument EGSE, with a well-documented system database (HPSDB) and MIB tailoring notes.
    • Rosetta, Mars Express and Venus Express - ESOC's interplanetary mission family, with a shared mission control system and the data delivery, flight dynamics and ground station interface documents that went with it.

    A fourth page, Other missions and SCOS-2000 users, collects what the sources say about everything else: the older control systems, INTEGRAL, XMM-Newton, PROBA, the Earth Explorers, Galileo, the Sentinels, BepiColombo, JUICE and the move to EGS-CC.

    Mission families and SCOS-2000 generations¶

    ESOC builds mission control systems in layers: generic infrastructure (SCOS-2000), a mission family kernel shared by similar missions, and a thin mission-specific layer specified only as a "delta".3132 The families named in the sources are interplanetary (Rosetta, Mars Express, Venus Express, with BepiColombo and Solar Orbiter expected to join), Earth observation (CryoSat, GOCE, Aeolus and later missions such as Swarm), observatory and navigation; the sources name members only for the first two.3132

    flowchart TB
  S2K["SCOS-2000 infrastructure (EGOS / MICONYS)"]
  S2K --> IP["Interplanetary family kernel"]
  S2K --> EE["Earth Explorer mission kernel"]
  S2K --> OTHER["Missions on SCOS-2000 without a named family kernel<br/>(e.g. INTEGRAL, Herschel/Planck MCS)"]
  IP --> ROS["Rosetta"]
  IP --> MEX["Mars Express"]
  IP --> VEX["Venus Express"]
  IP -.-> BC["BepiColombo (expected to join, 2007 source)"]
  IP -.-> SO["Solar Orbiter (expected to join, 2007 source)"]
  EE --> CRY["CryoSat"]
  EE --> GOCE["GOCE"]
  EE --> AEO["Aeolus"]
  EE -.-> SW["Swarm (future mission, 2005 source)"]
  S2K ==> CC["EGS-CC (from 2026; Swarm pilot, Ariel first launch)"]

    The solid lines are memberships stated in the 2005 and 2007 sources, the dotted lines are missions those sources expected to join; the EGS-CC arrow reflects the 2025 SpaceOps paper.313230 Placing INTEGRAL and the Herschel/Planck MCS outside a named family is this wiki's simplification, because the sources do not assign them to one.

    Sharing a control-system kernel is distinct from sharing spacecraft hardware. For hardware, the 2007 Solar Orbiter Experiment Interface Document retained commonality with BepiColombo, especially for the high-gain antenna and the solar array, said the attitude control system and avionics might also be common, and considered re-using elements from Herschel-Planck, Mars Express or Venus Express.37

    Where the missions show up in the standards pages¶

    The mission pages are the best place to see standards in use. Some cross-references:

    • Packet structures: Solar Orbiter's APID split into process ID and category (Solar Orbiter), the Herschel test-equipment packet headers with a 3-bit command source in the sequence count (Herschel and Planck), and a decoded Rosetta telecommand with a verified CRC (Rosetta, MEX, VEX); background on Space Packet and PUS packet structures.
    • PUS service selection: the Herschel/Planck table of mandatory, optional and not applicable services and How Solar Orbiter uses PUS; see the PUS message type catalogue.
    • Instrument-level definitions: the Solar Orbiter packet ICD allocates private service ranges to each instrument (for example 200-205 to SWA and 236-240 to STIX); the SWA DPU ICD and the SWA and STIX instrument databases show how one instrument fills its range and how its definitions reappear in the spacecraft MIB - see SWA, STIX and instrument interfaces.3938
    • Data delivery: GDDS for Rosetta/MEX/VEX, EDDS for Solar Orbiter - compare GDDS and mission DDIDs and EDDS.
    • Time correlation: the Rosetta/MEX/VEX coefficient packets and time quality flags, and the Solar Orbiter time stamps - see Time correlation in SCOS-2000.
    • MIB tailoring and heritage: Herschel's HPSDB rules and repatriated CCS tables, the Rosetta/MEX MIB extensions and CryoSat's command parameter types, all visible in the Solar Orbiter release - see Mission tailoring of the MIB and History of the Database Import ICD.

    What is not covered¶

    • The mission identifiers that other missions (for example BepiColombo or JUICE) use in the SCOS-2000 packet header are not stated in the available sources; for Solar Orbiter the spacecraft and mission ID is 650.3
    • No MIB of any mission other than Solar Orbiter (and its SWA and STIX instruments) is in the library; the databases of the other eight Solar Orbiter instruments are not in it either. The Herschel/Planck MIB releases (MOC issues 2 to 13, 2009-2011) are listed online but could not be copied.33
    • Launch dates, mission status and science results are mentioned only where the cited documents state them.
