    TM synchronization and channel coding (CCSDS 131.0-B-5)¶

    TM Synchronization and Channel Coding is the CCSDS standard for the lower sublayer of the Data Link Layer on telemetry links. It takes the fixed-length Transfer Frames produced by the TM, AOS or USLP Space Data Link Protocol and turns them into a continuous stream of channel symbols that survives a weak, noisy radio link: it marks where each frame starts (the Attached Sync Marker), optionally protects the frame with an error-correcting code, and scrambles the bits with a pseudo-randomizer so that the receiver can stay locked.25 The ECSS communications handbook quantifies what is at stake: depending on the scheme, coding lowers the \(E_b/N_0\) needed for a frame error rate of 10⁻⁴ by roughly 4 to 12 dB.69

    Role of the sublayer¶

    The sublayer performs three functions: error-control coding (including the frame validation that tells the upper layer whether a decoded frame can be trusted), synchronization (delimiting frames or codeblocks with a marker) and pseudo-randomizing (guaranteeing bit transitions and a clean spectrum).6 The TM SDLP assumes exactly these services from below, and requires that all frames of a Physical Channel use the same coding options so that the receiver can decode them.70

    The order of operations is fixed. At the sending end, block codes come first, then randomization, then the marker, and convolutional coding (or slicing into LDPC codeblocks) is last because it works on a continuous stream; the receiver undoes the steps in reverse.732

    The service¶

    The sublayer offers a single service: one-way transfer of fixed-length frames at a constant frame rate on a Physical Channel, with optional error detection and correction, and only one user per Physical Channel. Its primitives are ChannelAccess.request (Frame) at the sending end and ChannelAccess.indication (Frame, Quality Indicator, Sequence Indicator) at the receiving end. The Quality Indicator flags an uncorrectable error in the frame; the Sequence Indicator flags that frames were lost because frame synchronization was lost.38

    History and the ECSS view¶

    Issue 5 (September 2023) revises the pseudo-randomizer to avoid spectral spikes on high-rate links: a 131071-bit sequence becomes the standard one and the old 255-bit sequence remains only for backward compatibility.134 Issue 6 (April 2026) reorganizes the standard around optional Transfer Frame slicing, adds a channel interleaver for turbo codewords and makes the randomizer mandatory. The rest of this page follows Issue 5; Issue 6 is summarized in its own section at the end.84

    ECSS originally published its own subset as ECSS-E-ST-50-01, which kept fewer options (Reed-Solomon E=8 only with 4D-8PSK-TCM and interleaving depth 8; turbo only at rates ½ and ¼).67106107 ECSS-E-ST-50C Rev.1 (2021) lists ECSS-E-AS-50-21, an adoption notice of the CCSDS Blue Book itself, as superseding ECSS-E-ST-50-01C. Rev.1 of the notice (13 January 2023) adopted CCSDS 131.0-B-4, and Rev.2 (5 December 2024) adopts 131.0-B-5, the issue this page follows.7475116 Whether ECSS has adopted 131.0-B-6 is not stated in the available sources. The ECSS texts are compared below; see also ECSS-E-ST-50C communications.

    Why code at all¶

    Channel coding lets data cross a noisy channel so that the possible messages stay distinguishable: for the same error rate it needs less energy per information bit, or for the same energy it gives a lower error rate.40 The Green Book adds three operational benefits: compressed data, which is far more sensitive to errors, becomes usable; adaptive (self-identifying) telemetry becomes safe; and anomalies in the decoded data can be trusted to be real, which enables more unattended operations.40

    Measuring performance¶

    Codes are compared on the Additive White Gaussian Noise (AWGN) channel as a function of the bit-energy-to-noise ratio \(E_b/N_0\). Three error rates are used: the Bit Error Rate (BER), the Word Error Rate (WER, per codeword) and the Frame Error Rate (FER). Only for independent errors does \(\text{FER} = 1-(1-\text{BER})^L\) hold for an \(L\)-bit frame, which is true for uncoded data but not after any of the recommended codes, because decoders make errors in bursts.41

    Shannon's capacity limits bound what any code can do. For a binary-input AWGN channel the low-BER asymptotes are about 0.2 dB for rate ½, −0.5 dB for ⅓, −0.8 dB for ¼ and −1.1 dB for ⅙, against −1.6 dB for rate → 0. Finite block lengths cost more: 1000-bit blocks are about 1.3 dB better than 100-bit blocks, 10,000-bit blocks just over 0.5 dB better again, and 100,000-bit blocks another 0.2 dB.42 These numbers explain why the newer codes use large blocks.

    Frame synchronization: the Attached Sync Marker¶

    CADU and ASM placement¶

    The Attached Sync Marker (ASM) is a known bit pattern placed immediately before every frame, codeblock or codeword, with no gap after the preceding one. The ASM plus what follows it is the Channel Access Data Unit (CADU):282930

    Coding scheme CADU = ASM + … ASM attached to
    None, or convolutional only the Transfer Frame each Transfer Frame
    Reed-Solomon, or concatenated the Reed-Solomon codeblock each codeblock
    Turbo the turbo codeword each codeword
    LDPC applied to a Transfer Frame the LDPC codeword each codeword
    LDPC applied to a stream of SMTFs (CSM and the LDPC codeblock) each Transfer Frame (ASM), each codeblock (CSM)

    The ASM is never fed into the Reed-Solomon, turbo or LDPC encoder; it is attached to the encoded block. For Reed-Solomon this prevents the encoder from regenerating a copy of the marker in the check symbols for certain repetitive data, which would confuse the frame synchronizer.30 With convolutional coding, the ASM is convolutionally encoded together with the frame; in the concatenated scheme it is encoded by the inner code but not by the outer code.29 The ASM is not part of the Transfer Frame and does not count in its length.3566

    Where the marker can be found¶

    The domain in which the receiver looks for the ASM depends on the code:28

    Code ASM acquired in
    Basic (7,½) convolutional, alone or concatenated channel symbols (before decoding) or decoded bits
    Punctured convolutional, alone or concatenated decoded bits only
    Turbo channel symbols only
    LDPC applied to frames channel symbols only
    LDPC applied to a stream of SMTFs decoded bits (codeblocks are first found with the CSM)

    For the rate-½ convolutional code, the 52-symbol invariant part of the encoded marker lets the receiver establish node synchronization and frame synchronization in one step. A turbo decoder cannot find a marker in the decoded-bit domain because each constituent decoder is too weak and the iterations need the codeword boundaries first; channel-symbol-domain synchronization in turn needs longer markers and faster processing.62

    How frame synchronizers work¶

    The Green Book describes two families of frame synchronizer:63

    stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Search
    Search: SEARCH - look for the ASM at every bit offset
    Search --> Check: tentative match
    Check: CHECK - confirm ASM at the expected position
    Check --> Search: ASM missing
    Check --> Lock: N consecutive confirmations
    Lock: LOCK - frames delivered
    Lock --> Flywheel: ASM missed
    Flywheel: FLYWHEEL - keep delivering, count misses
    Flywheel --> Lock: ASM found again
    Flywheel --> Search: M consecutive misses (loss of lock)
    • State-machine synchronizers search expensively for a marker, verify a number of subsequent markers before declaring lock, and tolerate a number of missed markers ("flywheel") before going back to search. They are cheap once locked, but a symbol slip costs several frames.63
    • Argmax synchronizers continuously score every possible offset once per frame interval and pick the best; they have no lock state. Massey showed that the optimal metric is not plain soft correlation; a simple approximation counts only symbols with the wrong sign, weighted by their magnitude. Using two consecutive markers improves performance further.63
    • For BPSK the synchronizer can run for both polarities to resolve the 180° ambiguity; QPSK and higher orders need four or more hypotheses.63

    With a single 64-symbol marker, soft or hard correlation limits a rate-½ LDPC link at FERs below about 10⁻³, which is why the better metrics matter.63 The N and M thresholds in the diagram are implementation choices, not values from the standard.

    The ASM as a time reference¶

    Because the leading edge of the first ASM bit is a precise, recognizable instant, the ESA Packet Telemetry Standard (as quoted in the Rosetta and Mars Express interface document) uses it for time correlation: the on-board clock is sampled when the ASM of a specific VC 0 frame is sent and the ground station time-tags the reception of the same bit.78 SCOS-2000 defines frame radiation time, frame reception time and the ground delay with respect to that edge.77 See time correlation.

    Pseudo-randomization¶

    Why randomize¶

    Every receiver needs a data stream that is random enough: a sufficient density of bit transitions for the bit synchronizer, no strong spectral lines, and no patterns that upset codeword synchronization and frame validation.32 None of the codes guarantees this alone. The inverter on one output of the basic convolutional code helps with BPSK but not with QPSK multiplexing or punctured codes; Reed-Solomon and LDPC codes, being quasi-cyclic, can also decode wrongly after false codeblock synchronization if the data are not randomized.81123 CCSDS therefore makes the randomizer required unless the system designer verifies that the system works without it, and both CCSDS and ECSS record that missions have had telemetry problems because they skipped it.3268109 Issue 6 removes the escape clause: the randomizer becomes mandatory with every coding option and Absent disappears from its managed parameter (see Issue 6).9799 ECSS-E-ST-50-01C (2008) had made presence or absence a managed property of the physical channel, never signalled in the telemetry. It also warns that a Reed-Solomon decoder fed with randomized data that was not derandomized, or the reverse, can perform meaningless corrections and deliver the frame as valid, in particular with I = 5.109105

    How it works¶

    Each bit of the codeblock, codeword or frame (not the ASM) is exclusive-ORed with a standard pseudo-random sequence. The generator restarts at the beginning of every codeblock, codeword or frame, during the ASM, so the ASM also synchronizes the randomizer. On the receiving end, derandomization is the same exclusive-OR on hard bits, or a sign inversion of soft symbols.3233 The randomizer is applied after Reed-Solomon, turbo or LDPC encoding and before convolutional encoding, and its presence is a managed parameter of the Physical Channel, never signalled in the data.32

    Sequence Polynomial Seed First 40 bits
    131071 bits (default since B-5) \(h(x) = x^{17} + x^{14} + 1\) 11000111000111000 0001 1100 0111 0001 1011 1001 0001 1011 1010 1001
    255 bits (legacy, backward compatibility) \(h(x) = x^8 + x^7 + x^5 + x^3 + 1\) all ones 1111 1111 0100 1000 0000 1110 1100 0000 1001 1010

    Both rows restate §10.4.34 The seed of the long sequence was chosen for performance and to avoid a long run of ones at the start; the short sequence may cause spectral lines at 1/255 of the symbol rate and may not guarantee compliance with ITU power-flux-density limits.34 For LDPC coding of an SMTF stream, the whole LDPC codeblock (after encoding) is randomized from its first bit, without reset at codeword boundaries, and the CSM is not randomized.27

    Why the randomizer changed

    The Green Book (2020) explains the problem: with the 255-bit randomizer and a QPSK link, the transmitted sequence repeats every 510 bits, producing spurious lines spaced by \(R_b / 510\). Keeping that spacing below the 4 kHz ITU resolution bandwidth limits the bit rate to about 2.04 Mb/s. Idle frames filled with zeros make it worse, since the randomizer output then dominates the transmitted signal. It discusses a 15-cell LFSR (period 32767) and filling idle frame data fields with a free-running pseudo-noise sequence.60 ECSS-E-ST-50-01C and the ECSS handbook had already flagged high-rate links above about 2 megasymbols per second as a problem with no agreed ECSS solution.10968 CCSDS 132.0-B-3 (2021) now mandates PN-filled OID frames, and 131.0-B-5 (2023) the 131071-bit sequence; the 2024 ECSS adoption notice records that the long sequence replaces the 255-bit one of ECSS-E-ST-50-01 unless legacy compatibility requires otherwise.711119

    The Green Book also notes that integration and test teams may like to see un-randomized data, and suggests a randomizer that can be switched off during early testing.61

    Decoding maximum likelihood (Viterbi); soft decisions with at least 3-bit quantization recommended

    The values are those of §3.3 and §3.2.4; the memory and state count follow from \(K = m + 1\) and \(2^m\) states.9844 With suppressed-carrier modulation, NRZ-L or NRZ-M may be used; if differential encoding (NRZ-L to NRZ-M) is wanted, it should be done at the encoder input, because differential encoding after a convolutional encoder costs considerable performance.965 The ASM is always inserted before convolutional encoding, and the FECF must validate the frame unless an outer Reed-Solomon code is used, because a Viterbi decoder whose correction capability is exceeded can emit undetected error bursts.8

    Rationale. An exhaustive search in the 1970s showed that, among rate-½ codes with \(K \le 7\), only this code (and symmetric equivalents) reaches a free distance \(d_{free} = 10\); the best \(K = 6\) code reaches 8 and \(K = 8\) cannot do better than 10. Since Viterbi decoding effort doubles with every unit of \(K\), \(K = 7\) was the sweet spot of the technology of the time.44 The code is transparent: inverting the input inverts the output, so a BPSK receiver locked 180° out of phase simply produces inverted bits, which known headers reveal. The \(G_2\) inverter bounds the number of consecutive symbols without a transition to 14. ESA, NASA-GSFC and NASA-JPL historically used different parity orders or inversions; CCSDS kept one convention.44

    Decoding. The Viterbi algorithm keeps one survivor path per state (\(2^{K-1} = 64\)) and has a cost linear in the number of bits.45 With 8-bit soft quantization it is within 0.2 dB of unquantized performance, while hard decisions lose more than 2 dB. The usual rule of thumb is a truncation (decision) length of about \(5K\); for rate ½, 30 bits (five times the memory \(m\)) already costs very little and 60 bits is nearly optimal.46

    Reed-Solomon coding¶

    Code parameters¶

    Parameter Value
    Symbol size \(J\) 8 bits
    Error-correction capability \(E\) 16 or 8 symbols per codeword

    The values restate §4.3.121315 The code is systematic: the \(kI\) information symbols pass unchanged and \(2EI\) check symbols are appended.1216

    Rationale. A Reed-Solomon code with minimum distance \(d = n - k + 1\) corrects any \(E = (n-k)/2\) symbol errors, regardless of how many bits inside a symbol are wrong, so it is excellent against bursts and poor on its own against random AWGN errors.47 The 8-bit symbol matches octet-based frames and the decoder technology; \(E = 16\) was chosen because it performed best when concatenated with the (7,½) code, and \(E = 8\) was added later for a higher rate. The generator polynomials are palindromes, which halves the multipliers in the encoder, and the dual-basis representation further simplifies the hardware.47 The parity overhead of (255,223) is about 15 %.47

    Decoding. The Reed-Solomon decoder is a bounded-distance decoder. Three outcomes are possible: at most \(E\) symbol errors are corrected; more errors are usually detected (the decoder knows it failed and can flag the frame); rarely, the received word falls within distance \(E\) of another codeword and is decoded wrongly. For (255,223) this last probability is of the order of \(1/E!\), about \(5 \times 10^{-14}\), which is why the Reed-Solomon decoder alone can validate frames; for \(E = 8\) the margin is much smaller.4964 The ECSS handbook quotes decoder-error probabilities of about 10⁻¹³ for \(E = 16\) and 10⁻⁵ for \(E = 8\), the reason ECSS restricted \(E = 8\) to 4D-8PSK-TCM links with \(I = 8\).67 Decoders can also accept erasures, which count half as much as errors (\(2t + e \le 2E\)).49

    Interleaving¶

    With interleaving depth \(I\), \(I\) codewords are interleaved symbol by symbol: the symbol stream entering the encoder is distributed round-robin to \(I\) encoders, and the check symbols are emitted the same way. A burst of channel errors is then spread across \(I\) codewords instead of overwhelming one.1648 For \(I = 2\), the transmitted order is: symbol 1 of codeword 1, symbol 1 of codeword 2, symbol 2 of codeword 1, symbol 2 of codeword 2, and so on, followed by the interleaved check symbols. The information symbols still appear in their original order, so the code remains systematic at codeblock level.16

    Codeblock, virtual fill and frame lengths¶

    The transmitted codeblock is the Transfer Frame followed by the \(2EI\) check symbols, preceded by the 32-bit ASM. A frame shorter than \(kI\) symbols is handled by virtual fill: \(Q\) leading zero symbols are assumed by both encoder and decoder but never transmitted.1314 Virtual fill must be all zeros, fixed for the Mission Phase, inserted only at the start of the codeblock and only in multiples of \(I\) symbols (\(8I\) bits).14 The allowed frame lengths in octets are therefore

    Concatenated coding¶

    The concatenated scheme uses a Reed-Solomon code as the outer code and a convolutional code as the inner code.17 It works because of the way Viterbi decoders fail: when the decoder leaves the correct trellis path it produces a burst of errors from a few bits to several constraint lengths long, with a little over half the bits wrong. Three or four such errors typically fall into one 8-bit Reed-Solomon symbol, which the outer code corrects as a single symbol error, and moderate interleaving spreads the longer bursts over several codewords.50 An interleaving depth of 5 already gives near-ideal performance.51

    The ASM is encoded by the inner convolutional code but not by the outer code.29 Frame lengths are the same as for Reed-Solomon alone.35 The Green Book adds a practical warning: some commercial Viterbi decoders, because of their node-synchronization thresholds, cannot work below about \(E_b/N_0\) = 4 dB at rate ½, which must be budgeted as an implementation loss.50

    Performance and caveats¶

    • With 10 decoder iterations, a BER of 10⁻⁶ needs about −0.1 dB, 0.15 dB, 0.4 dB and 1.1 dB of \(E_b/N_0\) for rates ⅙, ¼, ⅓ and ½; the same thresholds give a FER of about 10⁻⁴. FER equals WER here because one codeword carries exactly one frame.53
    • At 8920 bits and BER 10⁻⁶, the corresponding turbo codes improve on the DSN's concatenated codes by 0.9 to 1.6 dB.53
    • Decoding is iterative, with two soft-input soft-output APP decoders exchanging extrinsic information; the reference curves assume at least 6-bit channel symbols and 8-bit metrics. Stopping rules raise average decoder speed by 50 to 100 % at almost no cost in \(E_b/N_0\). Latency is at least one block.5253
    • Turbo curves flatten at an error floor; the CCSDS codes were designed to push it down to perhaps 10⁻⁹ BER, against about 10⁻⁵ for the original turbo codes.54
    • Turbo codes are vulnerable to burst noise (for example a tumbling spacecraft or RF interference), a weakness inherited from the convolutional components; a channel interleaver would help, and Issue 5 specifies none. Issue 6 (2026) adds an optional row-column channel interleaver of depth 4 for exactly this reason (see Turbo channel interleaver).5594
    • The codes are non-transparent, so differential encoding cannot be used and phase ambiguity must be resolved (with the ASM) before decoding.22
    • A wide class of turbo codes is covered by a patent of France Télécom and Télédiffusion de France; the standard notes that the US patent (5,446,747) expired in 2012.4

    ECSS-E-ST-50-01 kept only rates ½ and ¼: rate ⅙ gains only about 0.2 dB over ¼ at FER 10⁻⁴ while expanding the bandwidth greatly, and rate ⅓ sits between the two.67107 The 2024 adoption notice lists the extra rates only as an informative difference from the superseded standard and contains no amendment restricting them.118119 Turbo codes suit power-limited links beyond low Earth orbit; the ECSS handbook notes that real-time turbo decoding was hard above about 20 Mbit/s at the time.3769

    LDPC codes¶

    Low-Density Parity-Check codes are defined by a sparse parity-check matrix and decoded iteratively by belief propagation.2359 CCSDS specifies two applications: coding each Transfer Frame (section 7) and coding a stream of sync-marked frames (section 8).

    The rate-223/255 code "C2"¶

    The (8160,7136) code, of rate about ⅞, is derived from a basic (8176,7156) code whose parity-check matrix is a 2 × 16 array of 511 × 511 circulants of row weight 2 (a regular (4,32) code, rank 1020). It is expurgated to (8176,7154), shortened by 18 virtual-fill zeros and extended by 2 zero bits:2458

    Part 18 virtual fill zeros 7136 information bits 1022 parity bits 2 zero fill bits
    Transmitted no yes yes yes

    The 7136-bit information length is a 892-octet Transfer Frame, the same as the (255,223) Reed-Solomon code with \(I = 4\), and all parameters are multiples of 32. It is used with the 32-bit ASM, and the only allowed frame length is 892 octets.242335 The Green Book describes it as the near-Earth code: very high rate, fast convergence and limited complexity for data rates of hundreds of Mb/s in a 375 MHz band.56

    The AR4JA family (rates ½, ⅔, ⅘)¶

    Nine codes from the AR4JA (Accumulate, Repeat-by-4, and Jagged Accumulate) family target deep space.56 Their parity-check matrices are built from \(M \times M\) permutation submatrices, the last \(M\) code symbols are punctured, and they use the 64-bit ASM.2523

    1024 128 2048 (512) 1536 (256) 1280 (128)
    4096 512 8192 (2048) 6144 (1024) 5120 (512)

    The values restate Tables 7-2 and 7-5.2535 The design choices are explained in the Green Book: the four rates (including C2) are spaced by about 1 dB on the capacity curve; the block lengths by about 0.6 dB on the sphere-packing bound; keeping \(k\) constant across rates lets the on-board data handling build frames without knowing the code rate; and the irregular degree distribution gains about 0.5 dB at rate ½.56

    Good belief-propagation implementations perform within about 0.1 dB of the published curves in the waterfall region, but error floors vary between decoders. The rate-⅔ and rate-⅘ codes with \(k = 1024\) can show a floor near FER 10⁻⁶ unless the decoder uses more than 50 iterations, at least 8-bit messages and careful clipping.59 The ½, ⅔ and ⅘ codes are covered by a US patent held by Caltech.4

    LDPC coding of a stream of SMTFs¶

    Section 8 decouples the frame length from the code: an ASM is attached to every Transfer Frame, the resulting Sync-Marked Transfer Frames (SMTFs) are concatenated into a stream, the stream is sliced into \(k\)-bit information blocks, each is encoded, \(m\) codewords (1 to 8) form a codeblock, the codeblock is randomized, and a Code Sync Marker (CSM) is prepended.2636

If a codeword fails to decode, the Quality Indicator of every frame it touched is set.2627 The maximum TM frame length is then 2048 octets, as for uncoded data.35 Section 13 prescribes this option for AOS or USLP ground-to-space links that use an SMTF stream.37 Issue 6 replaces this section with a generic slicing procedure for Reed-Solomon, turbo and LDPC codes and drops the multi-codeword LDPC codeblock (see Transfer Frame slicing).89

    Ground-to-space and space-to-space use¶

    The codes were designed for telemetry, but AOS and USLP can also be used upwards and between spacecraft. For such links CCSDS allows any of the turbo codes (best for power-limited links beyond low Earth orbit, rates ≤ ½), any of the LDPC codes of section 7 (best for high-rate, bandwidth-limited links with at least two code symbols per modulation symbol, such as QPSK), or the SMTF-stream LDPC option. Low-rate turbo codes near threshold can put the symbol SNR below −5 dB, which demands unusually narrow tracking loops.37 The telecommand link itself uses the different TC coding and CLTU standard.

    Transparency and bit remappings¶

    Inverting the received symbols (a 180° phase error with BPSK) inverts the output of a transparent code. The convolutional and Reed-Solomon codes are transparent (Reed-Solomon only without virtual fill), the turbo and LDPC codes are not, so their phase ambiguity must be resolved before decoding, typically with the ASM.34447182223 The optional NRZ-L to NRZ-M conversion is a differential precoding that resolves the ambiguity for transparent codes; placed at the output of a convolutional encoder it costs a lot of performance, which is why it is only allowed before the encoder (or, the Green Book notes, between the outer and inner codes, which the Blue Book does not clearly permit).65

    Transfer Frame slicing (Issue 6)¶

    Most block codes accept only a few information block lengths, which can force awkward frame lengths on the rest of the system. Slicing removes that coupling: a 32-bit ASM 1ACFFC1D is prepended to each M-bit frame, the resulting stream of (M + 32)-bit SMTFs is cut into k-bit information blocks, and each block is encoded (for Reed-Solomon, I blocks are encoded and interleaved into one codeblock), randomized and given a CSM. Slicing also allows frames longer than the largest information block and reduces the overhead when long frames are used, at the price of a second level of synchronization. It is optional, is managed and fixed for a mission phase, is allowed with Reed-Solomon, turbo and LDPC coding, and must not be used with uncoded data or with a convolutional code alone.8789 The frame ASM can share the CSM's bit pattern because it is randomized inside the codeword and so cannot be mistaken for it.89

    The receiver reverses the steps: it finds the CSMs and extracts codewords or codeblocks, derandomizes and decodes them, concatenates the decoded information blocks back into an SMTF stream, and finally finds the ASMs to cut out the frames. Without slicing there is one level of synchronization and the decoded information blocks are the frames.89 Frame validation follows the code: with no coding, convolutional or turbo coding the FECF is mandatory; with Reed-Solomon, concatenated or LDPC coding the decoder may validate and the FECF is optional. With slicing, a frame is invalid if any part of it lies in an invalid information block.90

    Worked example: a 2048-octet TM frame on a rate-½ turbo code (illustrative, computed by this wiki)

    Without slicing, a turbo-coded TM frame must be 223, 446, 892 or 1115 octets.98 With slicing, a 2048-octet frame becomes a 16,416-bit SMTF. With \(k\) = 8920, each information block yields a 17,848-symbol codeword and, with its 64-bit CSM, a 17,912-symbol CADU.1996 Because \(\operatorname{lcm}(16416, 8920) = 18{,}303{,}840\) bits, the alignment of frames and blocks repeats every 1115 frames, which fill exactly 2052 information blocks; on average a frame costs about 32,965 channel symbols, and the slicing overhead is only the 32-bit ASM, 0.2 % of the SMTF. A frame overlaps two or three information blocks and a block overlaps one or two frames, so one decoding failure invalidates at most two frames.90

    Turbo channel interleaver (Issue 6)¶

    Turbo codes are vulnerable to burst errors, for example from a tumbling spacecraft or solar scintillation. Issue 6 therefore says a channel interleaver should be used unless the system designer verifies that the problem is handled by other means, such as a larger fading margin in the link budget, and makes its presence a managed parameter. It permutes the coded symbols of one codeword and must not be confused with the turbo code's own permutation, which reorders information bits between the two component encoders.94

    The interleaver is a row-column block of depth 4. The \(n = (k+4)/r\) symbols of a codeword are written row by row into rows of \(N_c = 4/r\) symbols, so each row holds four consecutive outputs of the two constituent encoders, and they are read out column by column; the column length is \(N_r = n/N_c\), and the receiver writes by columns and reads by rows.94
