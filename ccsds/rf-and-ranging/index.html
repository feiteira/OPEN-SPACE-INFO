    Radio frequency, modulation and ranging¶

    Everything else on this wiki, from Space Packets to CLTUs and CADUs, eventually becomes a modulated radio carrier. The physical layer decides on which frequency that carrier sits, how the coded symbols are impressed on it, how much spectrum and power the emission may use, how the ground station and spacecraft find and lock onto each other, and how the same carrier is used to measure the spacecraft's distance and velocity. CCSDS covers this in one omnibus Blue Book, CCSDS 401.0-B, Radio Frequency and Modulation Systems, Part 1: Earth Stations and Spacecraft; ECSS covers it in ECSS-E-ST-50-05C (Radio frequency and modulation) and ECSS-E-ST-50-02C (Ranging and Doppler tracking).199148175 This page is a structured overview of all three, not a reprint of every recommendation; it links to the TM coding and TC coding pages for the sublayer immediately above.

    Where the physical layer sits¶

    The CCSDS layered architecture places these recommendations in the physical layer, which the Foreword itself subdivides into technical characteristics, policy constraints and procedures.1 The ECSS communications handbook draws the same boundary: its protocol map puts ECSS-E-ST-50-05 and ECSS-E-ST-50-02 at the bottom, under the TM and TC synchronization and channel coding standards, with CCSDS 211.1-B as the physical layer of Proximity-1 beside them; it also warns that the choice of TC and TM modulation must take into account whether a ranging service compatible with ECSS-E-ST-50-02 is needed.174176 The figure below shows the chain in both directions; the boxes in the middle are the subject of this page.

    How CCSDS 401.0-B is organised¶

    CCSDS 401.0-B is unusual among CCSDS Blue Books: it is a loose-leaf collection of short recommendations, each written in the ITU-style "considering ... recommends" form, so that agencies can add, update or delete recommendations independently.4 They are grouped into three sections: technical (section 2), policy (section 3) and procedural (section 4), plus terminology and a glossary (section 5).4

    Section Subsections Character
    2 Technical 2.1 Earth-to-space RF, 2.2 Telecommand, 2.3 Space-to-Earth RF, 2.4 Telemetry, 2.5 Radio metric, 2.6 Spacecraft quantitative characteristics of equipment and signals
    3 Policy 3.1 Frequency utilization, 3.2 Power limitations, 3.3 Modulation methods, 3.4 Operational procedures, 3.5 Testing, 3.6 Spacecraft systems directive rules for sharing the spectrum
    4 Procedural 4.1 Design tools, 4.2 Computational algorithms link design control table, modulation-loss and bandwidth formulas
    5 5.1 Terminology, 5.2 Glossary definitions

    Each recommendation has a self-describing number. Page 2.5.3A-1, for example, is the first page of a technical recommendation (2) in the radio metric subsection (5), third in that subsection (3), and applicable to Category A only (A); a number without a letter applies to both categories.47 The terms "Telecommand" and "Telemetry" are kept for historical reasons but cover any data sent to or from the spacecraft, including science and file transfers.5

    Issue 32 (October 2021) updated recommendations 2.4.18 and 2.4.23 and added 2.6.13 and 2.6.15; the document control table records 32 issues since January 1987, each adding, updating or deleting individual recommendations (for example the deletion of 2.1.8A/B by resolution CMC-R-2018-02-001).23

    Mission categories and radio services¶

    Four ITU radiocommunication services matter to CCSDS: Space Research, Space Operation, Earth Exploration-Satellite and Meteorological-Satellite.6 Within Space Research, some bands are reserved for deep space. WARC ORB-88 moved the ITU deep-space boundary from lunar distance to 2.0 × 10⁶ km with effect from 16 March 1990; because the ITU has no name for the nearer region, CCSDS defines Category A (altitude below 2.0 × 10⁶ km) and Category B (2.0 × 10⁶ km or more).6 The same split appears in the terminology of §5.1, while the glossary of §5.2 places the boundary case the other way (Category A "less than or equal to", Category B "greater than"); the difference only matters for a spacecraft exactly at 2 × 10⁶ km.8 ECSS-E-ST-50-05C and ECSS-E-ST-50-02C adopt the same two categories, and ECSS adds that bands allocated to Space Research (deep space) shall only be used by Category B spacecraft.100149102

    ECSS-E-ST-50-05C defines the services by reference to the ITU Radio Regulations (ITU/RR): the Space Operation service (SO) is concerned exclusively with operating spacecraft (tracking, telemetry and telecommand, TTC); Space Research (SR) uses spacecraft for scientific and technological research; Earth Exploration-Satellite (EES) obtains information about the Earth from orbiting sensors, and, for this standard, includes the Meteorological-Satellite service.10099 A service with a primary allocation coordinates only with co-primary services and need not protect secondary users; a secondary service must not cause harmful interference to, and cannot claim protection from, primary services.102

    The B25 mask in the 8 400–8 450 MHz deep-space band¶

    Mars-region spacecraft cannot be separated spatially, and a single unrestricted high-rate mission could occupy the whole 50 MHz allocation. CCSDS 3.1.2B therefore limits each mission's B25 bandwidth, the width outside which the power spectral density is at least 25 dB below its peak (discrete lines such as a residual carrier do not count), as a function of symbol rate.81 In the transition region the limit is

    \[B_{25}\,[\text{MHz}] = \frac{k\,R_s}{0.41 + R_s},\qquad k = 8.53 \text{ (all missions)},\ k = 12.5 \text{ (non-Mars missions, non-interference basis)}\]

    with \(R_s\) in Msps; the curves flatten at 8.0 MHz and 12.0 MHz. Outside B25 the spectral power flux density at the Earth's surface is limited to −266 dB(W/Hz/m²).81 ECSS-E-ST-50-05C §4.2.3 imposes the same mask using the equivalent symbol rate \(R_{ES}\), which is twice the NRZ symbol rate for SP-L and equal to it otherwise.104 With GMSK (BTs = 0.5), the 12 MHz and 8 MHz caps correspond to maximum coded symbol rates of 9.3 and 6.2 Msps respectively; missions needing more must move to 31.8–32.3 GHz.55

    Frequency assignment procedure¶

    ECSS-E-ST-50-05C makes the frequency coordinator the exclusive authority for assignments. The project requests its frequencies before the design phase, marking preliminary data as such, and must supply final data for the ITU advance publication, coordination and notification procedures (ITU/RR articles 9 and 11) no later than three years before launch.105 Annex E adds that the ITU Radio Regulations cannot be tailored.146

    Category B channel frequency plan¶

    CCSDS 3.1.6B asks agencies to choose Category B frequencies from a single channel plan, coordinated through the SFCG, so that up- and downlink pairs automatically respect the turnaround ratios.82 Each channel derives from a 2 GHz downlink reference \(F_{2DN} = N \cdot 10/27 + 2295\) MHz, with channel number \(N + 14\); the 7 GHz uplink is computed from \(F_{2DN}\) and the other bands are derived from the 7 GHz value through the frequency factors 221, 240, 749, 880, 3328, 3344, 3360 and 3599, each result rounded to the nearest Hz.83

    Worked example: channel 14 (values from Table 3.1.6B-1)

    For channel 14, \(N = 0\) and \(F_{2DN}\) = 2 295.000000 MHz. The table then gives 2 113.312500 MHz (2 GHz up, factor 221), 7 162.312500 MHz (7 GHz up, 749), 8 415.000000 MHz (8 GHz down, 880), 31 977.000000 MHz (32 GHz down, 3344) and 34 415.437500 MHz (34 GHz up, 3599).83 Multiplying the 2 295 MHz reference by 221/240, 749/240 and 880/240 reproduces 2 113.3125, 7 162.3125 and 8 415 MHz exactly, which shows how the plan embeds the turnaround ratios (this wiki's arithmetic).

    Coherent transponders and turnaround ratios¶

    For coherent Doppler tracking the spacecraft transponder derives its transmitted carrier from the received one by phase-lock techniques, multiplying it by a fixed transponder turnaround frequency ratio (TTFR).10674 A defined ratio is only needed for missions that require both cross-support from other agencies' stations and up/down coherency, and on-board implementations may deviate from the nominal value and add a significant downlink delay that orbit determination must account for.74

    Polarization, bandwidth and emissions¶

    Polarization. Both CCSDS and ECSS require circular polarization. Linear polarization would vary with aspect angle on near-omnidirectional antennas and suffer Faraday rotation at low frequencies; CCSDS asks Earth stations to switch between LCP and RCP with a carrier interruption of no more than 5 seconds, and ECSS requires stations to transmit either sense at the user's choice.1436108

    Occupied bandwidth. Occupied bandwidth is the band holding all but 0.5 % of the mean power on each side (ITU/RR 1.153); spurious emissions are those beyond ±2.5 times that bandwidth.100 ECSS-E-ST-50-05C Table 5-5 gives the maxima, where \(f_t\) is the ranging tone and \(R_s\) the symbol rate:109

    Residual-carrier modulation¶

    A residual-carrier system phase-modulates the carrier with a peak deviation (the modulation index, in radians) small enough that a discrete carrier line remains for a conventional phase-locked loop (PLL) receiver to track. CCSDS recommends residual-carrier phase modulation for the uplink and retains residual-carrier receivers on the ground for low-rate telemetry because most receivers and autotrack systems need that line.1330 ECSS requires phase modulation for telecommand and ranging in the 2, 7 and 34 GHz uplink bands, for ranging in the 2, 8 and 32 GHz downlink bands, and for telemetry in those downlink bands unless suppressed-carrier modulation is adopted.117

    Suppressed-carrier modulation¶

    When all the power goes into the data, the carrier line disappears and the receiver recovers the carrier with a suppressed-carrier tracking loop such as the Costas loop, which still supports Doppler measurement.29 CCSDS notes that a suppressed-carrier loop frequently has more than 10 dB better carrier SNR than a residual-carrier loop, at least as good symbol-error performance, and makes PFD limits easier to meet; short periodic data patterns can however leave zero power at the carrier frequency, and the CCSDS randomizer is required.31

    When to use it. CCSDS 2.3.2 requires (filtered) BPSK, (filtered) QPSK, filtered OQPSK or GMSK when a residual carrier would exceed the PFD limits, up to 2 Msps in the 2 and 8 GHz bands, 10 Msps at 26 GHz and 20 Msps at 32 GHz, recommends them whenever practicable at lower rates, prefers filtered OQPSK or GMSK, and requires the CCSDS 131.0 randomizer.31 ECSS-E-ST-50-05C §6.2.1 requires suppressed carrier for telemetry in the 2 200–2 300, 8 025–8 400 and 8 400–8 500 MHz bands when PFD limits would be exceeded and in any case above 1 Msps; above 2 Msps (below 8.5 GHz) or 20 Msps (31.8–32.3 GHz) only TCM 8PSK (8 025–8 400 MHz only), filtered OQPSK (below 8 400 MHz and in 8 450–8 500 MHz) or GMSK (all bands) may be used, and new projects may use BPSK, QPSK, UQPSK and OQPSK only below those rates.127 ECSS-E-ST-50-02C ranging cannot be combined with these modulations.127155

    GMSK¶

    GMSK is a constant-envelope continuous-phase modulation whose phase is the integral of Gaussian-filtered frequency pulses. ECSS-E-ST-50-05C §9.1 defines the carrier as \(x(t) = \sqrt{2P}\cos(2\pi f t + \varphi(t) + \varphi_0)\) with \(\varphi(t) = \sum_k a_k \frac{\pi}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{t-kT_s} g(\tau)\,d\tau\). The frequency pulse \(g\) is a rectangle of one symbol duration convolved with a Gaussian of standard deviation \(\sigma T_s\), \(\sigma = \sqrt{\ln 2}/(2\pi BT_s)\), B being the one-sided 3 dB bandwidth; the transmitted symbols are precoded as \(a_k = (-1)^k d_k d_{k-1}\) from the information symbols \(d_k = \pm 1\).141 Because GMSK is inherently differential, the precoder removes the need for differential decoding and improves receiver performance.14154 GMSK and baseband-filtered OQPSK can both be received with a conventional OQPSK receiver, at the price of extra losses if the receiver is not matched.54

    The tone-plus-code ranging signal¶

    The baseband ranging signal is a sine-wave tone of frequency \(f_t\) that is phase-modulated by a series of codes used to resolve the ambiguity of the tone phase. The n-th code is \(C_n = Q_1 \oplus Q_2 \oplus \dots \oplus Q_n\), where \(Q_i\) is a square wave at \(2^{-i} f_t\) (the outputs of a divide-by-two chain driven by the tone), and code transitions occur when the unmodulated tone phase is 90°. Each code is sent for a fixed time so that the ground can correlate and align its replica; a simple implementation sends \(C_{n-1}\) followed by its complement.154 The link-control sequence is tone alone, tone with the sequence of codes, then tone with the final code; deep-space missions may restart the sequence continuously, trading accuracy for faster re-acquisition.152

    As codes are added, the spectrum evolves from discrete lines at multiples of \(f_t\) to a quasi-continuous spectrum between \(2^{-N}f_t\) and \((2-2^{-N})f_t\), N being the longest code.157 On the Earth-to-space link the tone is selectable from 100 kHz to 1.5 MHz, the code period is \(2^n/f_t\) with n = 0 … 20, the modulation is PCM/PSK/PM, and the tone-to-code modulation index is 45° during ambiguity resolution and 22.5° during measurement.161

    Illustrative arithmetic: ambiguity of a 698.26 kHz tone

    The tone period of \(f_t\) = 698 260 Hz (the Herschel/Planck tone, see the worked example below) is 1.432 µs, which repeats every 214.7 m of one-way range. A code with n = 20 has a period of \(2^{20}/f_t\) = 1.502 s, so the ambiguity grows to about 225 000 km one way. These figures are this wiki's arithmetic from the code-period definition, not values stated in the standard.161

    Choosing the tone¶

    ECSS selects the lowest tone compatible with TC, TM and ranging to minimise bandwidth, keeps \(f_t\) (plus or minus Doppler) within 100 kHz–1.5 MHz in a region of stable transponder group delay, and gives spacing rules against the telemetry and telecommand spectra:153158

    • against telemetry with subcarrier \(f_{sc}\) and symbol rate \(f_{symb}\), with \(D\) the one-way Doppler factor: \(|Df_t - f_{sc}| \ge 2.5 f_{symb}\), \(|Df_t - 3f_{sc}| \ge f_{symb}\) and \(|k f_{symb} - Df_t| \ge 5\) Hz; for SP-L telemetry take \(f_{sc} = f_{symb}\), and if no compatible tone results, place \(f_t\) near an SP-L spectral null but more than the Doppler shift plus 5 Hz away;158
    • against PCM(NRZ-L)/PSK/PM telecommand with subcarrier \(f_{TC}\) and bit rate \(f_b\): \(|2m f_{TC} - f_t| \ge 5\) Hz and \(|(2n-1) f_{TC} - f_t| \ge 2f_b\); for PCM(SP-L)/PM telecommand, \(|f_t - f_b| \ge 2.5 f_b\), \(|f_t - 3f_b| \ge f_b\) and \(|k f_b - f_t| \ge 5\) Hz.159

    Simultaneous ranging and telecommand should be used to avoid scheduling conflicts, with time sharing if the link budget does not allow it; slow on-board baseband AGC can transiently over-modulate the downlink when TC or ranging starts, which ramping the uplink modulation can mitigate.156 Suppressed-carrier telemetry is not compatible with simultaneous tone ranging, so orbit determination must then use integrated Doppler on the regenerated carrier, time sharing between telemetry and ranging, or a separate ranging transponder.155

    Performance and ancillary measurements¶

    Integrated Doppler is not a series of independent velocity measurements but the continuous phase of the received carrier relative to its reference: \(\varphi(t) - \varphi_0 = 2\pi\int\Delta f\,d\tau \approx -(2\pi q F_T \cdot 2/c)\int v\,d\tau\), a high-resolution measurement of the change in range up to an unknown constant ("non-destructive range-rate measurement"). One-way Doppler is also possible with the transponder non-coherent, relative to the downlink carrier.173 Its error budget distinguishes bias (station reference drift, digital synthesis offsets, acceleration effects) from jitter (reference, converters, transponder stability, thermal and phase noise, quantization below 1/1000 of a carrier period).167 For ranging, the quantization must be at most 1/65 536 of the tone period and the station's measurement accuracy 1 ns or 1/1000 of the tone period, whichever is larger; station calibration before and after a pass is required when group-delay drift would exceed the mission need.168

    Propagation corrections need meteorological data at the station (pressure ±1 hPa, temperature ±1 °C, relative humidity ±1 %), multiple-frequency ranging or Doppler, or differenced range versus integrated Doppler (DRVID), and the antenna phase centre known to 0.5 m.169 CCSDS 3.4.2 tabulates which charged-particle calibration methods (Faraday rotation, DRVID, dual frequency) apply to which tracking modes.88 At ESA's ESTRACK stations the IF and modem system (IFMS) makes these measurements available to data clients such as the NCTRS as Doppler, AGC, meteorological, open-loop and ranging data sets; see NCTRS, NIS and the ground station interface.197

    Delta-DOR¶

    Differential One-way Ranging measures the angular position of a Category B spacecraft by VLBI: two widely separated stations record sine-wave DOR tones phase-modulated on the downlink, and the difference of their arrival times, corrected by observing an angularly nearby quasar (ΔDOR), gives a geometric delay. The delay precision is \(\varepsilon_\tau = 1/(\pi f_{BW}\sqrt{2 (P_{DOR}/N_0) T_{obs}})\), where \(f_{BW}\) is the spanned bandwidth (usually twice the tone frequency).72

The analog part of each block (from octet 29 for DST-A) holds the non-coherent and coherent AGC in dBm, the "SPE" in Hz, subcarrier, ESA-ranging and PN-ranging AGCs, the measured RF output power, three internal temperatures and the software version; octets 81–96 carry four receiver and transmitter voltages.198 ECSS-E-ST-50-02C expands SPE as static phase error, and CCSDS 2.5.3A/B list static phase error, with voltage and temperature, among the telemetered data used to calculate the transponder's ranging group delay.15068

    Inference

    Several readings above are this wiki's, not documented in the MIB: that WB, IB and NB denote wide, intermediate and narrow carrier-loop bandwidths (supported by the AGC curves being selected per bandwidth); that "SPE" (static phase error) is the loop-stress measurement ECSS-E-ST-50-05C §7.2.2c requires for estimating best lock frequency; that the "High" and "Low" DOR indices drive the two 8 GHz DOR tones of CCSDS 2.5.6B; and that in rate-mode names such as GMSK_4_2000000 the middle digit is the turbo code-rate denominator (2 or 4, matching TURBO_1_2 and TURBO_1_4 of NCFT1YN0, see TM coding) and the last number the symbol rate (the calibration's own description, "Dst TM symbol rate modulation id", and pairs such as BPSK_2_21/BPSK_4_42, whose symbol rates differ by exactly the factor of two between rate ½ and rate ¼ coding, support this). The BPSK_… modes stop at 52 428 sps and the SPL_…/GMSK_… modes start at 60 787 sps, which matches the ECSS rule that subcarriers are not used above 60 ksps, so "BPSK" plausibly means PSK on a subcarrier; the transponder's own scheme parameter only offers GMSK and SPL. How the 6-bit ranging-index field relates to the 0–1023 domain of its calibration and of the 12-bit command parameter is not stated in the available sources.198121136

    DST commands¶

    The transponder is commanded with TC(131,1) "Perform Functions" of Solar Orbiter's private service 131, Private Function Management, which controls on-board software functions that are not standard services, for example equipment management functions that switch units on or off. Its application data is a 2-octet Function ID followed by function-specific parameters (see How Solar Orbiter uses PUS).196 In the MIB every DST command goes to APID 236 and starts with that 16-bit Function ID, followed by an 8-bit unit identifier (transmitter: DST_TX_CTR_A, DST_TX_CTR_B, DST_TX_INUSE, DST_TX_NOTUSE; receiver: DST_RX_CTR_A, DST_RX_CTR_B) and an 8-bit or 16-bit setting; 143 commands mention "DST" (case-insensitive match) in both MIB releases.198199

    Inference

    The MIB stores the first 16 bits of each command as a fixed area (CDF_ELTYPE = A, element description "Identification number of", truncated in the MIB) whose values (3409, 340F, ...) contain hexadecimal digits. Matching this field to the 2-octet Function ID of TC(131,1) follows the packet layout in the TM/TC ICD; the list of Function IDs itself is in a document the ICD references but which is not in the library. That Solar Orbiter is a Category B mission is likewise deduced, not stated as such: from its "deep space transponder", from the E-FECS reference to ESA's three deep-space stations, and from the EID-A's planned approach to 0.22 AU from the Sun, which puts the spacecraft at least about 0.78 AU (roughly 1.2 × 10⁸ km) from Earth, far beyond 2 × 10⁶ km.198195196194
