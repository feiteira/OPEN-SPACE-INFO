    Space Data Link Security (CCSDS 355.0-B-2)¶

    The Space Data Link Security (SDLS) protocol is CCSDS's way of adding authentication, encryption, or both to the contents of transfer frames. It works inside the Data Link Layer, which is OSI Layer 2. It defines a Security Header and a Security Trailer that wrap the frame's data field, together with procedures for applying and checking security on each frame.211 It is not a stand-alone protocol. It is an optional extra service of four data link protocols: TM, TC, AOS and USLP. It does not apply to Proximity-1.11 SDLS fixes no cryptographic algorithm. CCSDS 352.0-B lists the algorithms CCSDS recommends, and annex E of SDLS gives an informative baseline.3

    This page follows CCSDS 355.0-B-2 (July 2022), held in the library. Issue 2 adds USLP support and makes the SDLS Extended Procedures (CCSDS 355.1-B-1) a normative reference. The Extended Procedures define key management, Security Association management and monitoring.113 The standard cites several companion documents.56 Three of them are now in this wiki's library and are described on Security in CCSDS space links: the Extended Procedures (355.1-B-1, February 2020), the Cryptographic Algorithms book (352.0-B-2, August 2019), and the concept-and-rationale Green Book, held as its issue 2 (350.5-G-2, January 2024) where 355.0-B-2 cites issue 1 (350.5-G-1).789 The key-management concept CCSDS 350.6-G-1 is not in the library. Statements on this page are limited to what 355.0-B-2 and the frame standards say. The wider context (threats, the choice of layer, algorithms, key and SA management) is on the security page.

    At a glance

    Property Value
    Layer Data Link Protocol Sublayer (with TM, TC, AOS or USLP)1114
    Security services Authentication, encryption, authenticated encryption ("encrypt-then-MAC")2232
    Security Header 16-bit SPI + optional IV, Sequence Number and Pad Length; at most 64 octets47
    Security Trailer MAC, present whenever authentication is used52
    Session state Security Association (SA): simplex and stateful, selected by the SPI20
    Replay protection Anti-replay sequence number checked against a window27
    Granularity Per Virtual Channel, or per MAP (TC with Segment Headers, USLP)2155
    Algorithms Not mandated; CCSDS 352.0-B lists the recommended ones3
    Key and SA management Out of scope; see the SDLS Extended Procedures1376

    Why SDLS exists, and its design goals¶

    The standard has four goals:4

    1. Apply security in the same way whatever cryptographic algorithm a mission chooses.
    2. Keep the existing frame headers and trailers, so that legacy frame-processing infrastructure can be used unchanged where appropriate.
    3. Stay compatible with the SLE forward and return services.
    4. Encourage common commercial implementations, so that agencies interoperate.

    ECSS sets the system-level requirements behind this. Only telecommands from authorized sources may be executed on board. Telecommand encryption is required when authentication alone does not meet the security requirements. Telemetry authentication and encryption are required when security requirements cannot be met by other means. For telecommand encryption and for telemetry security, notes in ECSS-E-ST-50C name the SDLS option of the TC (ECSS-E-AS-50-25), TM (AS-50-22) and AOS (AS-50-23) adoption notices as one way to meet the requirement at the data link layer.8384 The ECSS adoption notice of the TC protocol, ECSS-E-AS-50-25C Rev.1, includes the interfaces for SDLS with TC frames. Its predecessor, ECSS-E-ST-50-04, did not have them.85

    The CCSDS overview calls SDLS optional within TM, TC, AOS and USLP. It also notes that no security requirements have yet been set for Proximity-1.82 The older green book on data link protocols describes the services as authentication, encryption and authenticated encryption.81

    Where SDLS sits¶

    flowchart TB
  N["Network and upper layers<br/>(Space Packets, Encapsulation Packets, private SDUs)"]
  subgraph DL["OSI Layer 2: Data Link Layer"]
    direction TB
    SDLP["Data Link Protocol Sublayer<br/>TM / TC / AOS / USLP"]
    SEC["SDLS<br/>ApplySecurity / ProcessSecurity"]
    CS["Synchronization and Channel Coding Sublayer<br/>(unaffected by SDLS)"]
  end
  N --> SDLP
  SDLP <-->|"function calls per frame"| SEC
  SDLP --> CS

    SDLS is used by the data link protocol entity through two functions. The sending end calls ApplySecurity and the receiving end calls ProcessSecurity. The Synchronization and Channel Coding Sublayer is not affected.1433 The headers and trailers reduce the space left for the data field, but SDLS keeps the quality of service the link protocol provides. It scales to any number of Virtual Channels. Whether a Security Header and Trailer are used on a Global Virtual Channel or GMAP, and how large they are, are managed parameters that stay constant for a mission.12

    The three security services¶

    Service What it gives How
    Authentication Integrity of the data and authenticity of its source, plus detection of replayed frames A MAC over selected frame fields, and an anti-replay sequence number in each frame25
    Encryption Confidentiality of the data The frame data ("plaintext") is replaced by "ciphertext". An initialization vector is often an input, and padding may be needed for block sizes31
    Authenticated encryption Both of the above Encrypt the data, apply the anti-replay sequence number, then compute the MAC over the result ("encrypt-then-MAC")32

    Each Security Association uses exactly one of these services. Its algorithms and modes are fixed for the life of the SA.2260 The standard warns that encryption without authentication can give a false sense of security. Missions should choose services only after a mission-specific threat and risk analysis.31 Annex B adds that encryption alone gives no protection against data substitution, and that predictable plaintext may weaken confidentiality.74

    For algorithms that take the plaintext and Additional Authenticated Data (AAD) as separate inputs, the so-called AEAD algorithms, SDLS fixes the split. The plaintext is the Transfer Frame Data Field. The AAD runs from the first octet of the Authentication Payload to the octet just before the data field, that is, the masked headers including the Security Header.63

    What is protected in a frame¶

    SDLS inserts its fields around the frame data. The Security Header carries the SPI, the initialization vector, the anti-replay sequence number and the pad length, and the Security Trailer carries the MAC:1523

    flowchart LR
  H["Frame header(s)<br/>(primary, secondary or segment header, Insert Zone)"] --> SH["Security Header"] --> D["Frame Data Field<br/>(encrypted and/or authenticated)"] --> ST["Security Trailer<br/>(MAC, optional)"] --> T["Frame trailer(s)<br/>(OCF, FECF)"]

    Encryption applies only to the Frame Data Field. Authentication covers more. Some fields are always excluded from the MAC, some are always included, and the rest depend on an authentication bit mask in the SA.2661

    Frame field In MAC? Notes
    Synchronization marker (ASM in TM/AOS/USLP, CLTU Start Sequence in TC) Never Added below the data link layer26
    Virtual Channel ID Always Mask bits all ones2661
    MAP ID (USLP) Always Mask bits all ones61
    Segment Header (TC) Always Mask bits all ones61
    Security Header, except the IV Always Mask bits all ones61
    Initialization Vector Not required §2.3.2.2 and item h) of the mask rules exempt the IV from the "always included" / "all ones" rule for the Security Header; the standard does not fix its mask bits2661
    Frame Data Field Always Mask bits all ones61
    Master Channel Frame Count (TM) Never Mask bits all zeros61
    Frame Header Error Control (AOS) Never Mask bits all zeros61
    Insert Zone (AOS, USLP) Never Mask bits all zeros2661
    OCF, FECF/ECF, the MAC itself Never Outside the authenticated part26
    Other header fields (for example the SCID or the TM secondary header) By default no Mask bits should be zero. A mission may include them by choosing another mask61

    The mask is ANDed with the frame from the first header octet to the end of the data field. That yields the Authentication Payload, over which the MAC is computed. For variable-length TC and USLP frames the mask is sized for the largest expected data field. If TM's Master Channel Frame Secondary Header service is used, the TM secondary header is left out of the authenticated data.6165

    Inference: why some fields are excluded

    The standard gives no rationale field by field. This wiki notes a pattern: the fields that are always excluded either lie outside the ApplySecurity Payload, which ends with the data field,35 or are filled in by functions that run after SDLS has processed the frame. The OCF and FECF follow the data field. The FECF is computed in All Frames Generation, after SDLS.89 In AOS, All Frames Generation also fills in the Insert Zone and the Frame Header Error Control,90 and in TM the Master Channel Frame Count is generated by the Master Channel Generation Function.94 The standard refers to CCSDS 350.5-G-1 for the security concerns that affect the choice of mask.61 Its issue 2, in the library, explains which fields the default mask includes and excludes; see Security Associations and the authentication bit mask.10

    Security Header¶

    The Security Header comes directly before the frame data. On a given VC or MAP it is either always present or always absent for the whole mission. It is mandatory whenever any security service is applied. It has one mandatory field and three optional fields, in this order:47

    Field Length Allowed lengths (managed) Content
    Security Parameter Index (SPI) 16 bits, mandatory fixed Identifies the SA. Values 1 to 65 534; 0x0000 and 0xFFFF are reserved by CCSDS4871
    Initialization Vector (IV) Integral octets, fixed for the SA 1 to 32 octets if used, otherwise 0 The IV, or an agreed part of it4971
    Sequence Number Integral octets, fixed for the SA 2 to 8 octets if used, otherwise 0 The anti-replay sequence number, when authentication or authenticated encryption is selected. Zero length if the IV doubles as the sequence number5071
    Pad Length Integral octets, fixed for the SA 1 to 2 octets if used, otherwise 0 Number of fill bytes added for the cryptographic process5171

    The whole header is at most 64 octets. The receiver works out which optional fields are present, and how long they are, by looking up the SPI's Security Association. The frame carries no length indicators.47

    Security Trailer¶

    The Security Trailer comes directly after the frame data. Its presence on a VC or MAP stays constant for the mission, and it is present whenever authentication or authenticated encryption is applied. It contains only the MAC, octet-aligned and of fixed length for the SA.52 The MAC field may be 8 to 64 octets.71 If the computed MAC is longer than the field, the least significant bits are truncated to fit.65

    Security Associations¶

    A Security Association (SA) is a simplex (one-way), stateful cryptographic session. It provides authentication, data integrity, replay protection and/or data confidentiality, and it holds the state for the duration of the session.20 All frames that share an SA on a physical channel form a Secure Channel, made of one or more Global Virtual Channels or, for TC, Global MAP IDs assigned to the SA when it is created.21

    The SPI, carried in every secured frame, is the key into an SA data base that holds all the managed information of every SA on the physical channel.21 Each SA on a physical channel has a unique SPI.58 §4.2.2.3 states the rule per Master Channel instead: frames with the same SPI on a Master Channel share a single SA.59

    flowchart LR
  F["Received frame<br/>GVCID / GMAP ID + SPI"] --> DB[("SA data base")]
  DB --> SA["SA record<br/>service type, field lengths,<br/>algorithms, key refs, mask,<br/>sequence number + window, IV"]
  SA --> P["ProcessSecurity<br/>verify / decrypt"]

    SA rules¶

    • Exactly one service type per SA (authentication, encryption or authenticated encryption). The algorithms, modes and field lengths are fixed for the SA's lifetime.6022
    • Exactly one active SA at a time per Global Virtual Channel or GMAP ID at the sending end.56
    • No SA on idle channels. SAs are never created for VCs that carry Only Idle Data frames.57 TM goes further: an OID frame that the Master Channel Multiplexing Function creates must use a VC that does not use SDLS.97
    • Protocol-specific limits. In TC each SA belongs to one VC only. In USLP each SA belongs to one VC only when COPs are used.7019 The TC and USLP standards also say that several VCs sharing an SA is not supported.102109 TM and AOS allow an SA to cover several VCs, when SDLS is called from the Virtual Channel Multiplexing Function (see AOS with SDLS).9688
    • "Clear mode". An SA with a "no-op" algorithm can keep header and trailer lengths constant, for example during integration testing before cryptography is available. It is not recommended in normal operation, and the conditions for activating it need careful analysis.6074

    SA parameters¶

    The standard defines the SA data base parameters and their allowed values. It does not define the management services that set them.4271

    Parameter Applies to Allowed values / meaning
    GVCID All SAs Global Virtual Channel(s) the SA applies to43
    GMAP_ID TC with Segment Headers, USLP Global MAP(s) the SA applies to4355
    Security Parameter Index All SAs 1 to 65 5344371
    SA_service_type All SAs Authentication, Encryption, Authenticated Encryption43
    SA_length_IV, SA_length_SN, SA_length_PL, SA_length_MAC All SAs Field lengths: IV 1–32, SN 2–8, PL 1–2, MAC 8–64 octets (when used)4371
    SA_authentication_algorithm Authentication, AE HMAC, CMAC, GMAC, GCM or agency-specific, with mode4471
    SA_authentication_key Authentication, AE The key value, or an index to it44
    SA_authentication_mask Authentication, AE Bit mask ANDed with the frame44
    SA_sequence_number Authentication, AE Current anti-replay value (sender: next frame; receiver: expected)4471
    SA_sequence_window Authentication, AE Accepted deviation, an integer greater than zero4471
    SA_encryption_algorithm Encryption, AE AES in counter mode, GCM, or agency-specific, with mode4571
    SA_encryption_key Encryption, AE The key value, or an index to it45
    SA_initialization_vector Encryption, AE Current IV value (algorithm-specific)4571

    Table 6-1 groups these parameters by how long they stay fixed.71

    • Fixed for the life of the SA: SPI, service type, context (GVCID/GMAP_ID), field lengths, algorithms, mask and window.
    • Fixed while the SA is active: the keys.
    • Varying dynamically: the sequence number and the IV.

    The sending and receiving data bases must match for the link to work.71

    SA life cycle and management¶

    Before an SA can secure frames, both ends must create it, associate it with key(s), associate it with its Global Virtual Channel(s) or GMAP IDs, and activate it.2454 SAs may be statically preloaded before the start of the mission, or created dynamically alongside active ones. Switching from one active SA to another is an Application Layer function. Over-the-air negotiation of SA parameters is an Application Layer function that is "currently undefined".24 Before operation, both ends initialize a common SA data base, and they should keep it synchronized.53

    flowchart LR
  C["Create SA<br/>(SPI, service type, lengths, algorithms)"] --> K["Associate key(s)"] --> X["Associate GVCID(s) / GMAP ID(s)"] --> A["Activate<br/>(one active SA per channel at the sender)"] --> U["Secure frames<br/>(sequence number and IV evolve)"]

    Separation from key management

    SDLS deliberately specifies no key management protocol. Key management and the criteria for changing keys in operation are outside its scope.7674 Creating, modifying and maintaining the SA data bases is also outside this document. These functions, together with SDLS monitoring and control, are specified in the SDLS Extended Procedures, CCSDS 355.1-B-1.135346 The SDLS standard itself names no SA states beyond "created" and "active". The finer life cycle (Null, Unkeyed, Keyed and Operational) comes from the Extended Procedures, which are in the library and described in SDLS Extended Procedures.7

    Sending end: the ApplySecurity function¶

    Inputs. The calling protocol passes three things:343536

    • The ApplySecurity Payload: the partially formatted frame from the first octet of the Primary Header to the last octet of the Transfer Frame Data Field, with the Security Header still empty. There is one payload type each for TM, TC, AOS and USLP.
    • The GVCID.
    • For TC and USLP, the GMAP_ID where applicable.

    Output. The ApplySecurity Return is the part of the frame from the first octet of the Security Header to the last octet of the Security Trailer, or of the data field if there is no trailer.37

    flowchart TB
  IN["ApplySecurity(payload, GVCID, GMAP_ID)"] --> SEL["Select SA from GVCID / GMAP_ID"]
  SEL --> T{"SA service type"}
  T -- "Encryption" --> E1["Encrypt Transfer Frame Data Field;<br/>set Pad Length if padding used"] --> E2["Complete Security Header"] --> R1["Return Security Header + ciphertext"]
  T -- "Authentication" --> A1["Increment SA sequence number by 1;<br/>place it in SN field (or IV)"] --> A2["Complete Security Header"] --> A3["AND authentication mask over frame<br/>= Authentication Payload"] --> A4["Compute MAC; truncate LSBs to MAC length;<br/>place in Security Trailer"] --> R2["Return Security Header + plaintext + Trailer"]
  T -- "Authenticated encryption" --> AE1["Encryption operations"] --> AE2["Authentication operations<br/>on the encrypted partial frame"] --> R3["Return Security Header + ciphertext + Trailer"]

    These steps follow §4.2.3.626465 The way frame data passes between the link protocol and SDLS is left to the implementation.87

    Receiving end: the ProcessSecurity function¶

    Inputs. The receiving end passes the ProcessSecurity Payload, the frame from the first octet of the Primary Header to the last octet of the Security Trailer (or of the data field), with the GVCID and the GMAP_ID where applicable.38

    Outputs.394041

    • Verification Status: either no failure, or a failure was detected.
    • Verification Status Code: says which failure occurred. Implementations must support at least five values: no failure, invalid SPI, MAC verification failure, anti-replay sequence number failure, and padding error. The numeric code values are not stated in the available sources.
    • ProcessSecurity Return: the data field, decrypted if the SA includes encryption. It runs from the first octet after the Security Header to the last octet of the data field. No decryption is done after a verification failure.
    flowchart TB
  IN["ProcessSecurity(payload, GVCID, GMAP_ID)"] --> V{"Frame has Security Header and<br/>its SPI's SA is associated with<br/>this GVCID / GMAP_ID?"}
  V -- "no" --> X1["Report exception (invalid SPI), discard"]
  V -- "yes" --> T{"SA service type"}
  T -- "Authentication or AE" --> M1["AND mask: Authentication Payload"] --> M2["Compute MAC, truncate, compare"]
  M2 -- "mismatch" --> X2["Report MAC failure, discard"]
  M2 -- "match" --> S1{"Received sequence number vs.<br/>stored value and window"}
  S1 -- "≤ stored" --> X3["Report anti-replay failure, discard"]
  S1 -- "> stored + window" --> X3
  S1 -- "within window" --> S2["Store received sequence number;<br/>remove padding if specified"]
  S2 --> D{"AE?"}
  D -- "yes" --> DEC["Decrypt data field"] --> OK["Return data, status = success"]
  D -- "no" --> OK
  T -- "Encryption only" --> DEC2["Decrypt data field;<br/>remove padding if specified"] --> OK

    These steps follow §4.2.4.66676869 Frames that are discarded can be archived for forensic investigation.6768 The data link protocol then discards the frame. Through the optional Verification Status Code of the relevant service, it tells the service user.91110

    Anti-replay in detail¶

    For an SA with authentication, both ends keep a sequence number, a sequence number window and the sequence number's location in the frame.27

    • The sender increments its sequence number by one for every frame of the SA.2865
    • The receiver accepts a frame only if the received number is greater than its stored value and no more than the window above it. Only then does it replace the stored value with the received one. Otherwise it reports an exception and discards the frame, leaving its stored value unchanged.2868
    • The window is a fixed positive value in the SA. It is sized for the delays and gaps expected on the RF link.29
    • The location is either the Sequence Number field (with a managed length), or, for counter-mode authenticated encryption, the IV field itself, in which case the Sequence Number field has zero length.3050
    • What a rollover to zero means is left to each mission.28

    Worked example: the receiver's sequence check (illustrative values)

    SA window = 10, stored value = 1000.

    Received SN Check Result Stored value afterwards
    1001 1000 < 1001 ≤ 1010 accepted 1001
    1001 not > 1001 rejected (repeat) 1001
    1005 1001 < 1005 ≤ 1011 accepted (frames 1002–1004 lost or delayed) 1005
    1003 not > 1005 rejected (arrived late) 1005
    1020 > 1005 + 10 rejected (outside window) 1005

    The last row shows why the window must suit the link.

    Inference

    Only accepted frames update the stored value.68 So if more consecutive frames are lost than the window allows, every later frame lies outside the window and is rejected. The stored value cannot catch up by itself. Recovering then means adjusting the SA's sequence number through SA management, which CCSDS 355.0-B-2 leaves to the Extended Procedures.46

    Interaction with COP-1 retransmission¶

    The data link standards fix the order of SDLS relative to the COP:

    • TC sending end: Frame Initialization, which reserves the security fields; then ApplySecurity; then FOP-1; then Frame Finalization.101
    • TC receiving end: FARM-1; then ProcessSecurity; then the rest of VC reception. This is the text as amended by Corrigendum 1 (October 2023) of the TC standard.103
    • USLP uses the same order with FOP/FARM for COP-1 or COP-P.108

    Inference

    Because ApplySecurity runs before the FOP, a frame resent by COP-1 keeps the sequence number it was first given. This is this wiki's reading of the processing order; the standards do not state it in these words, but their notes describe its consequence (below).

    Both the TC and the USLP standards warn about the consequence. Suppose an authenticated VC mixes sequence-controlled Type-AD frames with expedited Type-BD frames, and Type-AD frames must be retransmitted. The anti-replay check will then reject the resent frames, because their sequence numbers are lower than those of BD frames already accepted. They are falsely labelled security failures. Mixing AD and BD frames on an SDLS-secured VC is therefore generally not advised, unless BD frames are sent only after all AD frames have been accepted.104111

    How SDLS plugs into each data link protocol¶

    In every protocol, the presence and lengths of the Security Header and Trailer are managed parameters: per VC for TM and AOS, per VC or MAP for TC and USLP. A Security Header that is present means SDLS is in use.9893105112

    Protocol Where the Security Header goes Services fully protected Not protected
    TM After the Frame Secondary Header (or Primary Header)95 Packet, VCA. VC_FSH: authentication only16 VC_OCF, MC_FSH, MC_OCF, VCF, MCF16
    TC After the Segment Header (or Primary Header), only in Type-D frames99 MAPP, MAPA, VCP, VCA70 COP management (Type-C control frames carry no security fields), VCF, MCF7099
    AOS After the Insert Zone (or Primary Header)92 Packet, Bitstream, VCA18 VC_OCF, Insert, VCF, MCF18
    USLP After the Insert Zone (or Primary Header); not in frames with Protocol Control Command Flag = 1106 MAPP, MAP Octet Stream, MAPA19 (USLP issue 3 adds VCP and VCA107) COPs management, USLP_MC_OCF, Insert, VCF, MCF19

    In all four protocols SDLS authenticates some Primary Header fields but never encrypts headers. Apart from TM's VC_FSH, which can be authenticated, it does not protect data in auxiliary fields such as the OCF or the Insert Zone. It never protects frames that outside sources supply through the VCF and MCF services.16171819

    Where the call is made. In TM and AOS, ApplySecurity may be called from Virtual Channel Generation, which gives the most flexibility and allows one SA per VC, or from Virtual Channel Multiplexing, where several VCs can share an SA.9688 The FECF is always computed after SDLS. That is why SDLS has no interface with All Frames Generation.89 In TC and USLP the call is part of Virtual Channel Generation, in the fixed order shown above.101108

    Worked example: overhead of the baselines (illustrative calculation by this wiki)

    TC with AES-CMAC baseline. The Security Header is 6 octets and the MAC is 16 octets.78 The TC data field with SDLS may be at most 1024 octets minus the primary header (5), the Security Header, and the Segment Header (1), Security Trailer and FECF (2) if present.100 ECSS makes the FECF mandatory for TC frames, with or without SDLS.86 That leaves 1024 − 5 − 1 − 6 − 16 − 2 = 994 octets of user data per frame with a Segment Header, or 995 without one.

    AOS or USLP with the AES-GCM baseline. The Security Header is 14 octets and the MAC is 16 octets, 30 octets per frame in total.7980 In a 1115-octet AOS frame that is about 2.7 %; see the AOS page.

    The informative baseline (annex E)¶

    Annex E defines baseline implementations for interoperability testing and operation:77787980

    Protocol Service Algorithm Key IV Sequence Number MAC Header / Trailer
    TM Authenticated encryption AES-GCM 256 bits 96 bits, all transmitted none (IV serves) 128 bits 14 / 16 octets
    TC Authentication AES-CMAC 256 bits none 32 bits, all transmitted 128 bits 6 / 16 octets
    AOS Authenticated encryption AES-GCM 256 bits 96 bits none 128 bits 14 / 16 octets
    USLP Authenticated encryption AES-GCM 256 bits 96 bits none 128 bits 14 / 16 octets

    In every baseline the Pad Length field has zero length, because neither GCM nor CMAC needs padding. The mask has zeros for every header field not otherwise fixed by §4.2.2.6.2. GCM normally uses an incrementing counter as its IV, so a separate anti-replay sequence number is not needed.77 In USLP truncated frames, SDLS uses a 6-octet Security Header and a 16-octet Security Trailer. That matches the TC baseline sizes, and the truncated frame must then be at least 28 octets long. A separate SA is advised for truncated frames.113

    Limits and threats the standard names¶

    Annex B lists what SDLS does not do. It is listed here so that readers understand the protocol's scope:74

    • It gives no protection against denial of service on the channel, such as RF jamming.
    • It gives no protection against traffic-flow analysis. Identifiers in the clear, such as the SCID, VCID, MAP ID, OCF and COP directives, remain visible as metadata.
    • It has no key management protocol.
    • It does not protect COP control commands, or the CLCW and PLCW reports in the OCF.
    • A clear mode, if implemented, needs careful analysis of how it can be activated.
    • Encryption without authentication does not protect against data substitution.

    The same annex states what is at stake. Without authentication, unauthorized commands or software could be uploaded, or data could arrive from a source posing as the spacecraft. Without integrity, corrupted commands or telemetry could cause wrong actions or even loss of the mission. Without confidentiality, sensitive data could be disclosed. Security may also be needed at other layers, for example across ground networks. That is outside the scope of SDLS.7475

    Managed parameters, conformance and SANA¶

    • Managed parameters. Table 6-1 lists the SA data base parameters shown above. In addition, every managed parameter of the underlying data link protocol also applies to SDLS.71
    • Conformance. An implementer completes the PICS proforma of annex A. It requires support of at least one of TM, TC, AOS or USLP, and at least one of the three security services. The SA parameters are mandatory or conditional on the services supported, and ApplySecurity or ProcessSecurity is mandatory for the sender or receiver role.7273
    • SANA. SDLS defines no new registries.74
