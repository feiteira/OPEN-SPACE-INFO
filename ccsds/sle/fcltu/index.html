    SLE forward service: FCLTU¶

    The Forward CLTU (FCLTU) service lets a control centre hand complete Communications Link Transmission Units to a ground station, together with instructions on when to radiate them. The station then radiates each CLTU, bit for bit, on the telecommand uplink and reports back what happened.12 It is the lowest-level forward SLE service. The user does all the telecommand work above the physical layer: packets, segments, TC frames, COP-1 and CLTU encoding. The provider adds the carrier, the acquisition and idle sequences, the timing, and the uplink status that it reads from the spacecraft's CLCWs.3

    The higher-level alternative is the Forward Space Packet service (FSP), where the user hands over Space Packets and the provider builds the frames, runs COP-1 and generates the CLTUs before radiating them with the same function that provides FCLTU; the two are compared below.8384 For the concepts shared with the other SLE services (BIND, states, security, service instances) see the SLE overview. The CLTU format itself is on TC synchronization and channel coding, and the frames inside it on TC SDLP and COP-1.

    What the provider does¶

    The Forward TC Space Link Processing functional group consumes two data channels. One is the CLTU data channel from the user. The other is an OCF data channel, from which it extracts CLCWs to judge whether the physical channel is available (service management may tell it to ignore CLCWs). It then generates the acquisition and idle sequences required by the PLOP in effect, steers the antenna, modulates the CLTUs onto the RF carrier, and radiates them.3

    The reference model describes the same group in terms of ports. To produce FCLTU, the Forward TC Space Link Processing SLE-FG has a consumer CLTU-Transfer port facing the user, a supplier Fwd-RF-Transfer port facing the spacecraft, a supplier management port TCSpLinkProc-Mgmt facing Complex Management, and a consumer Rtn-MCOCF-Transfer or Rtn-VCOCF-Transfer port through which the CLCWs arrive.94 Its functions are to consume the CLTU data channel, consume the OCF channels and judge from the CLCW whether the physical channel is available, generate the acquisition and idle sequences of the PLOP in effect on the asynchronous physical channel, inject the CLTUs, use the antenna steering of the ground element, and modulate and radiate.95 The users that the model names for the CLTU-Transfer port are an MUE and the Forward CLTU Generation SLE-FG, the next group up, which turns TC frames into CLTUs.92

    Delivery. In the reference model, forward online delivery happens during the forward space link session and cannot extend beyond it. Forward offline delivery would transfer a complete data set into the SLE system, a transfer that must be finished before the data are transmitted to the spacecraft during the session, at a time set by SLE management.96 FCLTU defines only the online mode; offline is outside the scope of the current specification.5

    Production versus provision. Production configuration parameters such as bit rate and modulation index affect complex resources, so only service management may change them. A user may change some provision parameters through operations. If the station and the mission user agree, the user may also change production parameters indirectly, by asking complex management to act through CLTU-THROW-EVENT.14

    The CLTU being radiated¶

    A CLTU is a start sequence, a series of codewords that hold one or more TC transfer frames (plus fill), and, for BCH coding, a tail sequence.15

    Field values from CCSDS 231.0-B-4.151617 With LDPC coding the start sequence is the 64-bit pattern 0x034776C7272895B0, the codewords are 128 or 512 bits, and a tail sequence is optional (128 bits for LDPC (128,64)).151817

    Worked example (Solar Orbiter frame limit, illustrative coding). The maximum Solar Orbiter TC frame is 256 octets, or 2,048 bits.19 If BCH coding is used, the frame needs 37 codewords of 56 information bits each (37 × 56 = 2,072 bits, so 24 bits of fill). The CLTU is then 2 + 37 × 8 + 8 = 306 octets. That is well inside FCLTU's maximum-cltu-length range of 12 to 4,096 octets.20 At an illustrative 2,000 bit/s uplink, the CLTU takes 2,448 bits ÷ 2,000 bit/s ≈ 1.22 s to radiate. The coding scheme Solar Orbiter actually uses is not stated in the sections of its ICD consulted here.

    Production, PLOP and the CLCW¶

    Carrier modulation modes and the PLOPs¶

    The physical layer operations procedures (PLOPs) of CCSDS 231.0 define how the carrier is modulated around CLTUs (see also TC coding and the CLTU and, for the carrier itself, Radio frequency, modulation and ranging). Missions whose planning began after September 2010 must use PLOP-2.21 There are four carrier modulation modes:22

    Mode Carrier is modulated with
    CMM-1 nothing (unmodulated carrier only)
    CMM-2 the acquisition sequence (alternating ones and zeros; preferred minimum 16 octets)
    CMM-3 a CLTU
    CMM-4 the idle sequence (alternating ones and zeros)

    Sources: carrier modulation modes and sequences.2223

    FCLTU binds these modes to its own parameters:24

    stateDiagram-v2
    direction LR
    state "PLOP-1 (per CLTU)" as P1 {
        [*] --> CMM1a: carrier on,<br/>unmodulated
        CMM1a --> CMM2a: CLTU received
        CMM2a --> CMM4a: acquisition-sequence-length<br/>octets sent
        CMM4a --> CMM3a: plop-1-idle-sequence-length<br/>octets (if > 0)
        CMM3a --> CMM4b: CLTU sent
        CMM4b --> CMM1a: trailing idle (if > 0);<br/>wait delay-time
    }
    stateDiagram-v2
    direction LR
    state "PLOP-2 (per session)" as P2 {
        [*] --> CMM1: uplink sweep
        CMM1 --> CMM2: sweep complete
        CMM2 --> CMM4: acquisition sequence sent
        CMM4 --> CMM3: CLTU available and<br/>previous delay-time expired
        CMM3 --> CMM4: CLTU sent;<br/>idle until delay-time expires
    }

    Diagrams based on §3.6.2.8 of the FCLTU specification.24

    • PLOP-1: the carrier stays unmodulated (CMM-1) until a CLTU arrives. For each CLTU the provider sends an acquisition sequence of acquisition-sequence-length octets (CMM-2), an optional leading idle sequence of plop-1-idle-sequence-length octets (CMM-4), the CLTU (CMM-3), and an optional trailing idle sequence (CMM-4). The carrier then returns to CMM-1 until the latest of three events: the delay-time expiring (measured from the last bit sent), the next CLTU becoming available, or the end of the session. Both lengths may be set to zero, so that a mission which does not fully follow PLOP-1 can put its own acquisition and idle bits inside the CLTU data.24
    • PLOP-2: after the uplink sweep (CMM-1) the provider sends the acquisition sequence once, then idle (CMM-4) for as long as needed. Each CLTU is sent (CMM-3) as soon as it is available and the previous CLTU's delay-time has expired, with idle in between. PLOP-2 ends with a return to CMM-1, but no standard mechanism decides when.24

    The idle sequence lets the spacecraft keep bit synchronisation when no CLTU is being sent. Under PLOP-2, CCSDS 231.0 recommends inserting at least one octet of idle between CLTUs. This avoids a rare synchronisation lockout, where a missed start sequence plus a false start pattern spanning the end of one CLTU would make the receiver miss the start of the next.2325

    Production status¶

    FCLTU production status has four values. In ASN.1 they are operational (0), configured (1), interrupted (2) and halted (3). Note that the numbering differs from the return services.1126

    Value Meaning
    configured Equipment assigned, but not yet able to radiate CLTUs (initial value)
    operational Configured, acquisition complete, able to radiate
    interrupted Stopped by a fault that may be transient
    halted Taken out of service by management, for example after a non-transient fault or to reassign resources

    [*] --> Configured
    Configured --> Operational: sweep done (+ RF available / bit lock if required)<br/>notify 'production operational'
    Operational --> Interrupted: fault, or CLCW shows No RF / No Bit Lock<br/>when required — notify 'production interrupted'
    Interrupted --> Operational: fault cleared — notify 'production operational'
    Configured --> Halted: operator — notify 'production halted'
    Operational --> Halted: operator
    Interrupted --> Halted: operator
    Halted --> Configured: operator restores configuration (no notification)

    Diagram based on figure B-1, table B-1 and B2.3–B2.4 of the FCLTU specification.2728

    The CLCW drives two optional checks, enabled by rf-available-required and bit-lock-required.

    • PLOP-1: production becomes operational at the end of the uplink sweep. If rf-available-required is 'yes', the CLCW must also show RF available. While the carrier is unmodulated, acquiring, radiating or idling, production drops to 'interrupted' if a required flag shows a problem. For bit lock, this check applies from the end of the acquisition sequence onwards.28
    • PLOP-2: the transition at the end of the sweep requires bit-lock-required to be 'no'. Otherwise production becomes operational during CMM-2 or CMM-4, as soon as a CLCW reports bit lock.29

    Uplink status is the provider's summary of the last CLCW it received:3026

    uplink-status Code Condition on the last CLCW
    uplink status not available 0 No CLCW received yet
    no rf available 1 No RF Available = 1
    no bit lock 2 No RF Available = 0, No Bit Lock = 1
    nominal 3 Both flags 0

    Those two flags are bits 16 and 17 of the CLCW (see the CLCW diagram on the return services page).31 The provider learns which telemetry channel carries the CLCW from the clcw-global-VCID and clcw-physical-channel parameters, either of which may be 'not configured'.3233

    The FCLTU GvcId type allows spacecraft identifiers 0 to 65535, but its ASN.1 comment gives 0 to 255 as the range for TM frames.34 The RCF and ROCF modules give 0 to 1023 for TM, which matches the 10-bit TM spacecraft identifier.35 A TM spacecraft such as Solar Orbiter (SCID 650) is therefore encodable in clcw-global-VCID, even though it lies outside the range stated in the FCLTU comment.

    CLTU-START¶

    START moves the provider to 'active' and fixes the first CLTU number.39

    Parameter Inv. Ret. Meaning
    invoke-ID M M Echoed
    first-cltu-identification M The cltu-identification expected in the first CLTU-TRANSFER-DATA

    CLTU-STOP¶

    STOP takes the provider back to 'ready'. Buffered CLTUs that have not started radiating are discarded. A CLTU that is being radiated is completed, and its requested report is still sent as an ASYNC-NOTIFY. Depending on the implementation, one more CLTU may get radiated after the STOP arrives. If STOP is rejected, nothing is discarded.48

    CLTU-TRANSFER-DATA¶

    invoke-ID M M Echoed
    cltu-identification M M Monotonically increasing sequence number; the return holds the next expected value
    earliest-radiation-time M 'null' or the earliest time the first bit may be radiated
    latest-radiation-time M 'null' or the latest time radiation may begin
    delay-time M Minimum gap in µs between this CLTU and the next

    result, diagnostic M, C

    The rules that make FCLTU deterministic:

    • Sequence numbers. The first CLTU after START must carry first-cltu-identification, and each accepted CLTU increments it. A positive return carries the invocation value plus one. A negative return carries the value the provider still expects.51
    • Radiation window. With a null earliest time, the CLTU is processed as soon as the previous CLTU's delay has elapsed and production is operational. With a null latest time, the CLTU waits as long as the instance is active and production is not halted. If a latest time is given and production is interrupted, processing is deferred until production recovers or the latest time passes; in the second case the CLTU is discarded. Equal earliest and latest times mean "radiate at exactly this time". A CLTU that has not started by its latest time raises 'sldu expired' and is not radiated.52
    • Delay. delay-time is a guard time after the CLTU, and must not be below the managed minimum-delay-time. Its exact effect depends on the PLOP (above).2449
    • Buffering. Accepted CLTUs are buffered and processed in the order received, according to their times and delays. cltu-buffer-available does not include any small extra buffer at the modulator input, which belongs to production.4953

    CLTU-THROW-EVENT¶

    THROW-EVENT asks the provider to pass an event to SLE complex management. The standard's example is changing the uplink bit rate.     Buffering, termination and protocol abort¶

    The service package states the provider's CLTU buffer size in octets, and only complete CLTUs are buffered.68 The conforming minimum is 4,194,304 octets, equivalent to 1,024 CLTUs of 4,096 octets each.37

    When an association ends, production stops at once, except for a CLTU that is being radiated, and all buffered CLTUs are discarded. The one exception is a protocol abort (loss of the communications link) when protocol-abort-mode is 'continue'. Buffered CLTUs are then kept and radiated as if nothing had happened. Notifications produced meanwhile are not delivered or queued (logging them is recommended). After re-binding, the user can rebuild the picture from a status report, and must choose a first-cltu-identification above any CLTU still in the buffer.6061 Sequence numbers that relate notifications to operations are not reset by a protocol abort.61

    Inconsistent naming in the standard

    §4.1.5.3 and the conformance matrix call the discarding value of protocol-abort-mode 'flush' (the default behaviour if the parameter is not supported). Table 3-11 and the ASN.1 call it 'abort' (abort (0)).61373220 This wiki treats them as the same value.

    Security¶

    Commanding is the most sensitive SLE service. FCLTU adds one integrity mechanism of its own: the monotonically increasing cltu-identification. A CLTU without the expected number is rejected, which constrains a third party's ability to inject extra commands. The strict operation sequence of the state table constrains a third party's ability to take over an instance without being detected.69 Everything else (authentication of BIND or of every PDU, confidentiality, denial-of-service protection, auditing) relies on the common SLE mechanisms, the underlying network and bilateral agreements. The standard warns that a hijacked forward service could be used to subvert or destroy a spacecraft.6970 See SDLS for protection of the TC frames themselves, which the FCLTU service carries transparently.49

    SCOS-2000, the NIS and throw events¶

    Throw events in the MIB¶

    SCOS-2000 has supported throw events as commands since release 5.0. The import ICD defines two special values of the command type CCF_CTYPE.7172

    • 'T' (SLE throw event): sent to the SLE service provider (the ground station equipment) and not uplinked. Its CDF entries define the Event ID and the following fields according to the NIS/NCTRS MCS interface ICD.
    • 'N' (NIS throw event): executed by the NIS itself and not forwarded to the station. Its CDF entries follow the NIS Throw Events ICD.

    The referenced documents are EGOS-NIS-NCTR-ICD-0002 "ICD NIS/NCTRS Volume 2 – MCS I/F" issue 4.0.2 and EGOS-NIS-NIS-ICD-0003 "NIS Throw Events ICD" issue 1.3. Neither is in this library.73 Throw events cannot be blocked with other commands in a sequence or command source.71

    The Solar Orbiter MIB (release 25925PFM, 2025-09-16) has no 'T' commands and six 'N' commands. All six use the packet header NOS2KHDR ("NIS Throw Events"), type 251, subtype 1 and APID 1964. That header has no PCDF fields.74
